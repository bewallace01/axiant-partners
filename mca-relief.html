What Is MCA Relief?

MCA relief is the process of getting out of an expensive merchant cash advance by replacing those punishing daily or weekly debits with financing you can actually live with. A merchant cash advance is fast to get, but its true cost—expressed as a factor rate rather than an APR—is often the equivalent of triple-digit interest. When one advance isn't enough and a business “stacks” a second, third, or fourth, the combined daily payments can swallow most of a day's revenue. That is the MCA cycle, and it is where most businesses get trapped.

Relief means breaking that cycle. Depending on your situation, that looks like consolidating multiple advances into a single payment, refinancing the balance into a longer-term loan or line of credit, or negotiating a restructured payoff with the funder. The goal is the same: stop the daily bleed and put your cash flow back under your control. If you're not sure which path fits, start by reading how to get out of an MCA, then apply to see your options.

Consolidating Stacked MCAs Into One Payment

Stacking is what turns a manageable advance into a crisis. Each new advance adds its own daily debit on top of the last, until four or five funders are pulling from your account every morning. MCA consolidation rolls those advances into a single financing product with one payment—typically weekly or monthly instead of daily—so the total leaving your account each week drops and your cash flow steadies.

Consolidation can extend how long you carry the balance, but for most businesses the immediate relief in weekly outflow is what keeps the doors open. Qualifying depends on your current revenue, the number and size of the advances, and lender criteria. Read how to get out of stacked MCAs without bankruptcy, then apply to see what you qualify for.

Refinancing Daily Debits Into a Term Loan

The cleanest exit, when you qualify, is to refinance the advance into a conventional term loan or line of credit. Instead of a factor rate and daily debits, you get an APR and a single predictable monthly payment over a longer term. For a business with steady revenue and decent credit, that can dramatically cut both the effective cost and the weekly cash drain.

Timing matters: it's far easier to refinance before you miss payments than after. Our 30-day refinance playbook walks through the exact steps and documents, and term loan vs merchant cash advance shows why the swap saves money. Get matched with refinance lenders that fit your file.

MCA Relief vs Other Paths

Getting out of an advance isn't one-size-fits-all. Here's how the common routes compare so you can see which fits your situation.

Consolidation vs Refinance

Consolidation: best when you carry two or more stacked advances and need one lower payment fast. Refinance: best when you have steady revenue and decent credit and want to swap daily debits for a fixed monthly payment at a lower cost. Many businesses use consolidation first for breathing room, then refinance as their profile improves. Consolidate stacked MCAs.

Refinance vs Restructure

Refinance: replaces the advance entirely with lower-cost financing—the cleanest long-term fix. Restructure: negotiates reduced or extended terms with the existing funder—useful for short-term breathing room when refinancing isn't yet available. See every path out of an MCA.

MCA Relief vs Bankruptcy

Relief: keeps your business open and your revenue yours by consolidating, refinancing, or restructuring. Bankruptcy: a last resort with lasting consequences. Almost always worth exhausting relief options first. Read the refinancing business debt guide.

Do You Qualify for MCA Relief?

Consolidation and refinancing lean on your current revenue and the health of your business—not just credit. Lenders that specialize in MCA payoff typically look for:

  • Ongoing revenue—consistent deposits the new financing can be repaid from. Strong, steady deposits open up more relief options and better terms.
  • Time in business—generally at least 6–12 months of operating history, though requirements vary by lender.
  • A clear advance picture—the balances, payments, and funders behind your current MCAs, so the payoff can be structured correctly.
  • No active default—it's far easier to refinance before you miss payments than after, so acting early matters.

Risks & Considerations

Relief is about trading a bad structure for a better one—but go in with eyes open:

  • Term length—Consolidating or refinancing usually lowers your weekly outflow, but can extend how long you carry the balance. Lower payment, longer payoff—weigh both.
  • Act before default—Relief is dramatically easier to obtain before you fall behind. Once an advance is in default, options narrow and costs rise.
  • Avoid re-stacking—The worst outcome is taking a new advance to escape the old one. Real relief replaces the advance with lower-cost financing—it doesn't add another layer. See how to get out of bad business debt.
  • Read the agreement—Some advances carry confessions of judgment or aggressive terms. Know what you signed; review red flags in MCA agreements.

We help you weigh total cost and cash flow impact before you commit, so relief actually moves you forward.

What is MCA relief?

How do you get out of a merchant cash advance?

Can you consolidate multiple stacked MCAs?

Will getting out of an MCA hurt my credit?
