Security Guard Invoice Factoring: Rates, Advances and How It Works

Guards get paid weekly and clients pay in 30 to 60 days. What factoring those invoices costs, what funders check, and when it is the wrong tool

← Back to Security Guard Company Financing Articles

Updated November 04, 2026

Quick answer

Security guard invoice factoring turns an unpaid client invoice into cash for payroll. Funders commonly advance 80-95% of the invoice within days and charge a fee of about 1-5% of its value, then release the balance when the client pays on its net-30 to net-60 terms. FeeAbout 1-5% of invoice value

Later invoicesCan fund in as little as 24 hours once verified
Client termsNet-30 is easier to fund than net-60

These are conventional ranges, not anyone's quote. Your clients' credit, invoice size, monthly volume and how long clients take to pay decide where you land.

How Factoring Works on a Guard Contract

  1. You bill the client for hours worked at each post, with signed timesheets or the client's approval behind it.
  2. The funder verifies that the invoice is real, that the service was delivered, and that nothing is disputed.
  3. You receive the advance, commonly in time for the next payroll run.
  4. The client pays the funder under a notice of assignment. Under UCC 9-406, once the client has that notice, it settles the invoice by paying the assignee rather than you.
  5. The funder releases the reserve, which is the balance of the invoice minus the fee.

Clients see this process, so tell your larger accounts before the first notice arrives. Factoring is common in the guard industry, but a surprise letter to a property manager is not a good first impression.

Worked Example: A $120,000 Month on Net-45

Illustrative numbers, not a quote. A guard company bills $120,000 a month to commercial clients on net-45 terms and factors all of it at an 85% advance and a 3% fee.

  • Advance within days of billing: $102,000
  • Fee at 3% of the invoice value: $3,600
  • Reserve released after the client pays: $14,400

That $3,600 on $102,000 for 45 days works out to roughly 29% annualized, so factoring is not cheap money. It competes with a missed payroll, not with a bank line. Guard margins are thin because labor is most of the cost, so run the fee against the margin on each contract before factoring all of them. If clients pay faster than the fee schedule assumes, the true cost falls; if they pay slower, tiered fees can push it higher.

Federal, State and Municipal Guard Contracts

Government clients usually pay reliably, which funders like, but assigning those payments takes more paperwork.

Federal contracts follow FAR Subpart 32.8. The contract must specify payments aggregating $1,000 or more, the assignment must go to a bank, trust company or other financing institution, it is generally limited to one party, and the assignee must send written notice to the contracting officer, any surety and the disbursing officer. Confirm that your registration on SAM.gov is current and that your funder has handled federal assignments before.

State and municipal contracts have their own rules, and some restrict assignment. Ask the funder and read the contract before you count on the advance. The same goes for private contracts: UCC 9-406 makes many assignment restrictions ineffective, but it has exceptions and does not replace a review of your agreement.

What Funders Check on a Guard Company

  • Client credit and concentration. Funders may cap how much of your receivables can sit with one client.
  • Clean invoices. Signed timesheets and clear rates reduce the disputes that hold up funding.
  • Payroll taxes. Funders check that employment taxes are current, because a federal tax lien can reach receivables. Under 26 USC 6323, protection for later advances is limited to about 45 days after a lien is filed.
  • Existing liens. A blanket UCC filing from an earlier lender may already cover your receivables and has to be released or subordinated.
  • Existing advances. A merchant cash advance can block factoring outright until it is dealt with.
  • License and insurance. A lapsed state license or policy puts the contract, and so the invoice, at risk.

Wages are the reason timing matters. Federal wage and hour rules apply whether or not the client has paid you.

Terms to Read Before You Sign

  • Recourse. With recourse factoring you are responsible if the client never pays. Non-recourse moves covered credit losses to the funder for a higher fee.
  • Fee schedule. Ask whether the fee is flat or rises every 10 or 15 days an invoice stays open. On net-60 clients that difference is large.
  • Minimums and term. Monthly volume minimums and long contracts can cost more than the fee if a big client leaves.
  • All-invoices clauses. Some agreements require you to factor every invoice, including the fast payers you do not need to fund.
  • Exit terms. Check notice periods, early termination fees and how the UCC filing gets released.

The FTC has warned small businesses about misrepresented financing costs, so get the full fee schedule in writing.

When Factoring Is the Wrong Tool

Factoring fits a timing gap on good invoices. It does not fix a contract priced too low, because the fee comes straight out of an already thin margin. It is also a poor fit if most of your clients pay in under two weeks, or if one client makes up nearly all of your billing. In those cases a line of credit, or repricing the contract at renewal, usually does more. If daily advances are already draining the account, deal with those first using MCA consolidation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does security guard invoice factoring cost?

Commonly about 1-5% of the invoice value, with an advance of 80-95% of the invoice. The cost depends on your clients' credit, your monthly volume and how long clients take to pay. These are typical ranges across funders, not a quote.

Can a new security guard company use factoring?

Often yes, because funders look mostly at your clients' ability to pay and not only your own credit or time in business. You still need real contracts, clean invoices, and current licensing and payroll taxes.

How fast does factoring fund guard payroll?

Setup often takes a few business days. After that, individual invoices can fund in as little as 24 hours once the funder verifies them.

Can guard companies factor government invoices?

Often, but with more paperwork. Federal contracts follow FAR Subpart 32.8 on assignment of claims, including written notice to the agency. State and municipal contracts have their own rules, and some restrict assignment.

Is payroll funding the same as factoring?

Many payroll funding products are factoring under another name: the funder advances against issued invoices. A smaller number are true payroll advances underwritten on your billing history and pay runs. Ask which one you are being offered.

Will my clients know I factor my invoices?

Usually yes. Standard factoring sends the client a notice of assignment telling it to pay the funder. Some funders offer non-notification programs, typically to larger and stronger companies.

Sources & Further Reading

  • FAR Subpart 32.8: Assignment of Claims — Conditions for assigning payments on a federal contract: payments of $1,000 or more, a bank, trust company or other financing institution, generally one party, and written notice to the agency and surety.
  • UCC 9-406: Discharge of Account Debtor — After notice of assignment, the customer pays the assignee, and many contract terms restricting assignment of an account are ineffective.
  • 26 USC 6323: Validity and Priority of Federal Tax Liens — How a filed federal tax lien ranks against accounts receivable financing, including the 45-day limit on protected advances.
  • IRS: Understanding Employment Taxes — The withholding and deposit duties every employer of guards carries, which funders check before advancing against invoices.
  • US Department of Labor: Fair Labor Standards Act — Federal wage and hour rules, including overtime - the obligations that make guard payroll non-negotiable and time-sensitive.
  • SAM.gov — The federal registration system for contractors - where public-sector guard contracts are advertised and awarded.
  • FTC: Protecting Small Businesses Seeking Financing — The FTC's guidance to lenders, brokers and merchant cash advance providers on misrepresented costs, unlawful collection and false claims of government affiliation.
