What’s driving it—and how to qualify for better

Get Matched with Lenders

1. Credit Score or Risk Tier

Lenders price by risk. Lower credit scores mean higher rates—or in some cases, a decline. You may have assumed you were in a better tier than the lender assigned. Credit pulls can also show something you didn’t expect: an error, an old account, or a score lower than what you see on free monitoring. Lenders use their own models and may weight factors differently.

Fix: Check your credit before applying. Dispute errors. Pay down revolving debt to improve utilization. If your score is borderline, improving it by 20—30 points can sometimes move you to a better tier. See how to prequalify for a business loan to see estimated rates without multiple hard pulls. Different lenders have different tier cutoffs—get matched to compare.

2. Product or Program Choice

Unsecured loans and lines typically have higher rates than secured. Working capital loans, MCAs, and revenue-based financing are often priced above traditional term loans or SBA. If you chose a product that’s inherently higher-cost—or the lender only offers that product for your profile—the rate will reflect it. SBA loans have rate caps but still vary by lender and program; non-SBA options have wider spreads.

Fix: Understand the product. Compare term loans, SBA vs. line of credit, and MCA vs. working capital loan. If you can offer collateral, a secured loan may have a lower rate. If you qualify for SBA, it often offers better terms than alternative lenders. See how to compare business loan offers.

3. Market Rates Have Risen

Rates follow the broader market. If the Fed or Treasury rates have moved up since you last shopped, or since you saw a quoted rate, new offers will be higher. Lenders price off a base rate (e.g., Prime, SOFR) plus a spread. When the base rises, your rate rises—even if your credit hasn’t changed.

Fix: Lock your rate when you have an approval if the lender allows it. If you’re rate-sensitive, time your application when possible. Compare current offers—don’t assume last year’s rate is still available. Use our loan calculator to model different rate scenarios.

4. Cash Flow or Financials Weaker Than Expected

Lenders look at debt service coverage, revenue trend, and profitability. If your financials are weaker than you assumed—or the lender interprets them more conservatively—they may price you in a higher risk tier. Declining revenue, thin margins, or high existing debt service can push rates up.

Fix: Strengthen your financials before applying if you can. Pay down other debt to improve DSCR. Ensure your P&L and tax returns tell a clear, consistent story. If you had a one-time dip, explain it. See how to prequalify for a business loan to understand what lenders see. Get matched—some lenders are more aggressive on pricing for certain profiles.

5. Industry or Business Model Risk

Some industries and business models are priced higher—restaurants, retail, startups, or businesses with lumpy revenue. Lenders apply industry risk adjustments. If your industry is considered higher-risk, the spread will be larger even when credit and cash flow are solid.

Fix: Shop lenders that specialize in your industry—they may offer better terms than generalists. Emphasize stability: tenure, contract revenue, or recurring income. See how to compare business loan offers. Get matched to reach lenders that work with your profile.

6. Loan Size or Term

Smaller loans and shorter terms sometimes have higher effective rates—there’s a minimum cost to underwrite and service. Very large loans can also carry pricing premiums if the lender is concentration-focused. The “sweet spot” varies by lender and product.

Fix: If you can consolidate or increase the loan size, see if that improves pricing. Extending the term may lower the payment but can increase total interest—run the numbers with our loan calculator. Compare offers at different amounts and terms. See how to compare business loan offers.

7. You Didn’t Shop

Rates vary widely by lender. If you only applied to one, you may have gotten that lender’s standard pricing—which might be high for your profile. Different lenders have different appetites, cost structures, and tier cutoffs. One may offer you Prime + 2% while another offers Prime + 3.5% for the same deal.

Fix: Get multiple offers. Use how to prequalify for a business loan to compare without hurting your credit. Get matched to reach multiple lenders with one application. Compare total cost—APR, fees, term—not just the stated rate. See how to compare business loan offers.

What to Do Right Now

If your rate is higher than expected: (1) Ask the lender why—credit tier, program, or something specific? (2) Shop. Get at least 2—3 offers. (3) Improve what you can—credit, collateral, financials—and reapply in a few months if the rate is a dealbreaker. (4) Compare total cost, not just rate: fees, term, and prepayment matter. Rate Driver Breakdown You Can Actually Influence

Loan pricing is a risk signal, not a random number. Lenders price for repayment uncertainty, documentation quality, and structure fit. If your offer is higher than expected, separate uncontrollable market factors from controllable borrower factors. Controllables include statement stability, debt service coverage quality, collateral clarity, and consistency across submitted documents.

Request a pricing rationale by component. Many lenders can disclose whether the premium came from leverage, concentration risk, volatility, or limited operating history. Once you know the driver, you can target improvements instead of guessing.

  • File quality: reconciled statements, clean debt schedule, and accurate use-of-funds narrative.
  • Cash strength: stable balances, stronger margin trend, and lower payment stress.
  • Structure fit: match product term to asset life and cash-cycle duration.
  • Competitive process: compare matched lenders, not random channels with different risk appetites.

90-Day Pricing Improvement Plan

Build a short cycle plan focused on measurable underwriting upgrades: reduce utilization volatility, improve receivable collection timing, and document one-time anomalies that distorted recent statements. Even a quarter of cleaner performance can move your risk tier in the next credit decision.

When re-engaging lenders, submit a memo showing what changed and why repayment risk is lower today. That context often improves final pricing more than a simple re-application.

Rate Improvement Scorecard for Next Credit Cycle

To reduce pricing in your next financing cycle, convert qualitative advice into measurable targets. Track debt-service coverage trend, average daily balance stability, receivable concentration, and reporting timeliness. Lenders price confidence. The cleaner and more predictable your profile, the lower the risk premium they usually require.

Use a quarterly scorecard with baseline, target, and current status for each metric. Share this proactively with your financing partners when you re-engage. Showing disciplined improvement often improves both pricing and structure flexibility.

  • Coverage quality: sustained DSCR buffer above minimum underwriting threshold.
  • Balance stability: reduce end-of-month volatility that signals liquidity strain.
  • Portfolio clarity: simplify outstanding debt mix and remove expensive overlap.
  • Reporting quality: consistent, timely statements with reconciled schedules.

Application Timing Strategy to Capture Better Terms

Timing can materially change pricing outcomes. Apply when recent statements reflect your strongest controllable performance period and before temporary headwinds distort metrics. If you know a short-term dip is coming, consider securing terms earlier rather than waiting for weaker trailing data to enter underwriting.

Good timing does not replace strong fundamentals, but it can prevent avoidable pricing penalties and improve final lender confidence.

Pricing Negotiation Prep: What to Bring to the Table

Negotiating rates effectively requires evidence, not pressure. Prepare a lender packet that includes updated financials, debt schedule cleanup, recent performance highlights, and an explanation of how requested structure reduces repayment risk. If you can show improved predictability and lower volatility, lenders have a reason to revisit pricing assumptions.

Ask for a term-by-term explanation of rate drivers: leverage, collateral quality, cash-flow variability, and industry risk. Then respond with targeted mitigants. For example, if variability is the concern, present stronger working-capital controls and rolling forecast discipline. If leverage is the issue, propose right-sized request amount or staged funding tied to performance milestones.

Also compare total economics, not just rate. Sometimes fee reductions, covenant flexibility, or prepayment improvements create more value than a small rate concession. A structured negotiation checklist improves outcomes and prevents accepting terms that look cheaper but behave worse in practice.

Pricing Review Cadence With Lenders

Set a quarterly lender review cadence even after funding. Share operational improvements, updated metrics, and refinancing intent when profile strength improves. Consistent communication can unlock repricing or better renewal terms sooner than waiting for maturity.

Lender Targeting Model to Avoid Mispriced Quotes

Mispriced quotes often come from applying to lenders whose credit boxes do not match your profile. Build a targeting model using industry appetite, collateral preference, deal size range, and documentation expectations. Submit only where your profile is likely to be treated as core, not edge-case, business.

This reduces noisy quotes and improves pricing consistency. Better channel fit often lowers total cost as much as metric improvements alone.

Execution Checklist and Monthly Review Rhythm

Use a monthly financing review rhythm to keep decisions anchored to operating reality. Start each month with an updated cash forecast, debt-service view, and variance commentary versus prior plan. Confirm whether financing assumptions still match current conditions and identify any terms that need proactive lender communication. This operating discipline reduces surprises and protects optionality for future capital decisions.

At each review, update a short action log with owners and due dates. Include top three risks, top three mitigation actions, and explicit trigger points for escalation. Teams that consistently run this cadence usually reduce financing friction over time because they present cleaner data, tighter narratives, and better risk controls.

  • Forecast check: refresh expected vs stress scenarios with current data.
  • Debt check: validate payment burden, covenant headroom, and renewal timeline.
  • Control check: confirm documentation quality and version consistency.
  • Escalation check: trigger lender outreach early when thresholds are at risk.

Documenting this process creates a reliable operating record that improves underwriting confidence. Over multiple cycles, disciplined borrowers typically access better pricing, stronger terms, and faster decision timelines because lenders see repeatable execution quality rather than one-off preparation.

Rate Improvement Next Steps

Set quarterly goals for file quality, cash stability, and lender targeting, then document progress so pricing discussions are supported by evidence.

Small improvements in reporting quality and lender-fit targeting often compound into materially better pricing over the next financing cycle.

Pricing Discipline Summary

Better pricing is usually the result of sustained preparation, not one negotiation call. Keep a recurring cadence that improves underwriting clarity: reconciled statements, stable cash management, accurate debt schedules, and a clear narrative connecting capital use to repayment strength. The more predictable your profile, the less uncertainty lenders need to price into terms.

When performance improves, communicate those changes with evidence and timing. A short lender update memo with trend charts and risk controls can reposition your file and support more favorable decisions.
