How to Build Business Credit in 7 Steps

Business credit files at Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business and Equifax Business only grow when someone reports a payment in the business name. Everything below is about getting reporting accounts open in the right order, under the right identity, and then paying them in a way the scores reward.

1. Form the LLC or corporationWeek 1The entity is what the bureaus build a file on
2. Get an EIN (free from the IRS)Week 1Business accounts report under the EIN, not your SSN
3. Open a business bank account; use one name, address and phone everywhereWeeks 1-2Mismatched details split your history across files
4. Get a D-U-N-S numberDays 15-30It is how Dun & Bradstreet ties payments to your company
5. Open 2-3 net-30 vendor accounts that reportDays 15-30PAYDEX needs 3+ payment experiences from 2+ vendors

The order matters. A vendor account opened before you have a D-U-N-S number, or under a slightly different business name, may never land in your file. For the month-by-month timeline, see how long it takes to build business credit.

What Is the Easiest Way to Establish Business Credit?

Net-30 vendor accounts. Suppliers will open a small account on a new EIN when a bank will not, and if they report, each paid invoice becomes a payment experience on your file. Buy things you already use: supplies, shipping, fuel, parts. Ask each vendor in writing which bureaus it reports to, because one that reports nowhere does nothing for you.

Then be patient for about a month. Most net-30 bills are not due until day 30, and Dun & Bradstreet will not give you a PAYDEX until at least three payment experiences from at least two vendors have reported. Pay early when you can: 80 is on time, and scores above 80 reflect paying before terms.

Why Isn't My Business Credit Growing?

If you set things up a while ago and the file is still thin, it is almost always one of these five.

1. Nothing Reporting in the Business Name

If all your financing is in your personal name, or your lenders do not report to the business bureaus, the business file stays thin or empty. Open accounts in the business name that report: vendor trade credit, a business credit card, or a small line of credit. Use them and pay on time.

2. Personal and Business Credit Mixed

If you use your SSN for everything, or a lender reports a business account to your personal report, the business file gets nothing and your personal file gets cluttered. Use the EIN and business name on business accounts, confirm the lender reports to the business bureaus, and keep business and personal spending separate.

3. High Utilization or Late Payments

High balances against your limits and late payments hurt business scores the same way they hurt personal ones. Pay on time every time, keep utilization under 30% (lower is better), and pay balances down before you apply for new credit so the file looks its best when a lender pulls it.

4. Wrong Entity or Inconsistent Info

If your business name, address or EIN differs across applications, the bureaus may not match the accounts to one file. Use the exact legal name and EIN everywhere, register with the bureaus if your business is not in their system, and correct old information. Check your files at month 3 and month 6.

5. Not Enough Time or Accounts

One account, or a file that is a few months old, will be thin. Get to two or three reporting trade lines, use them regularly, and give it time: a first score typically shows up in 3-6 months, and a file strong enough for larger limits takes 12 months or more.

Can a New LLC Get a Business Loan Before It Has Business Credit?

Yes, but for roughly the first two years the lender is mostly looking at you. Here is what carries the decision as the business ages:

  • Under 6 months: your personal credit, cash in the bank, and collateral such as equipment or a vehicle.
  • 6-24 months: bank deposits and their trend, personal credit, and a clean business file.
  • 2 years and up: business tax returns and cash flow, and this is where business scores start to carry weight.

That is why the usual first loans are equipment financing, where the machine is the collateral, and SBA microloans. A loan that reports to the business bureaus does double duty: it funds the purchase and adds a different account type to your file. See small business loans for a new LLC and startup loans with no revenue for the options, or startup financing for every option by stage.

Once you have six months of deposits, working capital loans and a business line of credit come into play, and a line that reports is one of the better accounts you can add to the file.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I build business credit?

Form the LLC or corporation, get a free EIN from the IRS, open a business bank account and list one address and phone everywhere. Then get a D-U-N-S number and open two or three net-30 vendor accounts that report to Dun & Bradstreet, Experian or Equifax. Pay them early, and add a business card or small loan that reports around month 4-6.

What is the easiest way to establish business credit?

Net-30 vendor accounts are the easiest start, because suppliers approve small accounts on a new EIN that a bank would not. Buy things you already use, like supplies, shipping or fuel, and ask each vendor in writing which bureaus it reports to. Dun & Bradstreet needs at least three payment experiences from at least two vendors before it gives you a PAYDEX score.

Why isn't my business credit growing?

The usual causes are accounts that do not report in the business name, business accounts reporting to your personal file, high utilization or late payments, a business name or address that does not match across accounts, or too few accounts. Fix by opening business-only accounts that report, paying early, and keeping utilization under 30%.

What PAYDEX score should a business aim for?

80 or higher. Dun & Bradstreet treats 80 as paying on time and puts 80-100 in its low-risk band. Scores above 80 come from paying before the due date, not just on it.

Can a new LLC get a business loan before it has business credit?

Yes. For roughly the first two years, lenders underwrite the owner's personal credit, cash in the bank and any collateral, usually with a personal guarantee. Equipment financing and SBA microloans are the common first loans, and a loan that reports to the business bureaus also helps build the file.

Does my business need its own credit?

Yes, for larger loans and better terms. Many lenders check both personal and business credit, and once the business has two years of returns its own file starts to carry real weight. A strong business file lets you borrow without leaning only on your personal credit.
