Business Credit Card vs Loan: Which One to Use

A card is for spending you clear every month. A loan is for big purchases you pay off over years. Here's what each costs and where a line of credit fits.

← Back to Articles

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

Use a business credit card for purchases you can pay off each month, and a business loan for anything you will carry longer. Cards run about 18–29% APR once an intro rate ends; a bank term loan runs about 7–14% APR with a fixed payment over 3–7 years. On $50,000 repaid over three years, that is about $1,544 a month at 7% versus $2,095 at 29%, roughly $19,900 more in interest. A card paid in full each month costs no interest at all.

Put it on the card or take out a loan? It comes down to how big the purchase is and how long you will carry the balance. A software renewal you will pay off next month belongs on a card. A $50,000 equipment purchase you will pay off over three years does not, because card interest on that balance runs thousands of dollars more than a loan.

How much would a $50,000 business loan cost compared with a card?

Here is $50,000 paid off in equal monthly payments over 36 months at each product’s typical APR range. The card row assumes you pay it down on the same schedule; paying only the minimum takes longer and costs more.

Online loan12–30%$1,661–$2,123$9,786–$26,413

The gap is the whole argument. At the low end, the bank loan costs about $5,579 in interest over three years; the card costs $15,074 to $25,430. That difference is why owners who carry a balance on a card for more than a few months usually save money by moving it to a business term loan.

When should you use a business loan instead of a credit card?

Take a loan when the need is large, one-time and best repaid over years: equipment, a build-out or second location, buying a business, or paying off higher-rate debt. The fixed payment is easy to budget and you know the day it ends. For machines and vehicles, equipment financing uses the equipment itself as collateral, which keeps the rate down.

When is a business credit card the better choice?

Use a card for smaller, recurring spend you clear every month: supplies, software, travel, fuel. Paid in full, the grace period means you pay no interest, you may earn rewards, and the statement keeps business spending in one place. The trap is rolling a big balance month after month at 18–29%. Once that starts, a loan or line is cheaper.

Business credit card vs line of credit: which is cheaper?

For balances you carry, a line of credit usually wins. Bank lines run about 8–13% APR and online lines 14–30% or more, against 18–29% on a card. A line works like a card with a higher limit: you draw what you need and pay interest only on what you have drawn. Watch the fees, though. Some lines charge 1–3% per draw plus an annual fee, and most are variable, tied to prime. See typical business line of credit rates or the business line of credit overview.

Does your credit score matter for a business loan or a card?

Yes, for both, but in different ways. A business card is approved mostly on your personal credit. Banks typically want a 680–720 FICO for a term loan, and online lenders often go down to about 625–660 at higher rates. Lenders also look at revenue and time in business, so a strong score alone does not guarantee a bank loan.

Why most businesses use both

The setup that costs least layers the tools: a card for day-to-day spend paid off monthly, a line of credit for short-term cash swings, and a term loan for big one-time investments. Each does the job it is cheapest at. A card from an issuer that reports to business credit bureaus also builds the business credit file you will lean on for larger loans later.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a business credit card and a business loan?

A business loan pays you a lump sum that you repay in fixed payments over a set term, often 3–7 years. A business credit card gives you a revolving limit you can spend, repay and spend again, with no payoff date. Cards cost more if you carry a balance, about 18–29% APR versus 7–14% for a bank term loan.

Is a business loan cheaper than a credit card?

Yes, if you carry the balance. $50,000 over three years costs about $5,579 in interest on a 7% bank loan and $15,074 to $25,430 on a card at 18–29%. A card you pay in full each month costs no interest, so for short-term spend the card can be cheaper.

Is a business credit card or a line of credit better?

For a balance you will carry for months, a line of credit is usually cheaper: bank lines run about 8–13% APR and online lines 14–30% or more. A card is simpler for small purchases you pay off monthly. Check a line for draw fees and an annual fee before you compare.

Can you use both a business loan and a business credit card?

Yes, and most established businesses do. A common setup is a card for everyday spend paid monthly, a line of credit for short-term cash gaps and a term loan for large one-time purchases. Using each for what it costs least at lowers your overall cost of borrowing.

Does a business credit card build business credit?

It can, if the issuer reports to the business credit bureaus (Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, Equifax Business). Paying on time and staying well under the limit builds the business credit profile lenders check later. Ask the issuer whether it reports before you rely on it.

Does credit score matter for a business loan?

Yes. Banks typically look for a 680–720 FICO on a term loan, and online lenders often accept about 625–660 at higher rates. Revenue and time in business matter too, so lenders weigh the score alongside your bank statements and tax returns.
