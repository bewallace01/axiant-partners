Ways to Finance Buying an Auto Repair Shop

Like other fixed-asset businesses, a repair shop is usually financed by matching the loan to what you're buying.

  • SBA 504 loan — the fit when you're buying the building and the lifts, with a long-term fixed rate and roughly 10% down.
  • SBA 7(a) loan — the better single-loan structure when the deal carries goodwill, a customer base, and working capital, or when you're not buying the real estate.
  • Equipment financing and seller notes — finance additional lifts, alignment racks, or scan tools separately, and use a seller note on standby to bridge equity.

Why the Customer Base Carries the Deal

Auto repair is a repeat-customer, recession-resistant business—cars need service in every economy—which is why lenders view a stable shop favorably. The value sits in the steady car count and customer base: a shop with a loyal local following, fleet accounts, or recurring service relationships has durable revenue that survives the ownership change. Reviews, retention, and any commercial or fleet contracts all strengthen the file. A shop dependent on a single large account or a departing owner-mechanic's personal reputation is a riskier proposition.

Equipment and Environmental Diligence

Two diligence items are specific to auto shops. First, the equipment: lifts, alignment racks, diagnostic and scan tools are expensive and central to throughput, so lenders want a condition assessment and may finance upgrades into the deal. Second—and easy to overlook—the environmental review. A property that has stored oil, solvents, and automotive fluids for years may require a Phase I (and sometimes Phase II) environmental assessment, and a problem there can delay or reshape the deal. Order the environmental review early so it does not become the bottleneck at closing.

Valuing an Auto Repair Shop

Repair shops are valued on a multiple of SDE or EBITDA plus the real estate where it's owned. What moves the multiple up is a steady car count, technician retention (ASE-certified techs are hard to replace), fleet or commercial accounts, and modern, well-maintained equipment. What moves it down is heavy dependence on the selling owner-mechanic, customer concentration, or aging lifts. The number of bays and the throughput they support directly shape the earnings a lender will underwrite.

Down Payment and What Lenders Require

With an SBA 504 or 7(a) acquisition loan, plan for at least 10% down; conventional financing wants more. Lenders will ask for two to three years of the target's tax returns and financials, an equipment condition assessment, an environmental review of the property, a real estate appraisal where applicable, your personal financials and credit (commonly 650-680+), evidence of relevant automotive or management experience, and a purchase agreement.

Steps to Get Funded

  1. Define the deal. Decide how much is real estate and equipment versus goodwill—that drives 504 vs 7(a).
  2. Order the environmental review early. It's the most common closing bottleneck on a repair property.
  3. Assess the equipment. Lifts, alignment, and diagnostics—condition and any upgrades.
  4. Document the customer base. Car count, retention, fleet accounts.
  5. Compare lenders. See how to compare business loan offers.
  6. Submit a clean package and expect 30-60+ days for an SBA close.

Next Steps

Financing an auto repair shop is very achievable when you match the structure to the deal and handle the environmental review up front. Lead with a steady customer base, sound equipment, and clean financials. When you're ready, get matched with lenders that fit your shop purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you use an SBA loan to buy an auto repair shop?

Yes. The SBA 504 program is often used when the real estate and lifts make up most of the price, because it offers a long-term fixed rate with a low down payment; the SBA 7(a) program is used when the deal includes more goodwill or working capital, or when you're not buying the property. Both typically require around 10% down.

What environmental diligence does an auto shop need?

Because repair properties store oil, solvents, and automotive fluids, lenders commonly require a Phase I environmental assessment (and sometimes a Phase II if issues surface). A problem can delay or reshape the deal, so order the environmental review early rather than at closing.

How are auto repair shops valued?

On a multiple of SDE or EBITDA plus the real estate where it's owned. A steady car count, technician retention, fleet or commercial accounts, and modern equipment push the multiple up; dependence on the selling owner-mechanic, customer concentration, or aging lifts push it down. Bay count and throughput shape the earnings a lender underwrites.

Why do lenders like auto repair shops?

Auto repair is repeat-customer and recession-resistant—cars need service in every economy. A shop with a loyal local following, fleet accounts, or recurring service relationships has durable revenue that survives an ownership change, which lenders view favorably.

How much down payment do you need to buy an auto repair shop?

With an SBA 504 or 7(a) acquisition loan, expect a minimum equity injection of about 10% of the project cost. Conventional acquisition financing often requires 20-30% down. A portion of the SBA injection can sometimes come from a seller note kept on standby.
