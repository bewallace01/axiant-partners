What Do Lenders Look for in a Business Term Loan?

Revenue, DSCR, credit profile, and underwriting factors that drive approval

← Back to Business Term Loans Articles

Updated March 3, 2026

Quick Facts

What review what lenders examine for business term loans, including revenue stability, DSCR, credit profile, debt load, collateral, and use of funds. Focus on Revenue Stability (Primary Factor), Cash Flow & Debt Service Coverage (DSCR), Credit Profile. This guidance applies to most U.S. lenders and programs.

Revenue Stability (Primary Factor)

Revenue is the foundation of term loan underwriting. Lenders need confidence that your business generates consistent cash flow to support fixed monthly payments. They review monthly trends, year-over-year growth, seasonality, and revenue concentration. A business with 24 months of stable or growing revenue typically receives stronger consideration than one with volatile or declining trends. Lenders review:

  • Monthly and annual revenue trends
  • Revenue consistency across seasons
  • Industry concentration risk
  • Business model durability

Most programs prefer stable revenue history over 12-24 months.

Cash Flow & Debt Service Coverage (DSCR)

DSCR measures whether business cash flow can cover required loan payments.

  • 1.20x+ is commonly preferred
  • Higher DSCR improves terms
  • Weak DSCR can reduce approval amounts

Credit Profile

Lenders evaluate both personal and business credit behavior.

  • Payment history
  • Revolving utilization
  • Derogatory events
  • Recent inquiries
  • Credit depth

600-700+ is common for many term structures, with higher scores producing stronger offers.

Existing Debt Obligations

Current liabilities affect approval and pricing. Lenders calculate total debt service to ensure you can handle the new payment.

  • Debt-to-income or debt-to-revenue metrics
  • Current payment burden
  • Refinance exposure
  • Short-term debt pressure

Time in Business

Most lenders prefer operating history:

  • 2+ years in business for standard programs
  • Longer history improves lender confidence
  • Startups may have limited options for term facilities

Industry Risk Profile

Lenders score risk based on sector behavior, volatility, and market outlook. Industries considered lower risk–such as healthcare, professional services, and essential retail–often receive more favorable terms. Higher-risk or cyclical sectors may face stricter limits or higher pricing. Some lenders specialize in particular industries and may offer better terms to businesses in sectors they understand. Discuss your industry with potential lenders to understand how they view your sector. For structural options, see secured vs unsecured business term loans.

Common industry considerations:

  • Stable industries usually receive better terms
  • Volatile sectors may require stronger collateral
  • Cyclicality impacts pricing and structure

Collateral (for Secured Loans)

Secured term loans require pledged assets. The collateral provides a secondary recovery path for the lender, which can improve terms and increase loan amounts. Lenders evaluate collateral quality, liquidity, and appraised value. Secured term loans may evaluate:

  • Equipment
  • Receivables
  • Inventory
  • Real estate or other business assets

Collateral can improve pricing and increase borrowing capacity.

Use of Funds

Lenders assess whether loan purpose matches term-loan structure. A clear, documented use of funds strengthens your application and supports approval. Vague or speculative purposes can raise concerns. Common accepted uses include expansion, refinancing, acquisition, equipment, working capital, and large project funding. Some programs restrict certain uses. Provide a concise narrative explaining how the capital will be deployed and the expected benefit. For scenarios where a term loan may not fit, see when is a business term loan NOT the right option.

Typical uses lenders expect:

  • Expansion
  • Refinancing
  • Acquisition
  • Large project funding
  • Asset-heavy growth initiatives

Requested Loan Amount

The amount you request relative to revenue and cash flow affects underwriting. Lenders compare your request to typical revenue multiples and DSCR thresholds for your industry. Requesting more than your profile supports may result in a counteroffer for a lower amount rather than a decline. Lenders assess whether the requested size is proportionate and supported by your financial profile. Requesting far more than typical revenue multiples can raise questions; requesting too little may limit growth potential. Base your request on actual capital needs, documented use of funds, and realistic repayment capacity. See how much you can qualify for with a business term loan for typical ranges by revenue.

Banking Activity

Underwriters often review operating account behavior to assess cash management discipline. Multiple overdrafts, erratic deposit patterns, or frequent low-balance periods can raise concerns even when revenue figures look strong. A business that maintains stable deposits, pays vendors on time, and shows predictable cash flow typically receives more favorable consideration. Keep at least 3–6 months of clean bank statements before applying. Underwriters often evaluate:

  • Deposit consistency
  • Overdraft frequency
  • Liquidity management discipline

Minimum Loan Amount

Business term loans usually start at $10,000 and scale with qualification, financial strength, and project scope.

What Matters Most?

In most cases, lenders prioritize:

  • Revenue and consistency
  • DSCR and repayment capacity
  • Credit profile
  • Debt obligations
  • Business stability

Documentation Checklist

Having these ready before applying can speed the process: business tax returns (2 years), profit and loss statements, balance sheets, 3–12 months of bank statements, debt schedule, use-of-funds narrative, ownership structure, and business formation documents. For secured loans, add collateral descriptions, appraisals if available, and lien documentation. Ensure consistency across documents–revenue figures in tax returns should align with bank deposits and P&L statements. Incomplete documentation is a leading cause of delays. Organize your financials and respond promptly to lender requests to keep the process moving. For approval timelines, see how fast you can get a business term loan.

How to Improve Approval Odds

  • Strengthen financial reporting
  • Reduce revolving debt
  • Increase operating account consistency
  • Improve credit discipline
  • Clarify use-of-funds documentation

Preparing Your Application

To improve approval odds, organize financial statements, tax returns, bank statements, and a clear use-of-funds narrative. Create a one-page executive summary covering your business, revenue trends, capital need, and repayment plan. This helps underwriters quickly understand your profile and can reduce follow-up questions. Address any recent negative events proactively with a brief explanation rather than leaving them unexplained. Ensure revenue and bank deposits align. Address any recent negative events proactively. Respond promptly to lender requests. Working with a financing advisor who understands term loan underwriting can help you present your business in the strongest light. Final Thoughts

Lenders evaluate business term loans through a complete risk framework, not personal score alone. Revenue stability, DSCR, credit, debt load, and use of funds all contribute to approval and terms.
