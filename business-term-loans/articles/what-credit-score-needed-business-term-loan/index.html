What Credit Score Is Needed for a Business Term Loan?
← Back to Business Term Loans Articles

Updated April 6, 2026

Quick answer

Most business term loan programs prefer 650-680+ FICO for SBA 7(a), 660-690+ for secured term loans, 680-700+ for unsecured, and 680-720+ for bank conventional. The 720+ tier unlocks the best pricing and broadest options; 680-719 gets solid conventional access; 650-679 still works for SBA or secured structures. Below 600, options narrow sharply — focus on equipment financing or lenders specializing in near-prime credit. Improving your score by 30-50 points can materially expand options.

Typical Credit Score Requirements by Structure

Loan Type Typical Preferred Credit Score
Unsecured Business Term Loan 680-700+
Secured Business Term Loan 660-690+
SBA 7(a) Term Loan 650-680+
Bank Conventional Term Loan 680-720+

Stronger credit typically results in lower rates, higher approval odds, larger loan amounts, and longer repayment terms.

Below 600: Limited Options

Most conventional business term loan programs prefer borrowers with scores of 600 or higher. Scores in the 500s significantly narrow your options. Below 600, options become limited. You may need to focus on improving credit before applying, consider alternative structures such as equipment financing (which is asset-backed), or explore lenders that specialize in near-prime business credit. Expect higher rates and more conservative terms if you do qualify. Improving your score by even 30–50 points can materially expand your options.

Credit Score Tiers Explained

720+ (Strong Tier)

  • Highest approval probability
  • Best pricing flexibility
  • Most lender options

680-719 (Solid Tier)

  • Strong approval profile
  • Good conventional access
  • Competitive pricing

650-679 (Moderate Tier)

  • SBA still possible
  • Secured structures more realistic
  • Slightly higher pricing

600-649 (Challenging Tier)

  • Unsecured options limited
  • Strong revenue required
  • Higher rates likely

Secured structures or SBA programs may still be possible in this range when revenue and cash flow are strong. Each lender has different thresholds; comparing multiple options can help you find programs that fit your profile.

Why Credit Score Matters for Term Loans

Because business term loans are usually fully disbursed upfront and repaid over fixed terms, lenders cannot recall the capital if your situation changes. They place significant weight on repayment behavior and risk indicators. Unlike a line of credit where you draw only what you need, a term loan commits the full amount from day one. That makes credit history and predictability more critical. Lenders look at both personal and business credit when available. For newer businesses, personal credit often carries more weight. See how much you can qualify for with a business term loan for how credit tier affects loan amounts.

Key risk indicators lenders evaluate:

  • Payment history
  • Revolving utilization
  • Public records
  • Bankruptcies
  • Overall debt behavior

How Lenders Pull and Use Your Score

Lenders typically pull personal credit from one or more bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). Business credit–Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, Equifax Business–may also be reviewed for established companies. Each pull can slightly impact your score; applying to multiple lenders within a short window (e.g., 14–30 days) is often treated as a single inquiry for scoring purposes. Understanding which bureau a lender uses can help you prepare if you know one report is stronger than another.

Improving Your Credit Before Applying

If your score is below your target tier, consider: reducing revolving utilization (aim for under 30%), correcting errors on credit reports, paying down high-interest debt, and avoiding new inquiries before applying. Even a 20–30 point improvement can open access to better programs and lower rates. Allow time for changes to reflect–payment history updates typically take 30–60 days to appear. For approval timelines, see how fast you can get a business term loan.

Personal vs. Business Credit

Lenders often review both personal and business credit. For newer or smaller businesses, personal credit typically carries more weight because the business has limited trade history. Established companies with strong trade lines may rely more on business credit. Building business credit over time–through trade accounts, vendor relationships, and business cards–can reduce reliance on personal scores for future financing. Ensure both profiles are in good standing before applying. See secured vs unsecured business term loans for how structure affects credit requirements.

What Matters Beyond Credit

Credit is important, but it is not the only factor. Lenders prioritize:

  • Revenue stability
  • Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
  • Time in business (2+ years preferred)
  • Existing debt load
  • Industry stability

Strong cash flow can offset moderate credit profiles. A business with 640 credit but consistent revenue, strong DSCR, and 5+ years in operation may qualify where a business with 680 credit but erratic revenue might not. Underwriting is holistic. Focus on presenting your strongest factors and addressing weaknesses proactively. For a full underwriting overview, see what lenders look for in a business term loan.

Summary: Credit Score Ranges at a Glance

Quick reference: 720+ qualifies for best terms; 680–719 for strong approval; 650–679 for SBA and secured options; 600–649 for limited programs with higher pricing. Below 600, options narrow significantly, though some asset-backed programs or alternative lenders may still be available at higher cost. Revenue strength and collateral can sometimes offset credit limitations; discuss your full profile with lenders before assuming you will not qualify. Revenue and cash flow can offset moderate scores. Use these ranges as a starting point, then discuss your profile with lenders or a financing advisor to confirm which programs fit.

Minimum Loan Amount

Business term loans usually start at $10,000 and scale with qualification. Higher credit tiers often qualify for larger amounts; lenders may cap exposure for lower scores even when revenue is strong. Understanding your tier before applying helps set realistic expectations and reduces time spent on applications that are unlikely to approve your desired amount. Final Thoughts

Most business term loan programs prefer scores in the 680–720+ range, depending on structure. Secured options may accept slightly lower scores when collateral is strong. SBA programs often work with 650+ when other factors are solid. Approval depends on the full financial profile–revenue, DSCR, time in business, and industry–not just the score alone. If your business generates consistent revenue and needs structured lump-sum capital, reviewing available business term loan options can help determine fit by credit tier.
