Quick Answer: Typical Minimum Term Loan Requirements

Most term loan programs look for:

  • Time in business: often 2+ years for standard programs (some accept 12 months)
  • Revenue: stable deposits and demonstrated cash flow
  • DSCR: commonly around 1.20x–1.25x+ (varies)
  • Credit: many lenders look for 600–680+; best tiers often 700+
  • Documents: bank statements + tax returns + current financials for larger amounts
  • Use of funds: a clear, financeable purpose that matches a term loan structure

1) Revenue Stability Requirements

Term loans are repaid through fixed payments. Lenders want confidence your revenue can support those payments through normal volatility. Underwriters typically look at:

  • year-over-year revenue trend
  • monthly deposit stability
  • customer concentration (how dependent you are on one account)
  • margin durability and expense control

If revenue is volatile but explainable (seasonality, project-based businesses), lenders may still approve—but often with a smaller amount or tighter terms.

2) DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) Requirements

DSCR is a key metric for term loan underwriting. It measures whether cash flow can cover total debt payments with a cushion. A simplified concept is:

DSCR = cash flow available for debt payments / total annual debt payments.

A DSCR of 1.25x means you have 25% more cash flow than required payments. Many lenders like to see that cushion to handle surprises (slow months, unexpected expenses, customer delays).

To improve DSCR before applying, you can:

  • reduce existing payment burden (especially high-cost short-term products)
  • improve margins through pricing or cost control
  • document add-backs clearly (non-recurring expenses, owner compensation normalization)

3) Credit Score and Credit Profile Requirements

Term loan pricing is sensitive to risk. Credit matters more for term loans than for some cash-flow-first products because lenders are committing capital for a fixed period. Many programs look for 600–680+ FICO, while stronger tiers (often 700+) improve pricing and approvals.

Beyond the score, underwriters care about:

  • recent delinquencies
  • utilization and revolving debt
  • public records and unresolved derogatories
  • credit depth (how established your credit history is)

For a tier breakdown, see what credit score is needed for a business term loan.

4) Time in Business Requirements

Time in business is one of the simplest screens because it correlates with survival and operating maturity. Many term loan programs prefer 2+ years. If you are newer, you can still qualify in some cases, but you may see:

  • smaller loan amounts
  • shorter terms
  • higher pricing
  • more documentation requests

5) Use of Funds Requirements (What Term Loans Are Best For)

Term loans work best when the use of funds is clear and the payback period is reasonable. Common financeable uses include:

  • expansion (hiring, marketing, new locations)
  • equipment or large projects not covered by equipment-specific programs
  • business acquisitions (in the right circumstances)
  • refinancing high-cost debt into a fixed-payment structure

Vague use-of-funds narratives are a frequent delay point. A simple one-paragraph explanation is usually enough: what you will do, why it improves cash flow, and why the requested amount is realistic.

6) Documentation Requirements (What You Need to Provide)

Term loans generally require more documentation than lighter products because lenders need to validate cash flow and risk more thoroughly.

Common requirements include:

  • Bank statements: typically 3–12 months
  • Business tax returns: 1–2 years
  • Personal tax returns: sometimes required depending on lender
  • Year-to-date P&L and balance sheet: especially for larger requests
  • Debt schedule: existing loans and monthly payments
  • Business formation docs: EIN, entity documentation, ownership

For secured loans, add collateral details, appraisals if available, and UCC information.

7) Collateral and Personal Guarantee Requirements

Collateral requirements vary. Some term loans are unsecured, especially at smaller sizes and for strong borrowers. Secured term loans can offer higher amounts and better pricing when collateral is strong and liquid.

Personal guarantees are common in small business lending. They don’t automatically mean the loan is predatory; they are a standard risk control. What matters is understanding the specific terms (cross-collateral, cross-default clauses, and covenants). If you want to understand these risks, read business loan guarantee traps.

Term Loan Requirements by Use Case (Expansion vs Refinance vs Acquisition)

Underwriters also evaluate whether your use case matches a term loan. The same borrower can qualify for a smaller “expansion” loan but get declined on an acquisition loan because the risk profile is different. Here is how lenders often think about common use cases:

  • Expansion (marketing, hiring, new location buildout): lenders want to see a stable baseline business and a clear plan for how the investment increases revenue or efficiency. Strong historical performance and clean reporting matter.
  • Refinancing high-cost debt: lenders want to see that the refinance improves cash flow (lower payment, longer term, better structure). If the refinance is just “buying time” without a stabilization plan, approvals get harder.
  • Acquisitions: acquisitions require deeper underwriting: historical financials on the target, deal structure, and confidence that combined cash flow supports the new debt. Many lenders want more documentation and stronger borrower credit.

If you are evaluating structures, it can help to compare a term loan with a revolving facility. See line of credit vs term loan for decision guidance.

Typical Terms, Rates, and Fees (What Strong Files Usually See)

Term loan pricing varies widely by lender tier and risk profile, but understanding the range helps you sanity-check offers. Factors that move pricing include credit tier, DSCR strength, time in business, collateral, and the requested amount relative to revenue.

Common cost components include:

  • Interest rate: fixed or variable depending on lender and program.
  • Origination fee: usually a percentage of the loan amount.
  • Prepayment terms: some loans include penalties; always confirm the rule.

When comparing offers, don’t focus only on the rate. Compare total cost and repayment fit. Use how to compare business loan offers to avoid a “cheap rate” offer that hides fees or restrictive terms.

Example: How Underwriters Think About a Term Loan Payment

Imagine a business with steady deposits and strong margins that requests $250,000 for expansion. The underwriter will estimate a realistic payment for the requested term and rate, then check whether the business’s cash flow comfortably covers that payment alongside existing obligations. If the payment consumes too much of monthly free cash flow (or if deposits are volatile), the lender may counter with a smaller amount or shorter term.

This is why “how much you qualify for” is really a cash-flow question, not just a credit score question. For more on amount sizing, see how much you can qualify for with a business term loan.

Application Steps: How to Apply Without Creating Delays

  1. Pre-package your file: bank statements, tax returns, current financials, and a debt schedule.
  2. Write a short use-of-funds summary: what you will do, why it helps cash flow, and what success looks like.
  3. Request a realistic amount: align the request with your DSCR and actual need.
  4. Respond quickly to follow-ups: lenders often have internal deadlines for documentation updates.

Common Reasons Term Loan Applications Get Denied

Most declines fall into a few buckets:

  • Weak DSCR: cash flow doesn’t cover existing and new payments with a cushion.
  • Declining revenue: lenders don’t want to lend into a downtrend.
  • High existing debt: payment burden already consumes cash flow.
  • Credit issues: recent delinquencies or unresolved derogatories.
  • Incomplete documentation: numbers don’t reconcile or key docs are missing.
  • Unclear use of funds: doesn’t match a term loan structure.

If you’ve been denied, read what to do if your application is denied before reapplying.

How to Improve Approval Odds (Practical Steps)

Most term loan improvements are operational, not theoretical. Here is what typically moves the needle:

  1. Stabilize cash flow: tighten collections and reduce volatility where possible.
  2. Reduce expensive debt: remove daily/weekly payment pressure to improve DSCR.
  3. Clean up credit: lower utilization and address errors or recent issues.
  4. Package financials: ensure tax returns, bank deposits, and P&L align.
  5. Request smartly: ask for an amount that your DSCR clearly supports.

Final Thoughts

Business term loan requirements are centered on repayment strength: stable revenue, healthy DSCR, responsible credit behavior, and a complete documentation package. If your business meets these benchmarks, a term loan can be an efficient way to fund expansion, refinance expensive debt, or finance a strategic project with predictable payments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the requirements for a business term loan?

Most lenders evaluate revenue stability, cash flow and DSCR, credit profile, time in business, and documentation quality. Many programs prefer 2+ years in business and 600-680+ credit, but requirements vary by lender, loan size, and whether the loan is secured.

What credit score do you need for a business term loan?

Many term loan programs look for 600-680+ FICO, with 700+ typically unlocking the best pricing and higher approvals. Underwriters also consider utilization, delinquencies, and credit depth.

What is DSCR and what DSCR do lenders require?

DSCR (debt service coverage ratio) measures how well cash flow covers loan payments. Many lenders prefer around 1.20x-1.25x or higher, meaning cash flow exceeds debt payments by 20-25% or more.

What documents are required for a term loan?

Commonly bank statements, tax returns, year-to-date financials, business formation documents, and a debt schedule. Larger or secured loans may require collateral details and additional reporting.

Why do term loan applications get denied?

Declining revenue, weak DSCR, high existing debt, recent credit issues, incomplete documentation, or an unclear use of funds are common decline reasons.
