Business Term Loan vs Line of Credit: Which Is Better?

Quick comparison of structure, cost, and flexibility

← Back to Business Term Loans Articles

Last updated: March 3, 2026 � 6 min read � Axiant Partners

If your business needs financing, you may be deciding between a business term loan and a line of credit. While both can provide capital, they are built for different uses and cash flow patterns. A term loan delivers a lump sum with fixed monthly payments and a defined end date. A line of credit provides revolving access�you draw what you need, repay, and reuse. Choosing the right structure avoids overpaying, undercapitalizing, or locking into the wrong repayment pattern for your situation.

Quick Comparison

Feature Business Term Loan Business Line of Credit
Funding Structure Lump sum Revolving
Repayment Fixed monthly payments Flexible repayment
Reusability No Yes
Best For Defined projects Ongoing liquidity

Interest Cost Comparison

With a term loan, you pay interest on the full principal from day one, even if you do not need all the funds immediately. With a line of credit, you pay interest only on the amount drawn. For a $200,000 need: a term loan disburses $200,000 and you pay interest on the full amount. A line of credit might have a $200,000 limit, but if you only draw $50,000 at a time, you pay interest on $50,000. For lump-sum, defined needs, a term loan can be more efficient. For variable, recurring needs, a line typically costs less. Use our loan calculator to compare scenarios.

What Is a Business Term Loan Best For?

A term loan is ideal when:

  • You need a defined amount
  • The expense is one-time
  • You are funding expansion
  • You are refinancing debt
  • You prefer predictable payments

Examples include opening a new location, major renovation projects, acquisition funding, and debt consolidation. Term loans provide predictable monthly payments and a clear payoff date, which helps with budgeting and financial planning. What Is a Line of Credit Best For?

A line of credit works best when your capital needs are variable or recurring. You draw only what you need, when you need it, and pay interest only on the outstanding balance. As you repay, the credit becomes available again. This revolving structure suits businesses with seasonal revenue, inventory cycles, payroll timing gaps, or opportunistic short-term needs. It is not ideal for large one-time investments where a lump sum and fixed repayment are preferable.

A line of credit works well when:

  • Cash flow is seasonal
  • You have unpredictable expenses
  • You need ongoing flexibility
  • You want reusable working capital

Funding Speed Differences

Lines of credit often fund faster than term loans because underwriting tends to be lighter�focus on revenue, bank activity, and credit rather than detailed use-of-funds or collateral. Unsecured term loans can close in a week or less; secured term loans and SBA structures typically take longer. If your timeline is tight, factor structure choice into your decision. Using Both Structures

Many established businesses use both a term loan and a line of credit. A term loan might fund a major expansion or equipment purchase with fixed monthly payments, while a line of credit covers seasonal cash flow, inventory buildups, or short-term opportunities. This hybrid approach matches the financing structure to each capital need. Ensure total debt service remains manageable�lenders consider all obligations when underwriting new credit. Cost Considerations

Compare both the stated rate and total cost over the expected borrowing period. Term loans often have lower nominal rates for strong borrowers but charge interest on the full principal from day one. Lines of credit may have slightly higher rates but charge only on drawn amounts, which can reduce total cost when needs are variable.

Term loans:

  • Interest applied on full principal
  • Defined fixed payments
  • Often lower rates for stronger profiles

Line of credit:

  • Interest only on drawn portions
  • May have draw fees
  • Better for liquidity management

Approval Differences

Underwriting focus varies by structure. Term loans typically require use-of-funds documentation and revenue projections. Lines of credit emphasize current bank activity and debt levels. Term loans often emphasize:

  • Strong revenue
  • Clear repayment capacity
  • Defined capital purpose

Lines of credit emphasize:

  • Cash flow management history
  • Credit discipline
  • Operational consistency

When to Choose a Term Loan

  • Capital need is defined
  • You do not need revolving access
  • Repayment timeline aligns with revenue
  • You prefer fixed structure

When to Choose a Line of Credit

  • You need ongoing access
  • Expenses are variable
  • Cash flow fluctuates
  • You want reusable liquidity

Minimum Loan Amount

Both structures typically start around $10,000 and scale with qualification. Maximum limits vary: term loans often scale to seven figures based on revenue and DSCR; lines of credit may cap lower depending on lender. If you need a large lump sum, a term loan is usually the right structure. If you need ongoing access up to a certain limit, a line of credit fits. Underwriting Differences

Term loans typically require use-of-funds documentation, revenue projections, and detailed financial statements. Lines of credit emphasize bank statements, revenue consistency, and existing debt levels. Understanding what lenders evaluate helps you prepare. Final Thoughts

A business term loan provides structured, lump-sum capital for defined growth initiatives. A line of credit provides flexible, revolving liquidity for ongoing operational needs. The right choice depends on how you plan to use the capital, your cash flow patterns, and whether you need one-time or recurring access. Many established businesses use both: a term loan for major projects and a line of credit for operational flexibility. If your business generates consistent revenue and requires capital, reviewing both structures side by side can help determine which aligns best with your strategy.
