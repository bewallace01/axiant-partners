It is organised by product, but most people arrive with a situation rather than a product in mind — a machine to buy, a payroll gap, a deal with a deadline. The sections below route from the situation to the guide that answers it, so you do not have to know what the product is called first.

Start with your situation

You need to buy a machine, truck or piece of equipment. Start with equipment financing requirements for what lenders actually check, then leasing versus a loan if you are weighing ownership against payment size. For trucks specifically, semi truck down payments covers the cash you need up front.

Cash is tight this month and the gap will recur. A line of credit fits a repeating gap better than a term loan. How fast approval runs sets expectations, and typical rates by lender shows what the flexibility costs.

You are buying property, or a partner out. Down payment requirements on commercial property is the most-read guide here. For a partner buyout, using an SBA loan explains the rules, and SBA approval timelines tells you whether the calendar allows it.

Credit is the obstacle. Options at 500–600 FICO covers what remains available, and credit score tiers for equipment financing shows why asset-backed money is the usual answer.

You already have financing and it is not working. Red flags in MCA agreements and revenue-based financing versus an MCA are the two most useful starting points.

The business is new and has no trading history. Most lenders price on time in business, so the startup hub is the place to start — startup financing guides cover what is available before two years of returns exist, and lines of credit under two years covers the one product that sometimes bends.

You are flipping a property. Down payments on a fix and flip explains why the ARV cap matters more than the purchase price, and bridge loan guides cover the case where the exit is a sale rather than a refinance. If the deal has a closing date, send it over now — bridge timelines are measured in weeks.

If none of those describe it, tell us the situation in your own words and we will point you at the right reading — or skip the reading and go straight to what you qualify for.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many business financing guides are there?

395 guides across 13 topics: equipment financing (97), SBA loans (48), applying for business loans (47), working capital (45), commercial real estate (33), lines of credit (21), startup financing (21), term loans (19), bridge loans (17), merchant cash advances (15), revenue-based financing (13), fix and flip (11) and securities-based lending (8).

Which guide should I read first?

Start from your situation rather than the product name. Buying equipment points to the equipment hub; a recurring cash gap points to lines of credit; buying property or a partner out points to commercial real estate and SBA. The routing section above links directly to the right guide for each.

Are these guides selling something?

They are published by a financing brokerage, so the incentive is disclosed rather than hidden: Axiant Partners earns a commission when a deal funds. The guides are written to be accurate whether or not you use us, because a guide that oversells is worth nothing to the reader who later finds out.

How current is the information?

Rates, terms and approval thresholds move, and the guides carry a last-updated date. Treat any figure as the shape of the market rather than a quote, and confirm on a specific deal before relying on it.

Do I have to read anything to get financing?

No. The reading exists for people who want to understand the trade-offs before committing. If you already know what you need, the match takes a few minutes, costs nothing, and does not affect your credit.

What if my situation is not covered?

Send it over. The library covers the common cases, and unusual ones are exactly where a broker is more useful than an article — the answer often depends on details no general guide can anticipate.
