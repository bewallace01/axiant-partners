"How fast can I get a working capital loan?" usually means "what's the soonest I can have cash in my account?" The honest answer is that it ranges from the same day to a couple of weeks, and the deciding factor is which product you use. Here are the typical timelines and what you can do to land on the fast end.

Funding Timelines by Product

These are typical ranges, not promises — your file, amount, and lender all move the number. If you genuinely need money in two days, see emergency working capital in 48 hours; if a week or two is fine, a cheaper bank or SBA-style option may be worth the wait.

What Speeds It Up — and What Slows It Down

The fast-funding products are fast because they underwrite on your recent bank-statement cash flow rather than full financials. That means the quality and clarity of your statements largely determines your timeline. Speeds it up: clean, recent statements that match your stated revenue; a complete application the first time; a realistic amount for your cash flow; and quick responses to any follow-up. Slows it down: inconsistent deposits, recent overdrafts or negative days, a request that's too large, unclear use of funds, missing documents, and slow replies. Larger amounts and bank/SBA products are inherently slower because the review is deeper — see what lenders look for and why an application gets stuck in review.

How to Fund Faster

  • Have 3–6 months of business bank statements ready — this is the document fast lenders need first.
  • Reconcile statements to your stated revenue so underwriting doesn't pause on a mismatch.
  • Ask for a realistic amount — an oversized request triggers extra scrutiny.
  • Submit a complete file up front (ID, voided check, recent statements, basic business info).
  • Respond to follow-ups within the hour — lender response time is often the real bottleneck.
  • Mind cutoff and ACH timing — a late-afternoon approval often funds the next morning.

Don't Pay for Speed You Don't Need

The fastest money is usually the most expensive. A merchant cash advance can fund tomorrow but carries a high effective cost; a bank line takes longer but is far cheaper to carry. Before defaulting to the fastest option, ask whether you truly need funds in 48 hours or whether a few extra days unlocks a much cheaper product. For genuine emergencies, fast is worth it; for planned needs, the wait often pays for itself. Compare the all-in cost, not just the speed, and run the numbers with our financing calculator.
