Invoice Factoring for Staffing Agencies: Fund Weekly Payroll Without Waiting

How factoring turns billed hours into payroll cash, what it costs, and when a loan is the better call.

Home › Working Capital Loans › Articles › Invoice Factoring for Staffing Agencies

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

Invoice factoring for staffing agencies means selling the invoices you bill clients for hours already worked to a factoring company, so you can make weekly payroll without waiting out net-30 or net-60 terms. Staffing factors typically advance 85-95% of each invoice within a day of verifying it, then release the rest, minus a fee of about 1-5%, when the client pays. On a $40,000 invoice at a 90% advance and a 3% fee, you get $36,000 now and $2,800 later. Approval leans on your clients' credit more than yours.

Why staffing agencies use invoice factoring

Your workers clock hours this week and expect to be paid on Friday. The client you billed pays on net-30 or net-60 terms. Every new placement widens that gap, because you owe wages weeks before the first payment arrives. Land a big contract and the problem gets worse, not better.

Banks rarely solve it. Young agencies often lack the credit history, collateral or audited financials a bank wants, and a fixed line does not grow when you add 40 workers in a month. Factoring is built for this: it is underwritten on the clients you bill, and the cash available rises as your invoices rise. It is a sale of receivables, not a loan, so it does not add debt to your balance sheet. For the general mechanics, see invoice factoring.

How does invoice factoring work for a staffing agency?

  1. Set up the account. The factor reviews your clients' credit and your contracts. Setup usually takes a few business days.
  2. Bill the client for hours worked, as you do now, and submit the invoice to the factor.
  3. Get the advance. After verifying the invoice, the factor sends 85-95% of its value, usually within a day. That money runs payroll.
  4. The client pays the factor on its normal net-30 or net-60 terms.
  5. The reserve comes back to you, minus the factoring fee.

Here is a single $40,000 invoice at a 90% advance and a 3% fee:

Step Amount When
Invoice billed to client $40,000 Week 1
Advance from the factor (90%) $36,000 Within a day of verification
Factoring fee (3%) $1,200 Deducted when the client pays
Reserve released to you $2,800 When the client pays, often 30-60 days

Many staffing factors also offer back-office help, including invoicing, collections and payroll processing, and some fund payroll straight from approved timesheets. That lets a small agency grow headcount without building a finance department.

What does invoice factoring cost for a staffing agency?

You pay a factoring fee, also called the discount rate, instead of interest. It typically runs about 1-5% of the invoice and is often quoted per 30 days, so a client who pays in 60 days can cost you twice as much as one who pays in 30. Flat fees are easier to compare than tiered fees that climb while an invoice stays open.

Where you land depends on your monthly volume, your clients' credit, average invoice size, how fast clients pay, and whether the deal is recourse. Ask every factor about setup fees, monthly minimums, wire fees and termination penalties before you compare headline rates. For benchmarks, see invoice factoring rates.

Recourse or non-recourse?

With recourse factoring you stay on the hook if a client never pays: you buy the invoice back or swap in another one. It is the more common and cheaper option because the factor takes less risk. With non-recourse factoring the factor absorbs the loss if a covered client becomes insolvent, for a higher fee. For an agency that depends on one or two large clients, that protection can be worth paying for, because a single default could sink the business. Read the carve-outs: many non-recourse contracts still push disputes, deductions and chargebacks back to you. More detail in recourse vs non-recourse factoring.

What are the downsides of factoring?

  • Cost. A few percent per month is more than a bank line costs for a borrower who qualifies for one.
  • Client contact. The factor collects from your clients directly, so how it treats them matters.
  • Contract terms. Monthly minimums, long terms and termination fees can lock you in.
  • Concentration limits. If one client is most of your billing, the factor may cap how much of it you can sell.
  • Recourse risk. On a recourse deal, an unpaid invoice comes back to you.

Factoring vs a working capital loan for payroll

Option Approved on Speed Cost Scales
Invoice factoring Your clients' credit Within a day per invoice after a few days of setup About 1-5% of each invoice Yes, grows with billings
Working capital loan Your credit and financials 3-10 business days About 10-30% APR No, fixed amount
Business line of credit Your credit and financials 1-2 weeks to open, then same-day draws Interest on what you draw Only up to the limit

For a fast-growing agency with strong clients and thin cash, factoring usually wins: it funds within a day and grows with billings. For a stable agency with modest, predictable gaps, a working capital loan or line of credit may cost less. Plenty of agencies use both: factoring for the weekly payroll cycle and a line for one-off needs. If you are weighing those options, see payroll funding for staffing firms.

Who is the best factoring company for a staffing agency?

There is no single best factor. The right one is the one that fits how your agency runs. Look for:

  • Staffing experience: a factor that understands timesheets, weekly payroll and back-office work.
  • Speed: next-day advances with a simple way to submit invoices.
  • Clear pricing: one discount rate and advance rate, with no hidden minimums or setup charges.
  • Flexibility: short or no-term contracts and the option to factor selected clients instead of your whole ledger.
  • Professional collections and quick release of reserves.

Get at least two or three quotes so the first offer does not set your expectations. Our guide on how to choose a factoring company has a longer checklist. Security firms use the same structure for officer payroll: see factoring for security guard companies.

Freight factoring works the same way

Trucking carriers have the same gap: fuel and driver pay are due this week, and brokers pay in 30-60 days. A freight factor advances 90-97% of a load invoice the same or next day once you send the bill of lading and rate confirmation, and many add fuel advances at pickup and free broker credit checks. Carriers should read freight factoring for carriers.

Next steps

Pull your accounts receivable aging report. It shows how much cash is tied up in unpaid invoices and how long each client takes to pay, which tells you roughly what factoring would cost. Then gather a sample invoice, your client list and your monthly billing so a factor can quote an advance rate and fee. Compare offers on speed, total cost and contract terms, not just the headline rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does invoice factoring work for a staffing agency?

You bill the client for hours your workers logged and send the invoice to the factor. The factor verifies it and advances 85-95% within about a day, which you use for payroll. When the client pays on net-30 or net-60 terms, the factor releases the reserve minus its fee.

How much does staffing factoring cost?

Fees typically run about 1-5% of the invoice, often quoted per 30 days, so a client who pays slowly costs you more. Volume, your clients' credit and recourse terms set where you land. Ask about setup fees, monthly minimums, wire fees and termination penalties, which change the real cost.

Can a new staffing agency qualify for factoring?

Often, yes. The factor is mainly underwriting the clients you bill, so a young agency with thin credit can qualify if it places workers with creditworthy businesses. Concentration in one or two clients can lower the advance or limit how much you can factor.

What are the downsides of factoring?

It usually costs more than a bank line, the factor contacts your clients to collect, and some contracts carry monthly minimums or termination penalties. With recourse factoring you still owe the money if a client never pays. Read the carve-outs in any non-recourse deal.

Is factoring better than a loan for staffing payroll?

For a growing agency with good clients and thin cash, factoring usually wins because it funds within a day and grows with billings. A working capital loan or line of credit can cost less if you have established credit and only need occasional help. Many agencies use both.

What is freight factoring?

Freight factoring is the trucking version: a carrier sells load invoices to a factor and gets 90-97% the same or next day instead of waiting on a broker. Many freight factors add fuel advances at pickup. See freight factoring for carriers.
