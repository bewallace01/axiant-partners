How to Get Out of Business Debt: Stabilize, Rebuild, Then Refinance

A realistic plan for owners stuck with daily debits and stacked advances.

Home › Working Capital Loans › Articles › How to Get Out of Business Debt

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

To get out of business debt, stabilize cash flow first, rebuild second, and refinance last. Stop taking new advances and list every obligation. Replace stacked daily or weekly debits with one payment, for example an MCA consolidation loan at about 12-25% APR over 6-36 months. Then spend 6-12 months paying on time with steady deposits. Once your debt service coverage ratio is back near 1.25x, refinance into a line of credit or term loan. Debt is the bad kind when it has daily debits, a 40-150%+ effective APR, or payments above 15-20% of revenue.

How business debt turns into a trap

It usually happens in the same order. An advance or short-term loan covers a slow month. The daily debit squeezes cash, so a second advance covers the first. Now two or three lenders pull money every morning, your credit and statements look worse, and the banks that could refinance you at a sane rate say no. You are paying a lot and getting nowhere.

There is no single fix, but there is a plan that works for a lot of owners: stabilize, rebuild, then refinance. It takes about a year, and it only works if you stop adding new debt along the way.

What counts as bad business debt?

Not all debt is a problem. An equipment loan that pays for itself or a line of credit you draw and repay is normal. Debt turns bad when it:

  • takes payments daily or weekly instead of monthly,
  • carries a factor rate that works out to a 40-150%+ effective APR,
  • eats more than 15-20% of your revenue in payments, or
  • forces you to borrow again just to make the payments.

Merchant cash advances fall into this category most often. They are fast and easy to get because the provider looks at deposits more than credit, but stacked advances are the most common way owners end up stuck.

Step 1: Stabilize cash flow

Start by writing down every obligation: lender, balance, payment amount, payment frequency and payoff date. Then stop taking new advances. That one decision matters more than anything else on this page.

Next, replace the stack with a single payment. The cleanest way is an MCA consolidation loan: specialty lenders typically charge 12-25% APR over 6-36 months and fund in 5-15 days for owners with 660+ credit, a year in business and $150,000+ in annual revenue. For example, $80,000 consolidated at 18% over 24 months is about $3,994 a month, one payment instead of several daily debits.

If you cannot qualify for a loan yet, some owners take one larger advance that pays off the smaller ones. It simplifies things and can stop collection pressure, but it is still expensive money. Treat it as a bridge with an exit date, not a fix. If you are already behind, look at business debt relief before you miss another payment.

Consolidation is worth doing when it cuts your monthly debt service by 10% or more or replaces several high-cost debts, not when it only trims the rate slightly after fees. More detail in business debt consolidation.

Step 2: Rebuild for 6-12 months

Once payments are under control, the job is to look like a business a bank wants to lend to. Lenders reviewing a refinance often want to see existing debt kept current for close to a year. During that time:

  • Keep deposits steady in one main operating account.
  • Make every payment on time. One missed payment resets a lot of progress.
  • Avoid overdrafts and NSFs, which show up on every bank statement a lender reads.
  • Take no new advances, even small ones.
  • Fix what caused the problem: thin margins, weak pricing or loose budgeting. Consolidation does not fix those on its own.

For what underwriters check, see what lenders look for in a working capital application.

Step 3: When can you refinance into cheaper debt?

When your deposits are steady and your debt service coverage ratio (cash flow divided by debt payments) is back to about 1.25x, you can usually move to lower-cost money. A bank term loan runs about 9-13% APR over 5-7 years for owners with 680+ credit and 2+ years in business. An SBA 7(a) loan runs about 9.75-11% with a 10-year amortization typical and takes 4-10 weeks.

A business line of credit is often the best last step. You pay interest only on what you draw, payments are monthly instead of daily, and it gives you a cushion so the next slow month does not send you back to an advance. For the refinancing process in detail, read the guide to refinancing business debt.

Will creditors accept a 50% settlement?

Settlement means a creditor agrees to take less than the full balance, usually because the alternative is a default and a costly collection fight. Some creditors settle and some do not; the result depends on the creditor, the type of debt, whether it is secured or personally guaranteed, and how clearly you can show you cannot pay in full. Anyone who promises a percentage before seeing your file is guessing.

Be careful with the common advice to stop paying to build leverage. That can accelerate the full balance, trigger a confession of judgment where one exists, and get your account frozen. Settlement is for a business that truly cannot repay; if you can still make payments, consolidation and refinancing cost you less. See business debt settlement for how it works, and how to get out of an MCA if advances are the main problem.

A realistic 12-month timeline

  • Month 0-1: list every debt, stop new advances, consolidate into one payment.
  • Months 1-12: pay on time, keep deposits steady, no overdrafts, no new advances.
  • Month 6-12 and after: once coverage is near 1.25x, refinance into a line of credit or term loan with monthly payments.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you get out of business debt?

Work in three phases. Stabilize by stopping new advances and replacing stacked daily debits with one payment, rebuild for 6-12 months with on-time payments and steady deposits, then refinance into a lower-cost line of credit or term loan. Most owners need about a year to get through all three.

What counts as bad business debt?

Debt with daily or weekly debits, factor rates that work out to 40-150% or more as an effective APR, or payments that eat more than 15-20% of revenue. Debt you take on just to make payments on other debt is the clearest warning sign.

Will creditors accept a 50% settlement?

Some do and some do not. Outcomes depend on the creditor, the type of debt, whether it is secured or personally guaranteed, and how clearly you can show you cannot pay in full. Anyone who quotes a percentage before reviewing your file is guessing.

When can you refinance out of high-cost business debt?

Usually after 6-12 months of on-time payments, once deposits are steady and your debt service coverage ratio is back to about 1.25x. A specialty consolidation lender may move sooner; banks typically want 680+ credit and 2+ years in business.

Is it smart to use an MCA to pay off other MCAs?

Only as a short bridge. One larger advance can replace several daily debits and stop the juggling, but it is still expensive, and it only works if you stop stacking and refinance into a cheaper loan within the year. If you can qualify for an MCA consolidation loan at 12-25% APR, that is usually the better first move.

Should I stop paying my debts to negotiate?

Be careful. Stopping payments can accelerate the full balance, trigger a confession of judgment where one exists, and lead to a frozen bank account. Talk through relief and settlement options before you miss a payment, not after.
