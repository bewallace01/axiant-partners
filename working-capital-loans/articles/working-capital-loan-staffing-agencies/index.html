Payroll Funding for Staffing Firms: How to Cover Weekly Payroll Before Clients Pay

Payroll funding, factoring and credit lines for agencies that pay weekly and get paid in 30-60 days.

Home › Working Capital Loans › Articles › Payroll Funding for Staffing Firms

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

Payroll funding for staffing firms is financing that pays your workers on schedule while clients take 30-60 days to pay your invoices. The most common form advances 85-90% of each billed invoice within days, then sends the rest, minus a fee of about 1-5%, when the client pays. A firm with $50,000 in weekly payroll and clients who pay in 45 days has about $321,000 of payroll waiting to be collected at any time; that is the gap to fund. Lines of credit, term loans and SBA loans cost less but are slower and harder to get.

The payroll gap every staffing firm has

You pay your workers every week or every other week. Your clients pay your invoices 30, 45 or 60 days after you bill them. The difference is cash you have to front, and it grows with every worker you place.

Here is how big it gets. With $50,000 in weekly payroll and clients who pay in 45 days, you are carrying about six and a half weeks of payroll at any moment: roughly $321,000. Land a contract that doubles your headcount and that number doubles before the new client pays a dollar. Payroll funding exists to carry that balance so growth does not break your cash flow.

How do staffing agencies get money to make payroll?

A staffing agency earns the spread between the bill rate it charges clients and the pay rate it pays workers. The money comes in when clients pay their invoices; payroll goes out every week. Most agencies close that timing gap with one or more of these:

Option How it works Speed Cost Best for
Payroll funding / factoring Advance against invoices you have billed; the funder collects from the client As little as 24 hours per invoice after setup Fee about 1-5% of each invoice Temp staffing, newer agencies, fast growth
Business line of credit Draw for payroll, repay when clients pay 1-2 weeks to open, then same-day draws Interest on what you draw Established agencies; 600+ credit
Term loan Lump sum for a defined ramp-up Days to weeks Fixed payments Onboarding one large contract
SBA 7(a) working capital Larger, longer-term loan 30-90 days Lowest of the four Established firms with strong financials

Many firms combine them: payroll funding for the weekly cycle and a line of credit for one-off needs. For a broader look at the product category, see working capital loans.

How does payroll funding work for a staffing firm?

  1. Your workers log hours and you bill the client as usual.
  2. You send the invoice, and usually the approved timesheets, to the funder.
  3. The funder verifies the invoice and advances 85-90% of it, in as little as 24 hours once your account is set up.
  4. You run payroll on schedule.
  5. The client pays the funder on its normal terms, and the funder releases the rest minus its fee.

Payroll funding for staffing is usually built on invoice factoring, which is why the terms look similar. Advantages: the money is tied to real receivables, there is no fixed monthly payment, and many funders take over invoicing and collections. Drawbacks: fees add up on thin margins, and some clients do not like paying a third party. For a closer look at the factoring side, read invoice factoring for staffing agencies.

How much can a staffing firm get?

Amounts typically run from $25,000 to $2 million or more, depending on payroll volume, client quality, contract terms and revenue. Invoice-based funding grows with your billings, so a firm that adds workers gets more room automatically. A line of credit or term loan has a set limit. Larger agencies with diversified clients and strong collections land at the top of the range. For general sizing, see how much you can qualify for.

What funders look at

  • Client concentration: one or two clients doing most of your billing raises the risk.
  • Client credit: invoices to large, creditworthy companies or government agencies are the strongest collateral.
  • Payment terms: net-30 is easier to fund than net-60 or net-90.
  • Collections history: few disputes and steady payment support approval.
  • Time in business: 1-2+ years helps, especially for a line of credit.
  • Gross margin: a healthy spread between bill rate and pay rate shows the model can carry the cost of funding.

Payroll funding is the most forgiving on credit because it leans on your clients. A traditional working capital loan or line of credit usually wants 600+ credit, and some working capital programs go down to 550. SBA 7(a) loans offer larger amounts and longer terms but take 30-90 days and want strong financials.

Temp, contract and direct-hire staffing

Model Cash flow Typical fit
Temp / contingent Weekly payroll, net-30+ invoicing Payroll funding, factoring, line of credit
Contract / project Payroll over the life of a project Term loan, line of credit
Direct-hire / permanent Placement fee, no ongoing payroll General working capital

Temp and contract staffing have the biggest payroll gap, so payroll funding matters most there. Security firms run the same model on thinner margins; see payroll funding for security guard companies.

Funding a big new contract

A large win means hiring and paying dozens of workers before the first invoice is paid. Plan for 30-60 days of payroll before the client pays, plus a buffer for disputes and slow approvals. Set up funding before you sign the contract, not the week payroll is due.

What to avoid

Staffing margins are thin, so expensive money eats profit fast. Know the full cost of any funding offer (advance rate, fee, how the fee grows with time, setup charges and minimums) and compare it with a line of credit if you qualify. Be careful with contracts on long payment terms or with one client that dominates your book.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is payroll funding for staffing agencies?

It is financing that advances cash against the invoices you bill clients for hours worked, so you can pay workers before the client pays you. The funder typically advances 85-90% within days and releases the rest, minus its fee, when the client pays. Many payroll funders also handle invoicing and collections.

How do staffing agencies get money to make payroll?

The agency bills its clients for the hours its workers put in, at a bill rate above the pay rate, and the spread is its margin. Because clients pay on net-30 to net-60 terms and workers are paid weekly, most agencies fund the gap with payroll funding, factoring or a line of credit.

How much does payroll funding cost?

Fees typically run about 1-5% of each invoice, often more when clients pay slowly. On thin staffing margins that matters, so compare the all-in cost, including setup fees and monthly minimums, with a line of credit if your credit and history qualify you for one.

Can a new staffing agency get payroll funding?

Often, yes. Payroll funders rely mostly on your clients' credit and the invoices, so a young agency placing workers with creditworthy businesses can qualify before a bank would lend. A traditional working capital loan or line usually wants about 600+ credit and a longer track record.

How much can a staffing agency borrow?

Typically $25,000 to $2 million or more, depending on payroll volume, client quality, contract terms and revenue. Funding tied to invoices grows as your billings grow, while a line of credit has a fixed limit you have to request increases on.

What do lenders look for in a staffing agency?

Client concentration, client credit, payment terms (net-30 is easier than net-60 or net-90), collection history, time in business and the spread between bill rate and pay rate. Diversified, creditworthy clients and clean collections get the best terms.
