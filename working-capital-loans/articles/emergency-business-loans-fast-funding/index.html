Emergency Business Loans: What Can Fund in 24-72 Hours and What It Costs

How fast each option really funds, what it costs, and how to pick one when the clock is running.

Home › Working Capital Loans › Articles › Emergency Business Loans

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

Emergency business loans are fast-funding products that can put cash in your account in days instead of weeks. A merchant cash advance usually funds in 24-72 hours, revenue-based financing in 1-3 business days, and a short-term working capital loan in 3-10 business days. Speed costs more: a $50,000 advance at a 1.25 factor repays $62,500, while a $50,000 loan at 15% APR over 12 months runs about $4,513 a month and $4,155 in interest. Most fast programs want 6+ months in business and $10,000+ in monthly deposits.

What is an emergency business loan?

An emergency business loan is any financing that can close fast enough to fix a problem that will not wait. Payroll is due Friday and your biggest customer paid late. A machine breaks and stops production. A supplier offers a bulk discount that expires this week. Banks and SBA loans are cheaper, but they take 30-60 days or more, so they rarely help with a deadline measured in days.

In practice, “emergency” means one of three products: a merchant cash advance, revenue-based financing, or a short-term working capital loan. They differ on speed, price and what the lender looks at. The right one depends on how many days you have and how much you can afford to pay for those days.

Merchant cash advance: fastest, most expensive

An MCA gives you a lump sum that you repay as a share of daily card sales or a fixed daily or weekly debit. The provider underwrites your card volume, deposit history and overdrafts rather than your full credit file, so approval can come the same day and money in 24-72 hours. Pricing is a factor rate, usually 1.15 to 1.40: at 1.25, every $1 advanced costs $1.25. Because the term is short, that converts to an effective APR of 40-150% or more. It fits a real short-term gap backed by strong card sales. It does not fit long-term needs or a business already carrying another advance; stacking advances is how owners end up in a debt cycle.

Revenue-based financing: fast with gentler payments

Revenue-based financing is repaid as a percentage of monthly revenue, so the payment drops in a slow month. Lenders look at revenue consistency, growth and customer concentration. Funding usually takes 1-3 business days after approval, terms run about 6-18 months, and the cost is usually below an MCA. It suits subscription, SaaS and service businesses with predictable monthly income. See how fast revenue-based financing funds.

Short-term working capital loan: cheapest if you have a week

A short-term working capital loan has a fixed term, usually 6-18 months, and a set payment. Most lenders fund in 3-10 business days, and expedited programs can do it in 1-5 when the file is clean. APRs usually run 10-30% depending on credit and time in business. You will hand over more paperwork than for an MCA, but the total cost is usually far lower. For timelines by structure, see how fast a working capital loan can fund.

The difference between a $4,155 interest bill and a $12,500 advance fee is the price of two or three days. Sometimes that is worth paying: missing payroll or losing a contract costs more. If you have two or three weeks, it usually is not. Run your own numbers in the business loan calculator before you sign anything.

How do you get emergency business funds quickly?

  1. Pull 3-6 months of business bank statements from your main operating account as PDFs or through a direct bank connection. This is the document every fast lender asks for first.
  2. Have the basics ready: EIN, formation documents, a year-to-date P&L or revenue summary, and merchant processing statements if you take cards. Working capital and revenue-based lenders may also ask for tax returns.
  3. Apply early in the day. Many providers have cutoffs between 2:00 and 4:00 PM Eastern. A file approved before the cutoff has a much better chance of next-day ACH funding.
  4. Compare at least two offers on total payback, payment frequency and term, not on the advance amount.
  5. Ask about a wire if you truly need the money the same day. It is possible with some providers, usually for a fee.

Most fast options do not require collateral. What slows a file down is usually small: a statement from the wrong account, an unexplained overdraft, or a missing page.

Who qualifies for an emergency business loan?

  • Time in business: 6-12 months is the usual floor; 2+ years gets better pricing.
  • Revenue: about $10,000 a month in deposits for most MCA programs, more for some working capital lenders.
  • Credit: MCA and revenue-based providers often accept scores in the 500s. Short-term working capital lenders usually want about 600, and some programs go to 550.
  • Bank history: consistent deposits and few NSFs matter more than almost anything else on a fast file.

If your credit is the sticking point, start with business loans for bad credit. For a fuller checklist, see what lenders look for in a working capital application.

Other fast funding options

Business line of credit. Setting one up takes 1-2 weeks, so it does not solve today's problem. Once it is open, a draw is available the same day. Opening one before you need it is the cheapest emergency plan there is. See business lines of credit.

Invoice factoring. If the emergency is a slow-paying B2B customer, a factor can advance most of the invoice (80-90% is common) within days of verifying it. See invoice factoring.

Equipment financing. If a machine or vehicle broke, equipment financing can approve in 1-5 days for qualified deals and keeps the cost off your cash flow.

Business term loans. Some term loans fund in 3-5 days on a short application.

Red flags to watch for

Fast money attracts bad actors. Walk away from anyone who wants a large “processing” or “guarantee” fee before you are funded, pushes you to sign within the hour, will not show the total payback in writing, or promises guaranteed approval with no review at all. A normal application fee is one thing; a big upfront fee is not. A legitimate provider shows the factor rate or APR, the holdback or payment amount, and the total you will repay. Read the whole agreement, including what happens if sales drop.

Two examples

Restaurant, $30,000 for payroll in three days. Strong card volume, 580 credit. An MCA can fund in 24-72 hours; a working capital loan would likely take 5-10 days. The owner takes the advance despite the cost because missing payroll is worse, and plans to pay it off before taking any other advance.

Subscription software company, $75,000 for a six-month project. $50,000 in monthly revenue and one week of lead time. Revenue-based financing can fund in 2-3 days, costs less than an MCA, and the payment moves with revenue.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fastest type of business loan?

A merchant cash advance is usually the fastest. Approval often comes the same day and money lands in 24-72 hours because the provider looks at card sales and bank deposits instead of a full credit review. Revenue-based financing and expedited working capital loans can fund in 1-5 business days.

Can I get a business loan the same day?

Same-day approval is realistic with an MCA and some online lenders. Same-day money is rarer: ACH transfers usually settle the next business day, and a same-day wire often carries a fee. Apply early in the day, before the provider's 2-4 PM Eastern cutoff, with your statements ready.

What credit score do I need for an emergency business loan?

MCA and revenue-based providers focus on deposits and many work with scores in the 500s. Short-term working capital lenders usually want about 600, and some programs go to 550. A higher score mostly buys you a lower price, not a faster deposit.

What is the monthly payment on a $50,000 business loan?

On a 12-month working capital loan, $50,000 runs about $4,396 a month at 10% APR, $4,513 at 15% and $4,874 at 30%. Total interest is about $2,750, $4,155 and $8,492. A $50,000 MCA at a 1.25 factor repays $62,500 through daily or weekly debits.

Are emergency business loans more expensive?

Yes. A factor-rate advance can work out to 40-150% or more as an effective APR, while short-term loans usually land around 10-30% APR. SBA and bank loans cost less but take 30-60 days or longer. If you can wait two or three weeks, the cheaper option usually wins.

Can I get emergency funding with bad credit?

Yes. MCA and revenue-based providers weigh revenue over credit and often approve scores in the 500s when deposits are steady and there are few overdrafts. Expect a higher factor rate and short repayment. See business loans for bad credit for the other options.
