Business Debt Consolidation & Refinancing

When refinancing business debt actually saves money — and the right product to do it

Quick answer

Refinancing business debt makes sense when a new loan saves 10%+ on monthly debt service, consolidates multiple high-cost loans, or extends term to relieve cash-flow pressure. The workhorse: SBA 7(a) for multi-debt consolidation. Alternatives: conventional bank term loan (strong credit + relationship), equipment refinancing (stranded asset loans), working capital refi (short-term unsecured). Refinancing just to drop the rate by 50 bps rarely pays after fees. Plan the timeline: SBA 7(a) refi takes 4-10 weeks; conventional 3-6; equipment 24-72 hours; working capital 3-10 days. The right move is product-matched to the debt being refinanced.

Most small businesses end up with debt scattered across multiple lenders, products, and rates — an MCA from a year ago, a working capital loan from a regional bank, an equipment loan from an asset-based lender, plus credit-card balances. Each works alone; together they create cash-flow stress and make existing debt service hard to manage. Refinancing consolidates and (usually) lowers the cost. This guide walks when it makes sense, what product to use, and the actual playbook. For broader context see SBA loans and business term loans.

When Refinancing Makes Sense

Three situations where refinancing pays:

  1. Multiple debts at high rates: consolidating 3-5 loans averaging 18% APR into a single SBA 7(a) at 10% saves materially. The math typically shows year-one savings exceeding the refi's closing costs.
  2. Cash flow constraint: current monthly payments are eating operating capital. Extending term (5-year to 10-year via SBA) cuts monthly payment 30-40%, freeing cash even at similar APR.
  3. Single loan with materially better available rate: existing loan at 14%, new loan at 9%. Cost typically pays back in 8-18 months.

If you are refinancing with SBA money, check SBA refinancing rules first.

Three situations where refinancing usually doesn't pay:

  • Reducing rate by 50-100 bps on a single loan — closing costs eat the savings
  • Refinancing 6 months before existing loan matures anyway — just wait
  • Refinancing into a longer-term loan with a much higher total cost — cash flow improves but total interest paid balloons

Products That Refinance Business Debt

SBA 7(a) — the workhorse

SBA 7(a) consolidates multiple business debts into one loan at lower rate and longer term. Standard 10-year amortization. Prime + 2.5-3% (≈9.5-11% in 2026). Up to $5M. Requires 10%+ savings on monthly debt service to qualify (SBA rule). Best for: multi-debt consolidation and cash-flow relief. It can't pay off an active MCA (SOP 50 10 8.1).

Conventional bank term loan

Refinances single loan at lower rate or different term. 5-7 year typical. 8-13% APR. Best for: borrowers with strong bank relationships, single existing loan to refinance, speed-sensitive deals.

Equipment refinancing

Refinances existing equipment loans. Particularly useful when the original loan's rate is high (often happens when the original loan was specialty/sub-prime) and current market rate is materially better. Approval often comes in 24-72 hours, with funding usually within 5-10 business days.

Working capital refinancing

Refinances short-term unsecured debt (MCAs, short-term WCLs, business credit cards). Often consolidates multiple short-term debts into one amortizing working capital loan. 3-10 day close.

Commercial real estate refinance

Refinances commercial mortgage when rate environment improves, balloon approaches, or property has appreciated. Cash-out refi can also fund business needs. See cash-out refinance commercial property.

How do you refinance a business loan?

You take out a new loan, the new lender pays the old one off at closing, and you make one payment going forward. The work is in picking the right replacement and proving it helps:

  1. List what you owe: each lender, balance, payment, rate, maturity and any prepayment penalty.
  2. Ask for payoff letters from each lender. The new lender needs them, and they show the real cost to get out.
  3. Match the debt to the product in the table below. Several debts usually point to SBA 7(a); a single loan with good credit points to a bank term loan.
  4. Apply with a full package: three years of tax returns, financial statements, a debt schedule and the payoff letters. Lenders check that the business can cover the new payment and, for SBA, that the new payment is at least 10% lower than the old ones combined.
  5. Close: the new lender wires the payoffs directly and the old loans close.

Why would a business refinance a loan?

For one of three reasons: to lower the monthly payment by stretching the term, to replace several high-cost debts with one cheaper loan, or to swap a single loan for a materially lower rate. A lower payment frees cash to run the business; a lower rate cuts total cost. The two don't always come together, so decide which one you need before you shop.

Real Example: $400K Across 4 Loans

Hypothetical small business with:

  • $100K short-term business loan, $684/business day = ~$3,420/wk (about $14,820/mo), roughly 7 months left
  • $80K working capital loan, 22% APR, 18 months remaining, ~$5,258/mo
  • $140K equipment loan, 14% APR, 4 years remaining, ~$3,826/mo
  • $80K business credit cards, 24% APR average, ~$2,400/mo minimum
  • Total monthly debt service: ~$26,304

Refinanced into a single SBA 7(a):

  • $400K SBA 7(a), 10-year, 10.25% APR
  • Monthly P&I: ~$5,342
  • Monthly savings: ~$20,962/mo, about $251,500/year
  • Closing costs: ~$8K (origination + filing). Pays back in 2 weeks of cash flow improvement.

The short-term loan and the cards were the cash-flow killers. The refinance frees about $21K a month of operating cash. Total interest over 10 years on the SBA is higher than the existing loans combined would have been — but the existing structure was unsustainable. Cash flow is the constraint that mattered.

If one of those debts had been an active merchant cash advance, the SBA loan could not have paid it off. MCAs are refinanced first, usually into a term or consolidation loan; see refinancing an MCA into a term loan.

What is the monthly payment on a $100,000 business loan?

It depends mostly on the term. Using this guide's ranges, $100,000 on an SBA-style 10-year schedule at 10.25% is about $1,335 a month, while a 5-year bank loan at 8-13% runs about $2,028-$2,275. That gap is why refinancing short debt onto a longer schedule frees so much cash, even when the rate barely moves.

The Refinancing Playbook

  1. Inventory all debt: lender, balance, payment, rate, term, maturity, prepayment penalty. Compute weighted average rate and total monthly debt service.
  2. Get payoff statements from each existing lender. New lender pays off existing at close; payoff statements are required documents.
  3. Pick the right product: SBA for multi-debt, conventional for single, equipment refi for asset-based, WCL refi for short-term unsecured.
  4. Submit complete package: universal docs + debt schedule + payoff statements + 3 years tax returns + financial statements.
  5. Underwrite: lender verifies the savings test (10%+ for SBA), runs DSCR on new structure, computes the borrower's improved position.
  6. Close and consolidate: new lender wires payoff amounts directly to existing lenders. Old loans close; new consolidated loan is the only debt.

Things to Watch For

  • Prepayment penalties on existing loans — especially MCAs and some equipment loans. Adds to total refi cost.
  • SBA savings rule: 10% reduction in monthly debt service required for SBA 7(a) refi. Compute upfront.
  • Maintaining covenants on the new loan — SBA and conventional loans often have ongoing financial-statement and ratio requirements.
  • Same-lender refinances: sometimes the existing lender will modify rather than refi, saving fees. Ask before going elsewhere.
  • Don't refi into another short-term product: the goal is to exit short-term high-cost debt, not roll into a different version.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does refinancing business debt make sense?

When the new loan saves at least 10% on monthly debt service (SBA threshold), consolidates multiple high-cost loans into one lower-cost loan, or extends term to relieve cash-flow pressure. Refinancing just to reduce the rate by 50 bps rarely pays after origination and SBA fees.

What products refinance business debt?

SBA 7(a) is the workhorse: consolidates multiple business debts at lower rate and longer term. Conventional bank term loans for borrowers with strong credit and bank relationships. Equipment refinancing for stranded asset-secured loans. Working capital refinancing for short-term unsecured debt. Each fits different debt types.

Can SBA refinance an existing SBA loan?

Yes — SBA 7(a) can refinance existing SBA debt if the new loan provides material benefit (typically 10%+ reduction in monthly payment). The SBA confirms the refi makes sense before approving.

Can I refinance MCAs into a term loan?

Often yes, with a conventional or consolidation loan that pays off MCA balances and replaces them with a single amortizing loan at lower cost. An SBA 7(a) generally cannot refinance an active MCA under SOP 50 10 8.1. The challenge is qualifying — lenders want to see the file isn't in distress before refinancing MCA debt. Most successful MCA refis happen before stacking gets out of control.

How long does refinancing take?

SBA 7(a) refi: 4-10 weeks (faster with Preferred Lenders). Conventional: 3-6 weeks. Equipment refi: approval in 24-72 hours, funding in 5-10 business days. Working capital refi: 3-10 business days. Plan the timeline; if cash flow is acute, the refi may not close fast enough.

How do I refinance a business loan?

Get payoff letters for the debt you want to replace, pick the product that fits (SBA 7(a) for several debts, a bank term loan for one, an equipment refinance for equipment), and apply with tax returns, financial statements and a debt schedule. The new lender pays the old loans off at closing. SBA refinances also need the new payment to be at least 10% lower than the old ones combined.

Why would a business refinance its loans?

To lower the monthly payment by stretching the term, to combine several high-cost debts into one cheaper loan, or to replace one loan with a materially lower rate. Trimming the rate by 50-100 basis points on a single loan rarely pays after fees.

What is the monthly payment on a $100,000 business loan?

About $1,335 a month over 10 years at 10.25%, an SBA-style refinance. A 5-year bank loan at 8-13% runs about $2,028-$2,275 a month, and over 7 years about $1,559-$1,819.
