Quick answer

A multifamily bridge loan is short-term, usually interest-only financing for an apartment property that is not ready for permanent agency debt yet. What Is a Multifamily Bridge Loan?

It is short-term debt that carries an apartment property from where it is today to the point where an agency lender will refinance it. Agency loans want stabilized occupancy and proven income. A bridge lender funds the gap: the renovation, the lease-up, or the months between a maturing loan and a permanent one. Lenders price that risk higher, and they underwrite one thing above everything else, which is how you will pay them back.

A $5M loan sits in the middle of the debt fund market. It is big enough for institutional bridge lenders and small enough that a well-prepared sponsor can close quickly. This page uses a $5M deal as the worked example; the same structure applies to smaller and larger apartment deals. For bridge loans on other property types, see commercial bridge loans.

When Do Sponsors Use One?

  • Value-add acquisition. Buying a 60-100 unit Class C property at 70-85% occupancy and repositioning it to Class B over 12-18 months. The bridge funds the purchase and light renovation; an agency loan takes it out at stabilization.
  • Lease-up. A newly delivered property at 60-75% leased needs 6-12 months to reach 90%+ occupancy.
  • Maturing debt. A CMBS or bank loan comes due in 60-120 days and the agency refinance cannot close in time. A bridge buys 12-18 months.
  • Cash-out or recap. Pulling equity for the next acquisition, or buying out a partner, when a permanent loan is planned within about 12 months.

What Are Multifamily Bridge Loan Rates and Terms?

Pricing follows how much work the property needs. Typical 2026 ranges:

Loan typeRatePointsLeverageTerm
Senior (90%+ occupied)9-11% fixed1-2Up to 65% LTV12-24 months
Value-add or lease-up10-12% fixed2-3Up to 70-75% LTC18-36 months
Heavy lift (gut rehab)11-14% fixed2-4Up to 70-75% LTC24-36 months

Floating loans price off SOFR: about SOFR plus 4.25-5.50% for senior bridge and plus 5.25-6.50% for value-add. With SOFR at 3.89% on November 10, 2026, a floating senior bridge came to about 8.1-9.4%. Expect an exit fee of 0-0.5% on senior loans and 0.5-1.0% on value-add, and 0.25-0.50% for each six-month extension. Current pricing is tracked in typical commercial bridge loan rates.

Senior bridge fits a property that is 90%+ occupied with a refinance planned in 12-18 months. It sizes to up to 65% of stabilized value (up to 70% of as-is value), wants about 1.10-1.25x DSCR on an as-stabilized basis, and is often non-recourse at debt funds.

Value-add bridge sizes to cost: up to 70-75% of purchase, renovation, closing and an interest reserve. The reserve, usually 6-12 months, is held back at closing. On $5M at 11%, six months of reserve is $275,000. Renovation money is released in 3-5 draws against inspected work. Current DSCR can sit below 1.0x as long as the as-stabilized number clears 1.25x.

The value-add bridge guide covers draws and reserves in more detail.

Is It Hard to Qualify for a Multifamily Bridge Loan?

At $5M, a debt fund looks at you as closely as the building. The realistic bar:

  • Experience: 2-3 prior multifamily acquisitions, or 100+ units owned or recently sold. A first-time multifamily sponsor usually needs a key principal with a track record, or more equity.
  • Net worth outside the deal: $1M+, ideally equal to the loan amount. $5M+ gets better pricing.
  • Liquidity after closing: $500K-$1M+.
  • Credit: 680+ FICO for the key principal and any guarantor. A bankruptcy in the last 7 years stops most lenders.
  • Equity: At 70% LTC, you bring 30% of total cost. A $5M loan at 70% of a $7.15M total cost means about $2.15M of your equity; at 75% LTC on the same cost, about $1.79M. Lenders check that the equity is sourced and seasoned, not borrowed from another loan.
  • A business plan with specifics: renovation scope and GC contract, written rent comps, lease-up assumptions and the permanent lender you plan to refinance with.

How Fast Can a Multifamily Bridge Loan Close?

A debt fund can close in 30 days, but the clock starts at the signed term sheet, and only a prepared sponsor makes it. 45 days is more typical, and 60 for complex deals.

Before the term sheet: a signed LOI or contract; your financial statement, 3 years of personal returns, schedule of real estate owned and proof of liquidity; a track record memo; the rent roll, T-12 and T-3; your business plan; and term sheets from 3-5 lenders.

Days 1-7: sign the term sheet and wire the good-faith deposit ($25K-$75K). Order the appraisal (2-3 weeks; a rush costs about $1K-$2K more), Phase I environmental (7-14 days), title and survey, and a property condition report. Loan committee usually approves within 5-10 days.

Days 8-20: third-party reports arrive (days 14-18 is typical), lender's counsel drafts loan documents, your counsel negotiates, insurance is bound, and the single-purpose borrowing entity is set up.

Days 21-30: final settlement statement, any pre-close walkthrough, then sign, fund and record. Reserves go to controlled accounts after closing.

If 30 days is not fast enough, hard money can close in 7-14 days at about 11-14% plus 2-4 points and 50-65% LTV. How fast a bridge loan can close covers what slows deals down.

Who Are the Multifamily Bridge Lenders?

Debt funds are the main source at $5M-$50M, usually non-recourse with bad-boy carve-outs (fraud, environmental, voluntary bankruptcy, unauthorized transfer). Examples include Madison Realty Capital, BridgeInvest, Inland Mortgage Capital, Acres Capital, Pacific Western Bridge, Lument Capital and Greystone. Expect to pay 50-150 bps more than a bank for non-recourse.

Banks lend from their balance sheet, usually with recourse, and work best where you already have a deposit relationship and 45-60 days. Customers Bank, Eastern Bank, Pinnacle Bank, KeyBank Real Estate Capital and regional community banks are in this group.

Agency-affiliated lenders such as Lument, Greystone and Walker & Dunlop offer bridge loans with a built-in path to their own Freddie Mac or Fannie Mae permanent loan, which is often called bridge-to-agency.

What Is the Exit on a Multifamily Bridge Loan?

"How are you paying me back?" is the question the lender is really underwriting. The common exits on a $5M deal:

  • Freddie Mac Conventional Small: on April 15, 2026, Freddie Mac folded its Small Balance Loan program into its conventional platform. Typically 5, 7 or 10-year fixed, 30-year amortization, non-recourse. Loan limits and pricing changed with the switch, so confirm current terms before you count on it.
  • Fannie Mae small loans: a similar structure. Get quotes from both.
  • FHA 223(f): 35-year fully amortizing, up to 85% LTV, non-recourse. The slowest to close (6-9 months) and usually the cheapest, for a long hold.
  • Bank debt: 5 or 10-year fixed with 25-year amortization, usually recourse.
  • A sale at stabilization.

Your plan has to show a stabilized NOI that supports the permanent loan at about 70-75% LTV and 1.25x+ DSCR. If that math does not work, the bridge lender will not fund. See commercial real estate loan rates for permanent pricing.

What Does a $5M Multifamily Bridge Loan Cost?

Example: a $5M senior bridge at 10% with 1.5 points, an 18-month term and a 6-month interest reserve.

  • Origination (1.5 points): $75,000 at closing
  • Interest reserve (6 months): $250,000 held back
  • Net proceeds at closing: $4,675,000
  • Monthly interest after the reserve runs out (months 7-18): about $41,667, paid from operations
  • Total interest over 18 months: $750,000
  • Exit fee (0.25%): $12,500
  • Third-party costs: appraisal $5,000, environmental $3,000, property condition $3,000, title $15,000, legal $25,000 = $51,000
  • Total: about $888,500, or roughly 18% of the loan over 18 months (about 12% a year)

That is expensive debt, and it only makes sense when the renovation or lease-up lifts NOI enough that the permanent loan pays off the bridge with room to spare. Before you sign, read what can go wrong with a bridge loan.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a multifamily bridge loan?

It is a short-term loan, usually 12-36 months and interest-only, secured by an apartment property that does not qualify for a permanent agency loan yet. Sponsors use it to buy and renovate, finish a lease-up, or replace maturing debt, then refinance into Freddie Mac, Fannie Mae, FHA or bank debt once the property is stabilized.

What are multifamily bridge loan rates in 2026?

Fixed rates typically run 9-11% for a stabilized property, 10-12% for value-add and 11-14% for a heavy renovation, plus 1-4 points. Floating loans price off SOFR: a senior bridge at SOFR plus 4.25-5.50% came to about 8.1-9.4% with SOFR at 3.89% on November 10, 2026.

Is it difficult to qualify for a multifamily bridge loan?

At $5M the lender looks at the sponsor as hard as the property. The usual bar is 2-3 prior multifamily deals or 100+ units, net worth of $1M+ outside the deal, $500K-$1M+ of liquidity after closing and 680+ credit. First-time sponsors usually need an experienced key principal or more equity.

What's the downside of a bridge loan?

Cost and timing. On the $5M example on this page, the all-in cost is about 18% over 18 months, and if the business plan runs late you pay extension fees of 0.25-0.50% each or face a refinance on bad terms. Bank bridge loans are also usually recourse.

How much would a $300K bridge loan cost?

At 10.5% interest-only with 2 points, a $300,000 bridge costs $2,625 a month in interest and $6,000 in points at closing. Held 12 months, that is about $37,500 before appraisal, legal and any exit fee.

Can a $5M multifamily bridge loan close in 30 days?

Yes, with a debt fund and a prepared sponsor: signed contract, financial package ready, third-party reports ordered on day one and counsel engaged. 45 days is more typical and 60 for complex deals. Faster than 30 days usually means hard money at about 11-14% plus 2-4 points.

What is the usual exit on a multifamily bridge loan?

Most sponsors refinance into a Freddie Mac or Fannie Mae small loan at stabilization; FHA 223(f), bank debt or a sale are the other exits. The lender underwrites that exit up front, so your stabilized NOI must support the permanent loan at about 70-75% LTV and 1.25x+ DSCR.
