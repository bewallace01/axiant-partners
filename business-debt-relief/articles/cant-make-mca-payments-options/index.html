MCA Payments: How They Work and Your Options If You Can’t Pay

How merchant cash advance debits are set, what they cost per day, and the right move for your situation: still current, behind, in default, sued or frozen.

← Back to Debt Relief Articles

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

MCA payments are taken out of your sales every business day or week: either a fixed share of daily card sales, often 10–20%, or a fixed ACH debit from your bank account. A $100,000 advance at a 1.25–1.45 factor means $496 to $1,151 a business day, depending on whether it runs 12 or 6 months. If you can’t keep up, ask for reconciliation while you are still current, restructure through mediation if you are behind, and never take another advance to cover the ones you have.

“I can’t make my MCA payment” covers very different situations, and the right move is different for each. A business that is current but drowning in daily debits needs a different play than one that has already defaulted or been sued. Start with how the payment works, then find your situation below. For every exit method side by side, see how to get out of stacked MCAs without bankruptcy.

How do MCA payments work?

A merchant cash advance is repaid from your sales, not on a monthly loan schedule. The agreement sets one of two methods:

  • A holdback on card sales. A fixed share of each day’s credit and debit card sales, often 10–20%, goes to the funder until the payback is met. Slow days mean smaller payments.
  • A fixed ACH debit. A set amount comes out of your business bank account every business day or every week, whatever your sales were.

The holdback sets how long the advance runs. At $5,000 of daily card sales and a 15% holdback, you pay $750 a day, so a $135,000 payback takes about 180 business days, roughly 8.6 months. If sales fall, a reconciliation clause should lower the debit; check whether your agreement has one and how to request it. The cost itself is fixed by the factor rate; see how a factor rate converts to APR.

How much is the daily payment on a $100,000 MCA?

Payback is the advance times the factor rate. Spread over 6 months (about 126 business days) or 12 months (about 252):

FactorPayback6-mo daily12-mo daily
1.25$125,000$992$496
1.35$135,000$1,071$536
1.45$145,000$1,151$575

At these factors that works out to roughly 46–158% APR. For typical pricing, see typical merchant cash advance rates.

What should you do first if you can’t make your MCA payments?

Write down the facts. Any funder, specialist or attorney will ask for them:

  • Each advance: balance, daily or weekly payment, days remaining, funder, and status (current, late, defaulted).
  • Total daily debits as a share of revenue. Over 25% is severe; over 40% means operations are failing.
  • Whether collection has started: default notices, collection calls, lawsuits, confessions of judgment or a frozen account.
  • Your true cash flow after debits. If it is negative, you have a runway problem that needs action now.

What if you’re still current?

You haven’t missed a payment, but the daily debits are eating the business. You have the most options now, so use them before that changes:

  • Invoke reconciliation. Most MCA contracts let you request a lower remittance when revenue drops. Few owners use it. Document the decline and ask in writing.
  • Reverse consolidation. Replace several daily debits with one lower weekly payment sized to your cash flow; see reverse consolidation.
  • Refinance, if you still qualify. Strong credit and revenue may let you pay everything off with one cheaper term loan.

What if you’re behind or about to default?

Speed matters, because collection escalates quickly:

  • Don’t go silent. Funders are far more flexible with owners who engage early.
  • Restructure through mediation. Debt mediation negotiates with every creditor at once to build one consolidated, lower payment, halt collections and remove UCC liens. It is built for stacked borrowers in or near default.
  • Get help before a judgment lands. Resolving the debt now is far easier than after a funder has a judgment and a frozen account.

What if collection has already started?

A frozen account or a lawsuit is an emergency with its own playbook. Handle the legal action first, then fold the resolution into a relief program:

  • Frozen account? Act within 24–48 hours; see my MCA froze my bank account.
  • Sued or hit with a COJ? Respond on time and get help; see being sued by an MCA or hit with a COJ.

Can you just stop paying an MCA?

Stopping on your own is usually the most expensive way out. Missed MCA payments can trigger reversed ACH fees, a default under the agreement and collection calls, and if the funder has a judgment or a confession of judgment, a restraining notice that freezes your bank account. The contract’s own reconciliation clause is the legitimate way to lower a payment when sales fall, and mediation is the way to restructure several advances at once. If you are thinking about stopping, talk to an attorney first.

The rules that apply in every situation

  • Never take another MCA to cover the ones you can’t pay. Stacking is the fastest route to collapse.
  • Act early. Every option is wider before default and collection start.
  • Document everything. Balances, payments, notices: you will need them for every path.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do MCA payments work?

You repay a merchant cash advance from your sales: either a fixed share of daily card sales, often 10–20%, or a fixed daily or weekly ACH debit from your bank account. Payments continue until you have repaid the advance times the factor rate. A holdback shrinks on slow days; a fixed ACH debit does not.

How much is the daily payment on a $100,000 MCA?

At a 1.25 factor you repay $125,000: about $496 a business day over 12 months or $992 over 6. At 1.45 you repay $145,000: about $575 a day over 12 months or $1,151 over 6. That is roughly 46–158% APR.

What happens if I can't make my MCA payments?

Missed payments can trigger reversed ACH fees, a default under the agreement and collection calls, and if the funder has a judgment or confession of judgment, a restraining notice that freezes your bank account. Depending on how far along you are, options range from the reconciliation clause to reverse consolidation to debt mediation. The earlier you act, the more options you keep.

Can I lower or pause my MCA daily payment?

Sometimes. Many MCA contracts include a reconciliation clause that lets you request a lower remittance when revenue drops; document the decline and ask in writing. Some funders agree to a temporary reduction for a cooperative borrower, and reverse consolidation or debt mediation can restructure several advances at once.

Should I take another MCA if I can't make my payments?

No. Taking a new advance to cover existing ones is stacking, and it is the fastest way to turn a cash-flow problem into a collapse. Each advance adds another daily debit and another UCC lien. Reduce and restructure what you already owe instead.

What is the first thing to do if I can't pay my MCA?

List each advance's balance, daily or weekly payment and status, and work out your true cash flow after the debits. Then match your situation: reconciliation or reverse consolidation if you are current, debt mediation if you are in or near default, and the frozen-account or lawsuit playbooks if collection has started.
