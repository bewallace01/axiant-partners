MCA Lawsuit or Confession of Judgment? What to Do Next

What an MCA lawsuit or a confession of judgment means for your business, what the funder can do with a judgment, and the four ways to respond.

← Back to Debt Relief Articles

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

If an MCA funder sues you, respond before the deadline with a business attorney. A missed deadline becomes a default judgment, and a judgment lets the funder freeze your bank account, levy funds, enforce UCC liens and pursue your personal guarantee. A confession of judgment (COJ) skips the lawsuit: you signed it at funding, so your first sign may be a frozen account. Your options are to respond, negotiate a discounted payoff, restructure every advance through mediation, or move to vacate a judgment entered improperly.

An MCA lawsuit or a confession of judgment is frightening because of how fast it can move from paperwork to a frozen account. Speed cuts both ways, though: owners who engage quickly almost always end up better off than owners who freeze. This guide covers what a COJ is, what an MCA lawsuit means in practice, and the concrete ways to protect your business. For the related emergency, see my MCA froze my bank account; for the broader way out, business debt relief.

What is the difference between an MCA lawsuit and a COJ?

Not every MCA collection action is a COJ. Both end in the same place, a judgment that lets the funder collect aggressively, but you get very different amounts of warning:

StepLawsuitCOJ
How it startsYou are served with a complaintThe funder files the COJ you signed at funding
NoticeA set window to respondOften none; the frozen account may be your first sign
SpeedNormal civil timelineJudgment in days
Your defenseAnswer on time with an attorneyMove to vacate if entered improperly
If ignoredDefault judgmentJudgment already entered

That difference in runway is why you read every notice the day it arrives and act on the deadline.

What is a confession of judgment?

A confession of judgment, also called a cognovit note, is a document many MCA funders require at funding. In it, you agree in advance that if you default, the funder may go to court and get a judgment against your business, and usually against you personally, without a lawsuit, notice or a chance to defend. It exists to make collection fast and one-sided.

  • It turns a missed daily debit into an enforceable judgment in days, not months.
  • With the judgment, the funder can restrain your bank account, levy funds and enforce UCC liens.
  • New York limited out-of-state COJ enforcement in 2019 after widespread abuse, and enforceability varies by state. A COJ that doesn’t meet the relevant state’s requirements may be vulnerable.

What happens after an MCA funder gets a judgment?

Once a funder holds a judgment, it can:

  • Restrain your bank account, freezing funds up to the judgment amount and often more; see our guide on frozen accounts.
  • Levy funds, pulling the money through a marshal or sheriff.
  • Enforce UCC liens on business assets and receivables.
  • Pursue you personally under the personal guarantee most MCA contracts include.
  • Add interest and costs, so the balance grows while the judgment stands.

Waiting to see what happens is expensive: the judgment doesn’t go away, and the tools to enforce it only get sharper.

What are your options if an MCA sues you?

1. Respond, don’t default

If you’ve been served, get a business litigation attorney and respond before the deadline. A default judgment hands the funder everything without a fight. Even a simple, timely response preserves leverage.

2. Negotiate a payoff or settlement

Funders often prefer a guaranteed, discounted payoff to a long collection battle with an uncertain recovery. A negotiated lump sum or structured settlement can resolve the judgment and stop enforcement.

3. Restructure the whole debt load

If the lawsuit is one of several stacked advances, settling one funder won’t fix the cash-flow hole. Business debt mediation firms negotiate with all your creditors at once to build a single resolution that reduces payments, halts collections and lowers legal exposure. Reverse consolidation can pay off several positions into one lower payment. Axiant connects qualifying, distressed businesses with the partners who do this.

4. Challenge the judgment

When a COJ or judgment was entered improperly (wrong venue, an inflated amount, defective paperwork, or against a state’s COJ rules), an attorney can move to vacate it. This is the right tool when the funder won’t negotiate or the paperwork is flawed.

Is it worth fighting an MCA lawsuit?

Responding is almost always worth it, because the alternative is a default judgment with full collection powers. Fighting doesn’t have to mean a long trial: a timely answer keeps your defenses alive and gives you leverage to negotiate a discounted payoff. Whether to go further and challenge the judgment depends on the paperwork, which is a question for your attorney.

What happens if you stop paying an MCA?

Missed payments can lead to a default under the agreement, collection calls and, if the funder holds a COJ or wins a lawsuit, a judgment and a frozen account. If the daily debits are the problem, the earlier options are better: ask for reconciliation while you are current, or restructure through mediation. See MCA payments: your options if you can’t pay.

What should you not do?

  • Don’t ignore a lawsuit or notice. Default judgments are the most common, and most avoidable, bad outcome.
  • Don’t take a new MCA to settle the suit. It adds a position to a stack that is already breaking.
  • Don’t sign anything new without reading it. Some “settlements” are just another advance with another COJ.
  • Don’t assume the demand amount is correct. Judgments and restraints often overstate the balance, and it is negotiable.
  • Don’t go it alone if assets are at risk. Pair a business attorney with a debt-relief specialist.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if an MCA funder sues me?

Get a business litigation attorney and respond before the deadline on the complaint. Missing it produces a default judgment, which lets the funder freeze your account, levy funds and enforce UCC liens. A timely response also gives you leverage to negotiate a payoff or restructure.

What is a confession of judgment (COJ) in a merchant cash advance?

It is a document some MCA funders require at funding in which you agree in advance that, if you default, the funder can get a court judgment without a trial or notice. It lets a funder turn a missed payment into an enforceable judgment quickly. New York restricted out-of-state COJ enforcement in 2019, and enforceability varies by state, so some COJs can be challenged.

What happens after an MCA gets a judgment against my business?

The funder can serve a restraining notice that freezes your business bank account, levy funds through a marshal or sheriff, enforce a UCC lien on business assets, and in some cases pursue you personally under the personal guarantee. The judgment also accrues interest. Negotiating a payoff or challenging the judgment quickly is what limits the damage.

Can I fight or vacate an MCA confession of judgment?

Sometimes. A business litigation attorney may move to vacate a judgment entered improperly, in the wrong venue, for an inflated amount, or under a COJ that does not meet the state's requirements. Even when a judgment stands, you usually keep leverage to negotiate a discounted payoff.

What happens if you stop paying an MCA?

Missed payments can trigger a default under the agreement, reversed ACH fees and collection calls, and if the funder has a COJ or wins a lawsuit, a judgment and a frozen account. Asking for reconciliation while you are current, or restructuring through mediation, is cheaper than stopping and waiting.

Is it worth fighting an MCA lawsuit?

Responding is almost always worth it, because the alternative is a default judgment with full collection powers. A timely answer keeps your defenses alive and gives you leverage to settle for less. Whether to go further and challenge the judgment depends on the paperwork, so ask your attorney.

Should I take another MCA to settle a lawsuit?

No. Taking a new advance to pay a funder that is suing you adds another high-cost position to a stack that is already failing. The lasting fix is to reduce and restructure the existing debt through settlement, mediation or reverse consolidation.
