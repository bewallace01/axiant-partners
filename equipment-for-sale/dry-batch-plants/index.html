What is a dry batch plant?

A dry batch plant weighs aggregate, sand and cement and dispenses them — still dry — into a mixer truck. The truck adds the mixing on the drive to the pour. So the plant is a weighing and loading machine, not a mixing machine.

That sounds like a limitation until you notice it is usually the right answer. If you already run mixer trucks, the truck is your mixer. Buying a central mixer you do not need adds cost to the purchase and a wearing part to maintain, and it slows the plant down, because a wet plant can only discharge as fast as it can mix. A dry plant does one job: it loads a truck accurately and gets it moving.

Throughput is the whole point

On a dry plant the number that decides everything is aggregate storage, because storage is what determines how many trucks you can load before a loader has to stop and refill the bins. These plants run from 5+ up to 36+ cubic yards, with three aggregate bins of 12 yards each on the largest.

  • 5–8 cu yd — a small yard or a single truck. The loader keeps up easily.
  • 12–16 cu yd — steady work. You stop feeding the plant every cycle.
  • 24–36+ cu yd — a multi-truck operation. At this size the plant stops being the constraint and the trucks become the constraint, which is exactly where you want to be.

The trap is buying storage for the average day instead of the busy one. A plant that keeps up comfortably in February and holds your trucks in a queue in July has cost you the whole summer. Size it for the days that pay.

Dry or wet? Choose by what you already own

This is the only real decision, and it is not close once you frame it properly:

  • You run mixer trucks — buy dry. The mixing capacity is already parked in your yard; a wet plant would duplicate it.
  • You need finished concrete at the point of use and have no trucks — buy wet. A dry plant would produce nothing usable without a truck to mix in.
  • You are supplying a ready-mix market — dry plant plus trucks is the conventional model, and it is conventional because it works: the truck mixes during the haul you were making anyway, so the mixing is free time.

A dry plant also pairs naturally with a portable cement silo, since bulk cement is what makes the economics of batching your own work at all.

Dry batch plant FAQs

What does a dry batch plant actually do?

It weighs aggregate, sand and cement to your mix design and dispenses them dry into a mixer truck. The truck does the mixing on the way to the pour. The plant handles accuracy and loading speed; the truck handles the mixing.

Dry batch or wet batch — which one do I need?

If you already run mixer trucks, buy dry: the truck is your mixer, and a wet plant duplicates capacity you own. If you need finished concrete at the point of use and have no mixer truck, buy wet, because a dry plant produces nothing usable without one.

How much aggregate storage do I need?

Storage decides how many trucks you can load before the loader has to refill the bins. Small yards manage on 5 to 8 cubic yards; steady operations want 12 to 16; a multi-truck operation wants 24 to 36 or more. Size for your busiest weeks, not your average one.

Can I finance a dry batch plant?

Yes, and financing is the normal way these are bought. The plant is a hard asset, so the equipment carries much of the deal. Get the quote from Mix Right and Axiant can price the monthly payment, usually with a decision in about 24 hours.
