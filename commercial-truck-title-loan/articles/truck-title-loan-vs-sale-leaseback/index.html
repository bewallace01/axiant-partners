Truck Title Loan vs Sale-Leaseback

Two ways to pull cash out of a truck you own - one keeps the title, the other sells it and leases it back

← Back to Commercial Truck Title Loans Articles

Updated September 02, 2026

Quick answer

A title loan lends against the truck and you keep the title, subject to the lender’s lien. A sale-leaseback sells the truck to a lessor for a lump sum and leases it back, so ownership actually changes hands. Title loans are faster and simpler; sale-leasebacks can release more cash and are treated differently for tax — a question for your CPA rather than your lender.

The Difference That Drives Everything Else

Both turn a truck you own into cash without stopping work. The difference is whether you still own it afterwards.

Under a title loan the truck stays yours. The lender records a lien, you make payments, and when the loan clears the lien is released. Nothing about ownership changes.

Under a sale-leaseback you sell the truck to a lessor and immediately lease it back. You keep driving it, but the lessor owns it, and at the end of the term there is a buyout — often nominal — to get it back.

That single distinction drives the cost, the paperwork, the tax position and what happens if things go wrong.

When a Title Loan Fits Better

  • You want the ownership position unchanged
  • Speed matters — a repair is holding the truck off the road
  • The amount you need sits well inside the equity
  • You want the simplest exit: pay it off, lien released
  • You would rather not introduce a new tax question mid-year

It is the more conservative instrument, and for a single truck and a defined gap it is usually the right one.

When a Sale-Leaseback Fits Better

  • You need more cash than a title advance releases
  • You are unlocking several units at once rather than one
  • Monthly cost matters more to you than the ownership position
  • Your accountant has a view on treatment that favors it

The trade is real. Ownership passes, the buyout has to be exercised to get the truck back, and the paperwork is heavier. Read the buyout terms before signing rather than at the end of the lease.

The Question to Settle First

Before comparing rates, settle what happens in a bad year. Under a title loan you are a borrower who has fallen behind. Under a leaseback you do not own the truck, and the lessor’s recovery route is different.

That is not an argument against a leaseback — it is an argument for reading whichever you pick as though the bad year will happen. See what happens if you default.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. The treatment of a sale-leaseback depends on how it is structured and on your circumstances — confirm with your CPA before committing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a title loan and a sale-leaseback?

A title loan lends against the truck and you keep ownership subject to the lender’s lien. A sale-leaseback sells the truck to a lessor for a lump sum and leases it back, so ownership transfers and you buy it back at the end of the term.

Which releases more cash?

A sale-leaseback usually can, because the lessor is buying the asset rather than lending a percentage of its value. A title loan advances a share of wholesale value less anything owed, which is more conservative.

Do I keep driving the truck under a sale-leaseback?

Yes — that is the point of the leaseback. Nothing changes operationally; what changes is who holds the title and how the payments are documented.

Which is faster to close?

A title loan, generally. It needs a valuation and a lien filing, where a sale-leaseback needs a title transfer and lease documentation. If a truck is off the road waiting on a repair, that difference matters.

How are the two treated for tax?

Differently, and it depends on structure. A title loan leaves you owning and depreciating the asset with interest as the cost; a sale-leaseback is a sale followed by a lease. This is general information, not tax advice — confirm the position with your CPA.
