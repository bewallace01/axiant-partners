What a Dump Truck Is Worth to a Lender

Start from the configuration, because the buyer pool for each one is different and a lender is pricing the buyer pool. New prices, as published on our dump truck financing page:

ConfigurationTypical new priceWhere it tends to sit in the advance range
Single-axle$70,000-$120,000Middle: easy to sell, but a low dollar base
Tandem-axle$120,000-$180,000Upper: the most common spec, the deepest resale market
Tri-axle / vocational$180,000-$280,000+Middle to upper, if axle spacing fits local weight rules
Specialty bodiesVaries widelyLower: side dumps and super dumps sell to fewer buyers

A used unit is valued as it sits, not against the new price. A five-year-old tandem is a different number again, and the lender's number is the forced liquidation value - what it would fetch sold quickly - not what a dealer would list it at.

Loan-to-Value: How the Advance Is Sized

Lenders that cash out equity in owned equipment commonly advance roughly 50-80% of forced liquidation value, then take off any payoff to an existing lienholder. That range is conventional across lenders, not anyone's quote, and dump trucks move within it for reasons specific to the trade:

  • Axle spacing and legal weight. What a truck can legally haul depends on axle count and spacing under the federal bridge formula and state rules. A truck that carries a full load where the next owner works is worth more than one that has to run light.
  • Body material and wear. A steel body that has hauled rock for years has a thinned floor and worn liner. Aluminum bodies weigh less and carry more payload, but cost more to repair.
  • Seasonality. Construction slows in winter across much of the country, and the auction market for dump trucks moves with it. A lender pricing a quick sale prices that in.

For the general arithmetic, see how much a truck supports; the dump-specific part is the body and the axles.

A Worked Example (Illustrative)

Take a used tandem whose forced liquidation value an appraiser puts at $90,000, with $20,000 still owed on it. The figures are illustrative, not a quote:

  • 50-80% of $90,000 is $45,000-$72,000
  • Less the $20,000 payoff leaves $25,000-$52,000
  • Less title work, lien filing and any inspection

Where the truck lands in that band is decided by the inspection below. A clean body, a sound hoist and service records push it up; a worn floor, frame cracks and no paperwork pull it down.

Who Lends on a Dump Truck

  • Equipment and vocational-truck finance companies that refinance owned units and pay out the equity. The most common home for a business-purpose dump truck title loan.
  • Heavy-equipment lenders that will take dump trucks alongside loaders and excavators in one package. The trucks carry a title lien; the yellow iron, which usually has no title, takes a UCC-1 filing. See borrowing against construction equipment you own.
  • Banks and credit unions, usually for an existing customer with financial statements, and usually slower.
  • Consumer car-title lenders. These are what a search often turns up, but they are built for personal vehicles under state consumer rules, with loan caps sized to cars. Many will not take a commercial truck or a business borrower at all.

What the Appraiser Checks on a Dump Truck

The chassis checks are the same as any heavy truck - see what a truck appraiser looks for. The dump-specific ones are where the value is won or lost:

AreaWhat they look at
Body and floorFloor thickness, liner, patched holes, tailgate hinges and latch
HoistCylinder leaks, raise and lower under load, pivot wear
PTO and hydraulicsPump, lines, valves, PTO hours where they are recorded
FrameCracks and repairs behind the cab and at the hoist mounts, from years of shock loads
Axles and suspensionConfiguration, lift or pusher axles, spacing against local weight rules
PaperworkClean title, registered weight, service records, any body replacement documented

A replaced body with an invoice is worth more than the same body without one. Keep the paper.

When a Title Loan Is the Wrong Tool

  • You are buying another truck. An equipment loan on the new unit is usually cheaper and longer than cashing out the old one. Use the title loan for the down payment only if that is the gap.
  • The problem is a general contractor paying slowly. That is a receivables gap, and invoice factoring or a line of credit matches it better than a lien on the truck.
  • It is January and the work is not there. Borrowing against the truck to cover a slow season puts the asset at risk exactly when it is earning least. Size any loan to the spring work you can see.
  • There is little equity. A truck most of the way through its original loan has little to lend against, whatever it is worth.

If none of those applies, send us the VIN, mileage and payoff and we will tell you plainly what the truck supports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a title loan on my dump truck?

Yes, as a business-purpose loan secured by the truck's title. Lenders advance against the truck's forced liquidation value, commonly in the range of 50-80% of it, less anything still owed, and you keep the truck working.

How much is my dump truck worth to a lender?

Less than its listing price. New dump trucks run roughly $70,000 for a single-axle to $280,000 or more for a tri-axle, but a lender values the used truck at what it would fetch sold quickly, then advances a percentage of that.

Does the dump body affect the loan amount?

Considerably. The appraiser checks floor thickness, liner, the hoist cylinder and frame cracks at the hoist mounts. A worn body or a leaking hoist lowers the valuation more than mileage on the chassis does.

Will a car title lender take a dump truck?

Often not. Consumer title lenders are built for personal vehicles under state consumer rules, with caps sized to cars. A dump truck title loan usually goes through an equipment or vocational-truck lender.

Can I borrow against dump trucks and excavators together?

Yes, with lenders that package construction equipment. The trucks carry a title lien and the machines, which usually have no title, take a UCC-1 filing, but both can secure one facility.

Sources & Further Reading

  • OCC Comptroller's Handbook: Asset-Based Lending — The federal bank examiners' handbook on collateral lending, including orderly and forced liquidation value - the bases lenders advance against instead of market value.
  • FHWA: Bridge Formula Weights — The federal axle-spacing and weight rule that decides how much a dump truck can legally haul, and so how useful it is to the next owner.
  • IRS Publication 946: How To Depreciate Property — The authority on Section 179 and depreciation for business vehicles, which is what determines a truck's book value against its market value.
  • FTC: Protecting Small Businesses Seeking Financing — The FTC's guidance to lenders, brokers and merchant cash advance providers on misrepresented costs, unlawful collection and false claims of government affiliation.
