What a Tow Truck Is Worth to a Lender

Tow trucks are a chassis with an expensive, specialized body on it, and the class decides how many operators could buy it. New prices, as published in our tow truck and wrecker financing guide:

ClassTypical new priceWhere it tends to sit in the advance range
Light-duty wrecker$60K-$120KMiddle to upper: many buyers
Rollback / flatbed$90K-$160KUpper: the most liquid tow truck there is
Medium-duty$120K-$250KMiddle
Heavy-duty wrecker$200K-$400KMiddle to lower: fewer buyers, CDL required
Rotator$400K-$500K+Lower: a thin resale market

A used unit is valued as it sits, at what it would fetch sold quickly, not against these new prices.

Loan-to-Value: The Body Is Half the Story

Lenders commonly advance roughly 50-80% of forced liquidation value on owned trucks. That range is conventional across lenders, not anyone's quote. What makes tow trucks different is where the value sits:

  • The body usually rides on the chassis title. The lien is recorded on one title, but the appraiser values the chassis and the wrecker, rollback or rotator body separately and adds them. A good body on a tired chassis can be moved to a new one, and buyers know it.
  • Specialization cuts both ways. A rotator is worth a great deal to a heavy-recovery operator and very little to anyone else. A quick sale has to find that buyer, so the advance is conservative even though the value is high.
  • Rollbacks are the easy case. Light-duty towing, repo, equipment transport and dealers all buy them, which is why they sit at the top of the range.

A Worked Example (Illustrative)

Take a used medium-duty wrecker with a forced liquidation value put at $70,000 - say $30,000 for the chassis and $40,000 for the body - and $15,000 still owed. The figures are illustrative, not a quote:

  • 50-80% of $70,000 is $35,000-$56,000
  • Less the $15,000 payoff leaves $20,000-$41,000
  • Less title work, lien filing and the inspection

If the chassis were tired and the body sound, the split above is what keeps the number up. Documented body service is how you show it.

Who Lends on a Tow Truck

  • Vocational-truck and equipment finance companies that know towing, refinance owned units and pay out the equity. They understand body value; a generalist may not credit it.
  • Towing-focused lenders and programs, sometimes tied to body builders. These mostly finance new purchases rather than cash-out, but some will refinance a unit they financed originally.
  • Banks and credit unions, mostly for established operators with financial statements.
  • Consumer car-title lenders, which appear in search results but lend to individuals against personal vehicles, with caps sized to cars. A wrecker is not what they are set up for.

Winches and cableCable condition, winch operation, replacement records
Rollback deckDeck tilt and slide, bed condition, hydraulics
PTO hoursRecovery work shows up in PTO hours, not odometer miles
PaperworkClean title, body invoice if remounted, inspection and service records

When a Title Loan Is the Wrong Tool

  • The gap is motor-club or police-rotation pay. Those accounts often pay on net-30 terms, and the shortfall comes back every month. A lien on a truck does not fix a recurring receivables gap; see financing motor-club net-30 cash flow.
  • You are adding a rotator or heavy wrecker. Finance the new unit with an equipment loan; cashing out an existing truck to buy one is usually the expensive way round.
  • Losing the truck would cost you a rotation slot. Rotation lists generally require trucks in service. A default that puts a truck at risk can cost the account, not just the asset.
  • There is little equity. A recent purchase with most of its loan outstanding has little to lend against.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a title loan on my tow truck?

Yes, as a business-purpose loan secured by the title. Lenders advance against the truck's forced liquidation value, commonly in the range of 50-80% of it, less any payoff, and the truck stays on calls.

Does the wrecker body count toward the loan?

Yes, and often for much of the value. The lien is recorded on the chassis title, but the appraiser values the chassis and the recovery body separately and adds them, so body condition and records matter.

Can I borrow against a rotator?

Yes, but expect a conservative advance relative to its value. A rotator sells to few buyers, and a lender sizes the loan to what a quick sale would fetch, which a thin market pulls down.

Should I use a title loan to cover slow motor-club payments?

Usually not. Net-30 motor-club and rotation pay is a recurring receivables gap, and a one-off loan against a truck does not close it. Working capital or receivables financing fits that problem better.

Will a car title lender take a tow truck?

Rarely. Consumer title lenders lend to individuals against personal vehicles under state consumer rules. A business-purpose tow truck loan goes through a vocational-truck or equipment lender.
