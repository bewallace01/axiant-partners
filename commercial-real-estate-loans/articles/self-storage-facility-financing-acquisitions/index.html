Self-Storage Facility Financing: SBA 504, CMBS, Bridge and Construction Loans

Which loan fits depends on occupancy: SBA 504 and CMBS for stabilized facilities, bridge for lease-up, construction for ground-up.

Home › Commercial Real Estate Loans › Articles › Self-Storage Facility Financing

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

Self-storage facility financing comes in four forms, and which one fits depends mostly on how full the facility is. Owner-operators buying a stabilized facility (85%+ economic occupancy) can use an SBA 504 loan with about 10% down and 25-year amortization. Larger stabilized facilities, usually $3 million and up, use CMBS at 6.5-8% fixed with 25-35% down. Facilities still leasing up use bridge loans at 9-12% interest-only for 12-36 months, and ground-up projects use construction loans at 70-75% of cost. Most lenders want a 1.25-1.35x DSCR and 680+ credit.

How is a self-storage facility financed?

Self-storage is one of the easier commercial property types to finance, and lenders have programs from small single sites to large portfolios. The first question every lender asks is how full the facility is. A stabilized facility, at roughly 85% economic occupancy or better, qualifies for long-term debt. One still filling up needs short-term money first. That single number decides most of what follows.

Loan type Best for Leverage Rate Term
SBA 504 Owner-operators buying a stabilized facility About 10% down (50/40/10) Long-term fixed on the SBA portion 25-year amortization
CMBS Stabilized facilities, usually $3M+ (sweet spot $5M-$50M) 65-75% LTV 6.5-8% fixed 10-year balloon, 25-30 year amortization
Bridge Lease-up or value-add under ~85% occupancy 65-75% LTV 9-12% interest-only 12-36 months
Construction Ground-up development 70-75% of cost 8-11% interest-only 18-24 month build, then permanent

Can you use an SBA loan for a self-storage facility?

Yes. Self-storage is an eligible SBA 504 use for owner-operators. The owner-occupancy rule is met by running the business from the site, with an office and a manager there. The 504 splits the purchase about 50% bank loan, 40% SBA debenture and 10% down, on a 25-year amortization, and the SBA portion is capped at $5 million (see what an SBA 504 loan is). SBA 7(a) can fit smaller deals or buyers who need more working capital in the loan; compare the two in SBA 7(a) vs 504.

Example: an operator buys a stabilized facility for $2.5 million. The bank lends about $1.25 million, the SBA debenture covers about $1 million, and the buyer puts down about $250,000. The lender underwrites economic occupancy, unit mix and rents against the local market, and the expense ratio.

What does it take to qualify for a large SBA storage loan?

Expect lenders to want 680+ credit (700+ for the best pricing), a net worth of about $500,000 to $1 million, a DSCR of about 1.25x, and 85%+ economic occupancy held for 90-120 days. Storage or commercial real estate operating experience helps; first-time operators often bring in a partner who has it.

CMBS for larger stabilized facilities

CMBS fits stabilized facilities from about $3 million to $100 million or more, with a sweet spot of $5-50 million and multi-property portfolios. Rates run about 6.5-8% fixed on a 10-year balloon with 25-30 year amortization, at 65-75% LTV, and the loan is usually non-recourse: the property is the collateral, with no personal guarantee on a stabilized deal. Lenders size it to about 1.30x DSCR.

Here is what that means in practice. On a $6 million facility at 70% LTV, a $4.2 million CMBS loan at 7% on a 30-year amortization runs about $27,943 a month, or about $335,312 a year. At 1.30x, the facility needs roughly $435,906 of net operating income to support it. If the NOI is lower, the loan amount drops, whatever the appraisal says.

Bridge loans for lease-up and value-add

A facility under about 85% occupancy, whether newly built or under-managed, usually does not qualify for permanent debt. The common path is a bridge loan at 65-75% LTV, 9-12% interest-only, for 12-36 months, often with 6-12 months of interest reserves built in. You lease up to 85%+ economic occupancy over 12-24 months, then refinance into SBA 504 or CMBS. See how fast a bridge loan can close and commercial bridge loans.

Construction and construction-to-permanent

Ground-up projects use construction loans at about 70-75% of total cost and 8-11%, interest-only through a typical 18-24 month build. Lenders want a general contractor with storage experience, a feasibility study with a demand analysis, a marketing or pre-leasing plan, and sponsor equity. At the certificate of occupancy or 80%+ economic occupancy, the loan refinances into SBA 504, CMBS or conventional debt; some lenders package construction and permanent financing together.

What lenders look at on a storage facility

  • Economic occupancy: rent-paying units divided by total units, not just units with something in them.
  • Unit mix: small units (5x5, 5x10) drive revenue per square foot; oversized warehouse-style units lease up more slowly.
  • Rent trends, including how often and how much existing customers' rates are raised.
  • Climate-controlled share, which lenders weigh favorably.
  • Trade area: population and household income within 3 and 5 miles, and competing supply.
  • Management: professional third-party management often prices better than owner-managed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you finance a self-storage facility purchase?

Match the loan to the facility's occupancy. A stabilized facility at 85%+ economic occupancy qualifies for SBA 504 (about 10% down) or CMBS (25-35% down, 6.5-8% fixed). A facility still leasing up usually starts with a bridge loan at 9-12% and refinances into permanent debt once it stabilizes.

Can you use an SBA loan for a self-storage facility?

Yes. Self-storage is an eligible SBA 504 use for owner-operators, and the owner-occupancy test is met by running the business from the site with an office and manager. The structure is about 50% bank, 40% SBA debenture and 10% down. SBA 7(a) can work for smaller deals or when you need more working capital.

What occupancy do lenders want for self-storage?

Permanent lenders usually want 85%+ economic occupancy (rent-paying units, not just occupied ones) held for 90-120 days. Below that, expect a bridge loan, or permanent debt at a lower 60-65% LTV with reserves.

How much down payment do you need for a self-storage facility?

About 10% with an SBA 504 for an owner-operator. CMBS and conventional lenders typically want 25-35%, which matches their 65-75% LTV limits. Bridge and construction deals depend on the business plan; construction loans cover 70-75% of cost.

What DSCR do self-storage lenders require?

Usually 1.25-1.35x on a stabilized facility: about 1.25x for SBA 504 and 1.30x for CMBS. Bridge lenders can accept less going in if the plan shows at least 1.30x within 18-24 months.

What credit score do you need for a self-storage loan?

680+ is standard for SBA 504 and CMBS, and 700+ gets the best pricing. Lenders also look at net worth, typically $500,000-$1 million for SBA 504 deals and $1 million+ for CMBS, and at storage or commercial real estate operating experience.

How is self-storage construction financed?

Construction loans typically cover 70-75% of total cost at 8-11%, interest-only during an 18-24 month build. Lenders want an experienced contractor, a feasibility study and sponsor equity. At the certificate of occupancy or 80%+ economic occupancy, the loan refinances into SBA 504, CMBS or conventional debt.
