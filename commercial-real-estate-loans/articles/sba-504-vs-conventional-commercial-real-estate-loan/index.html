SBA 504 vs Conventional Commercial Real Estate Loan: Key Differences

Down payment, terms, rates, and when to use each for owner-occupied property

SBA 504 vs Conventional Commercial Real Estate Loan: Key Differences
← Back to Commercial Real Estate Loans Articles

Updated August 1, 2026

Quick answer

SBA 504 wins on equity and rate stability — 10% down, long-term fixed rates, 20-25 year terms on the CDC portion, structured as 50% bank/40% SBA/10% borrower — but takes 60-90 days to close and only finances owner-occupied real estate. Conventional CRE wins on speed and flexibility: closes in 30-60 days, allows investment property and lighter documentation, but requires 20-30% down and typically uses 5-10 year terms with 20-25 year amortization (and a balloon). Pick 504 to preserve capital long-term; pick conventional when you need speed or flexibility.

What Is an SBA 504 Loan?

Designed specifically for owner-occupied commercial property and large equipment purchases. SBA 504 loans typically use a structure of 50% bank loan (first lien), 40% SBA-backed loan (second lien via CDC), and 10% borrower injection. The CDC portion often carries a long-term fixed rate, which provides payment stability over 20-25 years. Because the SBA guarantees a portion of the financing, banks can offer more favorable terms than they would on a fully conventional basis. The 10% down payment is one of the main advantages for businesses that want to preserve capital. For equity requirements by program, see how much down payment is required for a commercial property loan.

Benefits: Lower down payment, long-term fixed rate stability, reduced equity requirement, designed for long-term business occupancy. Requirements: Strong documentation, cash flow analysis, longer closing timeline, SBA eligibility reviews.

What Is a Conventional Commercial Real Estate Loan?

Issued directly by a bank or private lender without SBA backing. Typical features: 20-30% down payment, 5-10 year term, 20-25 year amortization, balloon payment at maturity, fixed or adjustable rates. Conventional loans may close faster and involve fewer parties than SBA 504. Requirements: stronger credit profile (680-720+ preferred), larger equity injection, stronger financial position. Conventional loans work well for borrowers who have built up equity and prefer a faster close with fewer parties. At maturity, you will need to refinance or pay off the balloon; factor that into your long-term planning. See credit score requirements for commercial real estate loans for tier expectations.

When SBA 504 Is the Better Option

Ideal if you want lower down payment (around 10%), prefer long-term fixed payments, plan to hold property long term, preserving liquidity is important, or are expanding operations. SBA guarantees lead to higher leverage offers from lenders.

When Conventional Financing Is the Better Option

Better if you have 20-30% down available, prefer faster closing (30-60 days), want fewer approval layers, have strong credit (700+ ideal), or are comfortable refinancing in 5-10 years. Works well for strong borrowers with stable revenue and liquidity.

Worked Example: A $2M Building, Both Ways

Take an owner-occupant buying a $2,000,000 commercial property. A typical SBA 504 splits into three pieces: a bank first mortgage around 50% ($1,000,000), a CDC/SBA second around 40% ($800,000) at a long-term fixed rate, and roughly 10% down ($200,000). A conventional CRE loan on the same building usually wants 20–30% down — $400,000 to $600,000 — often with a shorter term, a balloon, and a variable rate.

That is $200,000 to $400,000 of cash the 504 leaves in the business, plus a fixed rate on the CDC portion that removes rate risk for the life of that piece. What conventional buys you is speed and simplicity: fewer parties, no SBA-specific paperwork, and a faster close.

Real Cost Example: $1.5M Owner-Occupied Office

Payments tell a different story than down payments. Same $1.5M project, two structures:

  • SBA 504: $750K bank at 9.5% (25-year amortization, 7-year balloon) plus $600K CDC at 6.25% (25-year fully amortizing) plus $150K equity. Bank payment ~$6,558/mo, CDC payment ~$3,963/mo — ~$10,521/mo total principal and interest.
  • Conventional: $1.125M at 9.75% (25-year amortization, 10-year balloon) plus $375K equity. ~$10,066/mo principal and interest.
  • The catch: conventional looks ~$455/mo cheaper, but the borrower fronted $375K instead of $150K. The 504 keeps $225K in the business. Put that capital to work at a 10% return and the 504 is effectively $22,500 a year ahead, despite the higher monthly payment.

This is the trade the comparison usually comes down to: conventional can win on monthly cost while losing on total capital efficiency. Model both against what the retained cash actually earns in your business.

Rate Considerations

SBA 504: Long-term fixed rate on SBA portion, predictable payments, designed for stability. Conventional: May offer competitive initial rates, may include adjustable rates, often includes balloon maturity. Long-term cost structure differs based on hold strategy.

Closing Timeline Comparison

SBA 504: 60-90 days typical, requires CDC coordination, more documentation. Conventional: 30-60 days typical, single lender underwriting, fewer external approvals. Conventional may be faster if timing is critical. See how long it takes to close a commercial real estate loan.

Credit & Qualification Differences

Credit score: SBA 504 typically 680-690+; Conventional typically 680-720+. Cash flow: Both require strong DSCR. Time in business: Both prefer 3+ years. Industry risk: Both consider it. SBA may offer slightly more flexibility for moderate credit borrowers. Learn more in credit score needed for commercial real estate loans.

Long-Term Strategy Consideration

If your goal is long-term property ownership: SBA 504 often aligns well due to stable monthly payments and preserving liquidity. The fixed-rate CDC portion locks in predictability for decades. If your goal is faster closing, strong initial rate, ability to refinance in future: Conventional may fit. Conventional loans typically have balloon maturities; plan for refinance or payoff at term end. Consider interest rate environment–if rates are low and you expect to hold long-term, SBA 504's fixed rate can provide significant value over time.

Eligibility and Use Restrictions

SBA 504 has specific eligibility requirements: for-profit business, tangible net worth under $15 million, average net income under $5 million after taxes for the two years preceding application, and the property must be owner-occupied. Conventional loans have fewer program restrictions but may have tighter credit and equity standards. Certain property types–e.g., spec buildings, passive investments–are ineligible for SBA. Review program guidelines with your lender before committing to a structure.

Can You Combine Strategies?

Some businesses start with conventional and refinance into SBA later when they have time for the longer process. Others evaluate both simultaneously and choose based on rate, timeline, and equity requirements. A financing advisor can help you compare term sheets side by side. Proper structuring is critical–choosing the wrong program can cost more over the life of the loan or delay your closing. Get matched with lenders to compare SBA and conventional options for your profile.

Documentation Differences

SBA 504 requires additional forms, disclosures, and CDC coordination. Conventional loans typically need standard commercial mortgage documentation: financial statements, tax returns, rent roll or operating data, and purchase contract. If you are pressed for time, conventional may be easier to assemble. If you prioritize lowest down payment and long-term fixed rates, the extra SBA paperwork is often worthwhile. See how long it takes to close a commercial real estate loan for timeline expectations.

Next Steps

Compare both structures before deciding. Request term sheets from lenders offering SBA 504 and conventional programs. Evaluate total cost over your expected hold period, not just the initial rate. Factor in closing timeline if you have a purchase contract deadline. A financing advisor can help you model scenarios and choose the structure that best fits your capital strategy and timeline. Final Thoughts

Both SBA 504 and conventional commercial real estate loans are strong options serving different borrower profiles. SBA 504 generally provides: lower down payment, long-term stability, structured growth alignment. Conventional loans typically provide: faster closing, simplified structure, strong fit for high-credit borrowers. For owner-occupied property financing, review structured commercial real estate options to align best with your capital strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an SBA 504 and a conventional CRE loan?

SBA 504 offers 10% down, long-term fixed rates, and 20-25 year terms on the CDC portion (50% bank, 40% SBA, 10% borrower) but takes 60-90 days and only finances owner-occupied real estate. Conventional CRE closes in 30-60 days and allows investment property, but requires 20-30% down.

Which is better, SBA 504 or conventional CRE?

SBA 504 wins on equity and rate stability; conventional wins on speed and flexibility. Pick 504 for owner-occupied with the lowest down payment; pick conventional for speed or investment property.

How much down payment for SBA 504 vs conventional CRE?

SBA 504 is 10% down, while conventional CRE is 20-30% down.

Can an SBA 504 loan fund investment property?

No. SBA 504 only finances owner-occupied real estate; investment property needs a conventional or DSCR loan.
