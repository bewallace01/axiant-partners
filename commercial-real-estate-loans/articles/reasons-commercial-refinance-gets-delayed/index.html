Reasons Commercial Refinance Gets Delayed

What’s holding up your refinance—and how to speed it up

← Back to Commercial Real Estate Loan Articles

Last updated: March 19, 2026

You want to refinance your commercial mortgage—lower the rate, extend the term, or pull cash out. But the timeline keeps slipping. Commercial refinance delays usually stem from a few repeat causes: appraisal or valuation delays, title or survey issues, DSCR or cash flow shortfalls, incomplete documentation, slow response to conditions, or payoff coordination with your existing lender. This guide explains why commercial refinance gets delayed and what you can do to keep it on track. For full timeline expectations, see how long to close a commercial real estate loan; for blockers, see what’s blocking your CRE loan from closing.

Quick Answer

Commercial refinance gets delayed when appraisal or valuation is slow, title or survey has issues, DSCR doesn’t meet the lender’s minimum, documentation is incomplete, you haven’t responded to conditions, or payoff coordination with the existing lender drags. Fix by ordering appraisal and title early, packaging complete docs, ensuring DSCR pencils, and responding to every request within 24–48 hours. See commercial real estate loan requirements and cash-out refinance for commercial property.

1. Appraisal or Valuation Delay

The lender needs a current valuation to size the new loan. If the appraisal is ordered late, the appraiser is backed up, or you’re in a busy market, closing slips. Commercial appraisals can take 2–4 weeks or more. Cash-out refinance in particular depends on value—the lender wants to know the property supports the requested loan amount. If the appraisal is delayed, the entire refinance stalls.

Fix: Order the appraisal as soon as the lender allows. Provide full access to the property and any operating data the appraiser needs. If you’re in a rush, ask the lender if they have preferred appraisers with shorter turnaround. For cash-out refinance, ensure your expectations are realistic—if you’re counting on a high value, a conservative appraisal can reduce or kill the deal. See cash-out refinance for commercial property.

2. Title or Survey Issues

Title defects, liens, or survey problems must be resolved before the new lender will fund. If title or survey isn’t ordered early, or issues surface late—a lien from a contractor, a boundary dispute, or an easement the lender doesn’t like—the refinance stalls. Commercial properties often have complex title histories; curative work can add weeks.

Fix: Order title and survey as soon as you have a commitment or term sheet. Resolve liens and defects the moment they appear. Don’t assume the title company will move quickly without follow-up. If you know of potential issues (prior litigation, boundary questions), address them before you apply. See what’s blocking your CRE loan from closing.

3. DSCR or Cash Flow Shortfall

Lenders underwrite commercial refinance on debt service coverage—the property’s income must support the new debt. If your DSCR is borderline or the lender’s rate/term assumptions push it below their minimum, they may reduce the loan, change terms, or pause to request more income documentation. Income that was acceptable at origination may not pencil at today’s rates or the amount you’re requesting.

Fix: Run DSCR early. Know the lender’s minimum (often 1.20x–1.35x) and ensure your rent roll and expenses support it at the proposed rate and term. If you’re doing a cash-out refinance, the higher loan amount increases debt service—make sure income covers it. Provide complete rent roll and operating statements so the lender can underwrite without coming back for more. See commercial real estate loan requirements.

4. Incomplete or Piecemeal Documentation

Commercial refinance needs a full file: rent roll, leases, operating statements, tax returns, entity docs, and payoff information. If you send documents in waves or with gaps, the lender keeps coming back and the clock resets. Inconsistent numbers—rent roll that doesn’t match the operating statement, or entity names that don’t align—trigger follow-up requests.

Fix: Assemble a complete package before submission. Use the lender’s checklist. Ensure every document has consistent entity names, addresses, and figures. Include a current payoff statement from your existing lender. See why your CRE loan keeps coming back for more documents for common doc gaps.

5. Slow Response to Lender Conditions

Every day you don’t respond to a condition pushes closing out. Lenders issue conditions—updated rent roll, estoppel, insurance certificate, signed subordination—and wait. If you take a week to respond, your file goes to the back of the queue. Multiple slow responses can add a month or more.

Fix: Designate one point of contact. Respond within 24–48 hours to every request. If you need time to gather something, reply immediately with a date: "Estoppels will be ready by Thursday." Silence is what keeps refinances delayed. See reasons your CRE loan approval is taking forever.

6. Payoff Coordination or Subordination Delays

Refinance requires payoff of the existing loan. The new lender needs a payoff statement—often good for 10–30 days. If closing slips past that window, you need a new payoff. The existing lender may have their own process for subordination if you’re layering debt. If the existing lender is slow to respond or has requirements you didn’t anticipate, the refinance stalls.

Fix: Request a payoff statement early. Confirm the existing lender’s requirements—notice period, payoff process, any subordination terms. If you need subordination (e.g., for a second lien), start that process as soon as you have a refinance commitment. Don’t let payoff coordination be the last item.

7. Rate Lock or Commitment Expiration

Refinance commitments and rate locks have expiration dates. If you don’t close in time, the commitment lapses. In rising rate environments, an extension may mean worse terms or a new approval. Some lenders won’t extend—you start over.

Fix: Know your commitment and rate lock expiration. Work backward from that date to ensure appraisal, title, and all conditions are cleared with buffer. If you’re approaching expiration, ask for an extension before it lapses. Proactive extension is easier than restarting.

What to Do Right Now

If your commercial refinance is delayed: (1) Order appraisal and title as soon as possible. (2) Package complete documentation—rent roll, leases, operating statements, payoff info—and ensure consistency. (3) Run DSCR early and confirm you meet the lender’s minimum. (4) Respond to every condition within 24–48 hours. (5) Stay on top of payoff coordination with your existing lender. For pitfalls to avoid, see CRE loan mistakes that delay or deny closing.
