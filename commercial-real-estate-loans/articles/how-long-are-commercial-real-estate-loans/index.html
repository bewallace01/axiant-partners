Term, Amortization and Maturity

Three numbers describe how long a commercial mortgage lasts, and they are often different:

  • Term (maturity): when the loan is due in full. Commonly 5, 7 or 10 years.
  • Amortization: the schedule used to set the payment, commonly 20, 25 or 30 years. A longer schedule means a lower payment and more principal left at maturity.
  • Fixed-rate period: how long the rate is locked. On some bank loans the rate resets at year 5 while the term runs on.

When the term is shorter than the amortization, the unpaid principal at maturity is the balloon. A homeowner’s 30-year mortgage is fully amortizing; most commercial loans are not. How long a loan takes to close is a separate question: see how long it takes to close a commercial real estate loan.

Worked Example: What the Balloon Looks Like

Illustrative, not a quote. A $1,500,000 loan at an assumed 7.0% with payments set on a 25-year schedule pays about $10,602 a month (principal and interest, computed by formula). What is still owed depends on when the term ends:

Early payments are mostly interest, so even a 10-year term leaves most of the principal to refinance or pay off. Plan the exit when you take the loan; the balloon refinance playbook covers the timing.

Stretching from 20 to 30 years cuts the payment by about $1,650 a month, which helps the property clear a lender’s debt service coverage test, but leaves about $285,581 more to refinance at year 10. Model your own numbers in the commercial real estate loan calculator.

The SBA Routes Have No Balloon

For owner-occupied property, the SBA programs are the main way to get a long, fully amortizing loan:

  • SBA 7(a): loans run the shortest appropriate term, generally 10 years or less, but loans for real estate can go up to 25 years, plus time for construction or improvements (13 CFR 120.212).
  • SBA 504: SBA publishes the debenture maturities in the Federal Register (13 CFR 120.933). Real estate projects use 20- or 25-year debentures that pay off in full; the 25-year option was added in 2018, and its accompanying bank loan must run at least 10 years (SBA Policy Notice 5000-17058). The bank’s first mortgage, about half the project, follows the bank’s own terms and may balloon. See the SBA 504 amortization schedule.

The term and the prepayment penalty go together: a 10-year CMBS loan is cheap to hold to maturity and expensive to leave early. Read the CRE loan red flags before choosing on payment alone.

What to Ask a Lender

  1. When is the loan due, and what is the amortization?
  2. How long is the rate fixed, and how does it reset?
  3. What is the estimated balloon at maturity?
  4. What does it cost to prepay in years 1, 3 and 5?
  5. Are there extension options at maturity, and on what terms?

Frequently Asked Questions

How long are commercial real estate loans?

Most have a 5- to 10-year term with payments figured over 20 to 30 years, leaving a balloon at maturity. SBA 7(a) real estate loans can run up to 25 years and SBA 504 debentures 20 or 25 years, both fully amortizing; bridge loans run 6-36 months.

What is the difference between term and amortization on a commercial loan?

The term is when the loan is due. The amortization is the schedule used to set the payment. When the term is shorter, the unpaid principal is due as a balloon.

Can you get a 30-year commercial mortgage?

Thirty-year amortization is common on CMBS and agency loans, but the term is usually 10 years or less. Long fully amortizing terms come mainly from FHA multifamily programs, some life companies and the SBA (up to 25 years).

How much is the balloon on a $1,500,000 commercial loan?

At an assumed 7% on a 25-year schedule, about $1,367,432 at year 5 and $1,179,501 at year 10. Illustrations, computed by formula, not quotes.

What happens when a commercial loan matures?

You pay the balance by refinancing, selling or paying cash. Lenders do not have to extend, so start the refinance about six months before maturity.

Is an SBA loan longer than a conventional commercial mortgage?

Usually. SBA 7(a) real estate loans can run up to 25 years and 504 debentures 20 or 25 years without a balloon, while conventional loans commonly mature in 5-10 years.

Sources & Further Reading

  • 13 CFR 120.212: SBA 7(a) loan maturities — Shortest appropriate term; 10 years unless real estate or long-life equipment; 25-year maximum plus construction time.
  • 13 CFR 120.933: 504 loan maturities — SBA publishes the available 504 debenture maturities in the Federal Register.
  • SBA Policy Notice 5000-17058: 25-year 504 debenture — Adds the 25-year debenture to the 10- and 20-year terms; the third-party loan must run at least 10 years.
  • HUD Section 223(f) — FHA-insured loans to buy or refinance existing multifamily properties, with long fully amortizing terms.
