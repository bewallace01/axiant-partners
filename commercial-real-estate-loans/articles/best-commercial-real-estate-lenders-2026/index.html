Quick answer

The “best” CRE lender depends entirely on your deal: property type, loan size, structure (recourse vs non-recourse), and timeline. For stabilized multifamily $1M–$50M: Walker & Dunlop, Greystone, Berkadia, Newmark (agency DUS lenders — Freddie/Fannie/FHA). For top-tier institutional assets $10M+: MetLife, Northwestern Mutual, New York Life, Prudential (life co). For non-recourse $5M–$50M, all property types: CMBS originators — JPMorgan, Goldman, Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank, Citi. For relationship-driven bank deals: JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, U.S. Bank, regional banks (PNC, KeyBank, M&T, Truist). For owner-occupied: SBA 504 with Live Oak, Wells Fargo, Byline. For bridge/value-add: Madison Realty, BridgeInvest, Inland Mortgage, debt funds. Most borrowers should apply through a marketplace that submits to multiple lender categories in parallel.

CRE financing has more lender categories than any other product — bank, life co, CMBS, agency, SBA, bridge, mezzanine, debt fund — and the right lender depends on the asset and structure. The same $10M loan request gets four wildly different answers from a community bank, a life co, a CMBS desk, and a Freddie SBL lender. This guide ranks the top players in each category for 2026. For rates see typical CRE rates 2026; for product overview see commercial real estate loans.

Top Bank CRE Lenders

Banks are the largest single source of CRE debt and the right answer for most deals under $25M, especially recourse, owner-occupied, or value-add. Top originators in 2026:

  • JPMorgan Chase Commercial Real Estate: Largest US bank CRE lender. Strong on multifamily, industrial, mixed-use. Best for borrowers with deposit relationships. Sweet spot $5M–$100M.
  • Wells Fargo Commercial Real Estate: Second-largest. Active in nearly every property type and structure. Strong life co conduit. Excellent for existing customers.
  • Bank of America Merrill Lynch: Top-3 by volume. Strong on larger deals ($15M+) and institutional sponsors.
  • U.S. Bank: Strong in the West and Mountain West. Active in multifamily, industrial, hospitality.
  • PNC Real Estate: Strong in Midwest and East Coast. Active in agency multifamily plus bank balance-sheet lending.
  • KeyBank Real Estate Capital: Strong agency multifamily originator plus bank balance-sheet. Active across CRE property types.
  • M&T Bank / Truist / Fifth Third / Regions: Regional banks particularly strong in their geographic footprints. Often more flexible than national banks on smaller deals.
  • Community banks (varies by market): Often the right answer for $1M–$10M deals with recourse, relationship pricing, and faster decisions. Check your local market.

Top Life Insurance Company CRE Lenders

Life cos offer the lowest rates on the highest-quality assets. Tight underwriting, 60–70% LTV, top markets, experienced sponsors. Top players:

  • MetLife Real Estate: Largest life co CRE lender. Active in multifamily, office, industrial, retail. Min loan typically $10M.
  • Northwestern Mutual Real Estate: Premium pricing for premium assets. Selective.
  • New York Life Real Estate Investors: Top-5 life co originator. Active across major property types.
  • Prudential / PGIM Real Estate Finance: Top-tier life co lender, active across property types and major markets.
  • TIAA / Nuveen Real Estate: Active across multifamily, industrial, office.
  • Allstate / State Farm / Massachusetts Mutual: Active life co lenders. Worth contacting for $10M+ stabilized deals.
  • Pacific Life: Active life co. Competitive on multifamily and industrial.

Life co loans usually require a mortgage banker intermediary — few life cos take direct borrower applications. Talk to a CRE mortgage broker or correspondent.

Top CMBS Conduit Originators

CMBS loans are securitized non-recourse loans, sold off the originator’s balance sheet. Top originators:

  • JPMorgan CMBS: Top-3 by annual issuance. Active across property types.
  • Wells Fargo CMBS: Top-3 by annual issuance.
  • Goldman Sachs CMBS: Top-5. Strong on larger, complex deals.
  • Morgan Stanley CMBS: Active conduit + single-asset/single-borrower (SASB).
  • Deutsche Bank CMBS: Active conduit + SASB.
  • Citigroup CMBS: Active conduit lender.
  • Barclays / Bank of America / Cantor Fitzgerald / Starwood Mortgage: Active CMBS originators.

CMBS is best for stabilized non-recourse deals $5M–$50M. Most borrowers access CMBS via a mortgage banker; few originators take direct applications. Inflexible mid-loan — hard to modify, refinance, or supplement.

Top Agency Multifamily (Freddie/Fannie/FHA) Lenders

Agency multifamily loans require approval through a designated “DUS lender” (Fannie) or “Optigo lender” (Freddie) or FHA-approved mortgagee. Top originators:

  • Walker & Dunlop: #1 Freddie + Fannie originator multiple years running. Active across multifamily, including affordable, market-rate, student housing, senior, manufactured housing.
  • Greystone: Top-3 across all three agency programs. Particularly strong on FHA.
  • Berkadia: Top-3 Fannie DUS originator. Joint venture between Berkshire Hathaway and Jefferies.
  • Newmark: Top-5 across agency programs.
  • CBRE: Top-5 agency originator. Also active in CMBS, life co, and bank intermediation.
  • JLL Real Estate Capital: Top-10 agency originator.
  • Capital One Multifamily Finance: Active Fannie DUS lender, particularly on smaller deals.
  • Arbor Realty: Active across agency programs, particularly Freddie SBL ($1M–$7.5M).
  • Bellwether Enterprise Real Estate Capital: Top FHA originator.

For Freddie SBL or Fannie Small Loan ($1M–$7.5M), Arbor and Greystone are particularly active. For larger deals, Walker & Dunlop is the most consistent option.

Top SBA 504 Lenders (Owner-Occupied)

SBA 504 deals have two pieces: the CDC (local) and the bank first mortgage. Top first-mortgage banks:

  • Wells Fargo: Largest 504 first-mortgage bank in most years.
  • Byline Bank: Top-5 nationally. Strong on multi-state owner-occupied deals.
  • Celtic Bank: Aggressive 504 first-mortgage pricing, strong online process.
  • Live Oak Bank: 504 specialist for vet, dental, hospitality, self-storage.
  • Local credit unions: Often most competitive in their footprint.

Top Bridge / Value-Add Lenders

Bridge lenders provide short-term financing for acquisitions, value-add, repositioning, or refinances of non-stabilized assets. Top players in 2026:

  • Madison Realty Capital: Active debt fund. Bridge + construction + mezzanine. $10M–$300M+ deals.
  • BridgeInvest: Active bridge lender across property types.
  • Inland Mortgage Capital: Active on smaller balance bridge ($2M–$50M).
  • Acres Capital: Active across bridge, mezzanine, construction.
  • Lument Capital: Bridge + permanent in one platform.
  • Pinnacle Bank / Customers Bank / Eastern Bank: Bank balance-sheet bridge for smaller deals.
  • Private debt funds (varies): Many regional debt funds active in $2M–$25M bridge.

How to Pick by Deal Size

  • Under $2M: Local community bank or credit union. SBA 7(a) if owner-occupied.
  • $2M–$5M stabilized multifamily: Freddie SBL or Fannie Small Loan via Arbor, Greystone, or Walker & Dunlop.
  • $2M–$10M other property types: Regional bank or community bank.
  • $5M–$50M stabilized institutional: Life co, agency (if multifamily), or CMBS via a mortgage banker.
  • $5M–$50M value-add / transitional: Bridge debt fund or bank balance-sheet bridge.
  • $50M+ stabilized: Life co, money-center bank, or large CMBS desk.
  • Owner-occupied $1M–$15M: SBA 504 with strong first-mortgage bank.

How to Pick the Right CRE Lender

Decision framework:

  • Define your structure first: Recourse or non-recourse? Fixed or floating? Long-term hold or 5-year exit? These determine which lender category fits.
  • Identify your property’s best lender category: Stabilized multifamily → agency. Stabilized institutional → life co. Non-stabilized or value-add → bridge or bank. Owner-occupied → SBA 504.
  • Engage a mortgage banker for $5M+ deals: Life co and CMBS are usually inaccessible direct. A mortgage banker shops multiple lenders for a 50–100 bp fee — usually worth it.
  • For sub-$5M, apply direct to 2–3 banks plus the right agency lender: Banks take direct apps and price competitively for relationship borrowers.
  • Compare on total cost (rate + fees + prepayment + leverage): Lowest rate isn’t always best deal. A higher-rate loan with better leverage or no prepayment may have lower total cost over your hold period.
