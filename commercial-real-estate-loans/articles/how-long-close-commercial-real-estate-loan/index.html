Typical Closing Timelines by Loan Type

Loan Type Typical Timeline to Close
Conventional Commercial Mortgage 30-60 days
SBA 7(a) Real Estate Loan 45-75 days
SBA 504 Loan 60-90 days

Simple files with complete documentation and straightforward property types can close faster. More complex files–multi-tenant properties, special-use assets, or borrowers with layered ownership–require additional diligence and documentation. See SBA 504 vs conventional CRE loan for how structure affects timeline.

1. Conventional Commercial Loan (Fastest Standard Option)

Conventional structures often close faster than SBA when documentation is complete and property risk is straightforward. With a single lender and no government program layer, the process can move quickly. Typical process includes:

  • Initial review and term sheet
  • Appraisal and environmental review
  • Financial underwriting
  • Final approval and legal closing

2. SBA 7(a) Real Estate Loan

SBA 7(a) can support owner-occupied transactions with flexible use of proceeds, but includes additional underwriting and compliance layers. The SBA guaranty process adds time compared to conventional loans. Typical range is 45-75 days. Plan for extra documentation and form completion when choosing SBA 7(a) for real estate. See SBA 504 vs conventional CRE loan for timeline comparison across structures.

3. SBA 504 Loan (Longest but Most Structured)

SBA 504 often provides strong long-term structure for owner-occupied property, but usually has the longest timeline because of multi-party coordination and extra documentation checkpoints. The process involves the bank lender, the CDC (Certified Development Company), and SBA review. Each step adds time. Plan for 60-90 days minimum; complex files can extend beyond that. The trade-off is often worthwhile for the lower down payment and long-term fixed rate. See SBA 504 vs conventional CRE loan for a full comparison.

Third-Party Report Timelines

Appraisals and environmental reports can add 2-4 weeks depending on property type and location. Phase I environmental assessments are common for commercial properties; Phase II may be required if concerns are identified. Order these as early as possible–some lenders allow you to start the process before final approval to compress the overall timeline. Delays in third-party reports are one of the most frequent causes of extended closings.

What Affects CRE Closing Speed?

Several factors influence how quickly your loan closes. The most controllable is documentation–having a complete file from day one eliminates back-and-forth and keeps the process on track. Credit strength, property type, and third-party report scheduling also play significant roles. See what lenders look for in a commercial real estate loan for a full documentation checklist.

Documentation Readiness

  • Business tax returns and financial statements
  • Personal financial statement and debt schedule
  • Property rent rolls and operating data
  • Purchase contract or refinance payoff detail

Missing documents are one of the top causes of closing delays.

Appraisal and Environmental Reports

Third-party reports can drive timeline variance. Faster scheduling and responsive data access help reduce delays.

Credit and Underwriting Strength

Stronger DSCR, cleaner credit profile, and clearer cash flow history typically move through underwriting with fewer conditions.

Property Type

Special-use or more complex assets often require deeper review than standard office, retail, or industrial properties.

Seller Timeline and Title Issues

Title, legal, and seller-side delays can affect timing even when credit and underwriting are strong. Title issues such as liens, easements, or boundary disputes must be resolved before closing. Coordinate with your title company and attorney early to avoid last-minute delays.

Fastest Realistic Closing Scenario

For a straightforward owner-occupied purchase with complete documentation, conventional CRE loans can sometimes close in about 30 days.

When Should You Plan for 60-90 Days?

  • SBA-driven structures
  • Complex business entities or multi-tenant files
  • Higher documentation volume
  • Appraisal/environmental scheduling delays

Minimum Loan Amount

Commercial real estate loan programs generally start around $10,000 and scale by qualification and property value. Many lenders have minimum loan sizes of $250,000 or higher. Smaller transactions may need to work with community banks. Loan size can also affect timeline–larger loans may require additional committee or senior approval steps. See commercial real estate loan options for program minimums and maximums.

How to Close Faster

Speed is largely within your control. The single biggest factor is having complete, accurate documentation ready at application. Respond to lender and third-party requests within 24–48 hours. Choose a loan structure that matches your timeline–if you need to close in 30 days, conventional is typically more realistic than SBA 504. Order appraisals and environmental reports as soon as you have a term sheet to avoid scheduling bottlenecks.

  • Prepare a complete underwriting file before application
  • Maintain current financial reporting
  • Respond quickly to lender conditions
  • Align loan structure with your scenario upfront

Refinance vs. Purchase Timeline

Refinances often close faster than purchases because there is no seller, no purchase contract contingency, and sometimes less third-party coordination. However, if your current loan has prepayment notice requirements or the lender needs updated appraisals, refinance timelines can still extend. Cash-out refinances follow the same general timeline as rate-and-term refinances. Discuss your specific scenario with lenders to get a realistic estimate before committing. See cash-out refinance for commercial property for refinance-specific considerations.

Coordinating with Your Purchase or Refinance

If you have a purchase contract, build contingency for financing into your timeline. Allow buffer beyond the minimum–if conventional typically takes 30-60 days, plan for 60-75 to account for delays. Communicate with your lender and real estate professional so everyone is aligned. For refinances, check your current loan for prepayment notice requirements; some loans require 30-90 days advance notice before payoff.

Final Thoughts

Most owner-occupied CRE deals close between 30 and 90 days depending on loan type and execution quality. Conventional loans are often fastest; SBA 504 usually takes longest. If timing matters, compare commercial real estate loan options and align your deal with the right structure before submission. How Long Close Commercial Real Estate Loan: CRE Underwriting Framework and Closing Controls

Commercial real estate lending rewards preparation quality. Start with a clear financing objective, realistic repayment narrative, and property-specific risk map. Underwriters look for alignment across borrower strength, property performance, and structure fit. Files that provide consistent documentation and practical contingency planning generally move faster and attract cleaner terms.

Before submission, build a lender-ready package that reconciles financials, rent/occupancy assumptions, debt schedules, and use-of-proceeds. Add concise notes for one-time anomalies or market shifts that affect projections. Clean narrative plus clean data reduces clarification loops and helps credit teams assess risk with less friction.

  • Borrower quality: liquidity, management depth, and performance consistency.
  • Property quality: tenancy durability, location dynamics, and collateral resilience.
  • Structure quality: leverage, amortization, reserves, and covenant practicality.
  • Execution quality: timeline ownership, document control, and proactive lender communication.

Scenario Controls and Post-Close Governance

Run base, moderate-stress, and severe-stress scenarios before finalizing terms. Include vacancy risk, operating expense pressure, valuation variance, and refinancing conditions. If stress-case durability is weak, resize request or enhance reserves before close. This discipline protects optionality and improves long-term financing outcomes.

After funding, maintain a monthly governance cadence with documented actions, owner accountability, and variance reporting. Consistent follow-through improves renewal confidence and reduces avoidable surprises in future financing cycles.

Closing Playbook and Credit-Committee Readiness

CRE approvals accelerate when the file is organized for committee logic, not just document completeness. Structure your packet in decision order: borrower profile and liquidity, property performance and tenancy quality, structure rationale, stress-test output, and mitigation controls. This sequence lets reviewers evaluate risk efficiently and reduces iterative follow-up requests.

For every core assumption, provide evidence and downside treatment. If rent growth, occupancy, or valuation assumptions drive coverage, show what happens when those assumptions underperform. Include action triggers for each downside case: reserve activation, expense controls, lease-up initiatives, or timeline adjustments. Borrowers who demonstrate concrete response capacity usually receive better terms than those relying on optimistic projections alone.

  • Committee narrative: align borrower, property, and structure into one coherent risk story.
  • Evidence discipline: tie assumptions to verifiable documents and market realities.
  • Downside controls: predefine mitigation actions with ownership and timing.
  • Timeline control: maintain one tracker for lender requests, legal items, and third-party dependencies.

Use a weekly execution cadence through closing and into early post-close monitoring. Track open items, due dates, and dependency risks in one source of truth. If critical milestones slip, escalate with a targeted recovery plan rather than broad document resubmission. Consistent control behavior is one of the strongest signals of lower execution risk and improves both approval quality and long-term relationship value.

Monitoring Cadence and Refinance-Readiness Discipline

Long-term CRE performance depends on consistent monitoring, not one-time underwriting quality. Build a monthly cadence that tracks occupancy durability, tenant concentration shifts, operating expense variance, debt-service resilience, and reserve adequacy. Keep this cadence documented with action owners and deadlines so corrective steps are visible and accountable.

For refinancing outcomes, maintain a rolling lender-ready packet that is updated quarterly. Include current rent roll, trailing financial performance, debt schedule, property condition updates, and variance commentary. Preparing continuously reduces scramble risk near maturity and improves negotiating leverage when market conditions change.

  • Monthly control review: occupancy, collections, expense drift, and cash coverage.
  • Quarterly readiness pack: updated documents and clear narrative on trend direction.
  • Trigger protocol: predefined actions if coverage, vacancy, or reserve thresholds weaken.
  • Communication protocol: concise lender updates when material assumptions shift.

This discipline helps prevent value erosion from avoidable delay and creates a predictable process for extensions, renewals, or takeout financing. Borrowers that demonstrate consistent governance usually retain stronger flexibility and pricing power over time.

Decision Checklist and Deal-Cycle Quality Controls

Before final approval, run a decision checklist that validates structure fit, reserve coverage, timeline realism, and borrower operating readiness. This checklist should be used consistently across opportunities so each deal is compared on the same quality standards. Inconsistent standards create hidden risk and delay responses when problems appear.

Add a deal-cycle quality control loop: pre-submission review, mid-process blocker audit, and post-close retrospective. Each stage should produce concrete outputs and accountable owners. This systematic loop improves process quality over time and reduces repeat errors that can slow closing or weaken refinance positioning.

Where timelines are sensitive, keep a high-frequency issue tracker with due dates and dependency mapping. Teams that maintain disciplined issue tracking typically resolve bottlenecks faster and preserve better lender confidence.

Final Readiness and Renewal-Leverage Strategy

As the transaction moves toward completion or refinance, shift focus to renewal leverage. Assemble a concise evidence package that shows execution quality: milestone completion consistency, variance management, reserve governance, and timely response behavior. Lenders view this operating evidence as a strong signal of lower execution risk.

Use that package to support extension, repricing, or takeout discussions with data instead of narrative alone. Include a one-page summary of key improvements since initial underwriting and a forward-looking risk control plan. This documentation improves negotiating position and reduces uncertainty for credit teams.

Maintain this readiness process each quarter, not only near deadlines. Consistent, documented discipline is what transforms one successful closing into a durable financing strategy across multiple property cycles.
