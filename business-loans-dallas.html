Dallas-Fort Worth business loan questions

How do I get a business loan in Dallas-Fort Worth?

Match the loan type to your need, then apply once to reach multiple lenders. Most look for 6+ months in business, ~$10,000+ monthly revenue, and a 550+ FICO for working capital. Short-term decisions often arrive in 24-48 hours.

Where is the SBA office in Dallas?

The SBA Dallas-Fort Worth District Office serves the metroplex and surrounding North Texas counties. DFW's dense corporate and banking community supports strong SBA 7(a) and 504 lender participation.

Is there financing for tech and corporate suppliers?

Yes. Tech firms along the Telecom Corridor and suppliers to DFW's many headquarters often have project- or contract-based revenue. Lines of credit, working capital, revenue-based financing, and equipment financing tend to fit better than rigid fixed-payment loans.

Are there commercial real estate loans for DFW?

Yes. Owner-occupied and investment CRE financing is widely available across submarkets like Uptown, Las Colinas, the Telecom Corridor, and the Alliance industrial corridor — via SBA 504, conventional commercial mortgages, and bridge loans for value-add or acquisitions.

Lending across the metroplex

Downtown Dallas grabs the headlines, but capital demand runs from Fort Worth to the northern suburbs.

  • Downtown & Uptown Dallas: corporate headquarters, finance, professional services, and a dense commercial real estate market — demand from working capital through large term and CRE loans.
  • Las Colinas & Irving: corporate campuses, logistics, and entertainment — lines of credit, term loans, and CRE.
  • Richardson & Plano (Telecom Corridor): tech, telecom, and corporate suppliers driving startup and revenue-based financing.
  • Fort Worth & Alliance: aerospace, defense, manufacturing, and the Alliance inland port — equipment, manufacturing, and factoring demand.
  • Arlington & the mid-cities: distribution, construction, hospitality, and family-owned services along the interstate corridors.

Dallas-Fort Worth-specific considerations

The metroplex's defining feature is corporate density — a striking number of large companies are headquartered or have major operations across DFW, and that pulls in a deep ecosystem of suppliers, professional-services firms, and contractors. For those businesses, revenue is often contract- or milestone-based: a big order or a renewed contract, then a quieter stretch. That pattern is a textbook case for a line of credit you can draw and repay, or revenue-based financing that flexes with sales, rather than a rigid daily-payment advance that assumes steady weekly receipts.

The other DFW throughline is growth and real estate. The metroplex's relentless expansion keeps commercial property, industrial space, and build-out in motion — owner-occupied buildings for practices and trades, corporate and flex space across the northern suburbs, and warehouse capacity around Alliance and south Dallas. That makes commercial real estate loans, SBA 504 for owner-occupied property, and bridge loans for timing-sensitive deals some of the most common financing conversations in the region. With the SBA's DFW District Office and one of the country's deepest banking communities, access to capital is strong; the planning question for most DFW owners is matching the product to a lumpy contract income and to the deal.
