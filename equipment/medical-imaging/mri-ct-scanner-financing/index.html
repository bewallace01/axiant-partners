Why Imaging Centers and Hospitals Finance MRI/CT

Imaging is a core revenue driver. Financing spreads the cost over 60-84 months, preserving capital for operations and other needs. Leasing is especially common for imaging—technology evolves, and upgrade flexibility matters. See equipment financing by type.

MRI and CT Scanner Costs

New MRI systems run roughly $500,000-$2,000,000+ depending on field strength (1.5T vs 3T) and features. CT scanners run $300,000-$1,500,000+ depending on slice count and capabilities. Used and refurbished units may be 30-50% less. See used medical imaging financing.

Financing Options: Loans vs Leases

Equipment loans spread the cost; you own at payoff. Leases often have lower payments and full deductibility; you can upgrade at term end. Many imaging centers lease. See equipment loan vs lease. Use our calculator.

Credit and Down Payment Requirements

Most lenders look for 600+ FICO; 680+ qualifies for the best rates. Large-ticket medical equipment may require stronger financials. Down payments vary. See credit score for equipment financing.

Documentation and Approval Timeline

Gather: equipment quote (make, model, specifications), 3-6 months bank statements, tax returns, P&L, formation documents, payer mix if applicable. Many lenders approve in 24-48 hours for standard transactions; large tickets ($500K+) may require additional underwriting. See equipment financing requirements and approval timeline.

MRI vs CT: Financing Considerations

Both MRI and CT scanners are routinely financed. MRI systems typically cost more ($500K-$2M+); CT $300K-$1.5M+. Lenders evaluate credit, facility, and revenue. Multi-modality centers may finance both in one package. Used and refurbished units (see used medical imaging financing) can reduce cost 30-50%.

Installation and Site Preparation

MRI and CT require significant site preparation—shielding, power, cooling. Some lenders allow installation to be bundled; others finance equipment only. Discuss with your vendor and lender. Document what's included in your quote. Site costs can add 10-25% to the project.

Where to Find MRI/CT Scanner Financing

Medical equipment lenders, OEM financing (Siemens, GE, Philips, Canon), and specialty healthcare financiers offer MRI/CT financing. Dealer programs are common. A marketplace like Axiant Partners submits one application to multiple lenders. Get matched for MRI and CT scanner financing.

Worked example: financing a $1.2M MRI

Suppose an imaging center finances a $1.2 million 3T MRI over 72 months. On an equipment loan at roughly 9% APR the payment is about $21,600 a month, and the center owns the scanner outright at the end of the term. Structured instead as a fair-market-value lease at a similar rate, the monthly payment falls to roughly $18,000–$19,000 because the residual value is carried to term end — freeing cash flow now, with the option to buy out, upgrade, or return the unit when the lease matures. Run your own figures with our equipment financing tools before choosing a structure.

New vs. refurbished MRI/CT financing

Refurbished scanners are common in imaging and change the financing picture. Because a refurbished MRI or CT can cost 30–50% less than new, the loan amount — and therefore the payment — is smaller, which can make a deal work for a startup center or a second scan room. Lenders treat refurbished equipment a little more conservatively: expect slightly shorter terms (often 48–60 months rather than 72–84), occasionally a higher rate, and a clear preference for OEM-certified or gold-seal refurbishment over gray-market units. Budget for deinstall, transport, and reinstall on a used system, and confirm whether the lender will bundle those costs or finance the equipment only.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an MRI or CT scanner cost?

New MRI systems run $500,000-$2,000,000+; CT scanners $300,000-$1,500,000+. Used and refurbished units may be 30-50% less.

Can I finance an MRI or CT scanner?

Yes. Equipment lenders specialize in medical imaging financing. Credit 600+, typical approval in 24-48 hours.

Lease or buy MRI/CT equipment?

Leases are common—lower payments, preserve capital, allow technology refresh. Many imaging centers lease.

What credit score do I need for MRI/CT financing?

Most lenders look for 600+ FICO. Scores of 680+ qualify for the best rates. Strong revenue improves approval.

How fast can I get approved for MRI/CT financing?

Many lenders approve within 24-48 hours. Large-ticket medical equipment may require additional underwriting.

Can a new imaging center finance an MRI or CT scanner?

Yes, though a startup should expect a larger down payment and closer scrutiny of the business plan, projected payer mix, and the owner’s personal credit. A signed referral or hospital agreement that demonstrates patient volume strengthens the application considerably.

Should an imaging center lease or buy an MRI?

It depends on how quickly the technology in your modality turns over and how much you value ownership. Leasing keeps payments lower and makes it easy to upgrade to a newer field strength or slice count at term end, which is why many centers lease imaging equipment. Buying through a loan costs more per month but builds equity and is usually the better long-run value for a scanner you intend to run for its full useful life.

Are there tax advantages to financing medical imaging equipment?

Often. Equipment that is purchased or financed — rather than rented under a true operating lease — may qualify for Section 179 expensing and bonus depreciation, letting the practice deduct a large share of the cost in the first year. Confirm the specifics with your accountant, since eligibility depends on how the financing is structured and when the scanner is placed in service.
