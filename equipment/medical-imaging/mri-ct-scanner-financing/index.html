How Much Does an MRI Machine Cost?

A new MRI runs roughly $500,000 to $2,000,000 or more. Field strength is the biggest driver: a 3T system costs more than a 1.5T, and features push it up from there. As a reference point, a new 1.5T MRI lists around $1,000,000 installed. Here is how MRI and CT pricing compares:

MRI (1.5T or 3T)$500,000-$2,000,000+About 30-50% less60-84 mo new; 36-60 mo used
1.5T MRI exampleAbout $1,000,000 installedRoughly halfSame as above
CT scanner$150,000-$500,000 typical; $1,500,000+ high-sliceAbout 30-50% less36-84 mo

These are typical ranges, not quotes. Get a written quote with the make, model and specs, and ask what it includes, because installation and site work are sometimes in the price and sometimes not.

Why Is an MRI Machine So Expensive?

Two reasons: the machine and the room. Higher field strength and more features raise the machine's price. Then the room has to be built for it: shielding, power and cooling. Site work commonly adds 10-25% to the project, so a $1,000,000 system can mean $1,100,000-$1,250,000 all in. Some lenders will finance the installation along with the scanner; others finance the equipment only, so settle that before you sign the purchase order.

How Much Does a CT Scanner Cost?

Less than an MRI. Typical CT scanners run about $150,000-$500,000, and high-slice-count systems can pass $1,500,000. Slice count and capabilities drive the price the way field strength does for MRI. Multi-modality centers often finance a CT and an MRI in one package.

What Does a Used or Refurbished MRI Cost?

Refurbished MRI and CT units usually cost 30-50% less than new. A quality refurbished 1.5T of the same generation as a $1,000,000 new system can run roughly half that, which can make the numbers work for a startup center or a second scan room. Lenders treat used imaging a little more conservatively: terms usually run 36-60 months instead of 60-84, the rate can run 1-3 points higher, and they prefer OEM-certified refurbishment over gray-market units. Budget for deinstall, transport and reinstall too. Used medical imaging financing covers the details.

What Is the Monthly Payment on an MRI Machine?

Most buyers finance imaging over 36-84 months with 0-20% down, at about 7-22% APR depending on credit (7-9% with 720+ and 2+ years in business). Two examples:

  • New 3T MRI, $1,200,000 at 9% over 72 months: about $21,631 a month, $357,406 in total interest. You own the scanner at the end.
  • Same MRI on a fair market value lease: lease payments usually run about 10-20% below a loan, so roughly $17,300-$19,500 a month, with the option to buy, upgrade or return it at the end.
  • Refurbished 1.5T, $500,000 at 12% over 60 months: about $11,122 a month, $167,333 in total interest.

Leasing is common in imaging because the technology moves. A loan costs more per month but is usually the better long-run value for a scanner you will run for its full life, and owning it opens Section 179 and bonus depreciation where they apply. Check the structure with your accountant. Equipment leasing vs a loan compares total cost.

Can You Just Buy an MRI Machine?

Imaging centers, hospitals and practices buy them from the manufacturer or a refurbisher, and most finance the purchase. Lenders look at your credit (600+ opens most lenders, 720+ gets the best rates), the facility and the revenue the scanner will bring in. Have these ready:

  • The vendor quote with make, model and specifications
  • 3-6 months of bank statements, tax returns and a P&L
  • Formation documents and, where it applies, your payer mix

Initial approvals typically take 24-72 hours, and tickets over $500,000 usually get extra underwriting. A new imaging center can finance a scanner too, but expect a larger down payment and closer review of the business plan, projected payer mix and the owner's personal credit. A signed referral or hospital agreement that shows patient volume helps a lot.

Where Do You Finance an MRI or CT Scanner?

Manufacturer financing from Siemens, GE, Philips and Canon is common, as are medical equipment lenders and specialty healthcare lenders. Their rates and structures differ, and the right lender for a $1,000,000 MRI is rarely the one that suits a smaller purchase. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an MRI machine cost?

How much does a CT scanner cost?

Typical CT scanners run about $150,000-$500,000, and high-slice-count systems can pass $1,500,000. Refurbished units cost about 30-50% less. CT is usually financed over 36-84 months, like other imaging.

How much is a used or refurbished MRI machine?

Expect about 30-50% below new. A quality refurbished 1.5T of the same generation as a $1,000,000 new system can run roughly half that. Lenders prefer OEM-certified refurbishment and usually offer 36-60 month terms on used units.

Why is an MRI machine so expensive?

The price climbs with field strength, and a 3T system costs more than a 1.5T. The room also has to be built for it, with shielding, power and cooling, which adds 10-25% to the project. On a used unit, deinstall, transport and reinstall add more.

Can you just buy an MRI machine?

Initial approvals typically take 24-72 hours, with more underwriting on deals over $500,000.

How profitable is an MRI machine?

It depends on scan volume, payer mix and what the payment costs you each month. That is also how a lender looks at it: they want to see the scans you expect to bill cover the payment with room to spare. Signed referral or hospital agreements that show patient volume make the case stronger.

Should an imaging center lease or buy an MRI?

Lease if you expect to move to a newer field strength or slice count when the term ends; the payment runs about 10-20% lower than a loan. Buy with a loan if you will run the scanner for its full life, since you own it at payoff. Owning also opens Section 179 and bonus depreciation, where they apply.
