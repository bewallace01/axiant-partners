Custom Applicators: Financing on Contracted Acres

Custom application businesses are underwritten on the acres they cover, not the farm they own. Lenders ask for signed or repeat customer lists, last season’s invoices and the acres per machine. As an assumption to replace with your own: 20,000 acres a season at $8 an acre is $160,000 of revenue, against the $58,838 annual payment above.

Show the season, not just the total: when spraying starts, when customers pay, and fuel, chemical-handling and labor costs. A second machine is easier to finance once the first has a season of invoices.

Precision Options That Change the Price

  • Boom section or nozzle control: shuts off over areas already sprayed.
  • Guidance and auto-steer: may be on the sprayer or moved from another machine.
  • Rate controllers and variable-rate maps.
  • Camera-based targeted spraying: sprays only detected weeds; often a factory option or a per-acre subscription.

Factory options on the quote are financed with the machine. Software subscriptions billed per acre or per year usually are not collateral, so budget them as an operating cost.

Licensing and Rules Lenders Ask About

Anyone applying restricted-use pesticides, or applying for hire, generally needs state certification under the federal standards in 40 CFR part 171. Spray drones fly under the FAA’s agricultural aircraft rules, 14 CFR part 137, and heavier drones also need an FAA exemption. Lenders financing a custom applicator or a drone commonly ask for the certificates and for liability insurance that covers drift.

Buying a Used Sprayer

  • Engine and pump hours, and service records.
  • Boom condition: welds, breakaways, and plumbing for leaks.
  • Tank and rinse system: chemical residue matters if you switch crops or products.
  • Electronics: whether the display and rate controller are still supported.
  • Dealer or auction: many lenders finance used sprayers with a dealer quote; auction purchases may need an appraisal or a larger down payment.

Sprayer Financing FAQ

How much is the payment on a sprayer?

At an assumed 8%, $150,000 is about $3,041 a month over 60 months, and $272,000 is about $4,769 a month over 72 months or $58,838 a year on six annual payments. Illustrations, not quotes.

Can sprayer payments be annual instead of monthly?

Often. Many ag lenders schedule annual or semi-annual payments after harvest. They cost more interest than monthly payments on the same loan.

Can a custom applicator finance a self-propelled sprayer?

Yes. Lenders underwrite the acres you cover: customer lists, last season's invoices and your costs. A first season with no invoices usually needs a larger down payment.

Can you finance a spray drone?

Usually, with a dealer quote listing the aircraft, batteries and charging. Lenders may ask for the FAA Part 137 certificate and your applicator license.

Can you finance a used sprayer?

Yes, commonly from a dealer with a written quote. Hours, boom and tank condition, and whether the electronics are still supported affect approval.

How long are sprayer financing terms?

Commonly 24-84 months. Self-propelled machines tend toward the longer end.
