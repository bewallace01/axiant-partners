How to Finance a Sprayer

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for crop protection and application equipment

Last updated: March 5, 2026

Agricultural sprayers cost $30,000–$400,000+ new depending on type (pull-type vs self-propelled) and capacity, and typically 30–50% less used. Most farmers finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to seasonal application needs.

What Is an Agricultural Sprayer?

An agricultural sprayer is a machine that applies liquid fertilizers, herbicides, pesticides, and fungicides to crops. Pull-type sprayers are towed by a tractor and range from small 60-gallon units to large 1,000+ gallon models with wide booms. Self-propelled sprayers are standalone vehicles with their own engine, typically offering larger tanks (500–1,200+ gallons), wider booms (60–120+ feet), and higher-capacity application. Sprayers support precision agriculture with GPS, section control, and variable-rate technology. They are essential for row-crop, specialty crop, and pasture operations.

How Do You Finance a Sprayer?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Sprayers are common agricultural equipment, so financing is widely available through agriculture lenders. Expect longer terms (5–7+ years) for self-propelled units given higher asset value.

How Much Does a Sprayer Cost?

Sprayer prices vary by type (pull-type vs self-propelled), tank size, boom width, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Sprayer Prices

New pull-type sprayers range from roughly $30,000–$80,000 for mid-size units to $80,000–$150,000+ for large-capacity models with precision tech. New self-propelled sprayers typically cost $250,000–$400,000+ depending on tank size, boom width, and technology. Top brands like John Deere, Case IH, Hagie, and Apache add cost. Tank capacity and boom width drive price.

Used Sprayer Prices

Used sprayers typically cost 30–50% less than new equivalents, depending on age, type, and condition. A 5-year-old pull-type sprayer might run $20,000–$50,000. A used self-propelled sprayer might run $120,000–$250,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 7–10 years old. Always inspect tank, pump, boom, and controls before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Type (pull vs self-propelled), tank size, boom width, brand, technology package (GPS, section control), and regional demand all affect sprayer pricing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Sprayers

Several financing structures work for sprayers. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the sprayer when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 24–84 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Sprayers hold value reasonably well, so financing is often accessible. Ideal if you plan to use the sprayer long-term.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return the sprayer, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Leasing suits operations that want to preserve cash flow or upgrade regularly as technology improves.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans can finance sprayers with longer terms and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. USDA guaranteed loans also support agricultural equipment. Suit established operations.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are better suited for operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use equipment financing for the sprayer itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Sprayer Financing Rates and Terms

Interest rates for sprayer financing typically range from 6–15% for equipment loans and leases. Terms commonly run 24–84 months for standard equipment financing; SBA loans can extend to 7–10 years. A $150,000 self-propelled sprayer financed at 8% over 60 months would result in roughly $3,040/month. Use our financing calculator to model different scenarios.

Step-by-Step: How to Finance a Sprayer

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose pull-type or self-propelled based on acreage and application needs. Get a written quote from the dealer.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and equipment quote.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple agricultural equipment lenders. Axiant Partners matches farmers with lenders experienced in agriculture equipment financing.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer.

Tips to Get Approved for Sprayer Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors, and avoid new credit before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements and organized financials speed approval.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used sprayer may cost significantly less. Inspect tank, pump, and boom carefully.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate.
  • Time your application. Apply before planting season if you need the sprayer by a specific date.

Sprayer Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used sprayer?

Yes. Many lenders finance used sprayers, typically those 7–10 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Tank size, boom width, and condition affect approval.

What credit score is required for sprayer financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Agricultural equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does sprayer financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Self-propelled vs pull-type—does it affect financing?

Lenders finance both. Self-propelled sprayers cost more and may qualify for longer terms. Pull-type sprayers are lower cost and often have shorter terms. Price and condition matter more than type.

Is leasing better than buying a sprayer?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the sprayer long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow and application needs.
