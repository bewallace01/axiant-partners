How Much Does It Cost to Open a Dental Practice, and How Do You Finance It?

What a new dental office costs by operatory and by machine, and how dentists split it between an SBA loan and equipment financing.

Quick answer

Opening a dental practice typically costs $300,000-$600,000 for a 4-operatory office, including equipment, sterilization and X-ray. Each basic operatory runs about $15,000-$40,000, or $40,000-$100,000 fully equipped, and a CBCT scanner adds $75,000-$150,000. Most dentists fund a startup with an SBA 7(a) loan for build-out and working capital, which needs at least 10% of the project cost from you and usually 650-680+ credit, plus equipment financing for chairs and imaging.

How Much Does It Cost to Open a Dental Practice?

Equipment lenders put a typical 4-operatory startup at $300,000-$600,000, including all equipment, sterilization and X-ray. Where you land in that range depends mostly on how many operatories you equip on day one, whether you buy a CBCT and CAD/CAM up front, and how much build-out the space needs.

Here is how the big pieces break down. Ranges are typical, not quotes; get dealer quotes for your own plan.

ItemTypical costNotes
Basic operatory (chair, light, delivery unit)$15,000-$40,000 eachFully equipped: $40,000-$100,000
Digital X-ray$15,000-$35,000Sensors and wall-mounted heads
CBCT (3D cone beam)$75,000-$150,000Often added after opening
Sterilization$5,000-$15,000Code-required
CAD/CAM (e.g. CEREC)$100,000 or moreOptional at opening
2-4 operatory fit-out$100,000-$300,000+Equipment and operatory build-out
4-operatory startup, all-in$300,000-$600,000Equipment, sterilization, X-ray included

One way to keep the opening number down: equip two or three operatories fully, plumb the rest, and add chairs as the schedule fills. CBCT and CAD/CAM can also wait until you can finance them against real production instead of projections.

How Do Dentists Finance a New Practice?

Usually with two tools. An SBA 7(a) loan ($50,000 to $5,000,000) is the common route for de novo practices because it covers build-out, working capital and equipment in one loan on long terms. SBA treats a business open one year or less as a start-up, so you need at least 10% of the total project cost as your own equity injection. Lenders look for 650-680+ credit, an active dental license, and clinical experience or solid projections. Plan on 30-60+ days to close.

Equipment financing covers chairs, imaging, sterilization and digital systems, with the equipment as collateral. Dental equipment lenders quote about 7-12% APR over 36-84 months with 0-20% down at 600+ credit, and decisions typically come back in 24-72 hours. A brand-new practice without 2 years of history usually prices toward the top of that range or above it; generic equipment lenders put businesses under 2 years at about 12-22% APR. See dental equipment financing for rates by credit tier, and dental practice financing for SBA, build-out and acquisition options.

What Does the Monthly Payment Look Like?

Say you finance a $250,000 package of chairs, X-ray and pano, sterilization and operatory build-out with 10% down ($25,000), leaving $225,000. At 12% APR:

  • Over 60 months: about $5,005 a month, $75,300 in total interest.
  • Over 84 months: about $3,972 a month, $108,637 in total interest.

The longer term saves about $1,033 a month while the practice ramps up, at the cost of about $33,337 more interest. If the loan has no prepayment penalty, you can take the longer term and pay extra once production is steady. Try your own numbers in the payment calculator.

What Credit and Down Payment Do You Need?

For equipment, 600+ opens most lenders and 720+ with 2+ years in business gets the best rates, about 7-9%. Down payments run 0-20% depending on credit and equipment. For the SBA side, plan on 650-680+ credit and the 10% equity injection: about $30,000-$60,000 on a $300,000-$600,000 practice. With no practice history, your personal credit, your production as an associate and a clear business plan carry the file.

Should You Lease or Buy the Equipment?

Lease the equipment you expect to upgrade; lease payments usually run about 10-20% lower than a loan, and leasing fits practices that refresh technology every 5-8 years. Buy the chairs, cabinetry and sterilization you will keep for a decade. Purchased or financed equipment, rather than equipment rented under a true operating lease, may qualify for Section 179, which can let you deduct a large share of the cost in the first year. Ask your CPA how that fits your opening year. Equipment leasing vs a loan compares total cost.

What Do Lenders Want to See?

  • Bundled equipment quotes from your dental dealer, covering every code-required item
  • A business plan with projections, location and opening timeline
  • Your license, CV and production history as an associate
  • 3-6 months of personal and business bank statements, and tax returns
  • The signed lease or build-out agreement, and formation documents

Financing the full fit-out in one package usually beats financing each item separately on terms and paperwork, and lenders like to see a complete, working office. For digital systems, see CEREC financing; if you are buying an existing office instead of starting one, read how to finance buying a dental practice. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to open a dental practice?

A typical 4-operatory startup runs $300,000-$600,000 including all equipment, sterilization and X-ray. A smaller 2-4 operatory fit-out can start around $100,000-$300,000+, while adding CBCT and CAD/CAM pushes the total higher. Your market, lease and build-out drive the rest.

How do dentists finance a new practice?

Most use an SBA 7(a) loan for the build-out, working capital and often the equipment, because it covers de novo practices and runs on long terms. Others pair a smaller SBA loan with separate equipment financing for chairs and imaging. Expect SBA lenders to look for 650-680+ credit, an active license and clinical experience or solid projections.

How much do you need to put down to open a dental practice?

For an SBA 7(a) start-up loan, at least 10% of the total project cost, so about $30,000-$60,000 on a $300,000-$600,000 practice. Equipment financing runs 0-20% down depending on credit and the equipment.

What credit score do you need to open a dental practice?

SBA startup and build-out loans typically favor 650-680+ credit. Equipment lenders start at 600+, and 720+ gets the best rates. Because a new practice has no history, the lender leans on your personal credit and your production record as an associate.

Should a new dental practice lease or buy equipment?

Leasing fits equipment you plan to upgrade, and lease payments usually run about 10-20% lower than a loan. Buying fits chairs and cabinetry you will keep for years, and purchased or financed equipment may qualify for Section 179. A common split is to buy the operatories and lease the digital equipment.

How long does it take to finance a new dental practice?

Equipment financing decisions typically come back in 24-72 hours, with funding in about 5-10 business days. An SBA startup or build-out loan usually takes 30-60+ days, so start it well before you sign contractors.
