Why Practices Invest in CEREC and CAD/CAM

Same-day restorations reduce patient visits, improve satisfaction, and capture revenue that might go to labs. Digital workflows also support implants, orthodontics, and diagnostic tools. One financed CEREC system can pay for itself through increased crown volume and efficiency. See equipment financing by type.

CEREC and CAD/CAM System Costs

CEREC and similar systems run roughly $80,000-$150,000+ including intraoral scanner, milling unit, oven, and software. Pricing varies by manufacturer (Dentsply Sirona, 3M, Planmeca, Ivoclar) and configuration. Training and support may be included. Get a written quote from your dealer. See dental equipment financing for new practices.

Financing Options: Loans vs Leases

Equipment loans spread the cost; you own at payoff. Leases often have lower payments and full deductibility; you can upgrade at term end. Digital technology evolves; many practices lease to preserve upgrade flexibility. See equipment loan vs lease. Use our calculator.

Credit and Down Payment Requirements

Most lenders look for 600+ FICO; 680+ qualifies for the best rates. Down payments: 0-15%. Established practices typically qualify readily. See credit score for equipment financing and down payment requirements.

Leasing CEREC: Why It's Popular

Leasing preserves capital and offers lower monthly payments. Technology refreshes (new scanners, software, materials) may make upgrading at term end attractive. Full payment deductibility can improve tax treatment. Many practices lease CEREC to maintain flexibility—digital dentistry evolves quickly. See benefits of equipment leasing.

Documentation and Approval Timeline

Gather: equipment quote (make, model, configuration), 3-6 months bank statements, tax returns, P&L, formation documents. For new practices: business plan and personal financials. Complete documentation speeds approval. Many lenders approve in 24-48 hours. See equipment financing requirements, equipment financing approval requirements, and approval timeline.

Rates and Monthly Payments

Rates typically run 6-14%. For a $100,000 CEREC system at 8% over 60 months with 10% down, expect roughly $1,700-$1,900/month. Use our calculator. See typical equipment financing rates.

What CEREC Financing Covers

A CEREC or CAD/CAM purchase is a small system, not a single device, and financing usually covers all of it: the intraoral scanner that captures the digital impression, the milling unit that cuts the restoration from a ceramic block, the design software, and — for zirconia and certain materials — a sintering oven or furnace. Lenders will typically fold in the startup block and material inventory and sometimes the training, so the practice can go live without a separate cash outlay for each piece. Because the equipment holds value and a dental practice is a stable, creditworthy borrower, these systems are among the easier equipment purchases to finance — often with little or nothing down for an established practice.

The Chairside ROI: Why CEREC Pays for Itself

CEREC is financed differently in spirit from most equipment because its return is so direct. Every same-day crown, onlay, or veneer you produce in-house does two things at once: it captures restorative revenue you might otherwise refer out, and it eliminates the outside lab fee on that unit (commonly in the low hundreds of dollars each) while sparing the patient a second visit and a temporary. Add those up and the system's monthly payment is frequently covered by a modest number of restorations a month — many practices model the break-even at just a handful of units. That's also why lenders are comfortable: the equipment generates identifiable revenue and savings, so the case for approval almost writes itself when you can show your restorative volume. Run your own numbers — units per month times (fee captured + lab cost saved) versus the payment — at the payment calculator; the figures here are illustrative, not a quote.

CEREC & Practice Cash Flow

Most practices finance rather than pay cash even when they could, because the goal is to keep capital available for payroll, supplies, and the rest of the operatory rather than sink six figures into one system. Financing the CEREC and keeping cash on hand is the same logic a healthy practice applies to any major equipment decision. A few practical notes: bundle related operatory upgrades (chairs, imaging, build-out) into one plan if you're modernizing, so approvals and delivery line up; match the term to how long you'll run the platform — digital dentistry refreshes, so some practices prefer a lease that builds in an upgrade (see the leasing section above); and weigh the tax treatment — Section 179 and bonus depreciation can let a profitable practice expense much of the cost in the year it's placed in service, which materially changes the after-tax cost of buying versus leasing. Your CPA can tell you which path fits your practice's books. For credit specifics, see credit score for equipment financing.
