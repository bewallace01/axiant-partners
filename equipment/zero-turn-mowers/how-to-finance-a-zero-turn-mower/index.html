What Is a Zero-Turn Mower?

A zero-turn mower is a riding mower with a zero-radius turning capability—the front wheels pivot 180 degrees, allowing the mower to turn in place. Zero-turn mowers are faster and more maneuverable than traditional lawn tractors, making them ideal for commercial lawns, parks, and large residential properties. Deck sizes typically range from 42 to 72 inches. Commercial zero-turns are built for daily use, with reinforced decks, commercial engines, and higher cutting speeds. They are essential for lawn care companies and landscapers who need efficient mowing capacity.

How Do You Finance a Zero-Turn Mower?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 550+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Zero-turn mowers are common landscaping equipment, so financing is widely available through landscaping lenders.

How Much Does a Zero-Turn Mower Cost?

Zero-turn mower prices vary by deck size, engine type, brand, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Zero-Turn Mower Prices

New commercial zero-turn mowers range from roughly $6,000–$10,000 for 48–54 inch decks to $10,000–$15,000+ for 60–72 inch commercial units. Residential zero-turns start around $3,500–$6,000. Top brands like Scag, Exmark, Toro, John Deere, and Ferris add cost. Deck size, engine horsepower, and commercial-grade components drive price.

Used Zero-Turn Mower Prices

Used zero-turn mowers typically cost 25–40% less than new equivalents, depending on age, hours, and condition. A 3-year-old commercial zero-turn might run $4,500–$9,000. Older units can start around $3,000–$5,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 5–7 years old. Always inspect deck, spindles, and engine hours before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Deck size, engine (Kawasaki, Kohler, etc.), brand, commercial vs residential grade, and regional demand all affect zero-turn mower pricing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Zero-Turn Mowers

Several financing structures work for zero-turn mowers. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the mower when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 24–48 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Zero-turn mowers hold value reasonably well, so financing is often accessible. Ideal if you plan to use the mower long-term.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return the mower, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Leasing suits businesses that want to preserve cash flow or upgrade regularly.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) loans can finance zero-turn mowers with longer terms and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. Suit established landscaping businesses combining equipment with working capital.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are better suited for operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use equipment financing for the mower itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose the zero-turn that fits your deck size and terrain. Get a written quote from the dealer.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and equipment quote.

  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer.

Tips to Get Approved for Zero-Turn Mower Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors, and avoid new credit before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements and lawn care contracts speed approval.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used zero-turn may cost significantly less. Inspect deck and engine hours carefully.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate.
  • Time your application. Apply before mowing season if you need the mower by a specific date.

Zero-Turn Mower Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used zero-turn mower?

Yes. Many lenders finance used zero-turn mowers, typically those 5–7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Condition, hours, and brand affect approval.

What credit score is required for zero-turn mower financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 550 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 550+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does zero-turn mower financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Can startups finance zero-turn mowers?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1–2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer. SBA loans can help newer businesses.

Is leasing better than buying a zero-turn mower?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the mower long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow and growth plans.
