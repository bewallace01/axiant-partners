What Is a Zero-Turn Mower?

A zero-turn mower is a riding mower with zero-radius turning-front wheels pivot 180 degrees, allowing the mower to turn in place. Ideal for commercial lawns, parks, and large residential properties. Deck sizes typically 42-72 inches. Commercial zero-turns are built for daily use with reinforced decks, commercial engines, and higher cutting speeds. Essential for lawn care companies and landscapers.

Why Zero-Turn Mower Financing Is Different

Three things make zero-turns unlike most equipment. The tickets are small, so many lenders approve on an application alone, without tax returns, for amounts in this range. Dealers often have manufacturer promotional financing on new mowers, which competes directly with any loan. And the buyer is frequently a one- or two-truck operator whose personal credit is the business credit.

Engine rules can matter too. California regulates small off-road engines separately: the Air Resources Board's SORE program covers spark-ignition engines rated at or below 19 kW, about 25 hp (CARB). Many commercial zero-turns run larger engines, but if you work in California, confirm with the dealer which models and fuel types can be sold there, including battery models, before you finance one.

Zero-Turn Mower Financing Options

For a mower, the real choice is usually between the dealer's offer and an outside lender.

Dealer or manufacturer promotion

Low or 0% rate offers on new mowers for a set number of months, for buyers with stronger credit. Check whether the 0% replaces a cash discount, and whether it is true 0% or deferred interest that is charged back if not paid in full.

Equipment loan

From an outside lender, for new or used mowers and for several at once. Commonly 24–48 months at rates that typically run 6–15%, with 0–20% down. Best when the dealer offer is not available or does not cover used machines. Equipment financing.

Lease-to-own or rent-to-own

Leases with a $1 or small buyout end in ownership. Rent-to-own programs let you return the mower, but usually cost the most in total. Read the buyout terms. Loan vs lease.

Line of credit

A revolving line suits crews that replace small equipment every season. Business line of credit. SBA loans are rarely worth the paperwork for a single mower.

Rent-to-Own, Lease-to-Own and 0% Offers Compared

These three get searched together but work differently:

  • 0% promotional financing is a loan with no interest for the promotion period, usually only on new mowers and for stronger credit. Some offers are deferred interest: if the balance is not paid off in time, interest from the start is added. Ask which kind it is.
  • Lease-to-own is a lease with a small buyout, often $1. You keep the mower at the end and the cost is close to a loan.
  • Rent-to-own lets you stop paying and return the mower. That flexibility is what you pay for, and the total usually ends up well above the cash price.

For a business with fair credit, an equipment loan for a used commercial mower is often cheaper than rent-to-own on a new one. If credit is the obstacle, see equipment financing with bad credit.

Because the amount is small, a lender may decide on credit and a few bank statements alone. Customer contracts, such as HOA or commercial property maintenance agreements, help a newer or lower-score applicant more than anything else. Credit score tiers.

What to Have Ready Before You Apply

  • The dealer quote: model, deck width, engine, and any bagger or mulch kit.
  • Bank statements covering at least one full season, so the lender sees spring through fall, not just winter.
  • Your customer list or maintenance contracts, if you are newer or rebuilding credit.
  • For a used mower: hour meter reading and photos of the deck and spindles.

For larger crew-expansion purchases, add tax returns and a profit and loss statement.

When to Apply for Zero-Turn Mower Financing

Late winter is the usual buying window: dealers stock up for spring and promotional offers tend to appear then. Apply a few weeks before you need the mower, so an approval is in hand when the model you want is on the floor. Ask lenders whether the first payment can start when the season does.

Tips to Get Approved

  • Buy commercial-grade. Lenders value a commercial mower more than a residential one at the same price.
  • Bundle the crew's equipment. Mowers, trimmers and the trailer on one approval are easier than three applications.
  • Show your contracts. Recurring maintenance agreements are the best evidence a mower will pay for itself.
  • Apply before the rush. Spring is when everyone applies.
  • Compare the promotion with the cash price. Sometimes the discount for paying with an outside loan beats 0%.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a consumer store card for a business mower. It builds no business credit and deferred interest can be expensive.
  • Stretching a mower over too long a term. Heavy daily use can wear out a deck before a long loan is paid off.
  • Rent-to-own as a first choice. It is the most expensive way to end up owning a mower.
  • Buying a residential model for commercial work. It wears faster and is worth less as collateral.

Why Businesses Finance Zero-Turn Mowers Rather Than Pay Cash

Lawn care runs on seasonal cash. Paying cash for a mower in March drains the reserves that carry payroll, fuel and insurance until the first customer payments come in. A financed mower can be paid for by the lawns it cuts. Owners can also depreciate business equipment, and Section 179 may allow much of the cost to be deducted in the first year (IRS Publication 946); check with your tax adviser.

Zero-Turn Mower Financing FAQ

How much does a commercial zero-turn mower cost?

Typically $6,000–$10,000 for a 48–54 inch commercial model and $10,000–$15,000+ for 60–72 inch decks, with residential models from about $3,500. Used commercial mowers often sell 25–40% below new.

Can you rent to own a zero-turn mower?

Yes, some dealers and rental companies offer it, and you can usually return the mower. It is typically the most expensive route to ownership. Lease-to-own with a small buyout, or an equipment loan, usually costs less in total.

Are 0% zero-turn financing offers worth it?

They can be, if you have the credit and the mower is new. Compare with the cash price, since some 0% offers replace a discount, and confirm it is not deferred interest.

Can I finance a zero-turn mower with bad credit?

Some programs start around 550. Expect a larger down payment or a shorter term, and bring customer contracts. See bad credit equipment financing.

Can I finance used commercial mowers?

Yes. Many lenders finance used commercial mowers; hours, deck condition and whether it is a commercial model matter most.

Can I finance two mowers and a trailer at once?

Yes. Crew packages on one approval are common, and small-ticket approvals are often same-day, so equipment can be ready within days once the dealer has it.

Can a new lawn care business finance a zero-turn?

Often, based on personal credit and a down payment. Signed customer agreements help. See lawn care business loans.
