How to Finance Industrial Robots

What Are Industrial Robots?

Industrial robots are programmable machines that automate welding, assembly, material handling, painting, and inspection. Articulated robots have multiple joints and are used for welding, assembly, and dispensing. Collaborative robots (cobots) work alongside humans. SCARA and Cartesian robots handle pick-and-place and assembly. Payload capacity (e.g., 6 kg, 20 kg, 50 kg) and reach determine the robot's capabilities. Brands like Fanuc, ABB, Kuka, Yaskawa, and Universal Robots are common. Industrial robots help manufacturers increase throughput, improve quality, and address labor shortages.

How Do You Finance Industrial Robots?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Industrial robots are common manufacturing equipment with strong resale value, so financing is widely available through manufacturing lenders.

How Much Do Industrial Robots Cost?

Industrial robot prices vary by payload, reach, application, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Industrial Robot Prices

New cobots (collaborative robots) range from roughly $25,000–$50,000. New articulated robots (6–20 kg payload) typically cost $50,000–$100,000. Larger robots (50–200+ kg) run $100,000–$200,000+. The complete system—robot, controller, end-of-arm tooling, programming—adds 20–50% or more. Top brands add cost.

Used Industrial Robot Prices

Used robots typically cost 25–45% less than new equivalents. A 5-year-old welding robot might run $40,000–$80,000. Older material handling robots can start around $15,000–$30,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 7–10 years old. Inspect hours, joints, and controller before purchasing used.

Financing Options for Industrial Robots

Several financing structures work for industrial robots. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the robot when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 36–72 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Robots hold value well, so financing is often accessible.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return, purchase at fair market value, or upgrade. Leasing suits manufacturers who want to preserve cash flow or upgrade as technology evolves.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) loans can finance industrial robots with longer terms and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. Suit established manufacturers combining automation with working capital.

Industrial Robot Financing FAQ

Can you finance used industrial robots?

Yes. Many lenders finance used industrial robots, typically those 7–10 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Hours, condition, payload, and brand affect approval.

What credit score is required for industrial robot financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Manufacturing equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does industrial robot financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Does application (welding vs assembly) affect financing?

Lenders consider price and useful life. Welding robots may cost more than material handling robots. Price and condition matter more than application for financing.

Is leasing better than buying industrial robots?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades as technology evolves. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the robot long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow and upgrade cycle.
