Reefer Trailer Financing: How Lenders Value the Trailer and the Unit

Financing a refrigerated semi-trailer, new or used: why the reefer unit is underwritten on its own, which structure fits, and what a payment looks like

Quick answer

Reefer trailers finance like other semi-trailers, commonly over 24 to 84 months, but the lender values two things: the insulated trailer and the refrigeration unit, which runs its own engine and hour meter. As an illustration, a $75,000 trailer with 10% down at 9% over 60 months is about $1,401 a month. Why a Reefer Trailer Is Underwritten Differently

A dry van is a box on wheels. A reefer is a box on wheels with a diesel or electric refrigeration unit bolted to the front, and that unit wears out on a different clock. Lenders look at the trailer's age and condition and, separately, the unit's hours, service history and model year. A five-year-old trailer with a recently rebuilt unit can be easier to finance than a three-year-old one whose unit has run hard with no records.

What the lender checksWhy it matters
Reefer unit hoursThe engine-hour meter is the unit's odometer; high hours shorten the term
Unit service recordsProof of maintenance supports value if the trailer is repossessed and resold
Insulation and wallsDamaged or wet insulation lowers holding performance and resale value
Floor and doorsFood-grade floors and tight door seals matter to shippers and buyers
Annual inspectionTrailers, like trucks, need a periodic inspection at least every 12 months
Title and liensThe trailer has its own title; check it for existing liens before you buy

Loan, TRAC Lease or Dealer Program

Three structures cover most reefer trailer deals. The right one depends on how long you'll keep the trailer and whether you want a lower payment now or full ownership.

StructureHow it worksFits when
Equipment loanYou own the trailer; the lender files a lien until it's paidYou'll run it for years
TRAC leaseLower payment with a set residual you guarantee at the endYou trade trailers on a cycle
$1 buyout leaseLease payments, then you own it for $1You want lease paperwork but ownership
Dealer or manufacturer programFinancing arranged where you buyA promotional rate beats outside offers

TRAC leases are available because the tax code's motor vehicle lease rule covers trailers as well as trucks. The catch is the residual: if the trailer sells for less than the residual at lease end, you pay the difference. For the general trade-offs see trailer financing; for trucks and trailers bought together, see 100% financing for Class 8 trucks and trailers.

Buying a Used Reefer Trailer

Used reefers are where most financing problems start, because the unit can fail long before the trailer does. Before you sign:

  1. Get the unit's hours and service history from the seller, and have it run down to temperature while you watch.
  2. Ask for the latest annual inspection report for the trailer.
  3. Check the title and lien search so you aren't buying someone else's loan.
  4. Price a unit rebuild or replacement into your offer if the hours are high, and ask the lender whether it can be financed with the trailer.
  5. If you'll run in California, confirm the unit can be registered and operated under the state's transport refrigeration unit rules.

Food Safety Rules That Affect the Deal

If you haul food, shippers will ask how you meet FDA's sanitary transportation rule. When a written agreement makes the carrier responsible, the carrier must pre-cool refrigerated compartments as the shipper specifies, be able to show the temperature was held during the trip when asked, and keep written procedures for cleaning and temperature control. A trailer with a working temperature recorder makes that easier, and some shippers won't load without one. Budget for the recorder and any telematics when you size the loan.

How to Apply

  1. Get a written quote with the trailer's VIN, year, length, and the unit's make, model year and hours.
  2. Gather three to six months of bank statements, your operating authority and, for larger deals, tax returns.
  3. Decide loan or lease based on how long you'll keep the trailer.
  4. Compare at least two offers on rate, term, fees and any prepayment terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you finance a reefer trailer?

Yes. Refrigerated semi-trailers are financed with equipment loans, TRAC leases, $1 buyout leases and dealer programs, commonly over 24 to 84 months. The lender values both the trailer and the refrigeration unit.

What credit score do you need for reefer trailer financing?

It varies by lender. Stronger credit gets the lower end of equipment financing rates, around 7-9% on our published tiers, while weaker files land in the 10-18% range or need a larger down payment.

Can I finance a used reefer trailer?

Yes, but expect shorter terms on older trailers and closer questions about the reefer unit's hours and service history. A recent annual inspection and maintenance records help.

What is the monthly payment on a reefer trailer?

As an illustration, $67,500 financed at 9% over 60 months is about $1,401 a month, and at 14% about $1,571. Your price, down payment, rate and term set the real number.

Is a TRAC lease available on a trailer?

Yes. The tax code's TRAC lease rule covers motor vehicles and trailers. It lowers the payment, but you guarantee the residual value at the end of the lease.
