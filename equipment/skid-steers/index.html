What Is a Skid Steer?

A skid steer (or skid steer loader) is a compact, rigid-frame loader with lift arms that can attach to a wide variety of tools-buckets, pallet forks, augers, trenchers, snow blades, and more. It turns by skidding (slowing one side of wheels or tracks), making it highly maneuverable in tight spaces. Skid steers are used in construction, landscaping, agriculture, snow removal, and material handling for loading, digging, grading, and a range of tasks depending on the attachment.

Skid steers range from compact models (under 1,500 lbs rated operating capacity) to large machines (3,000+ lbs). Brands like Bobcat, Caterpillar, John Deere, Kubota, and Case dominate the market. Understanding what skid steers are and how they're used helps lenders assess your financing application-they want to see that the equipment supports revenue-generating work. Landscapers, general contractors, and farmers rely on skid steers as versatile workhorses. Construction equipment financing often covers skid steers alongside mini excavators and compact loaders.

Skid Steer Financing Options

Several financing structures work for skid steers. Choose based on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the equipment at the end of the term.

Equipment Loans

Borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the skid steer when the loan is paid off. Typically 0-20% down, terms 24-72 months. Interest rates 6-15% depending on credit. Ideal if you plan to use the skid steer long-term and want to build equity. See typical rates.

Equipment Leasing

Lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, return the equipment, purchase at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Leasing suits businesses that want to preserve cash flow or upgrade regularly. Loan vs lease.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans offer longer terms (7-10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically 30-60+ days. Best for established businesses planning larger purchases or combining equipment with real estate. View SBA loans.

Working capital loans are flexible but generally carry higher rates and shorter terms. They're better suited for payroll and materials than equipment purchases. Use working capital for operating expenses; use equipment financing for the skid steer itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset. Compare equipment loan vs lease in detail.

How Much Does a Skid Steer Cost?

Skid steer prices vary by size (rated operating capacity), type (wheel vs track), brand, and whether you buy new or used. New skid steers range from roughly $25,000-$40,000 for compact models to $40,000-$60,000 for mid-size machines, and $60,000-$75,000+ for large units with cab and attachments. Top brands like Bobcat, Caterpillar, John Deere, and Kubota typically command premium prices. Track loaders cost more than wheel models. Attachments (buckets, forks, augers) add cost. Factor in delivery and setup when budgeting.

Used skid steers typically cost 30-50% less than new equivalents. A 5-year-old mid-size skid steer might run $25,000-$45,000. Older machines or those with high hours sell for less but may require more maintenance. Many lenders finance used skid steers up to 5-7 years old; older machines may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Always get an equipment inspection before purchasing used. Rated operating capacity, track vs wheel, brand, cab type, attachments, and regional demand all affect pricing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer or seller-lenders use this to structure your financing. Financing used equipment guide.

What to Have Ready Before You Apply

Gathering documentation before you start speeds approval and reduces back-and-forth. Have these ready when applying for skid steer financing:

Tips to Get Approved for Skid Steer Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors on your report, and avoid new credit applications before applying. Even a modest improvement can lower your rate.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements, consistent deposits, and organized financials speed approval and improve terms.

  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate or improve approval when credit is borderline.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Financing a Skid Steer

Avoid these when financing skid steers:

  • Skipping the equipment quote. Lenders need it to structure the loan. Get a written quote from your dealer before applying. Include attachments if you're financing them together.
  • Applying with incomplete financials. Missing bank statements or tax returns causes delays. Gather everything upfront.
  • Focusing on rate alone. Terms, fees, prepayment penalties, and flexibility matter. Read the full agreement before signing.
  • Waiting until the last minute. Rush approvals may limit your options. Apply early when you have time to compare offers.
  • Ignoring used equipment. A quality used skid steer can cost 30-50% less and often qualifies for financing with shorter terms.

Can you finance a used skid steer?

Yes. Many lenders finance used skid steers, typically those 5-7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Resale value and condition affect approval.

What credit score is required?

Most lenders look for 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ qualify for the best rates. Asset-backed financing sometimes works with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does approval take?

1-5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans add 30-60+ days. Having documents ready-financials, equipment quote, business info-speeds the process.

Is leasing better than buying?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the skid steer long-term. Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1-2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer with strong credit.
