What Is a Skid Steer?

A skid steer (or skid steer loader) is a compact, rigid-frame loader with lift arms that can attach to a wide variety of tools-buckets, pallet forks, augers, trenchers, snow blades, and more. It turns by skidding (slowing one side of wheels or tracks), making it highly maneuverable in tight spaces. Skid steers are used in construction, landscaping, agriculture, snow removal, and material handling for loading, digging, grading, and a range of tasks depending on the attachment.

Skid steers range from compact models (under 1,500 lbs rated operating capacity) to large machines (3,000+ lbs). Brands like Bobcat, Caterpillar, John Deere, Kubota, and Case dominate the market. Understanding what skid steers are and how they're used helps lenders assess your financing application-they want to see that the equipment supports revenue-generating work. Landscapers, general contractors, and farmers rely on skid steers as versatile workhorses. Construction equipment financing often covers skid steers alongside mini excavators and compact loaders.

How Much Does a Skid Steer Cost?

Skid steer prices vary by size (rated operating capacity), type (wheel vs track), brand, and whether you buy new or used. New skid steers range from roughly $25,000-$40,000 for compact models to $40,000-$60,000 for mid-size machines, and $60,000-$75,000+ for large units with cab and attachments. Top brands like Bobcat, Caterpillar, John Deere, and Kubota typically command premium prices. Track loaders cost more than wheel models. Attachments (buckets, forks, augers) add cost. Factor in delivery and setup when budgeting.

Used skid steers typically cost 30-50% less than new equivalents. A 5-year-old mid-size skid steer might run $25,000-$45,000. Older machines or those with high hours sell for less but may require more maintenance. Many lenders finance used skid steers up to 5-7 years old; older machines may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Always get an equipment inspection before purchasing used. Rated operating capacity, track vs wheel, brand, cab type, attachments, and regional demand all affect pricing. What to Have Ready Before You Apply

Skid-steer files are usually the simplest in the earthmoving category, and at this ticket size many lenders will approve on an application and bank statements alone.

  • Written quote with model, serial and hydraulic flow rating · Standard versus high flow determines which attachments the machine can run, and it belongs on the quote for that reason.
  • Hour meter reading · Skid-steers cycle constantly, so hours accumulate fast. A five-year-old machine with low hours is genuinely unusual and worth verifying.
  • Three months of business bank statements · Often the only financial documentation required below common program thresholds.
  • EIN and formation documents · The entity on the application must match the entity on the quote.
  • Attachment list with the machine · This matters more on a skid-steer than on any other machine, because the attachments are frequently worth as much as the carrier.
  • Tracks or tires, and which you are buying · Compact track loaders and wheeled skid-steers finance similarly but appraise differently. Be explicit about which one the deal is for.

If you are trading in an existing machine, get the trade value in writing before applying. It changes the amount financed and therefore the structure. Credit score requirements for equipment financing.

When to Apply for Skid Steer Financing

Apply as soon as you know the configuration you want, because configuration is what takes time to source. A high-flow machine with the right coupler and a specific attachment package is not something most dealers have in stock, and the wait for the right unit is usually longer than the wait for an approval.

For landscaping, snow and small site contractors, the timing question is seasonal in the other direction. Buying a skid-steer in late summer for fall and winter work means paying for it before the season that justifies it, so match the structure to the cash flow — some lenders will arrange a deferred first payment where the seasonality is documented.

Tips to Get Approved for Skid Steer Financing

  • Buy the flow rating you need for the attachment. A mulcher or a cold planer on a standard-flow machine will disappoint. Deciding this before the quote avoids financing a carrier that cannot run the tool.
  • Put the attachment package on the invoice. Buckets, forks, augers, grapples and breakers are financeable with the machine. Bought later, they usually are not, at least not on the same terms.
  • Check the couplers and the lift arms. These take the abuse on a skid-steer. Play in the pins is normal wear; cracks around the mounting points are not.
  • Use the small ticket to move quickly. Application-only approvals at this size are frequently same-day. That speed is worth using when a well-specified machine appears.
  • Ask about seasonal payment structures. If your work stops for three months, say so. Structures exist for it, but only if the lender knows before the deal is documented.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Financing a Skid Steer

The mistakes here are less about the finance and more about the specification:

  • Choosing on rated operating capacity alone. Lift height, reach and hydraulic flow decide what the machine is useful for. Capacity is only one of the three.
  • Buying a tracked machine for hard-surface work. Compact track loaders are superb in soft ground and expensive on concrete, where the tracks wear quickly. Match the machine to the surface.
  • Underestimating attachment cost. A full attachment package can approach the price of the carrier. Plan the whole purchase, then finance it once.
  • Applying to a single lender. Skid-steer programs differ sharply between dealers, captives and independents, and the spread on identical machines is real.
  • Ignoring prepayment terms on a machine you will trade. Skid-steers are traded more often than they are kept. Know the early payoff position before you sign.

Read the collateral description before signing and make sure every attachment you paid for is listed on it.

Why Businesses Finance Skid Steers Rather Than Pay Cash

Cash spent on equipment is cash not available for payroll, materials or the next opportunity. Financing spreads the cost over the working life of the skid steer and keeps reserves intact, and Section 179 and bonus depreciation reduce the after-tax cost further. Contractors paid on draw schedules feel it most sharply: mobilization, materials and payroll all fall due before the first progress payment arrives.

Skid Steer Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used skid steer?

Yes. Many lenders finance used skid steers, typically those 5-7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Resale value and condition affect approval.

What credit score is required?

Most lenders look for 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ qualify for the best rates. Asset-backed financing sometimes works with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does approval take?

1-5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans add 30-60+ days. Having documents ready-financials, equipment quote, business info-speeds the process.

Is leasing better than buying?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the skid steer long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals. Equipment loan vs lease comparison.

Can startups finance skid steers?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1-2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer with strong credit. SBA loans and some specialized programs can help newer businesses. Apply and we'll match you with lenders that fit your profile.

What documents are needed for skid steer financing?

Typical requirements: 3-6 months of business bank statements, tax returns (business and personal if required), a recent profit and loss statement, equipment quote from your dealer, and business formation documents. Having these ready speeds approval. What lenders look at.

Can I finance skid steer attachments?

Yes. Many lenders include attachments (buckets, forks, augers, etc.) in the same financing package as the skid steer. Get a combined quote from your dealer and include it in your application. Bundling can simplify the process and sometimes improve terms.
