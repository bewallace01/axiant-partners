What Is a Refrigerated Truck?

A refrigerated truck (reefer) is a commercial vehicle with an insulated, temperature-controlled cargo area. Reefer units use refrigeration or freezer systems to maintain specific temperature ranges-from chilled (32-40-F) to frozen (0-F or below). Configurations include straight trucks (box truck with reefer body), tractor-trailer combinations (tractor + reefer trailer), and smaller delivery vans with refrigerated cargo.

Refrigerated trucks are used in food distribution, grocery delivery, pharmaceutical transport, and cold-chain logistics. Industries served include restaurants, retail, agriculture, and healthcare. Understanding what reefers are and how they're used helps lenders assess your application-they want to see that the equipment supports revenue-generating cold-chain operations.

Refrigerated Truck Financing Options

Several financing structures work for refrigerated trucks and reefer trailers. Choose based on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the equipment at the end of the term.

Equipment Loans

Borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the reefer when the loan is paid off. Typically 0-20% down, terms 24-84 months. Interest rates 6-15% depending on credit. Ideal if you plan to use the truck long-term. See typical rates.

Equipment Leasing

Lower monthly payments than loans. TRAC leases use a residual to reduce the financed amount. At lease end, return the truck, purchase at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Loan vs lease.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans offer longer terms (7-10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically 30-60+ days. Best for established food distribution or logistics businesses planning fleet expansion. View SBA loans.

Working capital loans are better suited for fuel, payroll, and operating expenses. Use working capital for day-to-day needs; use equipment financing for the reefer itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset. Compare equipment loan vs lease in detail.

How Much Does a Refrigerated Truck Cost?

Refrigerated truck prices vary by configuration (straight truck vs trailer), refrigeration capacity, and whether you buy new or used. New refrigerated straight trucks range from roughly $60,000-$120,000 for smaller units to $120,000-$180,000 for larger box trucks with advanced refrigeration. Reefer trailers (53') typically cost $25,000-$80,000 new depending on insulation, refrigeration unit (carrier, thermo king), and specs.

Used reefers typically cost 30-50% less. A 5-year-old reefer trailer might run $15,000-$50,000. Refrigeration unit age and condition are critical-repairs are expensive. Many lenders finance used reefers up to 5-7 years old. Inspect the refrigeration unit and body integrity before purchasing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer-lenders use it to structure financing. Financing used equipment guide.

What to Have Ready Before You Apply

Gathering documentation before you start speeds approval. Have these ready when applying for refrigerated truck financing:

  • 3-6 months of business bank statements - Lenders want to see consistent deposits and cash flow.
  • Last year's tax returns - Business and personal if required by the lender.
  • Recent profit and loss statement - Shows revenue and expenses.
  • Equipment quote or proposal - Written quote from your dealer with make, model, VIN, and total cost. Include refrigeration specs if applicable.
  • Business formation documents - Articles of incorporation, operating agreement, or similar.
  • Basic business information - EIN, formation date, ownership structure.

Lenders may ask for a voided check for ACH. The more organized your documentation, the faster you'll get funded. What do lenders look at when approving equipment financing.

Tips to Get Approved for Refrigerated Truck Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors on your report, and avoid new credit applications before applying.

  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Financing a Refrigerated Truck

Avoid these when financing refrigerated trucks:

  • Skipping the equipment quote. Lenders need it to structure the loan. Get a written quote from your dealer before applying.
  • Applying with incomplete financials. Missing bank statements or tax returns causes delays. Gather everything upfront.
  • Ignoring refrigeration unit condition. For used reefers, unit age and repair history affect value and approval.
  • Waiting until the last minute. Rush approvals may limit your options. Apply early when you have time to compare offers.
  • Focusing on rate alone. Terms, fees, prepayment penalties, and flexibility matter. Red flags in equipment finance agreements.
