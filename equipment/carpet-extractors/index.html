What Is a Carpet Extractor?

A carpet extractor is a hot-water extraction machine that injects cleaning solution into carpet fibers under pressure, agitates, then vacuums the dirty solution back out in a single pass. The hot-water extraction method (sometimes called steam cleaning, though no actual steam is used) is the cleaning standard endorsed by every major carpet manufacturer for warranty maintenance and is the IICRC's recommended approach for both routine and restoration work.

The asset class splits cleanly into two categories. Portables are wheeled units that roll into the building, plug into 120V outlets, and run from inside the structure. They suit small commercial spaces, residential routes, and operators who park a single van and walk equipment in. Brands like Mytee, Hydramaster, and Sandia dominate the portable market, and a typical entry portable runs $1,500 to $3,500 with high-flow restoration portables hitting $8,000. Truck-mount extractors are van-installed systems that stay mounted in the vehicle and pump hot water and vacuum through 100–200 feet of hose into the building. Truck-mounts heat water with the van's engine or a dedicated heat exchanger, clean carpet 3 to 5 times faster than portables, and dry the fiber significantly quicker because of higher water temperature and vacuum lift. Hydramaster, Prochem, and Butler are the dominant truck-mount brands.

The buyers split similarly. Carpet cleaning route operators, residential cleaners, and small commercial janitors typically run portables. Restoration contractors handling water damage and full-service commercial carpet cleaners run truck-mounts because the daily volume justifies the equipment cost. Some operators run both: portables for small jobs and difficult-access buildings, truck-mounts for everything else.

How Do You Finance a Carpet Extractor?

The right financing depends on which side of the portable/truck-mount line you sit on. A $3,500 portable purchase rarely warrants a separate financing deal — many operators charge it to a business credit card or fold it into a working capital advance. Once you cross $10,000, dedicated equipment financing makes more sense because the rate beats unsecured credit and the asset itself collateralizes the loan. Equipment financing through specialty lenders is the default tool for portables in the $5,000 to $10,000 band and for nearly all truck-mounts.

Truck-mounts have a financing wrinkle worth flagging: the extractor and the van are different assets, often sold separately, and the same lender may not finance both. Two patterns work. The first is buying the van separately (used or new) through a commercial vehicle program and financing the truck-mount on its own equipment loan. The second is bundling van plus truck-mount with a single lender that handles both. Bundled deals close faster but require both assets be quoted together and a single lender willing to write the package. Restoration contractors often add SBA 7(a) financing when launching a new branch with multiple trucks — bundle vans, truck-mounts, dehumidifiers, air movers, and 6 months of working capital into one $250K+ SBA loan.

What drives price: vacuum lift (measured in inches of water lift), solution heat (cold, heated, or thermo-bonded), flow rate (gallons per minute), and blower size. A truck-mount with 250 CFM and 230°F water cleans differently from a 110 CFM portable with cold water. Brand premium runs 20 to 35 percent on Hydramaster and Prochem versus second-tier brands. For truck-mounts, factor in van prep cost ($1,500 to $4,000 for a custom shelf install, fuel line tap, and electrical) and dual-wand kits ($1,500 to $3,000) when deciding what gets financed. Restoration-focused buyers should also budget air movers and dehumidifiers separately rather than rolling them into the extractor loan.

Financing Options for Carpet Extractors

Equipment Financing (Loans)

Standard equipment loans cover both portable and truck-mount purchases. The asset is collateral, terms run 36 to 60 months for portables and 48 to 72 months for truck-mounts. Down payment is typically 0–10% with 600+ FICO; expect 15–20% down on used truck-mounts older than 5 years. APR sits at 8–15% for established operators (2+ years) and 10–18% for newer businesses. The loan books on your balance sheet, you take Section 179 in year one, and the machine is unencumbered at payoff.

Equipment Lease

Operating leases work especially well on truck-mounts because the technology cycles — thermo heat, dual wand, lithium-managed power, and electronic controls all reset every 4 to 6 years. A 48-month operating lease lets you upgrade at end-of-term without absorbing residual loss. Capital leases ($1-buyout) are economically similar to equipment loans but stretch terms further. Lease payments run 15 to 25 percent below an equivalent loan because you only finance the depreciation slice.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) financing fits restoration startups and existing carpet cleaners launching new locations. Bundle van, truck-mount, restoration drying equipment, and 6 months of working capital into a single SBA loan with 10-year terms and 10–20% down. Approval is 30 to 60 days, so plan ahead. SBA pricing typically runs prime + 2.75–4.75 with monthly amortization. Most SBA lenders want 680+ FICO, 2+ years of business operating history (or strong personal industry experience for startups), and a clear contract pipeline.

Working Capital

Working capital makes sense when buying multiple portables for a route expansion or covering startup costs that don't fit into equipment financing — certifications, vehicle wraps, marketing, and crew uniforms. Working capital advances close in 1 to 3 days but cost more than equipment financing, so use it for non-asset spend rather than the extractor itself.

Credit Score & Approval

Carpet extractor approvals tier the same way as most equipment. 550–599 FICO works for portables under $10K through asset-backed specialty lenders; expect 15–20% down and rates 14–20%. 600–649 opens standard equipment financing for both portables and truck-mounts at 0–10% down. 650–679 nets better APR (typically 9–12%) and unlocks van+truck-mount bundled programs. 680+ qualifies for SBA pricing and lowest rates across all carpet equipment classes. Restoration contractors with IICRC certifications often see slightly better approvals because lenders read the credential as evidence of operational competence.

Documentation scales with deal size. Application plus 3 months of bank statements closes most portable deals under $10K in 24 to 48 hours. Truck-mounts over $25K add a year of tax returns, a current profit and loss, and the dealer's installation quote. Bundled van+truck-mount deals add the van VIN and any vehicle history. Most cleaning operators in operation 12 months can finance a truck-mount; startups in their first 6 months may need 20% down or a co-signer. Cleaning business financing programs specifically rate carpet cleaners and restoration contractors as separate risk classes from general janitorial.
