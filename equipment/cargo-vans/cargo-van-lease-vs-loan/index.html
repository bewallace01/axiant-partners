Cargo Van Lease vs Loan: Payments, Lease to Own and Upfit

Which structure fits a delivery or trades van, what lease to own really means, and what happens to your racks and wrap at the end

← Back to Cargo Van Financing

Updated November 07, 2026

Quick answer

A loan fits a van you'll keep and upfit, while a fair market value lease fits a van you'll replace on a schedule. On a $60,000 van at an illustrative 9% over 60 months, a loan runs about $1,246 a month and a lease with a 25% residual about $1,047. Lease to own costs about the same as the loan. Axiant Partners, a business-loan broker in Boca Raton, compares both. Typical van rates run 6-15%, so these are examples, not quotes.

Loan, Lease or Lease to Own

These three get mixed up in ads. The difference is who carries the van's value at the end of the term. All three are for business use.

Equipment loanFMV leaseLease to own
Who owns itYou, from the startThe lessorYou, at the end for a nominal price
Monthly paymentHighest of the three, or equalLowestAbout like the loan
End of termKeep it, no balanceReturn, renew or buy at market valueKeep it
UpfitYours to changeMay need consent or removalUsually your choice
Miles and wearNo lease limitsLimits and return chargesCheck the contract
Best fitKeep for yearsReplace on a cycleOwn it, with lease paperwork

Vans on our cargo van financing page commonly run $35,000-$75,000 before upfit, with typical rates of 6-15%, terms of 24-72 months and 0-20% down, depending on credit and age.

Worked Example: A $60,000 Van on Each Structure

Illustrative, not a quote. Assume a $60,000 van, nothing down, a 9% rate on each structure and 60 months. The lease assumes the van is worth 25% of its price ($15,000) at the end, which is an assumption, not a market figure.

StructureMonthlyPaid over 60 monthsTo keep the van
Equipment loanAbout $1,246About $74,730Nothing more
FMV lease, 25% residualAbout $1,047About $62,800About $15,000 more
Lease to own, $1 buyoutAbout $1,245About $74,730$1

The lease saves about $199 a month, or 16%, which sits inside the 10-20% we describe in equipment leasing vs a loan. If you buy the van at the end at that price, you've paid about $77,800, which is about $3,070 more than the loan. The lease pays off if you hand the keys back and the return is clean, or if the van is worth less than the residual and that's the lessor's problem.

What Lease to Own Really Means

“Cargo van lease to own” generally describes a lease with a small purchase option at the end, such as $1. Legally and economically that's close to a loan. Under UCC 1-203, a lease you can't cancel, with an option to own for nominal extra payment, is generally treated as a security interest, not a true lease. A fair market value option generally isn't. So:

  • The payment won't be much lower. You're paying for the whole van, as the table shows.
  • You usually can't walk away early. Read the early-termination language before you sign.
  • Ask how it's taxed. A lease treated as a purchase can follow purchase rules, so have your CPA check.
  • Be careful with weekly-pay offers. Some rent-to-own programs price high and take the van after a missed payment. Ask for the total of payments in writing.

Upfit Is Where Vans Differ

A van is often not finished when it leaves the lot. Racks, partitions, refrigeration, ladder racks and wraps can add real money, and they change the answer:

IssueWith a loanWith an FMV lease
Financing the upfitOften can be included if on the invoiceAsk, since it may not add resale value
Drilling and wiringYour decisionMay need lessor consent
RefrigerationKeep it and move it laterPlan who owns it at return
Vinyl wrapRemove it if you sellRemoval and paint damage can be billed
Moving racks to the next vanYours to moveCheck for repair charges on holes

If the van earns money because of what's built into it, such as a mobile workshop or a refrigerated route, that usually favors a loan. A plain high-roof van on a courier route that you swap on a schedule is where an FMV lease works best. See the Sprinter van financing guide for high-roof examples.

Miles, Contracts and Return Charges

Route work is hard on leases. Before signing, get these in writing:

  • Mileage allowance. The yearly limit and the charge per mile over it. Estimate from your actual route logs.
  • Wear standard. Dents, floors and door damage are normal on a delivery van. Find out what's billable.
  • Contract match. A 60-month lease on a 24-month delivery contract leaves you with payments after the work ends, so match the term or plan for a loan you can sell out of.
  • End options. Whether the buyout is a set price or market value at the time.

A loan avoids those charges, but you take the resale risk. Used vans commonly trade 25-45% below comparable new prices, depending on age, miles and wear, so check whether you might owe more than a van is worth if you sell early. See financing used equipment for older vans.

Taxes: One Point Specific to Vans

Heavy SUVs have a lower Section 179 cap, but vans built for cargo may not fall under it. IRS Publication 946 (2025 edition) excludes from that limit a vehicle with an integral enclosure around the driver and load area, no seating behind the driver's seat and a short nose, so a cargo van may qualify differently from a passenger van. On an operating lease you'd generally deduct the payments instead. Rules change and they depend on your facts, so ask your CPA before choosing a structure for the deduction.

How to Decide

  1. Estimate how long you'll keep it. For most of its useful life, a loan usually wins.
  2. Total the upfit. More custom work leans toward owning.
  3. Check your miles. High-mileage routes make lease limits costly.
  4. Compare totals. Add down payment, all payments, buyout and likely return charges.
  5. Get both quoted. Use one invoice with the VIN and the upfit listed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to lease or finance a cargo van?

Finance it if you'll keep it for years or add custom upfit. Lease it if you replace vans on a schedule, stay inside the mileage limit and want a lower payment. In our $60,000 example, the lease saves about $199 a month, but costs about $3,070 more if you buy the van at the end.

What is a cargo van lease to own?

It's generally a lease with a nominal purchase option at the end, such as $1. The payment is close to a loan's, and the contract may be treated as a purchase rather than a true lease.

How much is a cargo van lease per month?

It depends on price, rate, term and residual. In one illustration, a $60,000 van at 9% over 60 months with a 25% residual is about $1,047 a month. That's an example, not a quote.

Can I lease a used cargo van?

Some lessors do, though age and miles limit the term. Many lenders finance used vans up to 5-7 model years; older vans may need more down or a shorter term.

Can a lease include racks and refrigeration?

Sometimes. Put the upfit on the invoice and ask how it's treated. Also ask who owns it at the end and what removal may cost.

What credit do I need to lease a cargo van?

Requirements are similar to a loan: most look for about 600 or higher, with the best terms from about 680. Newer businesses may need more down or a cosigner.
