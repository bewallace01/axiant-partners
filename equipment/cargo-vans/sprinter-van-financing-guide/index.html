What Lenders Want to See on a Business Sprinter Deal

A Sprinter is easy collateral, so most of the file is about the business that will run it:

  • Use: routes, a delivery or service contract, or invoiced trade work the van supports.
  • Registration: titled to the business entity that borrows, not to an owner personally.
  • Deposits: three months of business bank statements showing room for the payment.
  • The exact van: VIN, wheelbase, roof height, drivetrain, and the upfit specification.
  • Credit: commonly 600+, with the best rates at 680+; see credit needed for equipment financing.

Worked Example: A High-Roof Van With a Refrigeration Upfit

Illustrative, not a quote. A catering company buys a $68,000 high-roof van and a $17,000 refrigeration and shelving upfit on the same invoice, $85,000 in all. It puts 10% ($8,500) down and finances $76,500 at 8% over 72 months.

  • Monthly payment: about $1,341
  • Total interest over the term: about $20,073
  • Funding: the lender pays the dealer for the van and the upfitter on completion, so the term starts when the van can work

Financed separately, the upfit would typically be a second, smaller deal on a shorter term and a higher rate.

Loan, Lease or SBA for a Sprinter

  • Equipment loan or $1 buyout lease: you own the van and the upfit; best when you keep vans past the term.
  • TRAC lease: common on titled vehicles; a set residual lowers the payment. See how TRAC leases work.
  • Fair market value lease: lowest payment for operators who turn vans over on a contract cycle.
  • SBA 7(a): slower, but can fund several vans as part of a larger expansion (SBA).

Purchased vans are generally depreciable business property; some may qualify for Section 179 (IRS Publication 946). Confirm with your tax adviser.

What to Have Ready Before You Apply

Sprinter-class vans sit above standard cargo vans in price and below box trucks in capability, and the documentation reflects that middle position.

Quote with the VIN and the exact configuration

· Wheelbase, roof height, drivetrain and whether it is a cargo, crew or cutaway chassis. These are not minor variations in value.

Upfit specification and cost

· Sprinters are upfitted more often than any other van — shelving, refrigeration, mobile workshops, passenger conversions. The upfit belongs on the same invoice as the van.

Odometer reading and maintenance records

· Diesel Sprinters run long distances and service history matters more than on a petrol van.

Three months of business bank statements

· The standard package for an established operator.

Entity documents and EIN

· The registered owner and the borrower must be the same entity.

Commercial auto insurance covering the upfit value

· A converted van is worth considerably more than a base van, and the policy should say so.

If the van is being converted after purchase by a third-party upfitter, tell the lender at application. Some programs will hold funds for the upfit stage; arranging that afterwards is harder.

When to Apply for Sprinter Van Financing

Apply before ordering rather than after, particularly for a configuration that has to be built. Sprinter lead times have been long enough in recent years that an order placed without financing in place can arrive into a very different credit position than the one you started in.

If the van is going straight to an upfitter, plan the financing around two dates rather than one: the chassis delivery and the completed conversion. Operators who treat it as a single event are the ones paying for a van that cannot yet earn.

Tips to Get Approved for Sprinter Van Financing

Decide the roof height by what you do inside the van.

Standing height changes the working day for a service technician. It also changes resale, because the buyers who want a high roof will not consider a low one.

Finance the conversion with the chassis.

A refrigeration unit or a full workshop fit-out is a large second cost. On one invoice it is one payment at one rate.

Check the emissions and aftertreatment history on a used diesel.

This is where the expensive faults live on higher-mileage vans. Records are worth asking for twice.

Consider the crew configuration if you carry both people and tools.

It is cheaper than running a second vehicle and it is financed as one asset.

Get more than one quote on the finance as well as the van.

The spread between programs on the same vehicle is real, and it is easiest to see when you ask several at once.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Financing a Sprinter Van

Sprinter buyers tend to get the vehicle right and the transaction wrong:

Buying the chassis and the upfit as separate transactions.

Two applications, two payments, and the second one usually on worse terms.

Ignoring service intervals and specialist labor.

Sprinter servicing is not the same cost as a domestic van's. Budget it before it arrives.

Choosing a configuration on price alone.

The cheapest van on the lot is cheapest because fewer buyers want that configuration — which is also true when you sell it.

Skipping the inspection on an ex-fleet or ex-rental unit.

High-mileage Sprinters can be sound or exhausted, and the odometer alone does not tell you which.

Not confirming the insurance covers the converted value.

Insuring a fitted-out van at base value leaves a gap you find only after a loss.

Confirm the title and lienholder process for your state before funding day, and ask what the lender requires if the van is later re-upfitted.

Sprinter Van Financing FAQ

Can a business finance a Sprinter van?

Yes. Sprinter-class vans are titled commercial vehicles with a broad resale market, so equipment lenders commonly finance them over 36-72 months with 0-20% down when the van is registered to the business and used in it.

How much is a Sprinter van payment?

At a typical 8%, a $70,000 van is about $1,419 a month over 60 months, and $85,000 with an upfit is about $1,490 over 72 months. These are illustrations, not quotes.

Can the upfit be financed with the Sprinter van?

Usually, if the shelving, refrigeration or workshop build is on the same invoice or the lender agrees to fund the upfitter directly. Arranging it after the van is funded is harder.

Can you finance a used Sprinter van?

Yes. Many lenders commonly finance used commercial vans about 5-7 model years old or newer. Mileage, service history and the diesel aftertreatment record affect the terms.

Do I need a USDOT number for a Sprinter van?

If the van is rated at 10,001 lb or more and used in interstate commerce, it is a commercial motor vehicle under 49 CFR 390.5, which brings FMCSA registration. Many Sprinter 3500 configurations cross that line; check the door-jamb rating.

Is it better to lease or buy a Sprinter van for a business?

Lease if you replace vans on a short cycle or run high miles under a contract; buy, or use a TRAC or $1 buyout lease, if you keep vans and the upfit for years.
