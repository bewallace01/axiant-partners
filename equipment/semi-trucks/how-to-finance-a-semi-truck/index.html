How to Finance a Semi Truck

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for freight and trucking businesses

← Back to Equipment by Type

Last updated: March 5, 2026

Semi trucks cost $130,000–$270,000+ new and $10,000–$100,000+ used. Most freight and trucking businesses finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to hauling revenue.

What Is a Semi Truck?

A semi truck (also called a tractor, semi-tractor, or big rig) is a tractor unit designed to haul semi-trailers. It consists of a powered cab and chassis that connects to a trailer via a fifth wheel. Semi trucks come in day cab configurations (no sleeper) for regional or local hauling, or sleeper cabs for long-haul freight. They are the backbone of freight transportation, used by trucking companies, owner-operators, and logistics firms to move goods across the country. Engine power, fuel type (diesel, natural gas), and cab configuration affect suitability for different hauling needs.

How Do You Finance a Semi Truck?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Semi trucks are essential for freight and trucking, so financing is widely available for established carriers.

How Much Does a Semi Truck Cost?

Semi truck prices vary by brand, configuration (day cab vs sleeper), engine, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing and set realistic down payment and monthly payment expectations.

New Semi Truck Prices

New semi trucks range from roughly $130,000–$180,000 for day cab models (regional hauling) to $180,000–$230,000 for mid-roof sleeper cabs, and $230,000–$270,000+ for premium sleeper cabs with advanced technology and fuel-efficient engines. Top brands like Freightliner, Peterbilt, Kenworth, Volvo, and International command premium prices. Engine size, transmission type, and cab amenities add cost. Factor in trade-in value and dealer incentives when budgeting.

Used Semi Truck Prices

Used semi trucks typically range from $10,000–$100,000+ depending on age, mileage, and condition. A 5-year-old sleeper might run $80,000–$120,000. Older or high-mileage trucks can start around $10,000–$40,000. Many lenders finance used trucks up to 5–7 years old; older trucks may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Always get a pre-purchase inspection and check maintenance records before buying used.

Factors Affecting Price

Brand, cab type (day vs sleeper), engine horsepower, mileage (for used), emissions compliance, and regional demand all affect semi truck pricing. Auction and dealer prices can differ. Obtain a written quote from your dealer or seller—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Semi Trucks

Several financing structures work for semi trucks. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the truck at the end of the term. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the semi truck when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 24–72 months (sometimes longer for trucks). Interest rates typically range from 6–15% depending on credit and lender. Because semi trucks are essential to freight operations, lenders often offer competitive terms. Ideal if you plan to use the truck long-term and want to build equity.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans because you're paying for use, not full ownership. At lease end, you may return the truck, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade to a newer model. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses for tax purposes; capital leases function like loans. Leasing suits trucking businesses that want to preserve cash flow, avoid downtime with newer equipment, or prefer off-balance-sheet treatment. Semi trucks with strong residual values often qualify for attractive lease terms.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans can finance semi trucks with longer terms (7–10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. SBA loans suit established trucking businesses planning fleet expansion or combining equipment with working capital. Requirements include 2+ years in business (typically), solid credit, and adequate cash flow. If timing allows, SBA financing can reduce monthly payments significantly.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are flexible but generally carry higher rates and shorter terms. They're better suited for fuel, repairs, and payroll than large equipment purchases. Use working capital for operating expenses; use equipment financing for the semi truck itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Interest rates for semi truck financing typically range from 6–15% for equipment loans and leases, depending on credit, down payment, term length, and lender. Monthly payments depend on loan amount, rate, and term. An $180,000 semi truck financed at 8% over 60 months would result in roughly $3,600/month. Step-by-Step: How to Finance a Semi Truck

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose the semi truck that fits your hauling needs—day cab or sleeper, brand, engine. Get a written quote from the dealer or seller including any trade-in.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and a balance sheet. Have your equipment quote and business info ready.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple equipment lenders. Consider rates, terms, down payment requirements, and approval speed. Axiant Partners matches businesses with lenders experienced in trucking equipment and logistics equipment.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation. Most applications can be submitted online.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer. Funds typically go directly to the seller; you take possession of the semi truck.

Tips to Get Approved for Semi Truck Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors on your report, and avoid new credit applications before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements, consistent deposits, freight contracts, and organized financials speed approval and improve terms.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used semi truck may cost significantly less, improving approval odds and cash flow. Get a pre-purchase inspection and check maintenance records.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate or improve approval when credit is borderline.
  • Choose equipment with strong resale value. Major brands and popular configurations hold value better, making lenders more comfortable.

Semi Truck Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used semi truck?

Yes. Many lenders finance used semi trucks, typically those 5–7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Mileage, condition, and maintenance records affect approval. Get a pre-purchase inspection before buying.

What credit score is required for semi truck financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Because semi truck financing is asset-backed, some programs work with 580+ when revenue, down payment, and time in business are strong.

How long does semi truck financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready—financials, equipment quote, business info—speeds the process. Apply early if you need a truck by a specific date.

Can startups finance semi trucks?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1–2+ years in business. New trucking businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer with strong credit. SBA loans and some specialized programs can help newer businesses.

Is leasing better than buying a semi truck?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the truck long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals.
