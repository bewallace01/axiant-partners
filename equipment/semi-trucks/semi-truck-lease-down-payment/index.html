How Much Down Payment Do You Need to Lease a Semi Truck?

What a lease really asks for up front, why it is usually less than a loan, and the cost you still carry at the end

← Back to Semi Truck Financing Articles

Updated November 04, 2026

Quick answer

Leasing a semi truck commonly takes about 0-10% down, plus the first payment, any deposit and documentation fees. Stronger files on newer trucks land at the low end. New authorities, weaker credit and older trucks often pay more. On a $150,000 truck, 10% is $15,000. Axiant Partners, a business-loan broker in Boca Raton, sees leases usually asking for less up front than a loan, because the lessor keeps a residual. These are typical ranges across lenders, not a quote.

Up-Front Cost by Borrower

Your fileTypical lease down payment
Strong, established fileAbout 0-5%, sometimes first payment only
Owner-operator, 2+ yearsAbout 5-10%
New or weaker file10% or more, if approved

A strong file means established, good credit and a newer truck. A new or weaker file means a new authority, weak credit or an older truck. These bands are conventional, not anyone's quote, and programs differ. Treat "$0 down" advertising with care: it usually means no down payment percentage, not no cash at signing.

What You Actually Pay at Signing

The down payment is only one line. Ask for all of these in writing:

  • Down payment or capital reduction, which lowers the amount financed.
  • First payment, and sometimes the last, collected up front.
  • Security deposit, and when and how you get it back.
  • Documentation and titling fees, which vary by lessor and state.
  • Insurance, since physical damage coverage is generally required before the truck is released.

Add these up to compare offers. A lower down payment with a large deposit may take as much cash on day one as a plain 10% down.

Why a Lease Usually Asks for Less Than a Loan

On a loan, the lender needs equity in the truck early in case you default. On a lease, the lessor still owns the truck and recovers part of its value through the residual at the end. That lowers the amount you finance, and usually the cash needed at signing.

Many semi truck leases are TRAC leases, which set a residual value up front and settle the difference when the truck is sold at lease end. The lower payment is real, but the residual does not disappear. If the truck sells below it, you generally pay the shortfall. For a full comparison, see semi truck lease vs loan. If you are buying, semi truck loan down payments are covered separately.

Worked Example: Same Truck, Lease vs Loan

Illustrative numbers, not a quote. A $150,000 Class 8 tractor, financed for 60 months at an assumed 8%, for an owner-operator with two years in business.

ItemTRAC leaseLoan
Down payment$15,000 (10%)$22,500 (15%)
Monthly paymentAbout $2,125About $2,585
Owed at the end$45,000 residual$0
Total if you keep itAbout $187,500About $177,600

The lease saves $7,500 at signing and about $460 a month, which is the point for a new owner-operator protecting cash. The loan costs less in total if you keep the truck, because you are not financing the $45,000 for the full term. The right choice depends on whether cash today or long-term ownership matters more to you.

What Moves Your Down Payment

  • Time in business. Under two years, and especially a new authority, usually means more cash up front.
  • Credit. Stronger personal credit generally opens the low-down programs.
  • Truck age and mileage. The residual is easier to predict on a newer truck, which is why leases favor them.
  • Revenue and contracts. Steady deposits and dedicated freight can offset a thin credit file.
  • Existing debt. Other truck notes or cash advances can push the lessor to ask for more.

Have 3-6 months of bank statements, your authority details and the truck spec ready. If credit is the obstacle, see semi truck financing with bad credit.

"Easy" Lease-Purchase Programs: Read These Closely

Searches for easy semi truck leasing often lead to carrier lease-purchase programs, which are a different deal from a lease through a finance company. The carrier may ask for little or nothing down, then take payments, escrow and charge-backs out of your settlements, and you may work only for that carrier during the lease.

Where a carrier leases your equipment, 49 CFR 376.12 requires a signed written lease that clearly specifies charge-backs and states any escrow amount. The carrier must account for escrow, pay interest on it, and return it no later than 45 days after termination. Before signing, ask:

  • Who holds title during the term, and what do you own at the end?
  • What happens to your payments if the freight dries up or you leave?
  • What is the total of all payments plus the final buyout?

Have an attorney or accountant read the agreement. The FTC has also warned small businesses about misrepresented financing costs.

How to Lower the Cash You Need

  1. Compare total cash at signing, not the advertised down payment.
  2. Look at newer trucks. A stronger residual can lower both the payment and the cash at signing.
  3. Trade in equity. A truck you own can count toward the down payment. If you own one free and clear, a truck title loan is another, costlier way to raise cash, so weigh it carefully.
  4. Show dedicated freight. A contract can help a newer business.
  5. Ask about taxes. Lease and loan payments are treated differently, and IRS Publication 946 covers depreciation on owned trucks. Check with your accountant.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much down payment do you need to lease a semi truck?

Commonly about 0-10% of the truck's price, plus the first payment, any deposit and fees. On a $150,000 truck, 10% is $15,000. New authorities, weaker credit and older trucks often pay more. These are typical ranges across lenders, not a quote.

Can you lease a semi truck with no money down?

Sometimes, usually for strong files on newer trucks, and some carrier lease-purchase programs advertise it. You should still expect the first payment and fees at signing, and low-down offers can carry higher payments or larger costs later.

Is it cheaper to lease or buy a semi truck?

A lease usually costs less at signing and per month. A loan usually costs less in total if you keep the truck, because a lease leaves a residual to pay or settle at the end.

What credit score do you need to lease a semi truck?

It varies by program, and there is no single minimum. Stronger personal credit generally opens low-down programs, while weaker credit usually means more cash up front, a higher payment, or both.

What is a TRAC lease on a semi truck?

A lease with a preset residual value and a terminal rental adjustment clause. The truck's sale value at lease end is compared with the residual, and you generally pay any shortfall or receive any excess.

Can a new owner-operator lease a semi truck?

Often, but new authorities commonly pay more up front, sometimes 10% or more, and have fewer programs to choose from. Dedicated freight, strong personal credit and driving experience all help.
