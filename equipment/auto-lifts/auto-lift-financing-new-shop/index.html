Why New Shops Need Lift Financing

Lifts are essential for brake work, suspension, exhaust, and undercarriage service. New shops face build-out, inventory, and working capital costs—financing spreads lift cost over 36-60 months, preserving cash for rent, payroll, and marketing. Most repair work requires lifting the vehicle; shops without lifts lose revenue and efficiency. Equipment is collateral, so lenders often work with new shops when credit and down payment support the deal. See equipment financing by type and equipment financing for new businesses.

Auto Lift Costs

Two-post lifts run roughly $3,000-$8,000 depending on capacity and make (BendPak, Rotary, Mohawk, etc.). Four-post lifts: $5,000-$15,000. Alignment racks: $15,000-$25,000+. Installation (anchoring, electrical) may add $500-$2,000 per lift. Multiple lifts can be financed in one package—a new shop with two two-post lifts might finance $10,000-$16,000. See diagnostic equipment financing and scan tool financing.

Financing Options: Loans vs Leases

Equipment loans spread the cost; you own at payoff and can depreciate. Leases often have lower monthly payments and full payment deductibility; at term end you can purchase or return. Lifts have long useful lives (15+ years); both structures work. New shops often prefer lower payments to preserve capital. See equipment loan vs lease. Use our calculator to model payments.

Bundling Lifts with Shop Equipment

Financing lifts with diagnostic tools, tire equipment, and tool storage in one package can simplify approval and improve terms. Lenders prefer complete shop fit-outs—it shows a viable business plan. Work with a dealer who can provide a bundled quote. See auto shop equipment package financing.

Credit and Down Payment for New Shops

Most lenders look for 600+ FICO; 680+ qualifies for the best rates. New shops may rely on personal credit; equipment is asset-backed. Down payments: 0-15% for qualified applicants. Strong personal credit, a business plan, and industry experience improve approval. See credit score for equipment financing and down payment requirements.

Two-Post vs Four-Post: What to Finance

Two-post lifts are most common for general repair—brakes, suspension, exhaust. They require less floor space. Four-post lifts suit storage, alignment, and heavier vehicles; they don't require floor anchoring. Both are routinely financed. Include make, model, capacity, and installation in your quote. Alignment racks combine a four-post lift with alignment capabilities—higher cost, higher value.

Documentation for Auto Lift Financing

Gather: equipment quote (make, model, capacity), 3-6 months personal and business bank statements, tax returns, business formation documents, business plan. For new entities: resume and industry experience. Complete documentation speeds approval. See equipment financing requirements.

Approval Timeline

Many lenders approve auto lift financing within 24-48 hours when documentation is complete. Bundled shop equipment packages often move quickly. See how fast equipment financing is approved. Get matched with lenders who finance auto lifts.

Worked example: financing a shop’s lifts

Take a new shop financing $24,000 in lifts — two two-post lifts and a four-post alignment rack — with 10% down, leaving $21,600 over 60 months. At about 10% APR the payment is roughly $459 a month, easily covered once the bays are billing. Lifts are durable, high-resale equipment, so lenders finance them readily and often let you bundle them with the rest of the shop package into a single application and payment rather than financing each item separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I finance auto lifts for a new repair shop?

Yes. Many equipment lenders work with new auto shops. Strong personal credit, down payment, and business plan improve approval.

How much does an auto lift cost?

Two-post lifts run $3,000-$8,000; four-post $5,000-$15,000; alignment racks $15,000-$25,000+. Multiple lifts can be financed together.

What credit score do I need for auto lift financing?

Most lenders look for 600+ FICO. New shops may rely on personal credit.

Two-post vs four-post: which is easier to finance?

Both are routinely financed. Include make, model, and capacity in your quote.

How fast can I get approved for auto lift financing?

Many lenders approve within 24-48 hours. Bundled shop equipment packages simplify approval.

How much does it cost to finance auto lifts?

Two-post lifts run roughly $4,000–$8,000 each and four-post and alignment racks more; financed over 48–72 months, a full set of lifts is a modest monthly payment. Bundling with other shop equipment can improve terms.

Can a new auto shop finance lifts?

Yes. Lifts are durable, high-resale collateral, so lenders finance them readily even for startups — though new shops should expect a larger down payment and may lean on the owner’s personal credit.

Is it better to lease or buy auto lifts?

Most shops buy lifts with a loan — they are durable, long-life equipment that holds value, so ownership and Section 179 usually win. Leasing can lower the upfront payment for a new shop conserving cash, with a buyout at term end.
