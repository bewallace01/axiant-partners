How to Finance a Commercial Mower

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for walk-behind and stand-on mowers

← Back to Commercial Mower Financing

Last updated: March 5, 2026

Commercial mowers—walk-behind and stand-on models—cost $3,000–$12,000+ new depending on deck size and features, and typically 25–40% less used. Most landscapers finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to seasonal mowing revenue.

What Is a Commercial Mower?

Commercial mowers include walk-behind mowers (push or self-propelled) and stand-on mowers. Walk-behind mowers handle tight spaces, residential properties, and areas where riding mowers can't go. Stand-on mowers offer a compact footprint with a standing operator position, ideal for trailer loading and maneuvering in gates and narrow spaces. Deck sizes typically range from 21 to 52 inches. Commercial-grade mowers are built for daily use with durable decks, commercial engines, and mulching or bagging options. They are essential for lawn care crews and landscapers who need versatile mowing capacity.

How Do You Finance a Commercial Mower?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Commercial mowers are common landscaping equipment, so financing is widely available through landscaping lenders.

How Much Does a Commercial Mower Cost?

Commercial mower prices vary by type (walk-behind vs stand-on), deck size, brand, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Commercial Mower Prices

New walk-behind mowers range from roughly $3,000–$6,000 for 21–36 inch decks to $6,000–$10,000+ for large commercial walk-behinds. Stand-on mowers typically cost $6,000–$12,000+ depending on deck size and features. Top brands like Scag, Exmark, Toro, and Honda add cost. Deck size, engine type, and commercial-grade components drive price.

Used Commercial Mower Prices

Used commercial mowers typically cost 25–40% less than new equivalents, depending on age, hours, and condition. A 3-year-old walk-behind might run $2,000–$4,500. Older units can start around $1,500–$3,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 5–7 years old. Always inspect deck, engine, and drive system before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Deck size, type (walk-behind vs stand-on), engine, brand, and regional demand all affect commercial mower pricing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Commercial Mowers

Several financing structures work for commercial mowers. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the mower when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 24–48 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Commercial mowers hold value reasonably well, so financing is often accessible. Ideal if you plan to use the mower long-term.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return the mower, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Leasing suits businesses that want to preserve cash flow or upgrade regularly.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) loans can finance commercial mowers with longer terms and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. Suit established landscaping businesses combining equipment with working capital.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are better suited for operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use equipment financing for the mower itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Commercial Mower Financing Rates and Terms

Interest rates for commercial mower financing typically range from 6–15% for equipment loans and leases. Terms commonly run 24–48 months for standard equipment financing; SBA loans can extend to 7–10 years. A $6,000 commercial mower financed at 8% over 36 months would result in roughly $188/month. Step-by-Step: How to Finance a Commercial Mower

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose walk-behind or stand-on based on your needs. Get a written quote from the dealer.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and equipment quote.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple equipment lenders. Axiant Partners matches landscapers with lenders experienced in landscaping equipment financing.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer.

Tips to Get Approved for Commercial Mower Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors, and avoid new credit before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements and lawn care contracts speed approval.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used commercial mower may cost significantly less. Inspect deck and engine carefully.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate.
  • Time your application. Apply before mowing season if you need the mower by a specific date.

Commercial Mower Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used commercial mower?

Yes. Many lenders finance used walk-behind and stand-on mowers, typically those 5–7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Condition and brand affect approval.

What credit score is required for commercial mower financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does commercial mower financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Walk-behind vs stand-on—does it affect financing?

Lenders finance both. Price and condition matter more than type. Stand-on mowers typically cost more and may qualify for slightly longer terms.

Is leasing better than buying a commercial mower?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the mower long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow.
