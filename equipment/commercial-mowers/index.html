Quick Answer

Yes. Walk-behind and stand-on mowers are among the easiest machines to finance: tickets are small, so many lenders approve on an application alone, commonly over 24-48 months with 0-20% down. New walk-behinds typically run $3,000-$10,000 and stand-ons $6,000-$12,000+. As an illustration, a $9,000 stand-on at 9% over 36 months is about $286 a month. Walk-Behind vs Stand-On: Prices and Payments

Price is what drives the loan, and the two mower types sit in different bands. Prices are typical new ranges by deck size and engine; get a written dealer quote for the real figure. Payments assume 9%, inside the equipment financing tiers on our business loan rates page.

Walk-behinds suit gated yards, slopes and tight residential lots. Stand-ons cost more but take less trailer space and let an operator step off quickly, which is why many crews replace walk-behinds with them as routes grow. Lenders treat the two the same way; the ticket size and the machine's age matter more than the type.

Financing Lawn Care Equipment Beyond the Mower

Searches for "lawn care equipment financing" usually mean more than one machine. Trimmers, blowers and edgers are too small for most lenders to finance alone, so they ride on something bigger:

  • On the mower contract. Many dealers will put hand-held tools on the same invoice and agreement.
  • With the trailer. An enclosed or open trailer is its own titled asset; see landscape trailer financing.
  • As a crew package. Mowers, trailer and tools on one agreement, sometimes with seasonal payments; see commercial mower and lawn equipment financing.
  • From working capital. A business card or line of credit for small tools and parts; see lawn care business loans.

Used Walk-Behinds and Stand-Ons

Used commercial mowers can be financed, most easily from a dealer. Lenders commonly cap the age of used equipment and ask for the hour meter reading, so a low-hour, two-season-old stand-on gets better terms than a tired private-party machine. Before you buy, check:

  1. Engine hours and any service records.
  2. The deck for cracks, rust-through and spindle wear.
  3. Drive system: hydro pumps and wheel motors are the expensive repairs.
  4. A bill of sale with the serial number, so the lender can file its lien on the right machine.

Private sales are harder to finance because no dealer stands behind the price. See can you finance used equipment.

Lawn care revenue is seasonal, and underwriters know it. Bank statements that show a strong spring and summer, and customer contracts for commercial properties, help explain a slow winter.

California and Battery Mowers

Many walk-behinds and stand-ons use engines at or under about 25 horsepower, the size California's Air Resources Board regulates under its small off-road engine (SORE) program. If you work in California, confirm with the dealer which gas and battery models can be sold there before you finance one. Battery mowers cost more up front and finance the same way; budget for spare batteries and charging on the trailer.

How to Apply

  1. Price the exact machine. Get a written quote with model, deck size, engine and any promotion the dealer offers.
  2. Decide what goes on the contract. Mower only, or mower plus trailer and hand-held tools on one agreement.
  3. Apply once. Bank statements and the quote are usually enough at this size; larger packages may need tax returns.
  4. Compare the offers. Weigh the dealer promotion against outside offers on total cost, not just the payment.

Commercial Mower Financing FAQ

Can you finance a commercial lawn mower?

Yes. Walk-behind and stand-on mowers are commonly financed with equipment loans, leases or dealer programs over 24-48 months, often on an application alone because the amounts are small.

What is the monthly payment on a commercial mower?

As an illustration at 9% over 36 months: about $127 on a $4,000 walk-behind, $254 on an $8,000 walk-behind and $286 on a $9,000 stand-on. Rate, term and down payment set the real number.

Is 0% dealer financing better than a loan?

Often, if you qualify and want that exact new mower. Promotions are usually limited to new units, set terms and strong credit, and some trade a cash discount for the 0%. Compare the cash price with the promotion before you sign.

Can I finance trimmers, blowers and other lawn care equipment?

Usually by adding them to the mower contract or financing them with a trailer or crew package, because hand-held tools are too small to finance alone. A business credit card or line of credit can also cover them.

Can you finance a used walk-behind or stand-on mower?

Yes, especially from a dealer. Lenders commonly limit the age of used equipment and look at engine hours and deck condition, so expect shorter terms than on new.

What credit score do I need to finance a commercial mower?

Many lenders look for 600 or higher, with the best rates commonly at 680+. Smaller tickets are easier, and a larger down payment can offset weaker credit.
