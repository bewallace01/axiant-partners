Why Machine Shops Finance CNC Equipment

CNC machines increase capacity, precision, and throughput. Financing spreads the cost over 60–84 months. Leasing is common—technology evolves, and upgrade flexibility matters. See equipment financing by type.

CNC Machine Costs

CNC mills and lathes run roughly $50,000–$500,000+. VMCs $75K–$300K; horizontals $150K–$500K+; lathes $50K–$250K+. Get a written quote from your machine tool dealer. See equipment financing requirements.

Financing Options: Loans vs Leases

Equipment loans spread the cost over 60–84 months; you own at payoff and can depreciate. Leases often have lower monthly payments and full payment deductibility; at term end you can purchase or upgrade. Many shops lease for cash flow and technology refresh—CNC controls and tooling evolve. See benefits of equipment leasing. Use our calculator to model payments.

Credit and Down Payment Requirements

Most lenders look for 600+ FICO; 680+ qualifies for the best rates. CNC machines are substantial collateral; machine shop revenue and backlog improve approval. Down payments: 0–20% depending on credit and ticket size. See credit score for equipment financing and down payment requirements.

Documentation for CNC Machine Financing

Gather: equipment quote (make, model, specifications), 3–6 months bank statements, tax returns, P&L, formation documents, machine shop revenue and backlog. Larger tickets ($200K+) may require additional financial documentation. Complete documentation speeds approval. See equipment financing requirements.

Approval Timeline and Multi-Machine Packages

Many lenders approve CNC machine financing within 24–48 hours for standard transactions. Larger tickets or multi-machine packages may require additional underwriting. Bundling a VMC with a lathe or automation can simplify approval. See approval timeline. Get matched with lenders who finance CNC equipment.
