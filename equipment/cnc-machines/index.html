Worked Example: A Five-Axis Machine for a New Contract

Illustrative, not a quote. A shop wins a multi-year aerospace bracket job and orders a $275,000 five-axis machining center, including probing and installation. It puts 10% ($27,500) down and finances $247,500 at 8% over 84 months.

  • Monthly payment: about $3,858
  • Total interest over the term: about $76,537
  • At an assumed $120 shop rate, about 32 billed spindle hours a month cover the payment

That last line is the one a lender wants to see: the customer order, the hours the job needs and the margin left after the payment. Bring the purchase order or contract with the application.

Deposits, Lead Times and Interim Funding

New machining centers are often built to order, and builders and dealers commonly ask for a deposit when the order is placed. Paying that from cash can drain the shop months before the machine earns anything.

  • Interim or progress funding: some equipment lenders pay the deposit and progress payments, then start the regular term when the machine is installed.
  • Acceptance: the term usually starts when you sign a delivery and acceptance certificate. Don’t sign it until the machine has run off a part.
  • Interim charges: ask whether interest or rent accrues between funding and acceptance, and how it is billed.
  • Installation: rigging, power and guarding are part of being ready to run. Guarding has to meet OSHA 1910.212.

Automation and Add-Ons on the Same Deal

Bar feeders, pallet changers, tool presetters, probing and robot loading cells raise output per shift, and they can usually go on the same approval if they are on the same quote. A robot cell bought later is a separate deal; see industrial robot financing.

Tooling, workholding and CAM software are soft costs that many lenders cap as a share of the deal; how to quote them is covered under tooling, rigging and power.

How Lenders Underwrite a CNC Deal

  • Backlog and customers: purchase orders and how much of your revenue one customer is.
  • Utilization: whether the machines you have are full, which is the case for adding one.
  • The machine’s resale market: widely sold brands and current controls hold value better as collateral.
  • Used machines: many lenders commonly finance machining centers up to roughly 7-10 years old, and ask for the control generation, spindle hours and any crash history.
  • The usual file: 3-6 months of bank statements, the quote, and tax returns or financials on larger deals.

Credit commonly starts around 600, with the best terms at 680+. See credit needed for equipment financing.

Lease Structures and Longer Terms

  • Fair market value lease: lowest payment; return or upgrade at the end. Fits a shop that refreshes five-axis capacity on a cycle.
  • $1 buyout lease or loan: you own the machine at the end. Fits a horizontal or turning center you will run for a decade.
  • SBA 504: a 10-year fixed-rate debenture for long-life machinery (SBA); see the 504 amortization schedule.

Purchased machines and $1 buyout leases are generally depreciable, and some may qualify for Section 179 (IRS Publication 946); confirm with your tax adviser. More on the choice: equipment loan vs lease.

Steps to Finance a CNC Machine

  1. Quote the full package: machine, options, automation, rigging and installation, listed separately.
  2. Gather the case: the order or backlog the machine serves, plus bank statements.
  3. Ask about the deposit and interim funding before you place the order.
  4. Compare lease and loan offers on total cost, not only payment.
  5. Sign acceptance after runoff, not at delivery.

CNC Machine Financing FAQ

How much does it cost to finance a CNC machine?

It depends on the price, rate and term. At a typical 8%, a $150,000 machining center is about $2,338 a month over 84 months, and a $300,000 five-axis machine about $4,676. These are illustrations, not quotes.

Can you finance the deposit on a CNC machine before it is delivered?

Often, yes. Some equipment lenders fund the builder's deposit or progress payments under an interim agreement and start the term when the machine is installed and accepted. Ask whether interim interest is charged.

Can you finance a used CNC machine?

Yes. Many lenders commonly finance used machining centers up to roughly 7-10 years old. The control generation, spindle hours and crash history matter more than the year.

What credit score do you need for CNC machine financing?

Most lenders look for about 600 or higher, with the best terms commonly at 680+. Backlog, customer contracts and a larger down payment can offset a lower score.

Is it better to lease or buy a CNC machine?

A fair market value lease suits a shop that expects to upgrade within the term. A loan or $1 buyout lease suits a machine you will run for a decade. Compare the total cost of each.

Can a new machine shop finance a CNC machine?

It is harder. Most lenders prefer 1-2+ years in business; a new shop may need a larger down payment, a signed customer order, or an SBA loan.
