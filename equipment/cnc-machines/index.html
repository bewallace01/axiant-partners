What Is a CNC Machine?

A CNC machine is a machine tool that uses programmed instructions for precise cutting, milling, turning, or drilling. CNC milling machines and machining centers remove material from workpieces using rotating cutting tools. CNC lathes rotate the workpiece while cutting tools shape it. How Much Does a CNC Machine Cost?

New CNC milling machines range from roughly $50,000-$150,000 for 3-axis vertical machining centers to $150,000-$500,000+ for 5-axis or horizontal. CNC lathes typically cost $80,000-$300,000+. Multi-tasking machines (mill-turn) can exceed $500,000. Used CNC machines typically cost 25-45% less. A 5-year-old 3-axis VMC might run $40,000-$90,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 7-10 years old. Obtain a written quote including installation. Estimate payments.

Why Businesses Finance CNC Machines Rather Than Pay Cash

Equipment loans and leases offer tax benefits-Section 179 and bonus depreciation; lease payments as operating expenses. Many manufacturers prefer to finance to keep reserves for opportunities or tech upgrades.

CNC Machine Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used CNC machine?

Yes. Many lenders finance used CNC machines, typically 7-10 years old or newer. Hours, condition, and control system affect approval.

What credit score is required?

Most lenders look for 600 or higher. 680+ qualifies for the best rates. Some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

Can startups finance CNC machines?

Most lenders prefer 1-2+ years in business. New manufacturers may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer. SBA loans can help.

How long does approval take?

1-5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA adds 30-60+ days.

Is leasing better than buying?

It depends. Leasing offers lower payments, tax benefits, and easier upgrades as technology advances. Buying builds equity. Compare both based on cash flow and tech refresh cycle. Equipment loan vs lease.

What documents are needed?

3-6 months of bank statements, tax returns, profit and loss, equipment quote (including installation), and business formation documents. Customer contracts and backlog help.
