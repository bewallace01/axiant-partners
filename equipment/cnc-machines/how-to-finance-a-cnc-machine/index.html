How to Finance a CNC Machine

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for manufacturers

← Back to CNC Machine Financing

Last updated: March 5, 2026

CNC machines—milling centers, lathes, and machining centers—cost $50,000–$500,000+ new depending on size and capabilities, and typically 25–45% less used. Most manufacturers finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to production revenue.

What Is a CNC Machine?

A CNC (computer numerical control) machine is a machine tool that uses programmed instructions to perform precise cutting, milling, turning, or drilling operations. CNC milling machines and machining centers remove material from workpieces using rotating cutting tools. CNC lathes rotate the workpiece while cutting tools shape it. Machining centers often combine milling and turning (multi-axis). CNC machines produce precision parts for aerospace, automotive, medical, and general manufacturing. They range from small benchtop units to large multi-axis machining centers. CNC equipment is essential for job shops and manufacturers who need repeatable precision and high throughput.

How Do You Finance a CNC Machine?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. CNC machines are high-value manufacturing equipment with strong resale value, so financing is widely available through manufacturing lenders. Expect longer terms (5–7+ years) given asset value.

How Much Does a CNC Machine Cost?

CNC machine prices vary by type (mill vs lathe vs machining center), size, axes, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New CNC Machine Prices

New CNC milling machines range from roughly $50,000–$150,000 for 3-axis vertical machining centers to $150,000–$500,000+ for 5-axis or horizontal machining centers. CNC lathes typically cost $80,000–$300,000+ depending on swing and bed length. Multi-tasking machines (mill-turn) can exceed $500,000. Top brands like Haas, DMG MORI, Mazak, and Okuma add cost. Travel, spindle, and control system drive price.

Used CNC Machine Prices

Used CNC machines typically cost 25–45% less than new equivalents, depending on age, hours, and condition. A 5-year-old 3-axis VMC might run $40,000–$90,000. Older or smaller used units can start around $25,000–$50,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 7–10 years old. Always inspect spindle hours, ways, ball screws, and control system before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Type (mill vs lathe vs machining center), number of axes, travel, spindle, brand, control (Fanuc, Siemens, Haas), and regional demand all affect CNC machine pricing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for CNC Machines

Several financing structures work for CNC machines. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the machine at the end of the term. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the CNC when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 60–84 months or longer. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. CNC machines hold value well, so lenders often offer competitive terms. Ideal if you plan to use the machine long-term.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return the machine, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Leasing suits manufacturers who want to preserve cash flow or upgrade as technology advances.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans can finance CNC machines with longer terms (7–10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. SBA loans suit established manufacturers planning capacity expansion or combining equipment with working capital. Requirements include 2+ years in business (typically), solid credit, and adequate cash flow.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are better suited for operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use equipment financing for the CNC machine itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

CNC Machine Financing Rates and Terms

Interest rates for CNC machine financing typically range from 6–15% for equipment loans and leases. Terms commonly run 60–84 months or longer for standard equipment financing; SBA loans can extend to 7–10 years. A $200,000 CNC machine financed at 8% over 72 months would result in roughly $3,500/month. Use our financing calculator to model different scenarios.

Step-by-Step: How to Finance a CNC Machine

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose the CNC that fits your part size and tolerances. Get a written quote from the dealer including installation.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and equipment quote.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple manufacturing equipment lenders. Axiant Partners matches manufacturers with lenders experienced in manufacturing equipment financing.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer. Installation and training may be included in the quote.

Tips to Get Approved for CNC Machine Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors, and avoid new credit before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements, customer contracts, and backlog strengthen your application.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used CNC may cost significantly less. Inspect hours and condition carefully.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate.
  • Choose equipment with strong resale value. Major brands hold value better, making lenders more comfortable.

CNC Machine Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used CNC machine?

Yes. Many lenders finance used CNC machines, typically those 7–10 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Hours, condition, and control system affect approval.

What credit score is required for CNC machine financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Manufacturing equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does CNC machine financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Can startups finance CNC machines?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1–2+ years in business. New manufacturers may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer. SBA loans can help newer businesses.

Is leasing better than buying a CNC machine?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, tax benefits, and easier upgrades as technology advances. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the machine long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow and tech refresh cycle.
