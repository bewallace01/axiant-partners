How to Finance Injection Molding Equipment

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for injection molding machines

Most plastic manufacturers finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to production revenue.

What Is Injection Molding Equipment?

Injection molding machines melt plastic resin and inject it into a mold to produce plastic parts. Clamp tonnage (50–3,000+ tons) and shot size (ounces) determine the machine's capacity. Machines range from small benchtop units for prototypes to large industrial machines for high-volume production. Brands like Engel, Arburg, Haitian, and Husky are common. Injection molding is used for automotive, medical, consumer, and industrial plastic parts. Molders and custom manufacturers rely on this equipment for production capacity.

How Do You Finance Injection Molding Equipment?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Injection molding machines are common manufacturing equipment with strong resale value, so financing is widely available through manufacturing lenders.

How Much Does Injection Molding Equipment Cost?

Injection molding machine prices vary by clamp tonnage, shot size, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Injection Molding Machine Prices

New machines (50–150 ton) range from roughly $50,000–$150,000. Mid-range (200–400 ton) typically cost $150,000–$350,000. Large machines (500–1,000+ ton) run $350,000–$500,000+. Top brands add cost. Electric vs hydraulic, multi-shot, and automation drive price.

Used Injection Molding Machine Prices

Used machines typically cost 25–45% less than new equivalents. A 5-year-old 200-ton machine might run $80,000–$150,000. Older units can start around $30,000–$50,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 7–10 years old. Inspect clamp, injection unit, and control system before purchasing used.

Financing Options for Injection Molding Equipment

Several financing structures work for injection molding machines. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the machine when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 36–84 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Injection molding machines hold value well, so financing is often accessible.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return, purchase at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Suits molders who want to preserve cash flow or cycle equipment.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) loans can finance injection molding equipment with longer terms and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. Suit established manufacturers combining equipment with working capital.

Injection Molding Financing FAQ

Can you finance used injection molding equipment?

Yes. Many lenders finance used injection molding machines, typically those 7–10 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Clamp tonnage, shot size, condition, and brand affect approval.

What credit score is required for injection molding financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Manufacturing equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does injection molding financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Does clamp tonnage affect financing?

Lenders consider price and useful life. Larger machines cost more and may qualify for longer terms. Price and condition matter more than tonnage alone.

Is leasing better than buying injection molding equipment?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the machine long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow.
