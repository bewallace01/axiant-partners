What Are Forklifts?

Forklifts are material handling vehicles used to lift, move, and stack pallets and loads in warehouses, distribution centers, and manufacturing plants. Types include sit-down counterbalance (electric, propane, diesel), reach trucks, order pickers, and pallet jacks. Capacity (3,000–10,000+ lbs) and lift height determine the forklift's capabilities. Electric forklifts suit indoor use; propane and diesel suit outdoor and heavy-duty applications. Brands like Toyota, Crown, Raymond, Yale, and Hyster are common. Forklifts are essential for logistics and warehousing and manufacturing operations.

How Do You Finance a Forklift?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Forklifts are common material handling equipment with strong resale value, so financing is widely available through equipment and logistics lenders.

How Much Does a Forklift Cost?

Forklift prices vary by type, capacity, fuel source, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Forklift Prices

New electric sit-down forklifts (3,000–5,000 lb capacity) range from roughly $25,000–$45,000. Propane and diesel forklifts typically cost $30,000–$55,000. Reach trucks and order pickers run $35,000–$60,000+. Large capacity (8,000–10,000+ lb) and specialty forklifts cost $50,000–$80,000+. Top brands add cost. Battery vs propane and mast height drive price.

Used Forklift Prices

Used forklifts typically cost 25–40% less than new equivalents. A 3-year-old electric forklift might run $18,000–$30,000. Older propane units can start around $10,000–$20,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 5–7 years old. Inspect mast, forks, hours, and battery (for electric) before purchasing used.

Financing Options for Forklifts

Several financing structures work for forklifts. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the forklift when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 36–60 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Forklifts hold value well, so financing is often accessible.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return, purchase at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Suits warehouses and manufacturers who want to preserve cash flow or cycle equipment.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) loans can finance forklifts with longer terms and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. Suit established businesses combining equipment with working capital.

Forklift Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used forklift?

Yes. Many lenders finance used forklifts, typically those 5–7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Hours, condition, capacity, and brand affect approval.

What credit score is required for forklift financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does forklift financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Electric vs propane forklift—does it affect financing?

Lenders finance both. Electric forklifts may cost more upfront but have lower operating costs. Price and condition matter more than fuel type for financing.

Is leasing better than buying a forklift?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the forklift long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow.
