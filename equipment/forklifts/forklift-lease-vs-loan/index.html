Forklift Lease vs Loan: Monthly Cost, Lease-to-Own and When Buying Wins

What a forklift lease costs each month, how lease-to-own works, and when a loan is the cheaper way to get the truck.

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

A forklift lease gets you the truck for a fixed monthly payment, usually over 36-60 months, with little or nothing down. A fair market value lease typically runs about 10-20% below a loan: on a $35,000 electric sit-down, roughly $680-$770 a month against about $854 on an 8%, 48-month loan. A $1 buyout lease is lease-to-own and prices close to the loan. Most lessors want 600+ credit, and approvals typically take 24-72 hours.

How Does a Forklift Lease Work?

The leasing company buys the truck from the dealer, and you pay a fixed monthly amount to use it for 36-60 months. You don't own it during the term. At the end you buy it, hand it back or renew, depending on which of the three common leases you signed. A loan works the other way around: the lender pays the dealer, you own the forklift from day one, and you pay it off over 36-72 months.

There are three lease types worth knowing. A $1 buyout lease is lease-to-own: you pay $1 at the end and the truck is yours, so the payment is close to a loan's. A 10% purchase option lease costs a little less per month and lets you buy the truck for 10% of its original price. A fair market value (FMV) lease has the lowest payment and lets you buy at market value, return the truck or renew.

How Much Does It Cost to Lease a Forklift?

A fair market value lease usually runs about 10-20% below a loan payment on the same truck. Here is what that looks like by forklift type, using new prices and a loan at 8% over 48 months:

ForkliftNew priceLoan, 8%FMV lease
Electric sit-down$25K-$45K$610-$1,099$490-$990
Propane or diesel$30K-$55K$732-$1,343$590-$1,210
Reach truck$35K-$60K+$854-$1,465$680-$1,320
Large capacity$50K-$80K+$1,221-$1,953$980-$1,760

Payments are for the full price with nothing down. They are illustrations, not quotes. Your rate depends mostly on credit: about 7-9% APR at 720+ with 2+ years in business, 10-13% at 680-719, 13-16% at 640-679 and 16-22% at 580-639. At 12% instead of 8%, the $35,000 truck's 48-month loan payment rises from about $854 to $922. Used trucks typically sell 25-40% below new, which shrinks every number above; see used forklift financing. Model your own deal in the payment calculator.

Is It Better to Lease or Buy a Forklift?

Lease when you refresh trucks every 3-5 years, run multiple shifts that wear trucks out faster, or want the lowest payment and no resale hassle at the end. Electric forklift batteries and chargers keep improving, and a lease lets you move to the newer model when the term ends.

Buy with a loan when you will run the truck 7 years or more. Once it is paid off, the payment stops and the truck keeps working, so total cost usually comes out lower. Owning also means you can sell or trade it whenever you choose.

Watch the total, not just the payment. An FMV lease with a low payment can end up costing more than a loan if you buy the truck at the end. Add up every payment, plus any buyout, before you decide. Equipment leasing vs a loan walks through the full comparison.

Is Renting a Forklift Cheaper Than Leasing?

For a short job, yes. Renting costs the most per month, but you stop paying the day you return the truck. For a peak season or a one-off project, that flexibility is worth the premium. Once you need the truck every day for more than a season, a lease or loan almost always costs less per month, and the money goes toward a truck you can keep or trade in.

How Are Forklift Lease Payments Taxed?

Operating lease payments are generally deductible as a business expense in the year you make them, with no depreciation schedule to track. If you own the forklift through a loan, you deduct the interest and depreciate the truck, with Section 179 and bonus depreciation available where they apply. Lease deductions follow the payments; a loan's follow the depreciation schedule, which Section 179 can pull into the first year. Which one saves more depends on your tax year, so check with your CPA.

What Do You Need to Qualify for a Forklift Lease?

Most lessors and lenders look for a 600+ FICO, and 720+ gets the best rates. Between 550 and 599 you usually need a specialty lender, and below 550 expect 25% or more down or a cosigner. Forklifts hold their value well, which helps: even a newer business can lease one, usually with a personal guarantor and about 12-22% APR with 10-20% down when it has under 2 years in business.

Have a dealer quote with the make, model and specs, 3-6 months of bank statements and your formation documents ready. Initial approvals typically come back in 24-72 hours. Frequently Asked Questions

How does a forklift lease work?

The leasing company buys the forklift and you pay a fixed monthly amount to use it, usually for 36-60 months. At the end you buy it, return it or renew, depending on the lease type. The truck itself secures the lease, so approval is usually quicker than a bank loan.

Can you lease to own a forklift?

Yes. A $1 buyout lease is lease-to-own: you make the payments and own the truck for $1 at the end, so the payment is close to a loan's. A 10% purchase option lease costs a little less per month and lets you buy the truck for 10% of its original price.

How much does it cost to lease a forklift?

It depends on the truck and your credit. New electric sit-downs run about $25,000-$45,000, and a fair market value lease usually costs 10-20% less per month than a loan. On a $35,000 truck that works out to roughly $680-$770 a month, against about $854 on an 8%, 48-month loan.

Is it better to lease or buy a forklift?

Buy with a loan if you will run the truck 7 years or more; once it is paid off, it keeps working for free. Lease if you refresh every 3-5 years, run multiple shifts or want the lowest payment. Usage and time horizon decide it more than the rate does.

Is renting a forklift cheaper than leasing?

Only for short jobs. Rental rates are the highest per month, but you can stop paying when the job ends. Once you need the truck for more than a season, a lease or loan almost always costs less per month.

What credit score do you need to lease a forklift?

Most lessors look for 600+, and 720+ with 2+ years in business gets the best pricing. Under 2 years in business, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor. Below 550, plan on 25% or more down or a cosigner.
