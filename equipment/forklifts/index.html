Forklift Financing

Forklift loans and leases for warehouses, logistics, and manufacturing

← Back to Equipment by Type

Financing forklifts for warehouses, logistics, and manufacturing helps you add or replace material handling equipment without draining working capital. Forklift financing—including forklift loans and forklift leases—spreads the cost over 36–60 months and typically approves in 1–5 business days. Whether you need electric, propane, or diesel forklifts for your warehouse or distribution center, this guide covers costs, financing options, approval requirements, and rates.

What Forklifts Cost

Forklift prices vary by type, capacity, fuel source, and whether you buy new or used. Electric sit-down forklifts (3,000–5,000 lb capacity) typically range from $25,000–$45,000 new. Propane and diesel models run $30,000–$55,000. Reach trucks and order pickers cost $35,000–$60,000+. Large-capacity forklifts (8,000–10,000+ lb) can reach $50,000–$80,000+. Brand (Toyota, Crown, Raymond, Yale, Hyster), mast height, and attachments add cost. Used forklifts typically run 25–40% less; a 3-year-old electric might cost $18,000–$30,000. Forklift Financing Options

The main forklift financing options are equipment loans, leases, and SBA loans. Equipment loans and leases are the most common—you apply with a lender or broker, provide business financials and an equipment quote, and receive approval in 1–5 business days. Loans give you ownership at the end of the term; leases offer lower monthly payments and flexibility to return or upgrade. SBA 7(a) and 504 loans can finance forklifts with longer terms (7–10 years) and lower down payments, though approval takes 30–60+ days. Forklifts hold value well and are widely used in logistics and manufacturing, so financing is readily available.

Loan vs Lease for Forklifts

A forklift loan lets you borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the forklift when the loan is paid off. Terms typically run 36–60 months with 0–20% down. Loans suit businesses that plan to use the forklift long-term and want to build equity. A forklift lease typically offers lower monthly payments because you pay for use, not full ownership. At lease end, you may return the equipment, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses for tax purposes. Leasing suits warehouses and manufacturers that want to preserve cash flow or cycle equipment regularly. See equipment leasing vs loan: which is better for a full comparison.

Used Forklift Financing

Many lenders finance used forklifts, typically those 5–7 years old or newer. Used equipment often costs 25–40% less than new. Lenders may require a larger down payment (10–25%) and shorter terms. Hours, condition, capacity, and brand affect approval. Electric forklift batteries have a finite life (often 5–7 years)—inspect battery age and condition before buying used. Replacement batteries can cost $3,000–$7,000+. Always get an equipment inspection and verify maintenance records. See can you finance used equipment for lender requirements.

Forklift Financing Requirements

Most equipment lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher; 680+ typically qualifies for the best rates. Expect 0–20% down depending on credit. Most lenders prefer 1–2+ years in business and proof of revenue (bank statements, tax returns, P&L). You'll need a written equipment quote from a dealer. See credit score requirements and what lenders look at for more detail.

How Fast Approval Takes

Forklift financing approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. Having documents ready—bank statements, tax returns, P&L, and equipment quote—speeds the process. SBA loans take 30–60+ days. See how fast equipment financing is approved for more detail.

Forklift Financing Rates

Interest rates for forklift financing typically range from 6–15% for equipment loans and leases, depending on credit and lender. Terms commonly run 36–60 months. A $40,000 electric forklift financed at 8% over 48 months results in roughly $977/month. See typical equipment financing rates for more detail.
