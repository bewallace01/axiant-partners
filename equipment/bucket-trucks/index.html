What Is a Bucket Truck?

A bucket truck (aerial lift truck, digger derrick, or cherry picker) is a commercial vehicle with a hydraulic or electric boom and work platform (bucket) that elevates workers to overhead heights. Bucket trucks are used for power line work, tree trimming, cable and fiber installation, and other elevated tasks. Working heights range from about 35 feet to 65+ feet depending on boom type and configuration.

Bucket trucks serve utilities (electric, gas, telecom), tree care and arborist companies, landscaping firms, and sign installers. Understanding what bucket trucks are and how they're used helps lenders assess your application-they want to see that the equipment supports revenue-generating work. Boom type (articulating vs telescopic), chassis, and working height affect suitability and cost.

Lenders view bucket truck financing favorably because aerial lift trucks hold value well and serve essential industries. How Much Does a Bucket Truck Cost?

Bucket truck prices vary by working height, boom type, chassis, and whether you buy new or used. New bucket trucks range from roughly $100,000-$150,000 for mid-height units (35-45') to $150,000-$200,000+ for high-reach models (55-65+'). Top brands like Altec, Terex, and Versalift command premium prices. Digger derrick configurations (with auger) add cost. Chassis (medium-duty vs heavy-duty) affects total price.

Used bucket trucks typically cost 30-50% less. A 5-year-old unit might run $60,000-$120,000. Boom and hydraulic condition are critical-inspect before purchasing. Many lenders finance used bucket trucks up to 5-7 years old. Obtain a written quote from your dealer-lenders use it to structure financing. Financing used equipment guide.

What to Have Ready Before You Apply

A bucket truck is an aerial device on a chassis, and the aerial device carries certification requirements that the chassis does not. Both go into the file.

  • Quote or bill of sale with the VIN and the boom details · Manufacturer, model, working height, and whether the unit is insulated or non-insulated.
  • Current dielectric test certificate for insulated units · Insulated aerial devices are tested on a defined interval. A current certificate is a safety requirement and a value question at the same time.
  • Annual inspection records for the aerial device · Aerial devices are inspected on schedule under the applicable ANSI standard. Records should be continuous.
  • Chassis mileage and boom hours · They diverge widely. A truck with low miles can have a heavily used boom, and the boom is the expensive part.
  • Three to six months of bank statements · Alongside any utility, telecoms or municipal contracts you hold.
  • CDL requirements for the specific truck · Depends on GVWR. Many smaller units are non-CDL; larger material handlers are not.

If you hold contracts with a utility, a telecoms carrier or a municipality, include them. Contracted line, tree or signal work is durable revenue and it reads that way.

When to Apply for Bucket Truck Financing

Apply before the storm and contract seasons, because that is when these trucks disappear from the market. Utility contractors, tree services and telecoms crews all expand at the same time, and a well-specified insulated unit is not an item you can source at short notice.

If the truck is being bought against an awarded utility or municipal contract, apply as soon as the award is issued. The contract does two things at once: it establishes the revenue and it specifies the working height and insulation rating the truck actually needs.

Tips to Get Approved for Bucket Truck Financing

  • Buy the working height the work requires. Reaching a conductor from the ground up is not a place to economise. Undersized reach means declining work you were otherwise qualified for.
  • Treat the dielectric certificate as a condition of purchase. For insulated units it is not optional documentation. Make the sale contingent on a current one.
  • Check boom hours and the pedestal. The boom, turret and pedestal are where structural wear shows. Have them inspected by someone who knows aerial devices, not a general truck mechanic.
  • Consider a material handler if you set poles or transformers. It is a different class of truck and a different price, but it removes a second machine from the job.
  • Keep certification current after purchase. Lapsed inspection records reduce the truck's value and can stop work on a utility site the same day.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Financing a Bucket Truck

The mistakes here are safety mistakes before they are financial ones:

  • Buying an insulated unit without current testing. You cannot lawfully or safely do energised work with it until it is tested, and testing is at your expense.
  • Assuming chassis condition reflects boom condition. They are independent assets bolted together. Inspect them independently.
  • Overlooking outrigger and stability requirements. Setup limitations decide which jobs the truck can do. Know them before you buy.
  • Underestimating aerial device insurance. Line and tree work at height carries its own premium. Quote it before committing.
  • Ignoring the inspection calendar. Annual and periodic inspections are ongoing costs and non-negotiable obligations.

Confirm what the lender requires by way of insurance and inspection documentation before funding, and diarize every certification date from the day you take delivery.

Why Businesses Finance Bucket Trucks Rather Than Pay Cash

Cash spent on equipment is cash not available for payroll, materials or the next opportunity. Financing spreads the cost over the working life of the bucket truck and keeps reserves intact, and Section 179 and bonus depreciation reduce the after-tax cost further. Carriers feel it most sharply: fuel, insurance and maintenance are due weekly while freight bills settle in thirty to forty-five days.

Bucket Truck Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used bucket truck?

Yes. Many lenders finance used bucket trucks, typically those 5-7 years old or newer. Boom and hydraulic condition, working height, and mileage affect approval. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms.

What credit score is required for bucket truck financing?

Most lenders look for 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ qualify for the best rates. Asset-backed financing sometimes works with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong. Credit score requirements.

How long does bucket truck financing approval take?

1-5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans add 30-60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process. How fast can equipment financing be approved.

Is leasing better than buying a bucket truck?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the truck long-term. Equipment loan vs lease comparison.

Can startups finance bucket trucks?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1-2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer. SBA loans can help. Apply and we'll match you with lenders that fit your profile.

What documents are needed for bucket truck financing?

Typical requirements: 3-6 months of business bank statements, tax returns, equipment quote from your dealer, profit and loss statement, and business formation documents. Having these ready speeds approval. What lenders look at.

Does working height affect financing?

Lenders finance bucket trucks of various working heights. Price and equipment specs are in the dealer quote. Higher reach units cost more and affect loan amount. Working height does not typically change approval criteria.
