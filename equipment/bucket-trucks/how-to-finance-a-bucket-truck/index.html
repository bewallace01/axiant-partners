How to Finance a Bucket Truck

What Is a Bucket Truck?

A bucket truck (also called an aerial lift truck or knuckle-boom truck) is a truck with an aerial lift and work platform (bucket) mounted on the chassis. The lift arm extends and articulates to raise workers to heights for maintenance, installation, or removal work. Bucket trucks are used in utilities (power lines, transformers), tree care (arborists, tree trimming), telecom (cable, fiber, antennas), and landscaping. They come in various working heights—typically 35–65 feet or more—and may have insulated buckets for live electrical work. The truck chassis provides mobility; the lift provides safe access at height.

How Do You Finance a Bucket Truck?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Bucket trucks are essential for utilities and tree care, so financing is widely available through forestry and landscaping lenders.

How Much Does a Bucket Truck Cost?

Bucket truck prices vary by working height, chassis type, insulation (for electrical work), brand, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing and set realistic down payment and monthly payment expectations.

New Bucket Truck Prices

New bucket trucks range from roughly $100,000–$150,000 for mid-height units (35–45 feet) to $150,000–$200,000+ for high-reach models (50–65+ feet) or insulated units for live electrical work. Top chassis brands like Ford, International, Freightliner, and lift manufacturers like Altec, Terex, and Elliott add cost. Working height is the primary price driver. Factor in delivery when budgeting.

Used Bucket Truck Prices

Used bucket trucks typically cost 30–50% less than new equivalents, depending on age, working height, lift condition, and mileage. A 5-year-old mid-height bucket truck might run $65,000–$100,000. Older or smaller used units can start around $50,000–$75,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 5–7 years old; older trucks may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Always inspect the lift, hydraulic system, and bucket before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Working height, insulation (digger-derrick vs material handler), chassis brand, lift manufacturer, mileage (for used), and regional demand all affect bucket truck pricing. Auction and dealer prices can differ. Obtain a written quote from your dealer or seller—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Bucket Trucks

Several financing structures work for bucket trucks. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the truck at the end of the term. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the bucket truck when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 24–72 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15% depending on credit and lender. Because bucket trucks are essential in utilities and tree care, lenders often offer competitive terms. Ideal if you plan to use the truck long-term and want to build equity.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans because you're paying for use, not full ownership. At lease end, you may return the truck, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade to a newer model. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses for tax purposes; capital leases function like loans. Leasing suits businesses that want to preserve cash flow, upgrade regularly, or prefer off-balance-sheet treatment. Bucket trucks with strong residual values often qualify for attractive lease terms.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans can finance bucket trucks with longer terms (7–10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. SBA loans suit established businesses planning fleet expansion or combining equipment with working capital. Requirements include 2+ years in business (typically), solid credit, and adequate cash flow. If timing allows, SBA financing can reduce monthly payments significantly.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are flexible but generally carry higher rates and shorter terms. They're better suited for fuel, payroll, and operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use working capital for day-to-day needs; use equipment financing for the bucket truck itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Step-by-Step: How to Finance a Bucket Truck

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose the bucket truck that fits your working height needs—35 ft, 45 ft, 55 ft, or higher. Get a written quote from the dealer or seller including delivery.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and a balance sheet. Have your equipment quote and business info ready.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple equipment lenders. Consider rates, terms, down payment requirements, and approval speed. Axiant Partners matches businesses with lenders experienced in forestry equipment and landscaping equipment.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation. Most applications can be submitted online.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer. Funds typically go directly to the seller; you take possession of the bucket truck.

Tips to Get Approved for Bucket Truck Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors on your report, and avoid new credit applications before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements, consistent deposits, utility or tree care contracts, and organized financials speed approval and improve terms.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used bucket truck may cost significantly less, improving approval odds and cash flow. Inspect the lift and hydraulic system carefully.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate or improve approval when credit is borderline.
  • Choose equipment with strong resale value. Major brands and well-maintained lifts hold value better, making lenders more comfortable.

Bucket Truck Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used bucket truck?

Yes. Many lenders finance used bucket trucks, typically those 5–7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Working height, lift condition, and mileage affect approval. Get an inspection before purchasing.

What credit score is required for bucket truck financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Because bucket truck financing is asset-backed, some programs work with 580+ when revenue, down payment, and time in business are strong.

How long does bucket truck financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready—financials, equipment quote, business info—speeds the process. Apply early if you need a truck by a specific date.

Can startups finance bucket trucks?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1–2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer with strong credit. SBA loans and some specialized programs can help newer businesses.

Is leasing better than buying a bucket truck?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the truck long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals.
