Which Dish Machine Fits Your Volume

Machine size is set by how many racks the kitchen washes at its busiest hour. Typical new prices before installation, as this page publishes them; not quotes:

MachineFitsTypical price
UndercounterBars, cafes, small dining rooms$3,000-$8,000
Door-type (pass-through)Full-service restaurants$8,000-$20,000+
Conveyor or flightBanquet, hospital, campus$15,000-$25,000+
Pot and pan sinkEvery kitchen with a dish pit$1,000-$5,000

Large flight machines with dryers and blowers can cost well above these ranges. Buying a machine one size too small shows up as a backed-up dish pit and overtime; one size too large wastes water, chemicals and power on every cycle.

High-Temp vs Low-Temp Machines

The choice changes the price, the utility bill and what else you must buy.

  • High-temperature machines sanitize with a hot water final rinse. The FDA Food Code sets the rinse temperatures (Food Code 4-501.112), and most buildings need a booster heater to reach them. Dishes dry faster and there is no sanitizer to buy.
  • Low-temperature machines sanitize with a chemical such as chlorine at the concentration the Food Code sets (4-501.114). The machine is often cheaper and needs no booster, but sanitizer is an ongoing cost.
  • Hoods and steam: some jurisdictions require a hood or condensate system over high-temp machines. Check with your local code official before ordering; see ventilation hood financing.

Either way the machine must carry a data plate with its operating specifications (Food Code 4-204.113), and health inspectors check it.

Chemical-Supplier Rental vs Buying

Many restaurants never buy a dish machine. Chemical and service companies commonly place a low-temp machine for a monthly fee, with service included, on condition that you buy their detergent, rinse aid and sanitizer.

Chemical-supplier rentalFinanced purchase
Upfront costLittle or none0-20% down
Monthly costFee plus required chemicalsLoan or lease payment
ServiceUsually includedYour contract or call-outs
Machine choiceSupplier’s modelsAny make, high or low temp
At the endMachine goes backYou own it

To compare, add a year of required chemical purchases to the rental fee and set it against a loan payment plus your own chemicals and a service contract. Read the rental agreement for its term, minimum chemical volume and early-cancellation charges.

Water, Energy and Running Cost

A dish machine runs every service, so its utility use often matters more over the term than its price. ENERGY STAR certifies commercial dishwashers that use less water and energy (ENERGY STAR), and many utilities offer rebates on certified models. Commercial machines are listed to NSF/ANSI 3, the warewashing equipment standard; ask for it on the quote.

What Lenders Ask For

  • The quote with machine, booster, tables and installation listed separately.
  • 3-6 months of bank statements; at this ticket size that is usually the whole file.
  • Time in business: 1-2+ years is typical; a new restaurant may need a larger down payment or to finance the dish machine with the full kitchen build-out.
  • Used machines: many lenders will finance a refurbished unit from a dealer with a written quote; private sales are harder.

Commercial Dishwasher Financing FAQ

How much does it cost to finance a commercial dishwasher?

At an assumed 10%, a $20,000 door-type package is about $507 a month over 48 months and a $6,000 undercounter unit about $194 over 36 months. These are illustrations, not quotes.

Is it better to rent a dishwasher from a chemical company or buy one?

Renting needs little cash and usually includes service, but you must buy the supplier's chemicals and the machine goes back. Buying costs more upfront and lets you choose any machine. Compare a year of rental fees plus required chemicals with a loan payment.

Do I need a booster heater for a commercial dishwasher?

Usually for a high-temperature machine, because the building's water heater rarely reaches the Food Code's final rinse temperature. Low-temperature chemical machines do not need one.

Can a booster heater and installation be financed with the machine?

Yes, when they are on the same quote. Added later, they are usually a separate deal.

Can a new restaurant finance a dishwasher?

Often, as part of the kitchen package. Most lenders prefer 1-2+ years in business, so a new restaurant may need a larger down payment or a chemical-supplier rental.

How long are commercial dishwasher financing terms?

Commonly 36-60 months. Smaller undercounter machines tend toward the shorter end.

Sources & Further Reading

  • ENERGY STAR Commercial Dishwashers — Certified models and efficiency criteria for undercounter, door-type and conveyor machines.
  • NSF food equipment standards — NSF/ANSI 3 covers commercial warewashing equipment.
