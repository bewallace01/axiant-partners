How to Finance Press Brake Equipment

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for press brakes and stamping equipment

Most metal fabricators finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to production revenue.

What Are Press Brakes and Stamping Equipment?

Press brakes bend sheet metal using a punch and die—used for forming channels, boxes, brackets, and panels. Stamping presses cut and form metal in a single stroke. Press brakes range from small manual units (20–50 tons) to large CNC machines (100–600+ tons) with automated tool changing. Stamping presses can be mechanical or hydraulic. Both are essential for job shops, metal fabricators, and manufacturers who produce formed metal parts. Brands like Amada, Bystronic, Trumpf, and Accurpress are common in the market.

How Do You Finance Press Brake Equipment?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Press brakes are common manufacturing equipment with strong resale value, so financing is widely available through manufacturing lenders.

How Much Does Press Brake Equipment Cost?

Press brake and stamping equipment prices vary by tonnage, bed length, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Press Brake Prices

New manual press brakes (20–80 tons) range from roughly $30,000–$80,000. New CNC press brakes (100–300 tons) typically cost $100,000–$300,000+ depending on tonnage and bed length. Stamping presses run $50,000–$300,000+ depending on tonnage and features. Top brands add cost. Automation (auto tool changing, angle measurement) drives price.

Used Press Brake Prices

Used press brakes typically cost 25–45% less than new equivalents. A 5-year-old 100-ton CNC press brake might run $60,000–$120,000. Older manual units can start around $15,000–$40,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 7–10 years old. Inspect ram, bed, and control system before purchasing used.

Financing Options for Press Brake Equipment

Several financing structures work for press brakes. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the machine when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 36–84 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Press brakes hold value well, so financing is often accessible.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return, purchase at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Suits fabricators who want to preserve cash flow or upgrade regularly.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) loans can finance press brake equipment with longer terms and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. Suit established manufacturers combining equipment with working capital.

Press Brake Financing FAQ

Can you finance used press brake equipment?

Yes. Many lenders finance used press brakes and stamping presses, typically those 7–10 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Tonnage, condition, and brand affect approval.

What credit score is required for press brake financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Manufacturing equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does press brake financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Press brake vs stamping press—does it affect financing?

Lenders finance both. Press brakes bend sheet metal; stamping presses cut and form. Price and tonnage matter more than type for financing.

Is leasing better than buying press brake equipment?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the machine long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow.
