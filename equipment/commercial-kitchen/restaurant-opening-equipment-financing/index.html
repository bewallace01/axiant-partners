Restaurant Startup Financing: How to Pay for Equipment and Opening Costs

What lenders will finance before your restaurant opens, what they charge, and how much of your own money you need.

Quick answer

Restaurant startup financing usually means an equipment loan or lease for the kitchen, plus your own cash and, for bigger projects, an SBA 7(a) loan. A kitchen fit-out alone often runs $50,000-$200,000+. Before you open, equipment lenders typically charge about 12-22% APR with 10-20% down or a personal guarantor, and an SBA start-up loan needs at least 10% of the total project cost from you. Working capital loans wait until you have about 6 months of deposits.

How Do You Finance a New Restaurant?

Most restaurants open on a stack, not a single loan. The kitchen goes on an equipment loan or lease, the build-out goes on an SBA loan or your own cash, and you keep reserves for the first few months of payroll and rent. Lenders split it this way because each piece is underwritten differently: equipment is backed by the equipment, while build-out money is backed mostly by you.

Here is what each option looks like for a restaurant that has not opened yet. Ranges are typical, not quotes.

How Hard Is It to Get a Loan to Start a Restaurant?

Harder than for most businesses, because there are no sales to underwrite. The lender is betting on you: your personal credit, your restaurant experience, the cash you are putting in, and how complete the plan is. A chef or GM with ten years in kitchens and a signed lease gets a very different answer than a first-time owner with a concept deck.

Equipment is the easiest piece. Many programs accept businesses with 6+ months of history and 660-680+ credit, and some will fund before you open when your personal credit is strong. Under 2 years in business, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor. Kitchen equipment also prices about 1-3 points above equipment that resells more easily, like trucks.

The SBA route is slower and more paperwork, but it is the one that covers build-out. SBA treats a business open one year or less as a start-up and requires at least 10% of the total project cost as your equity injection. Restaurant SBA lenders typically favor 650-680+ credit, and approvals take 30-60+ days.

What does not work before opening: working capital loans and merchant cash advances. They are sized off your deposits, and working capital lenders usually want at least 6 months in business and $10,000 or more in monthly revenue.

What Does It Cost to Open a Restaurant?

The kitchen is usually the biggest financeable piece. A full commercial kitchen runs roughly $50,000-$200,000+ depending on concept, size and cuisine; quick-serve concepts sit at the lower end and full-service at the higher end. On top of that you have build-out, deposits, licenses, opening inventory and payroll before the first sale, and those vary too much by market to put one number on.

The useful way to size it: list every item with a quote, total it, then decide which pieces go on equipment financing, which on an SBA loan, and which come from your cash. A lender reads a quoted list far more favorably than a round number labeled "opening costs." The startup funding needs worksheet walks through it.

What Does the Payment Look Like?

Say you finance $100,000 of kitchen equipment and smallwares with 10% down ($10,000), leaving $90,000 over 60 months. At 12% APR, the low end for a business under 2 years old, the payment is about $2,002 a month and $30,120 in total interest.

Bundling the whole kitchen into one package, cooking line, refrigeration, prep and ventilation, means one application and one payment, and usually better terms than financing each piece separately. Buying some items used lowers the financed amount. Before you sign, make sure that payment fits a slow first quarter. Try other amounts in the payment calculator.

Should You Lease or Buy the Kitchen?

Many new restaurants lease, because lease payments usually run about 10-20% lower than a loan and the cash you keep matters more in the first year. At the end you buy the equipment, return it, or renew. A loan costs less over time if you will run the equipment until it wears out. Restaurant equipment leasing compares the two on a $120,000 kitchen.

What Do Lenders Want to See?

  • Equipment quotes from the dealer, covering every code-required item (hood, exhaust, refrigeration)
  • A business plan: concept, market, projections and opening timeline
  • Your resume and restaurant experience
  • 3-6 months of personal and business bank statements, and tax returns
  • The signed lease or build-out agreement, and formation documents

A complete file gets an initial answer on equipment in 24-72 hours, and funding usually lands within 5-10 business days. Allow 1-2 weeks so the kitchen arrives before opening day. Frequently Asked Questions

How hard is it to get a loan to start a restaurant?

Harder than for most businesses, because there are no sales yet and the lender is underwriting you. Equipment financing is the easiest piece to get, since the kitchen secures it. An SBA start-up loan takes longer, 30-60+ days, and needs at least 10% of the project cost from you.

How much would a $50,000 business loan cost?

At 12% APR over 60 months, $50,000 costs about $1,112 a month and $16,733 in total interest. At 19% over 36 months it is about $1,833 a month and $15,981 in interest. New restaurants financing equipment usually land somewhere in the 12-22% range.

Is $20,000 enough to open a restaurant?

Rarely on its own, when a commercial kitchen fit-out typically runs $50,000-$200,000+. It works better as your equity: at the 10% minimum an SBA start-up loan requires, $20,000 supports about $200,000 of total project cost. Taking over a space with equipment already in place also lowers what you need.

What credit score do you need for restaurant startup financing?

A 600+ score opens most equipment lenders, and many programs for newer businesses look for 660-680+. SBA lenders typically favor 650-680+. Below 600, expect a specialty lender, a larger down payment or a cosigner.

Should a new restaurant lease or buy kitchen equipment?

Many new restaurants lease, because lease payments usually run about 10-20% lower and leave more cash for opening. Buying with a loan costs less over time if you will run the equipment for years. Some operators do both: buy the hood and walk-in, lease the rest.

How long does restaurant equipment financing take?

Initial approvals typically come back in 24-72 hours when the file is complete, and funding usually lands within 5-10 business days. Allow 1-2 weeks from application to delivery so the equipment arrives before opening day.
