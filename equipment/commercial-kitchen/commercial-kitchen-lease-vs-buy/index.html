Restaurant Equipment Leasing: When to Lease and When to Buy

How a kitchen equipment lease works, what it costs next to a loan, and which one fits your restaurant.

← Back to Commercial Kitchen Financing All Articles

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

Restaurant equipment leasing lets you use ovens, hoods, refrigeration and the rest of a kitchen for a fixed monthly payment while the lessor owns the gear. Payments usually run about 10-20% lower than a loan on the same equipment, and at the end you buy it for $1, 10% or fair market value, return it, or renew. Most lessors want 600+ credit. Kitchen equipment prices about 1-3 points above other equipment, so 720+ with 2+ years in business usually lands around 8-12% APR. Approvals typically take 24-72 hours.

How Does a Restaurant Equipment Lease Work?

A lessor buys the equipment from your dealer, and you pay a fixed monthly amount to use it, usually over 36-60 months. You do not own it during the lease. At the end you buy it, return it, or renew, depending on the kind of lease you signed. A loan works the other way: the lender pays the dealer, the equipment is yours from day one, and you pay it off over 36-72 months.

The choice matters because a kitchen is expensive. A full fit-out can run $50,000-$200,000+, and the structure you pick decides your monthly payment, your taxes and whether you own anything when the payments stop.

Which Lease Structure Fits Your Kitchen?

Most restaurant equipment leases fall into one of three types. Here is how each compares with a straight loan:

StructureMonthlyAt the endBest for
Equipment loanHighestYou own it from day oneGear you will run until it wears out
$1 buyout leaseClose to a loanPay $1 (or a nominal amount) to own itOwning at the end, works like a loan
10% option leaseLowerOption to buy for 10% of the original costKeeping a set price to own
FMV leaseLowestBuy at fair market value, return or renewGear you plan to replace or upgrade

The lower the monthly payment, the more you usually pay at the end if you decide to keep the equipment. An FMV lease is cheap month to month, but buying a working combi oven at fair market value after five years can cost more than a loan would have.

What Do Restaurant Equipment Leases Cost?

Lessors and lenders price kitchen equipment off your credit tier, then add about 1-3 points because ovens and fryers resell for less than trucks or construction machines. Starting points by tier, before that add-on: 720+ with 2+ years in business, 7-9% APR; 680-719, 10-13%; 640-679, 13-16%; 580-639, 16-22%. A 600+ score opens most lenders, and most programs want to see about $10,000 or more a month in deposits. Typical restaurant deals run $25,000-$150,000. Full rate tables are on the commercial kitchen equipment financing page.

Lease vs Buy: A $120,000 Kitchen Example

Say you finance a $120,000 kitchen fit-out with 10% down ($12,000). As a loan of $108,000 at 11% APR over 60 months, the payment is about $2,348 a month, $140,891 over the term, and the equipment is yours at the end.

A lease on the same package usually runs about 10-20% lower, roughly $1,879 to $2,113 a month. You keep that difference every month, but you do not build ownership, and keeping the gear at the end means paying the buyout. For a concept you expect to run for years, the loan usually wins on total cost. For a first location, or equipment you expect to swap out, the lower payment and the option to upgrade often win. Model your own numbers in the payment calculator.

Are Lease Payments Tax Deductible?

Operating lease payments are generally deductible as a business expense. With a loan, you deduct the interest and depreciate the equipment, often with Section 179, which can let you deduct a large share of the cost in the first year. A $1 buyout lease works like a financed purchase, so it may qualify for Section 179 the way a loan does; a true operating lease does not. Ask your CPA which structure helps more this year; lease tax benefits covers the details.

When Should a Restaurant Lease Instead of Buy?

Lease when you are opening a first location and want to keep cash for payroll and inventory, when the menu or concept may change, or when the equipment dates quickly. Buy when the concept is proven and you will run the equipment until it wears out, which is how many established operators handle ranges, hoods and walk-ins. Plenty of restaurants mix the two: a loan for the hood system and walk-in, a lease for the combi oven and dish machine. If you have not opened yet, read restaurant startup financing first.

What Restaurant Equipment Can You Lease?

Most of the kitchen: ranges, griddles, fryers and ovens, hoods and exhaust, reach-in and walk-in refrigeration, prep equipment and dishwashing. You can bundle several items into one lease from one dealer. For cold storage specifically, see restaurant refrigeration financing and walk-in cooler financing.

How to Get Approved

Lessors and lenders ask for much the same file: the dealer quote with every item listed, 3-6 months of bank statements, and for larger deals tax returns. Leases and loans have similar credit requirements. Initial approvals typically come back in 24-72 hours, and funding usually lands within 5-10 business days. Under 2 years in business, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor. Get matched with lenders that offer both leases and loans and compare them on the same package.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to lease or buy restaurant equipment?

Buy with a loan if you will run the equipment for years: you own it, and you can claim depreciation, often with Section 179. Lease if you want a lower payment, usually about 10-20% lower, or expect to replace the gear. A first location or a menu that may change often favors a lease.

How does a restaurant equipment lease work?

A lessor buys the equipment from the dealer and you pay a fixed monthly amount to use it, usually over 36-60 months. At the end you buy it ($1, 10% or fair market value, depending on the lease), return it, or renew. Read the end-of-term terms before you sign, because that is where the total cost is decided.

Are restaurant equipment lease payments tax deductible?

Operating lease payments are generally deductible as a business expense. With a loan you deduct interest and depreciate the equipment instead, often using Section 179. Which works better depends on your tax year, so confirm with your CPA.

What credit score do you need to lease restaurant equipment?

A 600+ FICO opens most lessors and equipment lenders, and 720+ gets the best rates. Between 550 and 599 you are usually looking at a specialty lender. Below 550, expect 25% or more down or a cosigner.

Can a new restaurant lease kitchen equipment?

Yes. Many programs accept businesses with 6+ months of history and 660-680+ credit, because the equipment secures the deal. Under 2 years in business, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor. If you have not opened yet, the lender leans harder on your personal credit and your opening plan.

Who has the best restaurant equipment financing?

There is no single best lessor. Dealer programs are convenient, banks often price lowest for strong, established files, and independent equipment lenders tend to take more restaurant risk. The best offer depends on your credit, time open and the equipment, so compare at least two or three quotes on the same package.
