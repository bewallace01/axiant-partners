What Is a Floor Scrubber?

A commercial floor scrubber is a powered cleaning machine that dispenses solution, agitates the floor with a brush or pad, and recovers the dirty water in one pass. The recovered solution leaves the floor close to dry, which means a contract crew can scrub a supermarket aisle or a school hallway during business hours without coning off the area. Auto-scrubber is the more modern term and refers to the same machine class.

Three sizes dominate the market. Compact walk-behind scrubbers with 17 to 20-inch scrub paths fit restrooms, classrooms, and tight retail floors. Larger walk-behinds with 24 to 32-inch paths handle supermarkets and warehouse aisles. Ride-on auto-scrubbers with 28 to 50-inch paths cover distribution centers, big-box retail, hospitals, and arenas where floor area runs into hundreds of thousands of square feet. Tennant, Karcher, Nilfisk, NSS, and Minuteman are the brands you will see on most dealer floors. Janitorial contractors, in-house facility teams, and BSC (building service contractor) operators are the typical buyers, alongside schools and hospitals running scrubbers in-house.

Pricing scales with scrub path, tank capacity, and battery type. New compact walk-behinds start near $3,000 and top out around $8,000. Larger walk-behinds run $8,000 to $15,000. Entry ride-on scrubbers begin around $15,000 and large ride-ons with lithium batteries can pass $50,000. Used scrubbers in good condition typically cost 30 to 50 percent less than new equivalents.

How Do You Finance a Floor Scrubber?

Four product categories cover the vast majority of floor scrubber purchases. Equipment loans put the scrubber on your balance sheet and use the machine itself as collateral, so down payments stay low (often 0–10%) and decisions come back fast. Equipment leases shift the asset off the balance sheet and lower the monthly payment by 15 to 30 percent compared to a loan, with a fair-market or $1 buyout at the end of term. SBA 7(a) loans work when the scrubber is bundled with vans, working capital, or an acquisition into a single $150K+ package. Working capital loans rarely fit a single-unit scrubber purchase but make sense when you are buying a fleet of 10+ units during a contract ramp.

Cleaning operators usually win the deal one of two ways. The first is a single-asset equipment loan when a contract win requires putting one or two scrubbers in service immediately — equipment financing closes inside 48 hours and matches the cash-flow profile of net-30 commercial invoices. The second is a multi-unit lease structured around a 36-month contract term, where lease payments line up with the contract revenue and the lessor takes the residual risk on resale. Both structures are common across cleaning business financing programs and lenders who specialize in this industry understand the seasonal demand swings.

Financing Options for Floor Scrubbers

Equipment Financing (Loans)

An equipment loan is the default tool for a single floor scrubber purchase. The scrubber collateralizes the loan, so 0–10% down covers most approvals at 600+ FICO. Terms run 36 to 60 months for walk-behinds and 48 to 72 months for ride-ons; rates currently sit at 8–15% APR depending on credit and time in business. The loan is on your books, you take Section 179 and bonus depreciation, and the machine is yours at payoff.

Equipment Lease

Operating leases keep the scrubber off your balance sheet and treat payments as a deductible operating expense. Monthly payment runs lower than a loan because you only finance the depreciation, not the full asset value. At end-of-term you return the machine, buy at fair market value, or upgrade to a newer unit. Capital leases (often $1-buyout) behave like a loan for tax purposes but stretch the term out further. Leases fit operators who replace scrubbers every 3 to 5 years.

SBA Loans

An SBA 7(a) loan rarely makes sense for a single $5,000 walk-behind. It does make sense when the scrubber package is part of a $150K+ acquisition, build-out, or multi-unit fleet purchase combined with vans and working capital. Terms stretch to 10 years on equipment-heavy SBA deals, with 10 to 20 percent down and rates pegged to prime + 2.75–4.75. Approval timelines are 30 to 60 days, so use SBA when you have planning runway. SBA loan structures reward strong personal credit (680+) and 2+ years of operating history.

Working Capital

Working capital loans suit fleet-scale scrubber purchases (10+ units) during a major contract ramp where speed matters more than the lowest possible rate. Funds hit your account in 1 to 3 days, you spend them however you need, and repayment is daily or weekly over 6 to 24 months. Rates run higher than equipment loans because the financing is unsecured, but the structure is right when timing trumps cost.

Credit Score & Approval

Floor scrubber financing breaks into clear credit tiers. 550–599 FICO qualifies for asset-backed equipment financing through specialty lenders, usually with 15–20% down and rates in the 14–20% range. 600–649 opens up the standard equipment financing market, 0–10% down, rates 10–15%. 650–679 unlocks better terms with most equipment finance companies and most lease structures. 680+ qualifies you for SBA pricing and the lowest equipment rates (8–11%). Most janitorial startups in operation 6 to 12 months can finance a scrubber at 600+ FICO with 12 months of bank statements showing consistent revenue.

Documentation is light for scrubber-sized deals. A one-page application, 3 months of business bank statements, and the dealer quote close most approvals under $25,000. Deals over $50,000 typically add a year of tax returns and a current profit and loss. Time to fund: 1–2 business days for application-only deals, 3 to 5 days when full financials are required. SBA adds 30 to 60 days of underwriting and closing time. Compare this to financing a heavier asset like a backhoe — scrubber deals close faster precisely because the asset is small and well-understood.
