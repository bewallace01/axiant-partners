Excavator Lease vs Loan: Payments, Lease-to-Own and Which Costs Less

How an excavator lease works, what it costs each month next to a loan, and when owning the machine wins.

← Back to Excavator Financing All Articles
Home › Equipment Financing Guides › Excavators › Excavator Lease vs Loan

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

An excavator lease lets you run the machine for a fixed monthly payment, usually over 36-60 months, without paying for it up front. A fair market value lease typically runs about 10-20% below a loan on the same machine. On a $150,000 excavator with 10% down, that is roughly $2,190-$2,460 a month, against about $2,737 on an 8%, 60-month loan. A $1 buyout lease ends in ownership and prices like a loan. Most lessors want 600+ credit.

How Does an Excavator Lease Work?

The leasing company buys the machine from the dealer, and you pay a fixed monthly amount to use it. You do not own it during the term. At the end you buy it, hand it back or renew, and which of those makes sense depends on the type of lease you signed. With a loan, the lender pays the dealer, you own the machine from day one, and the lender files a lien until it is paid off.

The decision matters because of the price. A compact excavator starts around $80,000, a mid-size machine runs $150,000-$250,000, and large production units go from $250,000 to $500,000 and up. On numbers like that, a few hundred dollars a month adds up fast.

What Kinds of Excavator Leases Are There?

Most contractors end up choosing between four contracts:

Equipment loanHighest; full price plus interestYou own itMachines you will run for years
$1 buyout leaseClose to a loanPay $1 and own itOwning, with lease paperwork
10% purchase optionA little lowerOption to buy for 10% of costA set price to own later
Fair market value leaseLowest, about 10-20% below a loanBuy at market value, return or renewUpgrading every 3-5 years

The real question is whether you want the machine after the term. If yes, a loan or a $1 buyout lease is usually cheaper in the end. If you expect to trade up in 3-5 years, a fair market value lease keeps the payment down and hands the resale risk to the lessor. Read the hour limits on any fair market value lease; going over them costs money at turn-in.

How Much Is an Excavator Lease Payment?

Take a $150,000 mid-size excavator with 10% down ($15,000), so $135,000 financed over 60 months:

  • Loan at 8% APR: about $2,737 a month.
  • Loan at 12% APR: about $3,003 a month.
  • Fair market value lease: about 10-20% below the 8% loan, roughly $2,190-$2,460 a month.

The lease payment is lower because you are paying for the machine's use during the term, not its full price. That gap shrinks with a $1 buyout lease, which is priced to pay the machine off. Your rate depends mostly on credit: about 7-9% APR at 720+ with 2+ years in business, 10-13% at 680-719, 13-16% at 640-679 and 16-22% at 580-639. Model your own numbers in the payment calculator.

Is It Cheaper to Lease or Buy an Excavator?

Per month, the lease. Over the life of the machine, usually the loan, if you keep it. Contractors who run a machine 7-10 years or more tend to come out ahead owning it: the payments stop, and the machine is still worth something on the balance sheet. Contractors who upgrade every 3-5 years often do better leasing, because they skip the trade-in and the resale guesswork.

Cash up front differs too. A loan on a new machine often needs 0-10% down, and a used one 10-20%. A lease may only ask for the first and last payment or a refundable deposit, and some programs go to zero down for strong credit. Compare total out-of-pocket over the term, not just the payment. How Are Excavator Leases Taxed?

Operating lease payments are generally deductible as a business expense (Section 162) in the year you pay them, with no depreciation schedule to track. With a loan you own the machine, so you deduct the interest and depreciate the excavator, either over 5-7 years under MACRS or faster with Section 179 where it applies. Which one saves more depends on your tax year, so run it past your CPA before you pick a structure.

Is It Easy to Lease or Finance an Excavator?

For a contractor with a couple of years behind them, usually yes. The excavator secures the deal, so lessors and lenders care about the machine's value as much as your score. A 600+ FICO opens most lenders, 550-599 usually means a specialty lender, and below 550 you will typically need 25%+ down or a cosigner. Under 2 years in business, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor.

Initial approvals typically come back in 24-72 hours, and funding usually lands within 5-10 business days. For a pre-owned machine, see used excavator financing.

Who Offers the Best Excavator Lease?

There is no single best lessor. Dealer and manufacturer programs are convenient and sometimes run promotional rates on new machines. Banks price well for strong files but move slower. Independent equipment lenders take more used machines and more credit profiles. The best offer is the one that fits your credit, the machine's age and how long you plan to keep it, and the only way to know is to compare a few. Frequently Asked Questions

How does an excavator lease work?

The leasing company buys the excavator and you pay a fixed monthly amount to use it, usually for 36-60 months. At the end you buy it, return it or renew, depending on the lease type. A fair market value lease can also cap the hours you run the machine.

Can you lease to own an excavator?

Yes. A $1 buyout lease is built for that: you make the payments and own the machine for $1 at the end, so it prices close to a loan. A 10% purchase option lease has a slightly lower payment and lets you buy the machine for 10% of its original cost.

How long is a typical excavator lease?

Most excavator leases run 36-60 months. A shorter term means a higher payment and a faster upgrade cycle. Loans on new machines usually run longer, around 48-72 months.

Is it easy to finance or lease an excavator?

For an established contractor, usually yes, because the machine secures the deal. A 600+ score opens most lenders, and initial approvals typically come back in 24-72 hours. Under 2 years in business, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor.

Are excavator lease payments tax deductible?

Operating lease payments are generally deductible as a business expense in the year you pay them. If you own the machine through a loan, you deduct interest and depreciation instead, including Section 179 where it applies. Check the structure with your CPA before you sign.

What credit score do you need to lease an excavator?

Most lessors look for 600+, and 720+ with 2+ years in business gets the best pricing, around 7-9% APR on the loan side. Between 550 and 599 you are usually looking at a specialty lender. Below 550, expect 25% or more down or a cosigner.
