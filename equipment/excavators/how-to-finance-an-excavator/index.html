How to Finance an Excavator

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for construction and earthmoving businesses

← Back to Excavator Financing

Last updated: March 4, 2026

Excavators cost $80,000–$500,000+ depending on size and condition. Most construction and earthmoving businesses finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to project revenue.

What Is an Excavator?

An excavator is a heavy construction machine with a boom, arm (stick), and bucket used for digging, trenching, demolition, and material handling. It moves on crawler tracks or wheels and rotates 360 degrees. Excavators range from compact models to large machines that move massive amounts of earth. They are commonly used in construction, mining, road building, and land development for tasks like digging foundations, grading, and loading trucks.

How Do You Finance an Excavator?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Excavators hold value well, so financing is often easier than unsecured credit.

How Much Does an Excavator Cost?

Excavator prices vary widely by size, brand, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing and set realistic down payment and monthly payment expectations.

New Excavator Prices

New excavators range from roughly $80,000 for compact/mini models (under 6 tons) to $150,000–$250,000 for mid-size machines (10–25 tons), and $250,000–$500,000+ for large excavators (30+ tons). Top brands like Caterpillar, Komatsu, and John Deere typically command premium prices. Hydraulic excavators with advanced features, cab options, and attachments add cost. Factor in delivery, setup, and any attachments (buckets, thumbs, hammers) when budgeting.

Used Excavator Prices

Used excavators typically cost 30–50% less than new equivalents, depending on age, hours, and condition. A 5-year-old mid-size excavator might run $70,000–$120,000. Older machines or those with high hours sell for less but may require more maintenance. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 5–7 years old; older machines may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Always get an equipment inspection before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Size (machine weight), brand, engine tier, cab type (standard vs sealed), attachments, and regional demand all affect excavator pricing. Auction and dealer prices can differ. Obtain a written quote from your dealer or seller—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Excavators

Several financing structures work for excavators. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the equipment at the end of the term. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the excavator when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 24–72 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15% depending on credit and lender. Because excavators hold value, lenders often offer competitive terms. Ideal if you plan to use the excavator long-term and want to build equity.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans because you're paying for use, not full ownership. At lease end, you may return the equipment, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade to a newer model. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses for tax purposes; capital leases function like loans. Leasing suits businesses that want to preserve cash flow, upgrade regularly, or prefer off-balance-sheet treatment. Excavators with strong residual values often qualify for attractive lease terms.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans can finance excavators with longer terms (7–10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. SBA loans suit established businesses planning larger purchases or combining equipment with real estate or working capital. Requirements include 2+ years in business (typically), solid credit, and adequate cash flow. If timing allows, SBA financing can reduce monthly payments significantly.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are flexible but generally carry higher rates and shorter terms. They're better suited for operating expenses than large equipment purchases. Use working capital for materials and payroll; use equipment financing for the excavator itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Step-by-Step: How to Finance an Excavator

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose the excavator that fits your jobs—size, brand, attachments. Get a written quote from the dealer or seller including delivery and any add-ons.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and a balance sheet. Have your equipment quote and business info ready.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple equipment lenders. Consider rates, terms, down payment requirements, and approval speed. Axiant Partners matches businesses with lenders experienced in construction equipment.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation. Most applications can be submitted online.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer. Funds typically go directly to the seller; you take possession of the excavator.

Tips to Get Approved for Excavator Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors on your report, and avoid new credit applications before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements, consistent deposits, and organized financials speed approval and improve terms.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used excavator may cost less and require a smaller loan, improving approval odds and cash flow.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate or improve approval when credit is borderline.
  • Choose equipment with strong resale value. Major brands and popular models hold value better, making lenders more comfortable.

Excavator Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used excavator?

Yes. Many lenders finance used excavators, typically those 5–7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Resale value and condition affect approval. Get an inspection and maintain records of maintenance and hours.

What credit score is required for excavator financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Because excavator financing is asset-backed, some programs work with 580+ when revenue, down payment, and time in business are strong.

How long does excavator financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready—financials, equipment quote, business info—speeds the process. Apply early if you need equipment by a specific date.

Can startups finance excavators?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1–2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer with strong credit. SBA loans and some specialized programs can help newer businesses.

Is leasing better than buying an excavator?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the excavator long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals.
