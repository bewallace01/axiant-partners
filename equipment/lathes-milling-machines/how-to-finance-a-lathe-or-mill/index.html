How to Finance a Lathe or Mill

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for lathes and milling machines

← Back to Equipment by Type

Last updated: March 5, 2026

Lathes and milling machines—manual and CNC—cost $20,000–$150,000+ new depending on size and type, and typically 25–45% less used. Most manufacturers finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to production revenue.

What Are Lathes and Milling Machines?

A lathe rotates a workpiece while cutting tools shape it—used for turning, boring, threading, and facing operations. A milling machine holds the workpiece stationary while rotating cutters remove material—used for milling, drilling, and boring. Both come in manual and CNC (computer numerical control) versions. Manual machines are operator-controlled; CNC machines use programmed instructions for repeatable precision. Lathes range from small bench lathes to large engine lathes. Milling machines include vertical mills, horizontal mills, and knee mills. They are essential for job shops, machinists, and manufacturers who produce precision parts.

How Do You Finance a Lathe or Mill?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Lathes and mills are common manufacturing equipment with strong resale value, so financing is widely available through manufacturing lenders.

How Much Does a Lathe or Mill Cost?

Lathe and milling machine prices vary by type (manual vs CNC), size, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Lathe and Mill Prices

New manual lathes range from roughly $20,000–$80,000 depending on swing and bed length. New manual mills (knee mills, bed mills) typically cost $25,000–$75,000. New CNC lathes range from roughly $80,000–$200,000+; CNC mills and machining centers from $80,000–$250,000+. Top brands like Haas, Mazak, Okuma, South Bend, and Grizzly add cost. Size, precision, and control system drive price.

Used Lathe and Mill Prices

Used lathes and mills typically cost 25–45% less than new equivalents, depending on age, hours, and condition. A 5-year-old CNC lathe might run $50,000–$120,000. Older manual machines can start around $5,000–$25,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 7–10 years old. Always inspect ways, spindle, and (for CNC) control system before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Type (manual vs CNC), size (swing, bed length, travel), brand, precision grade, and regional demand all affect lathe and mill pricing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Lathes and Milling Machines

Several financing structures work for lathes and mills. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the machine when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 36–72 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Lathes and mills hold value well, so financing is often accessible. Ideal if you plan to use the machine long-term.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return the machine, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Leasing suits manufacturers who want to preserve cash flow or upgrade regularly.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) loans can finance lathes and mills with longer terms and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. Suit established manufacturers combining equipment with working capital. Requirements include 2+ years in business (typically), solid credit, and adequate cash flow.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are better suited for operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use equipment financing for the lathe or mill to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Lathe and Mill Financing Rates and Terms

Interest rates for lathe and mill financing typically range from 6–15% for equipment loans and leases. Terms commonly run 36–72 months for standard equipment financing; SBA loans can extend to 7–10 years. A $75,000 CNC lathe financed at 8% over 60 months would result in roughly $1,520/month. Step-by-Step: How to Finance a Lathe or Mill

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose manual or CNC lathe/mill based on your part requirements. Get a written quote from the dealer.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and equipment quote.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple manufacturing equipment lenders. Axiant Partners matches manufacturers with lenders experienced in manufacturing equipment financing.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer.

Tips to Get Approved for Lathe or Mill Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors, and avoid new credit before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements and customer contracts strengthen your application.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used lathe or mill may cost significantly less. Inspect ways and spindle carefully.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate.
  • Choose equipment with strong resale value. Major brands hold value better, making lenders more comfortable.

Lathe and Mill Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used lathe or mill?

Yes. Many lenders finance used lathes and milling machines, typically those 7–10 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Hours, condition, and brand affect approval.

What credit score is required for lathe or mill financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Manufacturing equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does lathe or mill financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Manual vs CNC—does it affect financing?

Lenders finance both manual and CNC lathes and mills. CNC equipment typically costs more and may qualify for longer terms. Price and condition matter more than manual vs CNC.

Is leasing better than buying a lathe or mill?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the machine long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow.
