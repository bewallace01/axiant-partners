How to Finance a Mini Excavator

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for construction and landscaping businesses

← Back to Equipment by Type

Last updated: March 5, 2026

Mini excavators cost $25,000–$100,000+ depending on size and configuration. Most construction, landscaping, and excavation businesses finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to project revenue.

What Is a Mini Excavator?

A mini excavator is a compact excavator, typically under 6 tons, that performs the same digging and trenching tasks as full-size excavators but in tight or confined spaces. It has a boom, arm, and bucket and often features rubber tracks to minimize ground damage. Mini excavators are commonly used in residential construction, landscaping, utility work, urban projects, and indoor demolition where full-size excavators cannot fit. They excel at foundation digging, small trenching, pool excavation, and site prep.

How Do You Finance a Mini Excavator?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Mini excavators hold value well and are widely used in construction and landscaping, so financing is often accessible.

How Much Does a Mini Excavator Cost?

Mini excavator prices vary by operating weight, dig depth, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing and set realistic down payment and monthly payment expectations.

New Mini Excavator Prices

New mini excavators range from roughly $25,000–$40,000 for compact models (under 2 tons, 5–8 feet dig depth) to $40,000–$70,000 for mid-size machines (2–4 tons, 8–10 feet dig depth), and $70,000–$100,000+ for larger compact excavators (4–6 tons). Top brands like Caterpillar, Kubota, John Deere, and Yanmar typically command premium prices. Cab options, rubber vs steel tracks, and attachments (buckets, thumbs, augers) add cost. Factor in delivery and setup when budgeting.

Used Mini Excavator Prices

Used mini excavators typically cost 30–50% less than new equivalents, depending on age, hours, and condition. A 5-year-old mid-size mini excavator might run $25,000–$50,000. Older machines or those with high hours sell for less but may require more maintenance. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 5–7 years old; older machines may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Always get an equipment inspection before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Operating weight, dig depth, brand, cab type, track type (rubber vs steel), attachments, and regional demand all affect mini excavator pricing. Auction and dealer prices can differ. Obtain a written quote from your dealer or seller—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Mini Excavators

Several financing structures work for mini excavators. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the equipment at the end of the term. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the mini excavator when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 24–72 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15% depending on credit and lender. Because mini excavators hold value and are versatile, lenders often offer competitive terms. Ideal if you plan to use the mini excavator long-term and want to build equity.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans because you're paying for use, not full ownership. At lease end, you may return the equipment, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade to a newer model. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses for tax purposes; capital leases function like loans. Leasing suits businesses that want to preserve cash flow, upgrade regularly, or prefer off-balance-sheet treatment. Mini excavators with strong residual values often qualify for attractive lease terms.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans can finance mini excavators with longer terms (7–10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. SBA loans suit established businesses planning larger purchases or combining equipment with real estate or working capital. Requirements include 2+ years in business (typically), solid credit, and adequate cash flow. If timing allows, SBA financing can reduce monthly payments significantly.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are flexible but generally carry higher rates and shorter terms. They're better suited for operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use working capital for materials and payroll; use equipment financing for the mini excavator itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Step-by-Step: How to Finance a Mini Excavator

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose the mini excavator that fits your jobs—size, brand, attachments. Get a written quote from the dealer or seller including delivery and any add-ons.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and a balance sheet. Have your equipment quote and business info ready.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple equipment lenders. Consider rates, terms, down payment requirements, and approval speed. Axiant Partners matches businesses with lenders experienced in construction equipment and landscaping equipment.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation. Most applications can be submitted online.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer. Funds typically go directly to the seller; you take possession of the mini excavator.

Tips to Get Approved for Mini Excavator Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors on your report, and avoid new credit applications before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements, consistent deposits, and organized financials speed approval and improve terms.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used mini excavator may cost less and require a smaller loan, improving approval odds and cash flow.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate or improve approval when credit is borderline.
  • Choose equipment with strong resale value. Major brands and popular models hold value better, making lenders more comfortable.

Mini Excavator Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used mini excavator?

Yes. Many lenders finance used mini excavators, typically those 5–7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Resale value and condition affect approval. Get an inspection and maintain records of maintenance and hours.

What credit score is required for mini excavator financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Because mini excavator financing is asset-backed, some programs work with 580+ when revenue, down payment, and time in business are strong.

How long does mini excavator financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready—financials, equipment quote, business info—speeds the process. Apply early if you need equipment by a specific date.

Can startups finance mini excavators?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1–2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer with strong credit. SBA loans and some specialized programs can help newer businesses.

Is leasing better than buying a mini excavator?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the mini excavator long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals.
