What Is a Stump Grinder?

A stump grinder is a machine with a rotating cutting wheel fitted with teeth that grinds tree stumps below grade, turning them into wood chips. It removes the visible stump and roots in the ground so the area can be replanted, paved, or used for other purposes. Stump grinders range from compact walk-behind or tow-behind units to large self-propelled or tracked machines.

Stump grinders are commonly used in tree care, landscaping, and forestry for residential and commercial stump removal after tree removal or land clearing. Arborists, landscapers, and forestry contractors rely on stump grinders as core equipment. Understanding what stump grinders are and how they're used helps lenders assess your financing application-they want to see that the equipment supports revenue-generating work. How Much Does a Stump Grinder Cost?

Stump grinder prices vary by size, type (walk-behind, tow-behind, self-propelled), brand, and whether you buy new or used. New stump grinders range from roughly $20,000-$45,000 for compact walk-behind or tow-behind units (small stumps, residential use) to $45,000-$70,000 for mid-size self-propelled machines (larger stumps, commercial use), and $70,000-$95,000 for professional tracked or ride-on grinders (large stumps, high-volume commercial work). Top brands like Vermeer, Bandit, and Carlton command premium prices.

Used stump grinders typically cost 30-50% less than new equivalents. A 5-year-old mid-size stump grinder might run $25,000-$45,000. Older or compact used machines can start around $10,000-$20,000. Many lenders finance used stump grinders up to 5-7 years old; older machines may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Always inspect the cutting wheel and engine before purchasing used. Obtain a written quote from your dealer or seller-lenders use this to structure your financing. Financing used equipment guide.

What to Have Ready Before You Apply

Stump grinders sit at the small end of financed equipment, and the file reflects that — short, quick, and frequently approved on an application alone.

Quote with model, serial and engine specification

· Towable and self-propelled machines finance identically but are valued very differently. Say which one the quote is for.

Hour meter reading on a used unit

· Grinders work in short bursts, so hours are lower than on most equipment and each one counts for more.

Cutting wheel and teeth condition

· The wheel is the machine's most expensive consumable. A worn wheel is a legitimate price adjustment.

Three months of bank statements

· Usually sufficient at this ticket size for an established business.

EIN and entity documents

· Tree services are often sole proprietorships or single-member LLCs; make sure the application matches how the business actually files.

Personal financial statement if the business is new

· A grinder is frequently the first financed asset for a tree service, and personal credit carries more of the decision at that stage.

Have the towing vehicle in mind. It is not part of the lender's file, but a machine you cannot move to the job is not a productive asset.

Tips to Get Approved for Stump Grinder Financing

Match the wheel to the stumps you actually grind.

Residential removals and municipal contract work call for different machines. Buying the larger machine for occasional big stumps rarely pays.

Budget teeth as an operating cost.

Teeth are consumed steadily and replaced routinely. Treating them as a surprise distorts your job pricing.

Check the frame and the bearings on a used unit.

Grinders live in dirt and grit. Frame cracks and worn bearings are the failures that end a machine's life, and both are visible on inspection.

Keep it simple at this ticket size.

Application-only programs exist precisely for equipment in this range. A clean, short application usually beats a thick one.

Consider financing the truck and grinder together.

If both are being bought, a single transaction is normally cheaper and simpler than two.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Financing a Stump Grinder

Small-ticket equipment attracts small-ticket mistakes that cost real money:

Paying cash and starving the payroll.

A grinder financed over three years leaves the cash in the business. Tree services are seasonal and cash is the constraint far more often than the equipment is.

Buying a machine too wide for the gates you work through.

Access decides which residential jobs you can take. Measure before you commit.

Ignoring the wheel condition on a used purchase.

Replacing a cutting wheel can be a significant fraction of what you paid for the machine.

Skipping the written quote.

Even at small ticket sizes, no lender funds without one.

Not asking about insurance.

Tree work carries its own liability profile. Confirm what the lender requires, and confirm your policy covers the machine off-site.

Compare two or three offers even at this size — the spread on small-ticket equipment finance is proportionally wider than on large iron, not narrower.

Stump Grinder Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used stump grinder?

Yes. Many lenders finance used stump grinders, typically those 5-7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Resale value and condition affect approval.

What credit score is required?

Most lenders look for 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ qualify for the best rates. Asset-backed financing sometimes works with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does approval take?

1-5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans add 30-60+ days. Having documents ready-financials, equipment quote, business info-speeds the process.

Is leasing better than buying?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the stump grinder long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow, tax situation, and ownership goals. Equipment loan vs lease comparison.

Can startups finance stump grinders?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1-2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer with strong credit. SBA loans and some specialized programs can help newer businesses. Apply and we'll match you with lenders that fit your profile.

What documents are needed for stump grinder financing?

Typical requirements: 3-6 months of business bank statements, tax returns (business and personal if required), a recent profit and loss statement, equipment quote from your dealer, and business formation documents. Having these ready speeds approval. What lenders look at.
