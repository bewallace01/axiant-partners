How to Finance a Combine

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for grain and row-crop farmers

← Back to Combine Financing

Last updated: March 5, 2026

Combines (combine harvesters) cost $300,000–$500,000+ new depending on size and features, and typically 25–45% less used. Most grain and row-crop farmers finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and match costs to seasonal harvest revenue.

What Is a Combine Harvester?

A combine harvester (combine) is a machine that harvests grain crops by reaping, threshing, and winnowing in a single pass. Combines harvest wheat, corn, soybeans, oats, barley, and other small grains. Header type (grain, corn, flex) and width vary by crop. Combines range from smaller 200-bushel-capacity units to large 500+ bushel models. They are essential for grain and row-crop operations and represent one of the largest equipment investments on a farm. Newer models offer precision agriculture features, auto-steer, and yield monitoring.

How Do You Finance a Combine?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Combines are high-value agricultural equipment, so financing is widely available through agriculture lenders. Expect longer terms (5–7+ years) given the asset value.

How Much Does a Combine Cost?

Combine prices vary by size, brand, header type, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Combine Prices

New combines range from roughly $300,000–$400,000 for mid-size models to $400,000–$500,000+ for large, high-capacity units. Headers (grain, corn, flex) add $50,000–$150,000+ depending on width and type. Top brands like John Deere, Case IH, New Holland, and AGCO add cost. Grain tank capacity and technology features drive price.

Used Combine Prices

Used combines typically cost 25–45% less than new equivalents, depending on age, hours, capacity, and condition. A 5-year-old mid-size combine might run $180,000–$280,000. Older or smaller used units can start around $80,000–$150,000. Many lenders finance used equipment up to 7–10 years old. Always inspect engine hours, separator, and header before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Grain tank capacity, header type and width, brand, engine hours (for used), technology package, and regional demand all affect combine pricing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Combines

Several financing structures work for combines. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the combine at the end of the term. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the combine when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 60–84 months or longer. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Combines are high-value assets, so lenders often offer longer terms to keep monthly payments manageable. Ideal if you plan to use the combine long-term.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans because you're paying for use, not full ownership. At lease end, you may return the combine, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Leasing suits operations that want to preserve cash flow or upgrade regularly.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans can finance combines with longer terms (7–10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. SBA loans suit established operations. USDA guaranteed loans also support agricultural equipment. Requirements include 2+ years in business (typically), solid credit, and adequate cash flow.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are better suited for operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use equipment financing for the combine itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Credit score: Most equipment lenders look for 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates. See credit score requirements for equipment financing. Because combine financing is asset-backed, some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

Down payment: Expect 0–20% down depending on credit and equipment. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down; weaker credit often requires 15–20%. A larger down payment can improve approval and reduce monthly payments.

Time in business: Most lenders prefer 1–2+ years. Newer operations may need a co-signer or may qualify through SBA or USDA programs.

Revenue and documentation: Prepare bank statements, tax returns (Schedule F or business returns), profit & loss, and equipment quote. Acreage and crop mix may be requested for larger loans.

Combine Financing Rates and Terms

Interest rates for combine financing typically range from 6–15% for equipment loans and leases. Terms commonly run 60–84 months or longer for standard equipment financing; SBA loans can extend to 7–10+ years. A $400,000 combine financed at 8% over 84 months would result in roughly $5,800/month. Step-by-Step: How to Finance a Combine

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose the combine that fits your acreage and crop types. Get a written quote from the dealer including headers and options.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and equipment quote.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple agricultural equipment lenders. Axiant Partners matches farmers with lenders experienced in agriculture equipment financing.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer.

Tips to Get Approved for Combine Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors, and avoid new credit before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements and organized financials speed approval.
  • Consider used equipment. A quality used combine may cost significantly less. Inspect hours and condition carefully.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate.
  • Time your application. Apply well before harvest if you need the combine by a specific date.

Combine Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used combine?

Yes. Many lenders finance used combines, typically those 7–10 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Hours, header size, and condition affect approval.

What credit score is required for combine financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Agricultural equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does combine financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Can startups finance combines?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1–2+ years in business. New farmers may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer. SBA and USDA programs can help.

Is leasing better than buying a combine?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier upgrades. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the combine long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow and harvest volume.
