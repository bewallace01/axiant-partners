Choosing the Right Lab Equipment for Your Practice

Before applying for lab equipment financing, clarify your testing volume, test menu, and turnaround needs. Chemistry analyzers, hematology analyzers, centrifuges, microscopes, and point-of-care devices serve different workflows·primary care vs reference lab vs specialty clinic. Brands such as Abbott, Roche, Siemens, and Sysmex offer different throughput and reagent costs. Get written quotes that specify make, model, and pricing. Compare new vs used·many lenders finance used lab equipment when CLIA and regulatory requirements are met. Bundling lab equipment with imaging or surgical purchases can sometimes secure better terms. Consider reagent agreements, service contracts, and calibration requirements. Use our financing calculator to estimate monthly payments before you apply.

Documents That Speed Up Lab Equipment Approval

Lab lenders want to see revenue stability and compliance. Put together: bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, P&L, and a vendor quote detailing each analyzer or instrument with make, model, and price. If you run a CLIA lab, include your certificate. Applications with everything in place often get a response in 24–48 hours. Lenders evaluate credit, revenue, and the equipment. Reagent and vendor programs sometimes offer promotional terms·worth comparing to general equipment financing.

How Lab Equipment Is Taxed

Analyzers, centrifuges, and microscopes generally qualify for Section 179 and bonus depreciation. Lease payments are typically deductible as operating expenses. For a full picture of how equipment financing affects your taxes, see our overview and discuss with your CPA. Labs often finance to capture these benefits without tying up capital that could go to reagents or staffing.

Financing vs Cash: How Lab Practices Decide

Adding an in-office lab or moving from send-out to in-house testing is a big capital outlay. Many practices finance to spread that cost. Leasing fits when analyzers and instruments evolve quickly·chemistry and hematology tech may justify refreshes every 5–7 years. Vendor programs that bundle equipment with reagents can change total cost of ownership; weigh leasing vs loans against your upgrade plans and cash flow.
