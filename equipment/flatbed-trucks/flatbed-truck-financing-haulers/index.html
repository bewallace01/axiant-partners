Why Haulers Use Flatbed Trucks

Flatbeds suit loads that need top or side access: lumber, steel, construction materials, machinery, equipment. Step-decks add versatility for taller cargo. One financed flatbed can expand your hauling capacity and routes. See equipment financing by type.

Flatbed Truck Costs: New vs Used

New flatbed straight trucks run roughly $35,000-$75,000; flatbed trailers $25,000-$45,000; full combinations $60,000-$85,000+. Used 3-5 year old units typically cost 25-40% less. Get a written quote with make, model, year, VIN, and specs. See financing used equipment.

Financing Options: Loans vs Leases

Equipment loans spread the cost over 36-72 months; you own at payoff. Leases often have lower payments. See equipment loan vs lease. Use our calculator.

Financing Truck and Trailer Together

Many lenders finance the power unit and flatbed trailer as a package. Include both in your equipment quote. Bundled financing simplifies approval and payments.

Credit and Down Payment Requirements

Most lenders look for 600+ FICO; 680+ qualifies for the best rates. Down payments: 0-15% for new, 10-20% for used. See credit score for equipment financing and down payment requirements.

Documentation and Approval Timeline

Gather: truck/trailer quote, 3-6 months bank statements, tax returns, P&L, formation documents. Complete documentation speeds approval. Many lenders approve in 24-48 hours. See equipment financing requirements and approval timeline.

Rates and Monthly Payments

Rates typically run 6-14%. For a $55,000 flatbed at 8% over 60 months with 10% down, expect roughly $950-$1,050/month. Use our calculator. See typical equipment financing rates.

Flatbed vs Step-Deck

Both are routinely financed. Flatbeds are standard; step-decks suit taller loads. Include length, axles, and load capacity in your quote. Resale value is similar for quality units.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a flatbed truck cost?

New flatbed straight trucks run $35,000-$75,000; flatbed trailers $25,000-$45,000; combinations $60,000-$85,000+. Used 3-5 year old units may be 25-40% less.

Can I finance a flatbed truck and trailer together?

Yes. Many lenders finance the power unit and flatbed trailer as a package. Include both in your equipment quote.

What credit score do I need for flatbed truck financing?

Most lenders look for 600+ FICO. Scores of 680+ qualify for the best rates. Some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment support the application.

Flatbed vs step-deck: which is easier to finance?

Both are routinely financed. Include specs (length, axles, load capacity) in your quote.

How fast can I get approved for flatbed truck financing?

Many lenders approve within 24-48 hours when documentation is complete. Marketplace applications can return multiple offers in 1-3 business days.

Worked Example: Financing a Used Flatbed

An owner-operator wants to add a second flatbed — a three-year-old day cab with 400,000 miles — priced at $70,000. With a 10% down payment ($7,000) and a five-year term, the financed amount is $63,000. Because the truck is the collateral and it generates revenue from day one, a hauler with a year or two of operating history and decent credit can usually structure this without tying up working capital. The payment is set against the loads the truck is expected to run, so the equipment effectively pays for itself out of the freight it hauls.

Mileage and age matter more on trucks than on most equipment: a higher-mileage tractor may carry a shorter term or a larger down payment because the lender is matching the loan to the asset's remaining service life. A clean maintenance record and a verifiable purchase from a reputable dealer both help.

What Haulers Should Weigh

  • New vs. used — used flatbeds cost far less to finance, but weigh the term against expected mileage and upcoming maintenance.
  • Down payment vs. cash flow — a larger down payment lowers the payment but ties up reserves you may want for fuel, repairs, and slow weeks.
  • Time in business and credit — established operators see better terms; newer authorities may need more down or a co-signer.
  • Spec for the freight — financing the right trailer length, axle configuration, and tarps for your lanes protects resale value.

Can you finance a flatbed truck and trailer together?

Often yes. Lenders can bundle the tractor and a flatbed or step-deck trailer into one equipment loan, so you finance the working rig in a single payment rather than two separate deals.

What is the difference between a flatbed and a step-deck for financing?

Both finance similarly as vocational equipment; the choice is operational. A step-deck carries taller loads under height limits, while a flatbed is simpler and cheaper. Lenders care more about condition, age, and your hauling revenue than the deck type.

How much down payment for a flatbed truck?

Plan for 10–20% on a flatbed tractor or trailer, with strong credit and newer equipment at the low end and used or higher-mileage units toward the high end. Strong hauling revenue and time in business help lower it.
