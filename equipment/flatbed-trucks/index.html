What Is a Flatbed Truck?

A flatbed truck is a truck with an open flat deck (no sides or roof) for hauling oversized or uncontained cargo. The deck allows loading and unloading from the sides and top, ideal for construction materials, steel, machinery, lumber, and goods that need forklift or crane access. Flatbed trucks come in straight truck configurations (single unit with integrated deck) or as tractor-trailer combinations pulling flatbed trailers.

Industries using flatbeds include construction (rebar, steel beams, lumber), manufacturing (machinery, manufactured components), and logistics (specialty hauling, oversized freight). Understanding what flatbed trucks are and how they're used helps lenders assess your application-they want to see that the equipment supports revenue-generating operations. Deck length, GVWR, and payload capacity affect suitability for different loads.

Why Flatbed Truck Financing Is Different

Lenders view flatbed financing favorably because flatbeds hold value well and serve multiple industries. Peterbilt, Kenworth, Freightliner, and Mack vocational models retain 50-70% of value after 5 years. If a borrower defaults, the lender can recover a significant portion through resale. Trucking-specific lenders assess freight revenue, route stability, and industry underwriting-not just static financials.

Owner-operators and small carriers are primary flatbed buyers. Lenders who specialize in trucking equipment financing understand construction cycles, manufacturing demand, and logistics rates. Used flatbeds 5-7 years old are commonly financed. Equipment financing approval timelines are typically 1-5 days for qualified applications. TRAC leases are popular for flatbed trucks-they use a residual value to lower monthly payments.

Flatbed Truck Financing Options

Several financing structures work for flatbed trucks. Choose based on cash flow, tax situation, and whether you want to own the truck at the end of the term.

Working capital loans are flexible but generally carry higher rates and shorter terms. They're better suited for fuel, repairs, and payroll than large equipment purchases. Use working capital for operating expenses; use equipment financing for the flatbed itself to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset. Compare equipment loan vs lease in detail.

Equipment Loans

Borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the flatbed when the loan is paid off. Typically 0-20% down, terms 24-84 months. Interest rates 6-15% depending on credit. Ideal if you plan to use the truck long-term and want to build equity. See typical rates.

Equipment Leasing

Lower monthly payments than loans. TRAC leases are common for flatbed trucks-they use a residual to reduce the financed amount. At lease end, return the truck, purchase at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Loan vs lease.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans offer longer terms (7-10+ years) and lower down payments. Approval typically 30-60+ days. Best for established businesses planning fleet expansion or combining equipment with real estate. View SBA loans.

How Much Does a Flatbed Truck Cost?

Flatbed prices vary by configuration (straight truck vs tractor-trailer), deck length, brand, and whether you buy new or used. New flatbed trucks range from roughly $80,000-$120,000 for straight trucks with shorter decks to $120,000-$200,000+ for tractor-trailer combinations. Top brands like Peterbilt, Kenworth, Freightliner, Mack, and International command premium prices. Deck length, GVWR, and trailer specifications add cost.

Used flatbed trucks typically cost 30-50% less. A 5-year-old flatbed tractor-trailer might run $60,000-$120,000. Older or smaller used trucks can start around $40,000-$70,000. Many lenders finance used flatbeds up to 5-7 years old; older trucks may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Inspect deck, chassis, and mechanical condition before purchasing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer or seller-lenders use this to structure financing. Financing used equipment guide.

What to Have Ready Before You Apply

Gathering documentation before you start speeds approval. Have these ready when applying for flatbed truck financing:

  • 3-6 months of business bank statements - Lenders want to see consistent deposits and cash flow.
  • Last year's tax returns - Business and personal if required by the lender.
  • Recent profit and loss statement - Shows revenue and expenses.
  • Equipment quote or proposal - Written quote from your dealer with make, model, VIN, and total cost. Include trade-in if applicable. Lenders use this to structure the loan.
  • Business formation documents - Articles of incorporation, operating agreement, or similar.
  • Basic business information - EIN, formation date, ownership structure.

If you have existing equipment loans or leases, have those statements available. Lenders may ask for a voided check for ACH. The more organized your documentation, the faster you'll get funded. What do lenders look at when approving equipment financing.

Why Businesses Finance Flatbed Trucks Rather Than Pay Cash

Paying cash ties up working capital that could fund fuel, payroll, materials, or growth. Financing spreads the cost over the truck's useful life, matches expenses to revenue, and preserves liquidity. Equipment loans and leases also offer tax benefits-Section 179 and bonus depreciation for purchases, lease payments as operating expenses for leases.

Many businesses prefer to finance so they can keep reserves for emergencies or opportunities. Construction and logistics firms especially benefit from preserving cash for project mobilization and irregular revenue cycles. If your cost of capital is lower than the return on that cash elsewhere, financing makes sense. Even strong businesses often finance equipment to optimize cash flow.

Can you finance a used flatbed truck?

Yes. Many lenders finance used flatbed trucks, typically those 5-7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Mileage, condition, and deck integrity affect approval.

What credit score is required?

Most lenders look for 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ qualify for the best rates. Asset-backed financing sometimes works with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does approval take?

1-5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans add 30-60+ days. Having documents ready-financials, equipment quote, business info-speeds the process. How fast can equipment financing be approved.

Is leasing better than buying?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. TRAC leases are common for flatbed trucks.
