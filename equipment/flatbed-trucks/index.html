What Is a Flatbed Truck?

A flatbed truck is a truck with an open flat deck (no sides or roof) for hauling oversized or uncontained cargo. The deck allows loading and unloading from the sides and top, ideal for construction materials, steel, machinery, lumber, and goods that need forklift or crane access. Flatbed trucks come in straight truck configurations (single unit with integrated deck) or as tractor-trailer combinations pulling flatbed trailers.

Industries using flatbeds include construction (rebar, steel beams, lumber), manufacturing (machinery, manufactured components), and logistics (specialty hauling, oversized freight). Understanding what flatbed trucks are and how they're used helps lenders assess your application-they want to see that the equipment supports revenue-generating operations. Deck length, GVWR, and payload capacity affect suitability for different loads.

Why Flatbed Truck Financing Is Different

Lenders view flatbed financing favorably because flatbeds hold value well and serve multiple industries. Peterbilt, Kenworth, Freightliner, and Mack vocational models retain 50-70% of value after 5 years. If a borrower defaults, the lender can recover a significant portion through resale. Trucking-specific lenders assess freight revenue, route stability, and industry underwriting-not just static financials.

Owner-operators and small carriers are primary flatbed buyers. Lenders who specialize in trucking equipment financing understand construction cycles, manufacturing demand, and logistics rates. Used flatbeds 5-7 years old are commonly financed. Equipment financing approval timelines are typically 1-5 days for qualified applications. How Much Does a Flatbed Truck Cost?

Flatbed prices vary by configuration (straight truck vs tractor-trailer), deck length, brand, and whether you buy new or used. New flatbed trucks range from roughly $80,000-$120,000 for straight trucks with shorter decks to $120,000-$200,000+ for tractor-trailer combinations. Top brands like Peterbilt, Kenworth, Freightliner, Mack, and International command premium prices. Deck length, GVWR, and trailer specifications add cost.

Used flatbed trucks typically cost 30-50% less. A 5-year-old flatbed tractor-trailer might run $60,000-$120,000. Older or smaller used trucks can start around $40,000-$70,000. Many lenders finance used flatbeds up to 5-7 years old; older trucks may face shorter terms or require larger down payments. Inspect deck, chassis, and mechanical condition before purchasing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer or seller-lenders use this to structure financing. Financing used equipment guide.

What to Have Ready Before You Apply

Flatbed work is specialized freight, and the file is strongest when it shows both the equipment and the experience to haul on an open deck.

Quote or bill of sale with the VIN

· Include the deck length, width and material, and the deck rating.

Securement equipment in the deal

· Straps, chains, binders, tarps and coil racks are working equipment. Where they are on the invoice they can be financed with the truck.

CDL and endorsements

· Class A for tractor-trailer combinations. Some flatbed freight also calls for specific certifications.

USDOT number and operating authority

· Active authority for for-hire interstate work, or the lease agreement if you run under a carrier.

Odometer, engine and transmission specification

· Flatbed work is heavy and often mountainous. Drivetrain matters to both operation and resale.

Three to six months of bank statements or settlement statements

· Whichever reflects how you are actually paid.

If you haul oversize or specialized loads, mention it. Permitted freight pays better and the experience to run it is a genuine credit strength.

When to Apply for Flatbed Truck Financing

Apply when you know the freight you intend to haul, because flatbed specification follows the freight more closely than in any other segment. Steel, lumber, machinery and building materials all favor different deck lengths, ratings and securement setups, and a truck specified for the wrong one is harder to keep loaded.

For established carriers the practical trigger is a lane or a shipper commitment. Apply once it is in place; the agreement does more for the file than any amount of projection, and it also tells you the term the financing should be matched to.

Tips to Get Approved for Flatbed Truck Financing

Specify the deck for the freight, not for flexibility.

A deck that suits everything usually suits nothing especially well, and flatbed shippers pay for the right equipment.

Include the securement package on the invoice.

A full complement of straps, chains and tarps is a substantial cost. Financed with the truck it is spread; bought afterwards it comes straight out of cash.

Check the deck and crossmembers carefully on a used unit.

Deck damage is the flatbed equivalent of frame damage. It is visible if you look underneath.

Show the tarping experience.

Flatbed freight has a higher barrier than dry van, and demonstrated experience is credit-relevant in a way it is not in other segments.

Keep safety scores clean.

Securement violations are recorded and visible. They affect both freight access and financing.

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Financing a Flatbed Truck

Flatbed operations fail on preparation more than on price:

Buying the truck before the freight.

Open-deck freight is relationship-driven. Secure the lane first.

Skimping on securement equipment.

It is a safety issue, a compliance issue and a load-access issue, in that order.

Ignoring tarping labor and time.

Tarping adds time to every load and it is physically demanding. Price it into your rate.

Overlooking deck rating against the loads you plan to carry.

Concentrated loads such as coil and machinery require the right deck. Assuming otherwise is expensive.

Comparing offers on rate alone.

Term, fees and prepayment terms decide the total. Read the whole agreement.

Confirm the insurance requirement, including cargo coverage appropriate to open-deck freight, before funding.

Why Businesses Finance Flatbed Trucks Rather Than Pay Cash

Cash spent on equipment is cash not available for payroll, materials or the next opportunity. Financing spreads the cost over the working life of the flatbed truck and keeps reserves intact, and Section 179 and bonus depreciation reduce the after-tax cost further. Carriers feel it most sharply: fuel, insurance and maintenance are due weekly while freight bills settle in thirty to forty-five days.

Flatbed Truck Financing FAQ

Can you finance a used flatbed truck?

Yes. Many lenders finance used flatbed trucks, typically those 5-7 years old or newer. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Mileage, condition, and deck integrity affect approval.

What credit score is required?

Most lenders look for 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ qualify for the best rates. Asset-backed financing sometimes works with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does approval take?

1-5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA loans add 30-60+ days. Having documents ready-financials, equipment quote, business info-speeds the process. How fast can equipment financing be approved.

Is leasing better than buying?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, potential tax benefits, and easier upgrades when the lease ends. TRAC leases are common for flatbed trucks. Buying builds equity and works if you plan to use the truck long-term. Compare both based on your cash flow and plans. Equipment loan vs lease comparison.

Can startups finance flatbed trucks?

Startups face tighter requirements. Most equipment lenders prefer 1-2+ years in business. New businesses may need larger down payments, shorter terms, or a co-signer with strong credit. SBA loans and some specialized programs can help newer businesses. Apply and we'll match you with lenders that fit your profile.

What documents are needed for flatbed truck financing?

Typical requirements: 3-6 months of business bank statements, tax returns (business and personal if required), a recent profit and loss statement, equipment quote from your dealer, and business formation documents. Having these ready speeds approval. What lenders look at.

How do I estimate monthly payments for flatbed truck financing?

Use our financing calculator to model different scenarios. A $120,000 flatbed at 8% over 60 months is roughly $2,430/month. A $80,000 straight truck at 9% over 48 months is about $2,000/month. Rates typically range 6-15% depending on credit and lender.
