How to Finance a Pallet Jack

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for pallet jacks and hand trucks

Pallet jacks and hand trucks cost $500–$15,000+ per unit depending on type (manual vs electric) and capacity, and typically 20–35% less used. Many warehouses finance equipment instead of paying cash—especially when scaling fleet size for seasonal peaks or new facilities. Equipment loans and leases spread payments and preserve working capital.

What Are Pallet Jacks?

Pallet jacks (pallet trucks) lift and move palletized loads. Manual pallet jacks require operator effort to pump the handle and move loads. Electric pallet jacks use a motor for lifting and propulsion. Hand trucks and carts move smaller loads. Manual units suit light to moderate use; electric units suit high-volume operations. Capacity typically ranges from 2,000–5,500 lbs. Brands like Crown, Raymond, Toyota, and Yale are common. Pallet jacks are essential for logistics and warehousing operations.

How Do You Finance a Pallet Jack?

Use equipment loans, leases, or bundle multiple units. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Smaller ticket sizes may qualify for quick-approval programs. Logistics and material handling lenders commonly finance pallet jacks.

How Much Does a Pallet Jack Cost?

Manual pallet jacks typically cost $500–$2,500. Electric pallet jacks range from roughly $5,000–$15,000+ depending on capacity and features. Hand trucks and carts run $200–$2,000+. Used equipment typically costs 20–35% less. Many lenders finance used equipment. Obtain a written quote—lenders use it to structure financing.

Financing Options for Pallet Jacks

Equipment loans and leases both work. Loans require 0–20% down, terms of 24–48 months. Rates typically range from 6–15%. Leasing offers lower monthly payments. See equipment loan vs lease. For larger fleets, SBA loans can combine pallet jacks with other equipment.

Pallet Jack Financing FAQ

Can you finance used pallet jacks?

Yes. Many lenders finance used pallet jacks and hand trucks. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Condition and brand affect approval.

Manual vs electric—does it affect financing?

Lenders finance both. Electric pallet jacks typically cost more. Price and condition matter more than type.

Can I bundle multiple pallet jacks in one loan?

Yes. Many operators finance multiple units in a single equipment loan or lease. Bundling can simplify approval and may improve terms.

Is leasing better than buying pallet jacks?

It depends. Leasing offers lower monthly payments and easier fleet replacement. Buying builds equity. Compare both based on your cash flow and replacement cycle.
