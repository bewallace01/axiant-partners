What Are Pallet Jacks?

Pallet jacks (also called pallet trucks) are material handling equipment that lifts and moves palletized loads horizontally through warehouses, distribution centers, and retail backrooms. They fit under pallets and use a hydraulic pump to raise the forks for transport. Types include:

  • Manual pallet jacks · Operator pumps the handle to raise the load; requires physical effort. Capacity 2,000–5,500 lbs. Best for light to moderate use.
  • Electric pallet jacks (walkie) · Motorized; operator walks alongside. Reduces fatigue and speeds up movement. Capacity 3,000–6,000 lbs.
  • Rider pallet jacks · Operator rides; faster and more efficient for long distances. Often used alongside forklifts in high-throughput operations.

Leading brands include Crown, Raymond, Toyota, Yale, and Hyster. Pallet jacks complement conveyor systems and pallet racking in modern warehouse layouts.

Pallet Jack Costs: New vs Used

Manual pallet jacks cost roughly $500–$2,500 new, depending on capacity and brand. Electric walkies run $5,000–$12,000; rider models $8,000–$15,000+. Used equipment typically runs 20–35% less. Fleet purchases (multiple units) often qualify for volume discounts. Many lenders finance used pallet jacks when condition is documented. Tax Benefits: Section 179 and Depreciation

Pallet jacks qualify for Section 179 and bonus depreciation. Section 179 lets you deduct the full purchase price in the year of purchase (subject to annual limits). Lease payments are typically deductible as operating expenses. These tax benefits can significantly reduce the net cost of pallet jack financing·consult your CPA for your situation.
