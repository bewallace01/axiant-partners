What Is Pallet Racking?

Pallet racking is warehouse storage that holds palletized inventory. Common types:

Selective racking

· Most common; direct access to every pallet. Best for high-SKU variety and fast turnover. Requires more aisles; lower storage density. Costs roughly $60–$120 per pallet position.

Drive-in / drive-through racking

· Forklifts enter the structure; pallets stored 3–10+ deep. Higher density (40–60% more positions than selective). LIFO rotation. $80–$150 per pallet position.

Push-back racking

· Carts on rails; each new pallet pushes the previous back. Good space utilization, LIFO. Ideal for freezers. $100–$180 per pallet position.

Cantilever racking

· For long items: lumber, pipe, furniture. Arms extend from uprights.

Leading brands: Ridg-U-Rak, Steel King, UNARCO, Kion (Dematic). Costs vary by bay count, height, and customization.

Why Pallet Racking Financing Is Different

Pallet racking is standard warehouse capital. Projects are often bundled with forklifts, conveyor systems, and dock equipment for new buildouts or expansions. Lenders understand the role storage plays in distribution. SBA 7(a) and 504 can include racking. Expandable systems can be financed in phases. Equipment approval typically 1–5 days; SBA 30–60+ days.

Pallet Racking Costs: New vs Used

Costs depend on type, height, and bay count. Selective: $60–$120 per pallet position; drive-in $80–$150; push-back $100–$180; pallet flow $150–$250. Small installations (20–50 bays): $5,000–$25,000. 200+ bays: $50,000–$100,000+. Used racking typically 20–35% less. Pallet Racking typically qualifies for Section 179 (deduct full purchase price in year of purchase, subject to limits) and bonus depreciation. Lease payments are usually fully deductible as operating expenses. These benefits can significantly reduce the net cost of financing—consult your CPA for your situation.
