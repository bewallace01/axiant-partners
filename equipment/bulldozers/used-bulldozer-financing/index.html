Used Heavy Equipment Financing: Rates, Terms and Age Limits

What lenders charge on a used dozer, loader or excavator, how old the machine can be, and what the payment looks like.

Quick answer

Used heavy equipment financing usually costs 1-3 percentage points more than financing the same machine new, with 10-20% down and 36-60 month terms. With 720+ credit and 2+ years in business, new equipment prices at 7-9% APR, so a clean used dozer or loader often lands around 8-12%. Most lenders want a 600+ score and a machine under 10-15 years old, and the best terms go to units about 7 years old or newer. Approvals typically come back in 24-72 hours.

How Does Used Heavy Equipment Financing Work?

An equipment loan pays the seller for the machine, and you pay the lender back in fixed monthly payments. The machine is the collateral, so if payments stop the lender takes it back and sells it. That is why a lender underwriting a used dozer looks at two things: you, and what the machine will be worth halfway through the loan.

Used iron is popular for a simple reason. A new bulldozer runs $100,000 to $500,000+ depending on size. A 5-year-old mid-size dozer might run $80,000 to $150,000 and push the same dirt. A lower price means a lower payment and less cash tied up, which is why many contractors run two used machines instead of one new one. The same logic applies to loaders, excavators and the rest of the fleet; this page uses dozers as the example.

How Old Can Used Heavy Equipment Be and Still Get Financed?

Machines about 7 years old or newer finance on standard terms. Many lenders go to 10 years for low-hour, well-kept machines from strong brands like Caterpillar, Komatsu and John Deere. The outer limit is usually 10-15 years, and older machines tend to need an independent appraisal and a shorter term.

Lenders also apply a rough rule: the machine's age plus the loan term should not pass about 15 years. A 9-year-old dozer, by that math, gets about 6 years at most, and in practice less. Hours matter as much as age. Hour limits often sit around 8,000-12,000 for mid-size and larger dozers, and a 3-year-old dozer with 3,000 hours gets better terms than a 7-year-old one with 10,000.

How Much Down Payment Does Used Equipment Need?

Plan on 10-20%. With 680+ credit you can often get 10% down on a quality used machine, and in the 600-650 range lenders usually ask for 15-20%. With 700+ credit on a newer used machine, some lenders will go to zero down, though a $0-down deal usually costs a point or two more. Below 550, expect 25% or more down or a cosigner.

What Does a Used Dozer Payment Look Like?

Say you buy a used mid-size crawler for $140,000 and put 15% down ($21,000). You finance $119,000:

  • 12% APR over 60 months: about $2,647 a month, $39,825 in total interest.
  • 15% APR over 48 months: about $3,312 a month, $39,969 in total interest.

The shorter term costs about $665 more a month for roughly the same total interest. If the machine is older, the lender may only offer the shorter term, so check that the payment fits your slowest months before you commit. Run your own numbers in the payment calculator.

Should You Lease or Finance a Used Machine?

A loan makes sense if you will run the machine for years: it is yours from day one, and owning it is what qualifies you for depreciation and Section 179. A lease makes sense if you want a lower payment, usually about 10-20% lower than a loan on the same machine, or expect to trade up. Leases come in three common forms: a $1 buyout (works like a loan), a 10% purchase option, and a fair market value lease where you can buy, return or renew at the end. Equipment leasing vs a loan walks through the total cost.

What Do Lenders Check on the Machine?

Brand, model, age and hours. Strong brands hold resale value, and that value is the lender's safety net.

Undercarriage. On a dozer, tracks, rollers, idlers and sprockets wear out, and replacing an undercarriage can cost $20,000-$50,000+. A worn one lowers the value the lender will lend against.

An inspection. A third-party inspection covering engine, transmission, hydraulics, undercarriage and blade protects you and helps the file. Dealers often include one on certified used equipment. For a private-party sale, hire an independent inspector.

The seller's quote. Make, model, year, serial number, hours and price, in writing. More on this in what lenders look at for equipment financing.

What Documents Do You Need?

  • The written quote (make, model, year, serial number, hours, price)
  • The inspection report and any maintenance records
  • 3-6 months of business bank statements
  • Tax returns, a P&L and business formation documents

With a complete file, initial approval typically comes back in 24-72 hours and funding lands within 5-10 business days. Used equipment rules apply the same way to used excavator financing; for new machines see heavy equipment financing, and for dozers specifically, bulldozer financing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you finance used heavy equipment?

Yes. Equipment lenders finance used dozers, loaders, excavators and other iron every day, because the machine itself secures the loan. Expect 10-20% down, a rate 1-3 points above new, and a shorter term, usually 36-60 months.

What credit score do you need for used heavy equipment financing?

A 600+ FICO opens most equipment lenders, and 720+ gets the best rates. Between 550 and 599 you are usually looking at a specialty asset-based lender. Below 550, plan on 25% or more down or a cosigner.

How old can a used machine be and still get financed?

The best terms go to machines about 7 years old or newer. Many lenders go to 10 years for low-hour machines from strong brands, and the outer limit is usually 10-15 years. A common rule is that the machine's age plus the loan term should not pass about 15 years.

What is the interest rate on a $50,000 equipment loan?

It depends mostly on your credit tier: about 7-9% APR at 720+, 10-13% at 680-719, 13-16% at 640-679 and 16-22% at 580-639, plus 1-3 points if the machine is used. At 12% over 60 months, $50,000 costs about $1,112 a month. At 19% over 36 months it is about $1,833 a month.

What are the disadvantages of leasing equipment?

With a fair market value lease you do not build ownership, and buying the machine at the end can cost more than a loan would have. You also give up the Section 179 deduction that comes with owning the equipment. The upside is a payment that usually runs about 10-20% lower than a loan on the same machine.

Can an LLC borrow against its equipment?

Yes. An LLC can finance a machine in its own name, with the machine as collateral, or borrow against equipment it already owns free and clear. If the business is under 2 years old, expect a personal guarantor, about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down.
