What Lenders and Appraisers Check on a Dozer

  • Undercarriage. Track chains, rollers, idlers and sprockets wear out and are expensive to replace. An inspection report that estimates percent remaining often sets how much a lender will lend on a used dozer.
  • Hours and service history. Engine and transmission work, and whether the machine has been rebuilt.
  • Blade and ripper. Straight, semi-U or six-way blade, and whether a ripper is included.
  • Grade control. GPS receivers and displays are often removable and valued separately, so say whether they're part of the sale.
  • Title and liens. Most dozers have no title, so the lender files a UCC lien and checks for existing ones.

Worked Example: A Used Mid-Size Dozer

Illustrative, not a quote. A site contractor buys a used mid-size crawler for $140,000, within the typical used range, and puts 15% down ($21,000). $119,000 is financed at 9%.

  • Over 60 months: about $2,470 a month, about $29,215 of interest
  • Over 48 months: about $2,961 a month, about $23,143 of interest
  • The 60-month term frees about $491 a month; the 48-month term saves about $6,072 of interest

If the inspection shows an undercarriage that will need work within a year or two, budget for it before choosing the shorter term.

Getting the Dozer to the Job

Most mid-size and large dozers move on a lowboy, and depending on width and weight a load can need a state oversize or overweight permit. States issue those permits under federal size and weight rules (FHWA). If you don't already haul your own iron, a lowboy and tractor can often be financed with the dozer or on a separate approval. See trailer financing and semi truck financing.

Buy, Lease or Borrow Against One You Own

  • Buy with a loan if the dozer will run for years on your own work.
  • Lease if you want a lower payment and plan to trade: bulldozer leasing.
  • Borrow against a paid-off dozer to raise cash without selling it: heavy equipment equity loans.

Land clearing and site work slow down in wet months in many regions. Some lenders offer seasonal payment schedules on heavy equipment; ask when you apply.

Bulldozer Financing FAQ

How much does a bulldozer cost?

New dozers typically run $80,000-$150,000 for compact crawlers, $150,000-$300,000 for mid-size machines and $300,000-$500,000+ for large ones. Used dozers often sell 30-50% below new, depending on hours and undercarriage.

What is the monthly payment on a dozer?

It depends on the amount, rate and term. As an illustration, $200,000 at 8% is about $4,055 a month over 60 months, and $119,000 at 9% is about $2,470 a month over 60.

Can you finance a used dozer?

Yes. Lenders look at hours, service history and especially undercarriage condition, and older machines usually get shorter terms or need more down.

What credit score do you need for bulldozer financing?

Many programs look for about 600 or higher, with the best pricing near 680+. Larger deals also look at financial statements and your work backlog.

Can I finance a lowboy trailer with a dozer?

Often, yes. A lowboy can go on the same approval or a separate one. Check whether your loads will need oversize or overweight permits.
