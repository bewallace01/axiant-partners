How to Finance Grain Equipment

Costs, loan options, requirements, and approval process for grain handling and storage

← Back to Grain Equipment Financing

Last updated: March 5, 2026

Grain handling and storage equipment—bins, augers, conveyors, and dryers—costs $10,000–$100,000+ depending on capacity and configuration. Most farmers finance equipment instead of paying cash. Equipment loans and leases spread payments, preserve working capital, and improve marketing flexibility by adding on-farm storage and handling capacity.

What Is Grain Handling and Storage Equipment?

Grain handling and storage equipment includes grain bins (silos), augers (grain legs), conveyors, grain dryers, and support systems. Grain bins store harvested grain on-farm, reducing reliance on commercial elevators and improving marketing timing. Augers and conveyors move grain from combines to bins, between bins, and into trucks. Grain dryers reduce moisture content for safe storage. Capacity ranges from small farm-scale (a few thousand bushels) to large commercial systems (100,000+ bushels). This equipment is essential for row-crop and grain operations that want to add storage, improve handling efficiency, and capture seasonal price opportunities.

How Do You Finance Grain Equipment?

Use equipment loans, leases, or SBA loans. Most lenders require credit 600+, down payment 0–20%, and proof of revenue. Approval typically takes 1–5 days. Grain equipment is common agricultural infrastructure, so financing is widely available through agriculture lenders. Note: Some lenders treat grain bins as real estate (SBA 504, ag mortgages) rather than equipment—clarify with your lender.

How Much Does Grain Equipment Cost?

Grain equipment prices vary by type, capacity, and whether you buy new or used. Understanding the cost range helps you plan equipment financing.

New Grain Equipment Prices

New grain bins range from roughly $2–4 per bushel for standard corrugated steel bins (e.g., $20,000–$40,000 for a 10,000-bushel bin). Augers and conveyors cost $3,000–$25,000+ depending on length and capacity. Grain dryers range from roughly $15,000–$80,000+ for batch and continuous-flow models. A complete grain handling and storage system (bins, augers, conveyors, legs) can run $50,000–$200,000+ depending on capacity. Top brands like Sukup, GSI, Brock, and MFS add cost.

Used Grain Equipment Prices

Used grain equipment typically costs 30–50% less than new equivalents, depending on age, capacity, and condition. Used bins, augers, and conveyors are commonly available at auctions and through dealers. Many lenders finance used equipment. Always inspect bins for rust, augers for wear, and conveyors for belt condition before purchasing used.

Factors Affecting Price

Capacity (bushels), number of bins, auger length and diameter, conveyor type, dryer capacity, brand, and regional demand all affect grain equipment pricing. Obtain a written quote from your dealer—lenders use this to structure your financing.

Financing Options for Grain Equipment

Several financing structures work for grain handling and storage. Choosing the right one depends on cash flow, tax situation, and whether bins are considered equipment or real property. See equipment loan vs lease for a detailed comparison.

Equipment Loans (Term Financing)

Equipment loans are the most common option for portable grain equipment (augers, conveyors) and some bin systems. You borrow a set amount, make fixed monthly payments, and own the equipment when the loan is paid off. Loans typically require 0–20% down, with terms of 24–84 months. Interest rates typically range from 6–15%. Ideal if the equipment is treated as personal property.

Equipment Leasing

Leasing typically offers lower monthly payments than loans. At lease end, you may return the equipment, purchase it at fair market value, or upgrade. Operating leases treat payments as operating expenses. Leasing suits operations that want to preserve cash flow. Note: Leasing may be less common for permanent bin installations.

SBA and Agricultural Real Estate Loans

SBA 504 loans and agricultural real estate loans can finance grain bins and permanent storage when treated as real property. Terms can extend to 10–20 years. Approval typically takes 30–60+ days. USDA guaranteed loans also support grain storage. Suit established operations with substantial storage projects.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans are better suited for operating expenses than equipment purchases. Use equipment financing for grain equipment to secure better rates and terms tied to the asset.

Credit score: Most equipment lenders look for 600 or higher. See credit score requirements for equipment financing. Agricultural equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

Down payment: Expect 0–20% down depending on credit and equipment. Strong credit may qualify for 0% down; weaker credit often requires 15–20%.

Time in business: Most lenders prefer 1–2+ years. Newer operations may need a co-signer or may qualify through SBA or USDA programs.

Revenue and documentation: Prepare bank statements, tax returns (Schedule F or business returns), profit & loss, and equipment quote. Acreage and grain production may be requested for larger storage projects.

Grain Equipment Financing Rates and Terms

Interest rates for grain equipment financing typically range from 6–15% for equipment loans and leases. Terms commonly run 24–84 months for standard equipment financing; SBA and ag real estate loans can extend to 10–20 years for bins. A $75,000 grain handling system financed at 8% over 60 months would result in roughly $1,520/month. Use our financing calculator to model different scenarios.

Step-by-Step: How to Finance Grain Equipment

  1. Determine equipment type and cost. Choose bins, augers, conveyors, dryer, or a complete package. Get a written quote from the dealer.
  2. Gather business financial documents. Collect bank statements (3–6 months), tax returns, profit & loss, and equipment quote.
  3. Compare lenders. Work with a broker or compare multiple agricultural equipment lenders. Clarify whether bins are financed as equipment or real estate. Axiant Partners matches farmers with lenders experienced in agriculture equipment financing.
  4. Submit application. Complete the lender's application with business and equipment details. Provide the quote and financial documentation.
  5. Receive approval and funding. Approval often takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans. Real estate/504 loans can take 30–60+ days. Once approved, sign documents and coordinate funding with the dealer.

Tips to Get Approved for Grain Equipment Financing

  • Improve your credit score. Pay down balances, correct errors, and avoid new credit before applying.
  • Provide strong revenue documentation. Clean bank statements and organized financials speed approval.
  • Consider a complete package. Financing bins, augers, and conveyors together can simplify the process and may improve terms.
  • Make a larger down payment. Putting more down reduces lender risk and can lower your rate.
  • Clarify equipment vs real estate. If financing bins, ask whether the lender treats them as equipment or real property—this affects terms and structure.

Grain Equipment Financing FAQ

Can you finance used grain equipment?

Yes. Many lenders finance used grain bins, augers, and conveyors. Used equipment may require a larger down payment and shorter terms. Age, capacity, and condition affect approval.

What credit score is required for grain equipment financing?

Most lenders look for a credit score of 600 or higher. Scores of 680+ typically qualify for the best rates and terms. Agricultural equipment financing is asset-backed, so some programs work with 580+ when revenue and down payment are strong.

How long does grain equipment financing approval take?

Approval typically takes 1–5 business days for equipment loans and leases. SBA and ag real estate loans can take 30–60+ days. Having documents ready speeds the process.

Can you finance grain bins and augers together?

Yes. Lenders often finance a complete grain handling and storage package—bins, augers, conveyors, dryers—as a single equipment loan. This can simplify documentation and may improve terms.

Is grain equipment considered real estate or equipment?

Portable grain equipment (augers, conveyors) is typically financed as equipment. Grain bins may be treated as equipment or real estate depending on lender and whether they're affixed to the land. Clarify with your lender to ensure the right financing structure.
