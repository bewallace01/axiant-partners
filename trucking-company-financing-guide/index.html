Owner-Operator Startup Playbook

Representative first-time owner-operator stand-up:

  1. CDL-A and 6+ months CMV experience as company driver before going independent
  2. MC + USDOT authority — 21-45 day FMCSA process
  3. First tractor — equipment loan with 20-30% down (specialty CDL-friendly lender). Used 2-3 year-old tractor typical for first truck.
  4. Insurance — truck physical damage + cargo + auto liability. New-authority insurance is materially more expensive than established.
  5. Fuel card — EFS, Comdata, or FleetOne. No cash required upfront.
  6. Factoring relationship — lined up before first load so cash hits the account when delivery happens, not 60 days later.
  7. First load to permanent operation — layer in line of credit and possibly SBA expansion as truck count grows.

Trucking Invoice Factoring

The single most important non-equipment financing product for trucking. Without factoring, a brand-new owner-op who delivers a $5,000 load on Monday doesn't see the cash from the shipper until 60 days later — long after fuel, payroll, and insurance bills are due.

  • Advance: 90-95% of invoice value, hits the bank account 1-2 days after the load delivers
  • Factor fee: 2-5% per 30 days the invoice is outstanding (typical 3% per 30 days)
  • Recourse vs non-recourse: recourse means you owe the factor back if the shipper doesn't pay; non-recourse means the factor takes the credit risk on the shipper. Non-recourse costs more
  • Notification vs non-notification: notification factoring sends shippers a notice of assignment (cheaper); non-notification preserves the relationship (more expensive)

Scaling from Owner-Op to Fleet

Owner-op (1 truck) → small fleet (2-5 trucks) → mid fleet (5-15) → established (15+) follows a typical financing progression:

  • 1-3 trucks: Equipment financing per tractor, fuel cards, factoring relationship. Personal-guarantee-driven.
  • 3-10 trucks: Layer in line of credit, possibly first SBA Express loan for fleet expansion. Operating company starts to build separate credit.
  • 10+ trucks: SBA 7(a) for major fleet expansion or maintenance facility. Treasury management with bank. May refinance existing equipment loans into a fleet-level credit facility.
  • 25+ trucks: Asset-based revolving facility (revolves against AR + equipment), syndicated bank financing, treasury management.

Watch-Fors

  • DOT score — tracked by FMCSA, affects insurance, financing, and customer relationships. Out-of-service violations are the worst signal.
  • CSA score — Compliance, Safety, Accountability score. Lenders check on fleet deals.
  • DAC report — Drive-A-Check report tracks CDL driver employment history. Lenders use to verify CMV experience.
  • MVR (Motor Vehicle Record) — recent DUIs, moving violations, accidents affect insurance and financing.
  • Authority age — new-authority insurance is 30-60% more expensive; many lenders want 6-12 months of authority before funding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What financing do trucking companies use?

Equipment financing for tractors and trailers (asset-based, 24-72 hour close), invoice factoring for AR cash flow (60-day shipper terms), fuel cards/lines for daily fuel costs, SBA 7(a) for fleet expansion, and working capital loans for general operations. Each product fits a different need.

How do owner-operators finance their first truck?

Specialty CDL-friendly equipment lenders fund first-time owner-operators with strong personal credit (680+), CDL-A, and 6+ months of CMV experience. Down payment typically 20-30% for first-time owner-ops. Established owner-ops with authority and a clean MVR can hit 0-10% down.

What is invoice factoring for trucking?

Trucking factoring is the largest specialty factoring market in the U.S. Factor advances 90-95% of an invoice immediately when a load is delivered; the factor collects from the shipper at net-30 or net-60 terms. Factor fee typically 2-5% per 30 days. Critical for cash flow because shipper payments lag fuel and driver pay.

Can a new trucking company get a fuel card?

Yes — major fuel card programs (EFS, Comdata, FleetOne) approve new trucking companies with MC authority. Card limits scale with fuel-purchase history; no cash required upfront.

How does SBA 7(a) work for fleet expansion?

SBA 7(a) finances fleet expansion when the borrower has 2+ years of authority, clean DOT score, sufficient cash flow. 10-year amort, prime + 2.5-3%. Faster monthly payment than equipment loans on the same fleet additions but slower close (4-10 weeks vs 24-72 hours).
