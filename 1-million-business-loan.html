How do you get a million dollar business loan?

Start with what the money buys, because that picks the loan. Buying a company or funding a broad expansion points to an SBA 7(a) loan. Buying the building you operate from points to SBA 504 or a commercial real estate loan, which can run 25 years and carries the lowest payment. A strong company that needs to close in weeks rather than months can look at a large conventional term loan.

Then build the file before you apply: three years of business and personal tax returns, current financial statements, a schedule of the debt you already carry, and a one-page use of funds. At this size the lender's real question is whether your cash flow covers the new payment with room to spare, so run the payment first in the business loan calculator.

How hard is it to get approved for a $1 million business loan?

Harder than a small online loan, because full underwriting means the lender reviews your tax returns, financial statements and debt schedule, not just a few months of bank statements. But $1 million is a routine size for SBA and bank lenders, and the main test is arithmetic. Lenders commonly want a debt service coverage ratio of about 1.25x: on a 10-year payment near $13,220 a month, that means about $198,000 a year of cash flow available to pay debt. Clear that with collateral behind the loan and your file is in good shape.

Going bigger raises the bar only a little. A $2 million business loan needs the same 680+ credit, documented financials and collateral, with an equity injection around 10% on SBA acquisition and real estate deals. At the 7(a) maximum, a $5 million loan usually means 3+ years in business and audited or reviewed financials.

$1 million business loan FAQs

How do I qualify for a $1 million business loan?

Lenders generally expect 3+ years in business, revenue around $100,000 a month or more, a 680+ credit score (700+ for the best SBA and bank pricing), collateral and a personal guarantee. You will also need multi-year tax returns, financial statements, a debt schedule and a written use of funds.

How hard is it to get a $1 million business loan?

It is full commercial underwriting, so it takes more paperwork than a small online loan, but it is a routine size for SBA and bank lenders. The deciding test is cash flow: lenders commonly want a debt service coverage ratio of about 1.25x, which on a 10-year payment of about $13,220 a month means roughly $198,000 a year of cash flow available for debt service.

Can the SBA fund a $1 million business loan?

Yes. The SBA 7(a) program goes up to $5 million, so $1 million is well within range, and SBA 504 covers owner-occupied real estate and long-lived equipment. Plan on 45-90+ days to close.

What is the monthly payment on a $1 million business loan?

It depends on the rate and term. At about 10% over 10 years the payment is roughly $13,220 a month; amortized over 25 years for real estate at about 9%, it is closer to $8,390.

How much would a $500,000 business loan cost per month?

About $6,610 a month over 10 years at roughly 10%, or about $4,200 a month amortized over 25 years at about 9% for real estate. The $500,000 business loan page covers what lenders want at that size.

What do you need to qualify for a $2 million business loan?

Much the same file as $1 million: a 680+ credit score, documented financials, healthy debt service coverage and usually collateral. SBA acquisition and real estate deals often expect an equity injection around 10%, and underwriting typically takes 30-90 days. See the $2 million business loan guide.
