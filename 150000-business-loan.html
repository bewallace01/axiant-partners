Quick answer

A $150,000 business loan is within reach for most businesses with 1+ year in business, about $25,000+ a month in revenue and a 600+ credit score (650-680+ opens SBA and the best term-loan rates). A term loan or equipment financing can fund in 2-7 days; an SBA 7(a) loan takes 30-60+ days but costs less. How do you get a $150,000 business loan?

Pick the loan by what the money is for, then by how fast you need it. One defined project (a build-out, a hire plan, a vehicle) fits a term loan. A machine or truck fits equipment financing, because the equipment secures the loan. Spending in stages fits a line of credit. If you can wait a month or two, an SBA 7(a) loan has the lowest rate and the longest term.

Next, check the size against your cash flow. Lenders at this amount look at your deposits, your existing debt payments and how long you've been open. Have three to six months of bank statements ready, and a P&L and tax returns if you're going the SBA or bank route. Not sure how much to take? See right-sizing your loan, or the loan decision guide.

How much income do you need for a $150K loan?

Plan on about $25,000 a month in revenue or more, but lenders care most about what's left after expenses. A common target is a 1.25x debt service coverage ratio. A $3,340 monthly payment (60 months at 12%) is about $40,000 a year, so you'd want roughly $50,000 a year of cash flow available to pay debt, on top of the payments you already make.

What if you only need $100,000?

The bar drops a little: about 1+ year in business, $15,000-$20,000+ a month in revenue and a 600+ score. See the $100,000 business loan page for payments at that size.

What lenders actually check at $150,000

$150,000 is the threshold where underwriting gets more thorough than the quick, statement-only review of smaller loans. Lenders still lean on your bank deposits, but at this size they also look harder at debt service coverage (whether your cash flow absorbs the new payment on top of what you already owe) and at how long you've been operating. A clean year or two of history and a profit-and-loss statement that ties to your deposits go a long way.

That extra scrutiny is the trade for better terms. Where a $25,000 loan is priced for speed, a well-qualified $150,000 borrower can reach genuine term-loan and even SBA rates. The stronger your file, the wider the gap between what a cautious lender offers and what a confident one will, so it pays to present clean numbers. Do you need collateral for $150,000, and is SBA worth the wait?

At $150,000 you'll often have a real choice between unsecured and secured options. An unsecured term loan or line of credit funds faster and pledges no specific asset (a personal guarantee is still standard), while a secured loan backed by equipment or other collateral can get you a lower rate or a longer term. If you're buying a specific asset, equipment financing is naturally secured by that asset and usually the cheapest route.

The other fork is speed vs. cost. An online term loan can fund a $150,000 expansion in days; an SBA 7(a) loan will take 30-60+ days but typically carries a lower rate and a longer term, which means a smaller monthly payment. If your move isn't time-sensitive and you qualify, the SBA route often wins on total cost. If a window is closing on a lease, a piece of equipment or a seasonal build, the faster option can be worth the premium. Not sure which fits? Start with the loan decision guide.

$150,000 business loan FAQs

How do I qualify for a $150,000 business loan?

Most lenders want 1+ year in business, about $25,000+ in monthly revenue and a 600+ credit score, plus a few months of bank statements. A 650-680+ score and tax returns open term loans and SBA options with better rates.

What's the best loan type for $150,000?

For a defined expansion or purchase, a term loan. A line of credit suits staged spending, equipment financing fits machinery, and SBA 7(a) offers the lowest rate if you can wait 30-60+ days.

What is the monthly payment on a $150,000 business loan?

It depends on the rate and term: about $5,050 a month over 36 months at 13%, $3,340 over 60 months at 12%, or $2,070 over 10 years at 11% on an SBA loan. Shorter terms cost more per month and less in total interest.

How much income do I need for a $150,000 loan?

Lenders look for roughly $25,000+ in monthly revenue, but the real test is whether cash flow covers the payment. At a common 1.25x coverage target, a $3,340 monthly payment needs about $50,000 a year of cash flow available for debt service.

What interest rate will I get on a $150,000 business loan?

Typical 2026 ranges: SBA 7(a) 9.5-11%, bank term loans 8-13%, equipment financing 7-18%, and working capital loans and lines of credit 8-30%. Your credit score, time in business, collateral and the lender set where you land.

How fast can I get $150,000?

Working capital can fund in 1-3 days, and term loans and equipment financing in 2-7 days. An SBA loan takes 30-60+ days in exchange for a lower rate.

Can I get a $150,000 loan with fair or bad credit?

It's harder than at smaller amounts but possible, usually through shorter-term or secured options at a higher rate. Cleaning up the file first (steady statements, lower balances, a clear use of funds) improves both approval odds and pricing. See business loans for bad credit.

Do I need collateral for $150,000?

Not always. Unsecured term loans and lines of credit exist at this size for qualified borrowers, backed by a personal guarantee. Pledging equipment or other collateral can lower your rate or stretch the term for a smaller payment.
