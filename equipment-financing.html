Quick Answer: How Equipment Financing Works

Equipment financing lets a small business buy the equipment it needs without paying cash up front. It's a loan or lease secured by the equipment itself. If payments stop, the lender can take the equipment back and sell it, so lenders care about its value and your cash flow, not only your credit. That's also why it usually closes faster than a bank or SBA loan.

  • Amounts: $10K to $5M, new or used
  • Rates: about 7-22% APR, mostly set by your credit tier (see the table)
  • Terms: 2 to 7 years, longer with stronger credit
  • Down payment: 0-20% for most borrowers; $0 down usually takes about 680+ credit
  • Next step: try the payment calculator or apply once to get matched

How It Works and What Lenders Look At

You choose the equipment and get a quote or invoice from the seller. The lender pays the seller, and you pay the lender back in fixed monthly payments. With a loan, the equipment is yours from day one. With a lease, the lender owns it, and at the end you decide whether to buy it, return it or renew.

Four things set your price:

  • Your personal credit. It's the biggest factor: a 720 and a 620 get very different offers on the same machine.
  • Time in business. Two or more years opens the best programs. Six to 24 months still qualifies, at higher rates and shorter terms.
  • Revenue. Most programs want to see about $10,000 or more a month in deposits.
  • The equipment itself. Trucks, trailers and construction machines resell easily, so they get the best rates. Restaurant equipment and used or high-hour machines usually cost 1-3 points more.

What Credit Score Do You Need?

There's no set minimum score. In practice:

  • 600+ FICO opens most equipment lenders.
  • 550-599 usually means a specialty asset-based lender.
  • Below 550, you'll typically need 25%+ down or a cosigner.

The equipment matters, too. Machines with strong resale value, like construction, trucking and manufacturing equipment, can lower the credit bar by about 30-50 points compared with soft assets. Strong revenue or 2+ years in business helps further.

If your score is under 600, start with equipment financing with bad credit. The full picture is in credit score requirements for equipment financing.

Can You Get Equipment Financing With No Money Down?

Yes, if your profile is strong. 100% financing is common for businesses with about 680+ credit and 2+ years in business that are buying new or late-model equipment from an established dealer. A 650-679 score sometimes qualifies with strong revenue.

  • Used equipment usually needs 10-20% down.
  • A $0-down deal often costs a point or two more than the same deal with money down.
  • True "no credit check" financing is rare. Most legitimate lenders still check credit, often with a soft pull that doesn't affect your score.

Financing Used Equipment

Most equipment lenders finance used equipment, especially in categories that resell well: construction, trucking, manufacturing and agriculture. Expect a few differences:

  • Age: usually under 10-15 years. Many lenders also cap the equipment's age plus the loan term at about 15 years.
  • Rate: about 1-3 points higher than new.
  • Down payment: 10-20% on older machines.
  • Paperwork: photos, serial numbers and sometimes an inspection.

Equipment Financing for Startups and New Businesses

A newer business can still qualify, because the equipment secures the loan. Many programs accept businesses with 6+ months of history, 660-680+ credit and steady revenue. Expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor. Approvals are routine for construction, trucking, manufacturing, restaurant and medical equipment.

When Equipment Financing Makes Sense, and When It Doesn't

It's usually the right call when you're buying a specific machine that will earn its keep for years, and you'd rather keep your cash for payroll and inventory. It isn't always the best fit:

  • Small, short-term purchases. A business credit card can be quicker, especially with a 0% intro APR you'll pay off before it ends.
  • Big purchases that can wait. On $250K or more, an SBA 7(a) or 504 loan's lower long-term rate can outweigh its 6-12 week wait and personal guarantee. Compare equipment financing vs an SBA loan.
  • Equipment that goes out of date fast, like tech and some medical equipment. A lease usually beats a loan here.
  • Paying cash only wins when that cash wouldn't earn more elsewhere in your business, which is rare for an established company. Here's the lease vs loan vs cash math.

Equipment Financing vs a Business Credit Card

Business credit cards run about 18-29% APR once any intro rate ends, with no set payoff date. Equipment financing runs about 7-22% APR depending on your credit, paid off over a fixed 2-7 years. A card can win for a small purchase you'll pay off quickly. For anything over about $10-15K that you'll keep more than a few months, financing usually costs less. Equipment Financing FAQs

What credit score do I need for equipment financing?

There's no set minimum. A 600+ FICO opens most equipment lenders, 550-599 usually means a specialty asset-based lender, and below 550 you'll typically need 25%+ down or a cosigner. More in our guides to credit score requirements and equipment financing with bad credit.

Can I get equipment financing with no money down?

Often, yes, if you have about 680+ credit and 2+ years in business and you're buying new or late-model equipment from an established dealer. A 650-679 score sometimes qualifies with strong revenue. Used equipment usually needs 10-20% down, and a $0-down deal often costs a point or two more. How no-money-down equipment financing works.

Is there equipment financing with no credit check?

Not really. True no-credit-check equipment financing is rare. Most legitimate lenders still check credit, often with a soft pull that doesn't affect your score. They then lean on the equipment's value, your time in business and your bank deposits. With weaker credit, expect a higher rate and commonly 10-20% down. What no credit check equipment financing really means.

How fast can I get equipment financing?

Initial approvals typically come back in 24-72 hours. Full underwriting takes 3-7 business days, and funding usually lands within 5-10 business days in total. Deals under $150K can close in under a week if your documents are complete. What speeds up approval.

Can I finance used equipment?

Yes. Most equipment lenders finance used equipment, especially construction, trucking, manufacturing and agriculture equipment. It usually needs to be under 10-15 years old, the rate runs 1-3 points higher than new, and older machines need 10-20% down. Many lenders also cap age plus loan term at about 15 years. Financing used equipment.

Can a startup get equipment financing?

Yes. Many programs accept businesses with 6+ months in operation, 660-680+ credit and steady revenue, because the equipment secures the loan. Under 2 years in business, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor. Equipment financing for new businesses.

Should I lease equipment or buy it with a loan?

Buy with a loan if you'll keep the equipment for its useful life; it's yours from day one. Lease if you upgrade often or want a lower payment; lease payments usually run about 10-20% lower. At the end of a lease you can buy the equipment ($1 buyout or 10% purchase option), return it, or renew. Leasing vs a loan, compared.

How much equipment financing can I get?

Amounts typically run from $10,000 to $5,000,000, depending on the equipment, your revenue and credit, and the lender. Heavy machinery and manufacturing assets often support larger deals; smaller items like restaurant or office equipment may start around $10,000.
