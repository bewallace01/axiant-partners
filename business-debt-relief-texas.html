When one more advance stops being the answer

Texas runs on cycles — energy prices, construction pipelines, freight rates — and when a job runs late or a downturn hits, a fast merchant cash advance feels like a bridge. Then a second covers the first, a third covers the second, and several daily ACH debits are pulling from the account faster than revenue comes in. Texas debt & the fine print that matters

Two legal realities shape how Texas MCA debt plays out — and both can work in your favor when you act early.

  • Confessions of judgment — many funders file COJs in New York, but since 2019 New York bars entry of COJs against out-of-state debtors, which can protect a Texas business hit with one. See how to vacate a COJ.
  • Frozen accounts — a funder generally needs a judgment before it can restrain your bank account; a freeze is contestable and negotiable if you move fast. See what to do.
  • Disclosure rules — commercial-financing disclosure requirements are expanding across states, and they can surface how expensive a Texas advance really was — useful leverage in a negotiation.

Texas business debt relief questions

How does business debt relief work in Texas?

A specialist reviews your debts — often several stacked MCAs — and matches you to a path: settlement (negotiating balances down), consolidation or reverse consolidation (one lower payment), or mediation (all creditors at once). The goal is one manageable payment, an end to daily ACH debits, and resolution of defaults, UCC liens, or judgments.

Can I settle merchant cash advances as a Texas business?

Yes. Distressed MCA balances are frequently negotiable, and many Texas businesses in oilfield services, construction, trucking, and restaurants settle or restructure stacked advances rather than continue debits they can't sustain. Which path fits depends on your cash flow and number of positions.

Can an MCA freeze my Texas bank account?

Usually only after a court judgment — often via a confession of judgment signed at funding — and a restraining notice on your bank. Many funders file in New York, but since 2019 New York bars COJs against out-of-state debtors, which can protect a Texas business. A frozen account is contestable if you act fast.

Is relief available across all of Texas?

Yes — Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio, El Paso, the Permian and energy regions, and statewide. Relief is handled remotely, so you don't need a local office; you need a specialist who can negotiate with your specific funders.

Why Texas businesses stack — and how to stop

Texas's economy rewards moving fast, and that same speed is what pulls owners into the advance spiral. When an oilfield contract slows, a construction draw is late, or freight rates dip, the quickest money available is a merchant cash advance — and because it funds in a day, it's easy to reach for again when the next gap appears. Before long the daily debits from three or four advances are pulling more out of the account than the business can put back, and each new advance just buys a little time at a steep price.

Debt relief stops the treadmill. Rather than adding another position, it consolidates or settles the whole stack into a single payment sized to real revenue — and where accounts are already in default, it works to lift restraints, release UCC liens, and resolve judgments. For a Texas owner, the turning point is usually the decision to stop out-borrowing the problem and start negotiating it down. The businesses that come back aren't the ones that landed one more advance; they're the ones that got an honest review of every position and restructured all of it at once.
