What you can get done in 30 days, when a D&B PAYDEX first appears, and the order of steps that gets a startup to a usable business credit file fastest

Business Credit Timeline, Month by Month

No bureau publishes an official time-to-score, so the table below is a typical path for a new business that does every step on time. The pace is set by the slowest part: vendors and lenders report on their own billing cycles, and a score needs several reported payments before it means anything.

WhenWhat you can haveWhat's still missing
Week 1-2Entity filed, EIN, business bank account, business phone and address listed the same way everywhereAny credit file at all
Days 15-30D-U-N-S number, 2-3 vendor accounts that report, first purchases madeReported payments: the first bills aren't due yet
Months 2-3First payments reported; a file exists at one or more bureausA PAYDEX if fewer than three experiences from two vendors have reported
Months 3-6First scores at D&B and often Experian; small store or fuel linesEnough history for a lender to rely on the business alone
Months 6-12A business card or equipment loan reporting alongside vendor lines; higher limitsTwo years of operating history, which many bank and SBA lenders still want
Year 2+A file with several reporting lines, a steady payment record and growing limitsNothing structural; the owner's credit still counts on most small-business loans

So "build business credit in 30 days" is half true. You can lay every foundation in a month. You can't make three vendors report three payments in a month, because most net-30 bills aren't even due until day 30.

What Each Bureau Needs Before It Scores You

There are three main business bureaus, and each scores from different data. A business can have a score at one and nothing at another.

BureauMain scoreWhat it's built from
Dun & BradstreetPAYDEX, 1-100; 80 means paying on timePayment experiences vendors report, dollar-weighted, over the past two years; needs 3+ experiences from 2+ vendors
Experian BusinessBusiness credit scores, 1-100Trade lines, balances, payment habits, utilization and public records
Equifax BusinessBusiness credit risk and payment scoresPayment data from lenders, card issuers and vendors that report to Equifax

Two details matter for speed. First, PAYDEX is dollar-weighted, so a large bill paid late hurts more than a small one paid late. Second, a payment nobody reports counts for nothing: Dun & Bradstreet says plainly that unreported payment experiences can't be considered. Paying rent, utilities and payroll on time is good business, but it usually builds no business credit at all.

How to Start Building Business Credit From Scratch

  1. Make the business a separate, findable entity. Form the LLC or corporation, get an EIN (free from the IRS), and use one legal name, address and phone on every account. Mismatches split your payments across two files.
  2. Open a business bank account and run every business dollar through it. Lenders read those statements long before your business score matters.
  3. Get a D-U-N-S number. It's how Dun & Bradstreet ties reported payments to your company.
  4. Open two or three vendor accounts that report. Buy things you actually use (supplies, shipping, fuel) on net-30 terms. Ask each vendor, in writing, which bureaus it reports to.
  5. Pay early, not just on time. On PAYDEX, 80 is on time and higher scores reflect paying before terms.
  6. Add one reporting card or small loan around month 4-6. A business card that reports to the business bureaus, or a small equipment loan, adds a different kind of account.
  7. Check your files at month 3 and month 6. Fix wrong names, addresses or industry codes with each bureau.

The Best Way to Build Business Credit Fast

Speed comes from more reported payments, sooner, not from more accounts. Three vendors that report monthly will build a file faster than ten accounts that report nothing. What tends to help and what tends to waste time:

Usually speeds it upUsually wastes time
Vendors that confirm they report to D&B, Experian or EquifaxAccounts that only report to the owner's personal file
Paying invoices a few days before the due datePaying for a "guaranteed" business credit package
One identity (name, address, phone) on every accountSeveral applications for cards in the same month
An equipment loan for something the business needs anywayBorrowing you don't need just to create a tradeline

Be wary of anyone selling a shortcut to a high score. The FTC warns small businesses about credit and financing offers that overpromise. There is no legitimate way to create a payment history you don't have.

What Lenders Do With a New Business Credit File

A new business score rarely decides a loan on its own. In the first two years most lenders look at three things together: the owner's personal credit and guarantee, the business bank statements, and whatever business credit file exists. A thin file mostly matters as a check that nothing is wrong: no liens, no judgments, no slow pays.

StageWhat usually decides the loan
Under 6 monthsOwner's personal credit, cash in the bank, collateral (equipment, a vehicle)
6-24 monthsBank deposits and their trend, personal credit, a clean business file
2 years+Business tax returns and cash flow; business scores start to carry weight

So build credit and apply for what you need now on two separate tracks. Our guide to startup financing credit scores covers the personal-credit side, and how to qualify for startup financing covers the rest of the file.

Example: A New LLC's First Six Months

Illustrative, not a promise. A landscaping LLC forms in January, gets its EIN the same week, opens a business checking account and lists one address and phone everywhere. In late January it gets a D-U-N-S number and opens net-30 accounts with a supply house, a fuel card and a parts store, each of which confirms it reports.

The first invoices come due in late February and are paid a few days early. By the end of March three vendors have each reported at least one payment, which meets D&B's minimum of three experiences from two vendors. A PAYDEX appears in the second quarter. In month five the LLC finances a trailer, and that loan begins reporting too. At month six it has a business file at more than one bureau, but a lender quoting a working capital loan still pulls the owner's personal credit and asks for six months of bank statements.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to build business credit?

Typically 3 to 6 months to get a first score, and 12 months or more for a file with enough history to support larger limits. The pace depends on how quickly vendors and lenders report your payments, and no bureau publishes an official timeline.

Can you build business credit in 30 days?

You can set it up in 30 days: entity, EIN, bank account, D-U-N-S number and reporting vendor accounts. A score usually takes longer, because Dun & Bradstreet needs at least three payment experiences from at least two vendors, and most first bills aren't due until day 30.

What is the fastest way to build business credit?

Open two or three accounts that report to the business bureaus, use them for real purchases, pay a little early, and keep one name, address and phone on every account. More reported payments sooner matters more than the number of accounts.

How do I start building business credit from scratch?

Form the entity, get a free EIN from the IRS, open a business bank account, get a D-U-N-S number, then open net-30 vendor accounts that confirm they report. Add a business card or small loan that reports once the first payments show up.

What is a good PAYDEX score?

80 or higher. Dun & Bradstreet treats 80 as paying on time and places 80-100 in its low-risk band; scores above 80 reflect paying before the due date.

Does business credit replace personal credit for a startup loan?

Not at first. For roughly the first two years most lenders still review the owner's personal credit and ask for a personal guarantee, along with business bank statements.
