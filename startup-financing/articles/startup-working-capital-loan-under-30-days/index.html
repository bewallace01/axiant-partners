Who Qualifies for a Startup Working Capital Loan?

A working capital loan is paid back out of your deposits, so the lender's first question is whether you have them. Most programs want at least 6 months in business, $10,000 or more a month in revenue, and 550+ credit. Hit those and a startup can qualify. Miss the first two and no amount of paperwork will get a working capital lender to yes.

Credit sets the price. With 680+ and 2+ years in business you can reach bank and SBA-backed pricing around 9-14%. Between 6 months and 2 years, online lenders are the realistic source. Below 600, expect short-term products priced at 30% or more unless you can secure the loan or show very strong deposits.

If you are under 6 months, skip working capital lenders for now. Equipment financing uses the equipment as collateral, and an SBA microloan goes up to $50,000 through nonprofit lenders. Startup loans with no revenue covers the rest.

How Much Would a $50,000 Working Capital Loan Cost?

  • Online term loan, 14% over 24 months: about $2,401 a month, $7,615 in total interest.
  • Online term loan, 30% over 12 months: about $4,874 a month, $8,492 in total interest.
  • Merchant cash advance, 1.30 factor: you repay $65,000. Over 6 months that is roughly 80% APR.

Many of these loans collect daily or weekly, not monthly, so check what the payment does to your account on a slow week before you sign.

How Much Can a Startup Borrow?

Lenders size working capital on revenue. Conservative programs, which is where most young businesses land, start around 10-30% of annual revenue. A business depositing $15,000 a month ($180,000 a year) might see offers between about $18,000 and $54,000. Most programs start at $10,000.

Ask for an amount you can tie to a table: payroll bridge, inventory, vendor deposits, a receivables gap. A round number with nothing behind it draws more questions and more declines. If the plan is bigger than one lender will cover, split it: fund the first phase now and come back once deposits are steadier. How much working capital you can qualify for goes deeper on the math.

How Fast Can You Get Funded?

Once approved, short-term working capital often funds in 48 hours to 1 week. The 30-day window is for founders still pulling the file together:

Days 1-3: Gather the documents and write a one-page memo on what the money does.

Days 4-7: Apply to lenders that fund your stage and answer first questions the same day.

Days 8-14: Underwriting and condition requests. Send everything they ask for in one bundle, within a business day.

Days 15-21: Review terms: net proceeds after fees, total payback, payment frequency.

Days 22-30: Sign, fund and keep the file organized for the next request.

What Documents Do You Need?

  • Business bank statements, 3-6 months, as complete PDFs (not screenshots)
  • Formation documents and owner ID
  • A voided check or account confirmation
  • A use-of-funds table and a one-page memo

Names, addresses, ownership and the amount should match across every document. The documents checklist has the full list.

Why Your Bank Statements Decide the File

For a working capital lender, the statements are the application. Overdrafts, long stretches of low balances and unexplained transfers slow the review or end it. If you can run one clean month before applying, do it. If you cannot wait, explain each problem in your memo; underwriters take a clear explanation far better than silence.

How Do You Move Fast Without Taking a Bad Deal?

The fastest money is usually the most expensive. Before you sign:

  1. Confirm the net proceeds after fees still cover your plan.
  2. Convert any factor rate to APR and compare total payback.
  3. Check whether paying early saves you anything.
  4. Walk away from any offer the lender cannot explain in writing.

Working capital is for gaps that turn back into cash within months. For long-term projects, a term loan or equipment financing usually costs less; line of credit vs term loan compares the two main options. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a startup get a working capital loan?

Yes, once it has some history. Most working capital lenders want at least 6 months in business, $10,000 or more in monthly revenue and 550+ credit, because they size and collect the loan from your deposits. Before that, equipment financing, an SBA microloan or personal credit are the usual routes.

How do you get working capital for a new small business?

Start with 3-6 months of complete bank statements, formation documents, owner ID and a table showing what the money pays for. Apply to lenders that fund your stage: online lenders at 6+ months, banks and SBA lenders usually at 2+ years. A clean file with no unexplained overdrafts moves fastest.

How much would a $50,000 working capital loan cost?

At 14% APR over 24 months, about $2,401 a month and $7,615 in total interest. At 30% over 12 months, about $4,874 a month and $8,492 in interest. A merchant cash advance at a 1.30 factor means paying back $65,000, which over 6 months works out to roughly 80% APR.

Which lenders give working capital loans to startups?

Online lenders are the main source for businesses between 6 months and 2 years old, at about 14-30% APR. Banks and SBA lenders price lower, about 9-14%, but usually want 680+ credit and 2+ years in business. Merchant cash advance providers accept 500+ credit but cost far more.

How fast can a startup working capital loan fund?

Once approved, short-term working capital often funds in 48 hours to 1 week. The usual delays are missing documents, an unclear use of funds and statement problems like overdrafts. Plan on 30 days end to end if you are still pulling the file together.

Is a line of credit better than a working capital loan?

For repeating gaps, like payroll before invoices clear, a line of credit is usually cheaper because you pay interest only on what you draw. For a defined one-time need, a loan with a fixed payment fits better. Startup lines tend to want 680-700+ credit, so many businesses get the loan first.
