Startup Business Loans for Veterans: What You Can Realistically Get

Serving helps with fees and support, but it doesn't replace a down payment or a plan. An honest look at what lenders fund for a brand-new business

Quick answer

No federal loan program funds veteran startups just for having served, and startups are hard to place. The usual routes are SBA microloans up to $50,000, or an SBA 7(a) loan with at least 10% equity from you. SBA Express waives its upfront fee for eligible veteran-owned firms. Being a Veteran Helps, but Not the Way Most People Expect

There's a common belief that veterans can get a special startup loan or a grant just for having served. The SBA's old Patriot Express program is gone, and lenders apply the same repayment tests to you as anyone else. What service does get you is real, though more modest:

  • Lower cost on some loans. SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 waives the upfront fee on SBA Express loans (up to $500,000) to businesses owned and controlled by eligible veterans, certain transitioning service members and spouses.
  • Priority handling. The SBA prioritizes veteran-owned applications it processes itself and encourages lenders to give them special consideration. That can mean speed, but it doesn't change approval standards.
  • Free help. Veterans Business Outreach Centers and Boots to Business can help you build a plan a lender can use.

Your service record helps in conversation, but it doesn't replace cash, credit or relevant experience. For veteran-owned companies already operating, see veteran business loans, which covers more choices.

Be careful with a merchant cash advance or a personal credit card used as your startup plan. A brand-new company has no sales to support daily debits. And SBA loans can't simply fund an idea with no plan behind it: lenders review your projections, assumptions and experience closely.

Why Startups Are Hard to Place

A lender's first question is how the loan gets repaid. An existing business can show tax returns. A startup has only a forecast, and many lenders we work with don't lend on projections alone. The SBA doesn't ban startup loans, but individual lenders can set tighter rules, which is why one bank's no may not be the final answer.

These are what usually tip the decision, framed as common lender practice rather than fixed rules:

  • Experience in that industry. A former aircraft mechanic opening a repair shop reads very differently from one opening a restaurant.
  • Personal credit. Many SBA lenders look for roughly 650-680 or higher.
  • Cash you've saved. SBA rules require equity, and more than the minimum makes you stronger.
  • Outside income. Retirement or disability income, or a spouse's job, can show you can survive the first year. Ask how a lender treats it.
  • Signed customers. A letter of intent or a contract can carry real weight, especially in government work.

The 10% Equity Rule and Where the Money Can Come From

Under SOP 50 10 8.1, a business that has operated for one year or less counts as a start-up, and every 7(a) start-up loan needs an equity injection of at least 10% of the total project cost. That means all costs needed to become operational, whichever source pays for them, except lines of credit and 504 loans.

  • Acceptable: cash that isn't borrowed, including from savings or a documented gift.
  • Possibly acceptable: debt on full standby, meaning no principal or interest payments for the term of the 7(a) loan. Ask your lender how much it counts.
  • Who can own the company: the SOP requires all direct and indirect owners and required guarantors to be U.S. citizens or nationals who live in the U.S. or its territories.

Note that a VA home loan is for a home, not a business. If you're thinking of borrowing against your house for equity, ask the lender first, and consider what happens to your family if the business fails.

Worked Example: Opening a $145,000 Shop

Illustrative numbers, not a quote. A veteran with ten years of relevant experience opens a shop needing $140,000 for buildout, equipment and early expenses, plus about $5,000 in closing costs. With an estimated guaranty fee of about $2,250, the total project comes to about $147,250.

On that structure, the business has to produce about $1,826 a month plus a cushion before the owner takes a paycheck, starting close to day one. The guaranty fee is figured using 2% of the 85% guaranteed share for loans of $150,000 or less, per the FY2027 fee notice; an SBA Express loan to an eligible veteran wouldn't carry it. Keep several months of operating cash outside the loan, because a line of credit is hard to get right after opening.

What to Do Before You Apply

  1. Talk to a VBOC or SCORE mentor. It's free, and a sharper plan helps with any lender.
  2. Write down the numbers. Include startup costs, monthly costs and how you'll live during the first year.
  3. Check your credit. Fix errors now, since you can't correct them in a hurry mid-application.
  4. Gather your DD-214. Lenders and the SBA may need proof of veteran status.
  5. Consider buying instead of starting. An existing business has cash flow to underwrite; see buying a business with 5% down.
  6. Apply to lenders that do startups. That's where a broker helps most. Get matched, or read how to qualify for startup financing.

Selling to the government later? Learn about veteran-owned certification once you're operating. It can open set-aside contracts. See startup financing for non-veteran routes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there startup business loans just for veterans?

Not as a separate federal loan program. Veterans use the same SBA and private options as other owners, with benefits such as an upfront fee waiver on SBA Express loans and priority handling when the SBA processes the application itself.

Can a veteran get an SBA loan with no money down to start a business?

Generally no. SOP 50 10 8.1 requires at least 10% equity on a 7(a) loan to a start-up, based on total project cost. Some of this may be able to come from debt on full standby, which your lender would need to review.

What credit score do veterans need for a startup loan?

There isn't a single veteran standard. Many SBA lenders look for roughly 650-680 or higher, while microloan intermediaries set their own criteria. These are typical ranges, not a quote.

Does the VA give business loans?

A VA home loan is for housing, so don't plan on it for a company. For business lending, veterans generally go through SBA programs and private lenders.

Can I get a grant instead of a loan?

Small-business grants exist, but they're competitive and rarely enough to fund a startup. Be suspicious of anyone charging a fee to get you a grant, and treat grants as a bonus.

Is it easier to buy a business than start one?

Often, because the business has tax returns a lender can underwrite. SBA rules still require at least 10% equity on a typical first acquisition, and half of that may come from a seller note on full standby.
