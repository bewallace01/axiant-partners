How Startup Equipment Financing Works

The lender pays the seller for the equipment, you make fixed monthly payments, and the equipment is the collateral. That's why a young business can get equipment financing before it can get an unsecured loan: if payments stop, the lender can take the equipment back and sell it. Your personal credit and early deposits still decide the price.

Approvals are routine for equipment that holds its value: construction, trucking, manufacturing, restaurant and medical equipment. Machines that resell easily can lower the credit bar by about 30-50 points compared with specialized or hard-to-resell equipment.

What You Need to Qualify

  • Time in business: 6+ months opens many programs. Some specialty lenders consider 3-4 months of revenue when credit and the equipment are strong. Two or more years opens the best programs.
  • Personal credit: 660-680+ for most startup programs; 700+ for the best terms.
  • Revenue: steady deposits matter more than the amount. Even 6-12 months of consistent deposits helps.
  • Down payment: plan for 10-20%, or a personal guarantor.
  • The equipment: a quote or invoice from an established seller, ideally for equipment that resells well.

The full checklist is in equipment financing requirements.

What Credit Score Do You Need as a Startup?

What a business under two years old can expect, by personal credit score:

  • 700+: the best startup terms; $0 down may be possible on new equipment.
  • 660-699: most startup programs open up.
  • 600-659: approvals happen, with a higher rate and about 10-20% down.
  • 550-599: usually a specialty lender, more down and a shorter term.
  • Below 550: typically 25%+ down or a cosigner.

True no-credit-check equipment financing is rare; most lenders still check, often with a soft pull. What no credit check really means.

Startup Equipment Financing Rates and Monthly Payments

Under 2 years in business, rates typically run about 12-22% APR, and terms are often shorter at first: 24-36 months is common, with longer terms as you build history. Here's what that means each month on a 3-year term (principal and interest only, before taxes and fees):

If You're Too New for Equipment Financing

With no deposits yet, most equipment lenders will say no. The realistic options:

  • An SBA microloan: up to $50,000 through nonprofit lenders, built for startups. How microloan approval works.
  • A bigger down payment with strong personal credit, through a specialty lender.
  • Used equipment: a smaller loan is easier to approve, though used equipment usually needs 10-20% down. Financing used equipment.
  • Waiting a few months: once you have 3-6 months of bank statements, far more equipment lenders will look at you.

SBA 7(a) loans offer lower rates but usually want 2+ years in business, a 6-12 week process and a personal guarantee.

Lease or Loan for a Startup?

A lease usually keeps more cash in the business: payments typically run about 10-20% lower than a loan on the same equipment, and at the end you can buy it, return it or renew. A loan makes sense for equipment you'll run for years, because it's yours from day one. Equipment leasing vs a loan compares the total cost.

Mistakes That Get Startups Declined

  • Applying too early. Wait until you can show at least a few months of deposits.
  • Buying for the business you hope to have. Size the request to the next 6-12 months of real demand, then add equipment once it's earning.
  • No down payment set aside. Budget 10-20% plus taxes, delivery and installation.
  • Skipping a credit check on yourself. Fix report errors and pay down card balances before you apply.
  • Applying to many lenders yourself. Several hard pulls in a short time can lower your score. One application through a broker reaches several lenders.

What to Have Ready

  • The equipment quote or invoice
  • 3-6 months of business bank statements
  • Business formation documents
  • A signed credit application
  • For very new businesses: a short business plan and budget

Initial approval typically takes 24-72 hours, and funding usually lands within 5-10 business days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a startup get equipment financing?

Yes. Many programs accept businesses with 6+ months in operation, 660-680+ personal credit and steady deposits, because the equipment secures the loan. Under 2 years, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor.

Can I get equipment financing with no business history?

It's hard. Most equipment lenders want to see at least a few months of bank deposits; some specialty lenders consider 3-4 months when your credit and the equipment are strong. Before that, look at an SBA microloan (up to $50,000) or a larger down payment.

What credit score do I need for startup equipment financing?

Most startup programs look for 660-680+. Scores of 700+ get the best terms. In the 600s you can still be approved with a higher rate and more down, and below 600 options narrow. See equipment financing with bad credit.

How much down payment does a new business need?

Usually 10-20% under 2 years in business. With 700+ credit, buying new equipment from an established dealer, $0 down is sometimes possible. More on no money down equipment financing.

Should a startup lease or buy equipment?

Lease if keeping cash matters most or you'll upgrade the equipment; lease payments usually run about 10-20% lower. Buy with a loan if you'll use it for years. Leasing vs a loan, compared.

How fast can a startup get approved?

Initial approvals typically come back in 24-72 hours. A complete file (the equipment quote, 3-6 months of bank statements, formation documents and a signed application) moves fastest.
