Can You Get a Business Loan With No Revenue?

You can, but the question a lender asks changes. With revenue, they ask whether the business can make the payment. With no revenue, they ask whether you can, and what they can take back if the plan slips. That is why a pre-revenue file lives or dies on four things: your personal credit, the cash you put in, collateral, and how specific the plan is.

On credit, most startup lenders want a personal FICO of 680 or better, and 700+ opens stronger terms (see startup financing requirements). Below that, secured structures can still work, but the down payment usually goes up.

What drops out is anything sized on your deposits. Working capital loans generally want at least 6 months in business and $10,000 or more a month in revenue, and a merchant cash advance wants at least 6 months of history. If someone offers you either before you have sales, read the terms twice.

SBA microloan. SBA microloans top out at $50,000 and come through nonprofit intermediary lenders, which care more about a clean, sized request than about time in business. The SBA microloan approval guide walks through the file.

Equipment financing. If the money is mostly for a truck, oven, machine or build-out equipment, finance that piece on its own. The equipment secures the loan, so history matters less. Under two years in business, equipment financing typically prices at about 12-22% APR with 10-20% down, or with a personal guarantor.

SBA 7(a) start-up loan. SBA treats a business operating one year or less as a start-up. Every 7(a) start-up loan needs an equity injection of at least 10% of the total project cost, so a $300,000 project means at least $30,000 of your own money in the deal. Expect a full business plan and projections.

Personal borrowing. A personal loan (typically $5,000-$100,000 at 8-29% APR over 3-5 years) is one of the few tools that works before revenue, because it is approved on your income. The trade-off is personal liability and a smaller cap; see personal loan vs business loan. Home equity is the other personal route, and it puts your house behind the business (HELOC for business covers that risk).

What Is the Easiest Business Loan to Get With No Revenue?

The easiest loan is the one where the lender has something to hold. If you are buying equipment, that is equipment financing. If you need less than $50,000 for mixed startup costs, a microloan is usually next. If your credit is strong and the need is small, a personal loan is often fastest, because it skips business underwriting entirely.

The hardest ask is a large, unsecured loan for "working capital" with no sales and no collateral. Few lenders will do it, and the ones that advertise it tend to be the ones charging upfront fees. The startup loan red flags guide lists what to watch for.

How Much Would a $50,000 Business Loan Cost?

It depends on rate and term more than on the amount. Two examples inside the 12-22% band startup equipment loans usually fall in:

  • 12% APR over 60 months: about $1,112 a month, $16,733 in total interest.
  • 19% APR over 36 months: about $1,833 a month, $15,981 in total interest.

Notice the shorter loan has the bigger payment even though total interest is similar. With no revenue yet, the monthly payment is what matters: it has to come out of your savings or income until sales start, so size the loan to a payment you can cover for at least the first several months.

What Lenders Look At When There Are No Sales

Your credit and track record. Personal credit carries more weight than it will later, and industry experience helps.

Exactly what the money buys. "A used reach-in cooler, prep line and POS system, per the attached quotes" gets further than a round number labeled "startup costs."

Whether you are ready to open. Formation documents, licenses, a signed lease, contracts or letters of intent all count as evidence.

A clean file. Formation docs, owner ID, recent bank statements, a launch timeline and a use-of-funds table. Add signed contracts, vendor quotes or booked work if you have them. Lenders do not need perfection; they need the numbers to match across every document.

If your need is bigger than one lender will cover, split it. Ask for a first phase tied to opening, then come back for the second phase once you have a few months of deposits to show. That second conversation is usually faster.

How Do You Raise Money for a Business Without a Loan?

Savings, friends and family, and investors who take a share of the company are the main sources. Grants exist but are narrow. Most founders end up mixing a little of each with a small loan, partly because every lender above wants to see your own money in the deal. If keeping ownership matters to you, read financing a new business without giving up equity.

What to Avoid in No-Revenue Applications

  • Vague use-of-funds language and round-number requests with no quotes behind them.
  • Asking for more than your plan supports.
  • Missing legal and ownership records.
  • Projections with no assumptions behind them.
  • Applying to lenders that require revenue you do not have yet. Every decline costs time, and some cost a hard credit pull.

A 30-Day Plan Before You Apply

Week 1: Build the use-of-funds table, launch timeline and milestones.

Week 2: Finalize formation documents, ownership records and bank statements.

Week 3: Gather evidence of demand: contracts, quotes, letters of intent.

Week 4: Check that every number matches across the file, then apply to lenders that fund your stage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you get a startup business loan with no revenue?

Yes, but the lender underwrites you instead of the business. Expect them to look at your personal credit (680+ is the usual floor, 700+ gets better terms), the cash you are putting in, any collateral, and a specific plan for the money. The products that work are SBA microloans, equipment financing, SBA 7(a) start-up loans and personal borrowing.

What is the easiest business loan to get with no revenue?

If you are buying equipment, equipment financing is usually the easiest, because the machine is the collateral. Under two years in business, expect about 12-22% APR and 10-20% down, or a personal guarantor. For smaller general needs, an SBA microloan of up to $50,000 from a nonprofit lender is often the next easiest.

How much would a $50,000 business loan cost?

At 12% APR over 60 months, $50,000 costs about $1,112 a month and $16,733 in total interest. At 19% over 36 months it is about $1,833 a month and $15,981 in interest. Startup equipment loans run about 12-22% APR, so most no-revenue borrowers land somewhere in that band.

Can an LLC get a startup loan with no revenue?

Yes. A new LLC can be the borrower, but with no history behind it the lender looks through to the owner and asks for a personal guarantee. Forming the LLC helps with structure and starts your business credit file; it does not replace personal credit on the first loan.

Why won't working capital lenders fund a business with no revenue?

Working capital loans and merchant cash advances are sized and repaid from your deposits. Most want at least 6 months in business, and working capital lenders commonly look for $10,000 or more in monthly revenue. With no deposits there is nothing for them to size the advance against.

How do you raise money for a business without a loan?

The usual sources are your own savings, friends and family, and outside investors who take equity. Grants exist but are narrow and competitive. Many founders combine some of their own cash with a small loan, since a lender wants to see you put money in anyway.
