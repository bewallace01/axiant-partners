USDA B&I Eligible Areas: How to Tell if Your Town Counts as Rural

The 50,000-person test, the urbanized-area catch that trips up edge-of-town sites, and how to check an address on USDA's map before you sign anything

Quick answer

The Rule in One Sentence

Under 7 CFR 5001.3, a rural area is any part of a state that is not in a city or town of more than 50,000 inhabitants and not in the urbanized area contiguous and adjacent to such a city or town. USDA uses the latest decennial census to decide. That is all of it, and the second half matters more than people expect.

So the question isn't how many people live in your town. It is whether the parcel sits inside the built-up area that has grown out from a bigger city. A town of 8,000 can fail if it has become a suburb of a metro. A town of 45,000 standing on its own can pass.

This page covers only location. For the program itself, see USDA Business & Industry loans.

How Typical Locations Fall Under the Rule

These are applications of the rule's text to common situations, not determinations. Only USDA's own review decides a specific address.

The last row is easy to miss. Under 7 CFR 5001.105(c), when a project sits partly in a rural area and partly outside it, the guarantee covers only the amount needed for the rural part.

How to Check an Address on USDA's Map

The rule names the USDA Property Eligibility Map as the place where areas next to an urbanized area are shown as ineligible. It is run from census tract data.

  1. Use the business programs map. The site also has housing maps, and USDA's housing programs use different rules. Pick the business program option that covers B&I.
  2. Enter the exact property address, not the mailing address of the business or the seller's office.
  3. Read the shading at parcel level. Zoom in. Boundaries often run along highways and town lines, and a site near one can land on either side.
  4. Save the result as a screenshot or printout with the date. Your lender will still confirm it, but it saves a wasted application.
  5. Check every parcel. A hotel with overflow parking across the road, or a plant with a separate warehouse, may cover more than one tract.

Eligibility is a condition of approval, not something to fix later. The rule says applications cannot be approved subject to meeting the rural area requirement.

If Your Site Is Borderline

Two exceptions in 7 CFR 5001.3 can pull a site inside an urbanized area back into eligibility. Both depend on a determination that the area is rural in character, made by the Under Secretary for Rural Development:

  • A large, spread-out urbanized area. It has two points on its boundary at least 40 miles apart and doesn't touch a city of more than 150,000 people or that city's urbanized area.
  • Close to a rural area. The site is in an urbanized area next to a city of more than 50,000 but within a quarter mile of a rural area.

A unit of local government, such as the town or county, files the petition through the Rural Development State Director. Determinations are project specific, so a neighbor's approval doesn't carry over to your site. Allow for the time this takes before you set a closing date.

Two kinds of projects can be outside rural areas entirely: cooperatives doing value-added agricultural processing for producers within 80 miles, and local food projects that meet the conditions in 5001.105(b)(15). For most hotels, plants and shops, the rural test applies.

What Location Doesn't Settle

Passing the map is only the first gate. A rural site still has to be an eligible project, with enough equity and collateral, and a lender willing to make it. Golf courses, racetracks, apartments and passive rental property are out wherever they are. Lenders also look harder at small markets, where resale value and demand are thinner.

For what the program will pay for, see USDA Business & Industry loans. For hotels and motels specifically, see USDA hotel loans. If your town is over the line, compare an SBA loan in USDA B&I vs SBA 7(a), since SBA has no population test.

Frequently Asked Questions

What population counts as rural for a USDA business loan?

For B&I, any area outside a city or town of more than 50,000 inhabitants and outside the urbanized area contiguous and adjacent to that city, based on the latest decennial census.

Can a small town near a big city be ineligible?

Yes. If the town sits inside the urbanized area attached to a city of more than 50,000, it is generally ineligible even if the town itself is small, unless a rural-in-character determination applies.

Where do I check whether an address is eligible?

USDA's Rural Development Property Eligibility Map, using the business programs map rather than the housing maps. Your lender and USDA confirm it during the application.

Can I apply and fix the location question later?

No. The rule says applications cannot be approved subject to meeting the rural area requirement.

What if part of my project is in an ineligible area?

USDA guarantees only the amount needed to finance the part of the project located in the eligible rural area.

Do USDA housing maps apply to business loans?

No. Housing programs use their own definitions, so use the business program view of the map for B&I.
