How a Fuel Surcharge Is Calculated

A fuel surcharge (FSC) is meant to move the cost of diesel above a set level from the carrier to the shipper. The common formula is:

FSC per mile = (current diesel price − base price) ÷ mpg divisor

  • Current price is usually the EIA weekly on-highway diesel average, national or regional, published each week.
  • Base price is the diesel price already built into the line-haul rate. Below it, no surcharge is paid.
  • Mpg divisor is the fuel economy the table assumes. A divisor of 6.0 pays for 6 miles per gallon whether your truck gets 5.5 or 7.

Each shipper, broker and carrier sets its own base, divisor and index, so two loads on the same day can carry very different surcharges.

Four Reasons the Surcharge Falls Short

  1. Deadhead isn’t paid. The surcharge is paid on loaded or billed miles. Fuel burned driving empty to the next pickup comes out of your rate.
  2. Your mpg is below the divisor. Heavy loads, mountains, idling and an older engine all cut real fuel economy. Every tenth of a mpg below the divisor is fuel you buy and aren’t paid for.
  3. Lag. A weekly index trails the pump. When diesel climbs fast you pay this week’s price and are reimbursed on last week’s.
  4. All-in rates. On many spot loads the surcharge is folded into one rate per mile. When diesel rises after you book, nothing adjusts.

On top of those, the cash timing: you pay for fuel at the pump, and the freight bill that carries the surcharge may not pay for 30 days or more. See the net-30 cash gap.

Worked Example: The Gap at Three Diesel Prices

Illustrative assumptions, not a quote. An owner-operator runs 10,000 loaded and 1,500 empty miles a month at 6.2 mpg. The surcharge table uses a $1.25 base and a 6.0 divisor, paid on loaded miles. That is about 1,855 gallons a month.

The gap grows with the price because the unpaid deadhead gallons get more expensive. Over a year at $3.75 it is about $5,600, before any lag or all-in loads. The total fuel bill in this example is about $6,956 a month at $3.75, paid weeks before the loads that carry the surcharge settle.

Fix the Contract Before You Finance the Gap

  • Ask for the table in writing: base price, divisor, index (national or regional) and the day of the week it resets.
  • Quote spot loads with a separate FSC, or price the all-in rate off today’s diesel, not last month’s.
  • Cut deadhead with backhauls and lane planning; it is the biggest unpaid line. See deadhead miles.
  • If you are leased on, federal truth-in-leasing rules apply. 49 CFR 376.12 requires your pay to be stated in the lease, gives you a copy of the rated freight bill when you are paid a percentage of the load’s revenue, and requires charge-backs to be itemized with how each is computed. That is how you check whether the surcharge the shipper paid reached you.

What Lenders Look at on an Owner-Operator File

  • Settlement statements and bank deposits, commonly three to six months, showing steady loads.
  • Your operating authority or lease agreement and how long you have run under it.
  • Who your customers are: factoring companies price on the brokers’ and shippers’ credit, not only yours.
  • Fuel cards and existing advances already drawing on settlements.
  • The truck: year, mileage and what is owed, if you borrow against it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why doesn't my fuel surcharge cover my fuel cost?

Because it is usually paid on loaded miles only, assumes an mpg your truck may not get, and trails the pump by up to a week. In the example on this page those leaks add up to about $470 a month at $3.75 diesel.

How is a trucking fuel surcharge calculated?

Commonly as (current diesel price minus a base price) divided by an mpg divisor, paid per mile. The current price is usually the EIA weekly on-highway diesel average.

Do I get a fuel surcharge on deadhead miles?

Usually not. Surcharges are paid on loaded or billed miles, so fuel burned running empty is a cost you carry unless the rate covers it.

Can a leased owner-operator see what fuel surcharge the shipper paid?

If you are paid a percentage of the load's revenue, 49 CFR 376.12 requires the carrier to give you a copy of the rated freight bill, and lets you examine the documents rates are computed from.

What is the best financing for high fuel costs?

For timing, freight factoring with a fuel advance or a business line of credit. Factoring typically costs about 1-5% per invoice; a line commonly runs 8-13% APR at a bank. Typical ranges, not a quote.

Should an owner-operator use a merchant cash advance for fuel?

Rarely. A factor of 1.15-1.50 repaid by daily debits adds a fixed payment to a business whose problem is already cash timing.
