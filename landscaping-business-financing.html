Industry Overview

Landscaping businesses face high upfront costs for mowers, excavators, skid steers, and trailers. Seasonal demand—spring and fall peaks—creates cash flow gaps between equipment purchases, material costs, and customer payments. Many landscapers need financing to add or replace equipment without draining reserves. Working capital helps cover fuel, mulch, plants, and labor during the busy season when payroll and material costs spike.

Commercial mowers and excavators can cost $15,000–$80,000 or more per unit. Equipment financing and working capital loans help landscaping companies grow their fleet and manage seasonal expenses. SBA loans support longer-term needs like real estate (nursery, yard, shop) and business acquisition, while lines of credit provide flexibility for materials and payroll.

Financing Options for Landscaping Companies

Landscapers use a range of financing products depending on the use of funds, timeline, and business profile. Below are the main options landscaping companies typically use.

Equipment Financing

Landscaping companies use equipment loans and leases to finance mowers, excavators, skid steers, stump grinders, and trailers. Loans spread the cost over the equipment's useful life; leases often require less upfront capital and can include trade-in options. Lenders commonly finance new and used landscaping equipment. Explore equipment financing options.

SBA Loans

SBA 7(a) and 504 loans offer longer terms and lower down payments than conventional bank loans. Landscaping companies use SBA financing for working capital, equipment, commercial real estate (yard, nursery, shop), and business acquisition. Approval timelines are typically 30–60 days or longer. Learn about SBA loan programs.

Working Capital Loans

Working capital loans help landscapers cover fuel, materials, payroll, and subcontractors during the busy season. When customer payments lag or projects ramp up, short-term working capital keeps operations running. Many lenders offer terms aligned with landscaping revenue cycles. Explore working capital options.

Business Line of Credit

A revolving line of credit gives landscaping companies flexible access to capital. Draw when needed—for materials, fuel, or bridging a slow period—and repay as cash comes in. Lines of credit are well-suited to seasonal revenue and project-based cash flow. Learn about business lines of credit.

Commercial Vehicle Financing

Pickup trucks, dump trucks, and trailers are financed through equipment loans or leases. Commercial vehicle financing covers trucks used for hauling equipment, mulch, and materials. Terms often match the vehicle's useful life. View equipment and vehicle financing.

Commercial Real Estate Loans

Owner-occupied real estate financing helps landscaping companies purchase or refinance yards, nurseries, and storage facilities. SBA 504 and conventional commercial mortgages are common. Explore commercial real estate financing.

Common Landscaping Equipment That Businesses Finance

Landscaping companies frequently finance mowers, excavators, and specialty equipment. Below are common types, typical cost ranges, and why businesses finance them.

Zero-Turn Mowers

Zero-turn mowers provide fast, efficient mowing for commercial lawns. New commercial models typically cost $6,000–$15,000 or more. Many landscapers finance zero-turns to add capacity or replace aging units without large upfront outlays.

Explore mower financing

Commercial Mowers

Walk-behind and stand-on mowers handle tight spaces and residential properties. They typically cost $3,000–$12,000. Financing helps landscapers add mowers for crew expansion and route growth.

Explore commercial mower financing

Skid Steers

Skid steers handle grading, material handling, and attachments for hardscape and landscape projects. They typically cost $25,000–$75,000. Financing helps landscapers add versatile equipment for larger projects.

Explore skid steer financing

Mini Excavators

Mini excavators perform digging, trenching, and grading for hardscape, irrigation, and tree work. They typically cost $25,000–$60,000. Financing spreads the cost over the equipment's productive life.

Explore excavator financing

Stump Grinders

Stump grinders remove tree stumps after removal. Tow-behind and self-propelled units range from roughly $5,000 to $30,000 or more. Financing helps tree and landscape companies add stump removal services.

Explore stump grinder financing

Landscape Trailers

Dump and utility trailers haul mowers, equipment, mulch, and materials. Trailers typically cost $3,000–$25,000 or more depending on size and features. Financing helps landscapers add trailers for crew capacity.

Explore trailer financing
