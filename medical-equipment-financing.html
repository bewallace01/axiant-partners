Medical equipment financing is a loan or lease used to acquire the equipment a practice runs on — imaging systems, dental and lab equipment, diagnostic and surgical devices, and exam-room technology — with the equipment itself securing the financing.

What Medical Equipment Financing Covers

A practice runs on expensive, long-lived equipment, and nearly all of it can be financed with the equipment as collateral. Axiant covers the full range, with dedicated guides for each category:

  • Medical imaging and radiology — MRI, CT, X-ray, ultrasound, and PET, including MRI and CT scanner financing. These are the largest single purchases most practices make.
  • Dental equipment — chairs, CBCT and digital imaging, CEREC and CAD/CAM, and full operatory build-outs; see also dental practice financing.
  • Laboratory equipment — analyzers, centrifuges, and diagnostic lab systems.
  • Diagnostic devices — the point-of-care and monitoring devices a practice depends on.
  • Surgical, exam-room, and practice equipment — tables, sterilization, EMR hardware, and the fit-out of a new or expanding practice.

Whether you are opening, relocating, or upgrading, financing spreads the cost across the years the equipment earns revenue instead of tying up practice capital.

Loan vs. Lease vs. Section 179 — How Medical Financing Is Structured

There are three levers in a medical equipment deal, and the right mix depends on your tax position and how long you will keep the equipment:

  • Equipment loan. You own the equipment and build equity; the equipment secures the loan, so rates are lower than unsecured borrowing. Best for long-lived assets you will keep for years — imaging systems, dental chairs, lab analyzers.
  • Equipment lease. Lower upfront cost and a fit for technology that dates quickly (some diagnostic and IT equipment) or when you want to preserve capital and upgrade at end of term. A fair-market-value or $1-buyout lease each has different ownership and tax treatment.
  • Section 179 and bonus depreciation. A financed purchase can often be expensed under Section 179 in the year it is placed in service — a major reason practices finance rather than pay cash (more below).

Section 179 and the Tax Case for Financing Medical Equipment

The tax treatment is what makes financing medical equipment so attractive. Under Section 179, a practice can often deduct the full purchase price of qualifying equipment in the year it is placed in service, up to the annual limit — even when the equipment is financed and you have only made a few payments. In practice that means a practice can acquire a major piece of equipment, put it to work generating revenue, deduct a large share of the cost this tax year, and pay for it over time out of that revenue. Bonus depreciation can apply on top for amounts above the Section 179 cap. The result is that financing frequently produces a first-year tax benefit that offsets a meaningful part of the payments. Always confirm the specifics with your accountant, since limits and eligibility change and depend on your practice's situation — but this is the core reason cash-rich practices still finance.

Rates, Terms, and Down Payment

Medical equipment financing terms track the equipment and the practice:

  • Term commonly runs two to seven years, matched to the equipment's useful life — longer for durable imaging and dental systems, shorter for technology that dates faster.
  • Down payment is often low, and 0%-down or 100% financing is available for established practices with strong credit; newer practices or used equipment may require a down payment.
  • Rate depends on the practice's credit and financials, the equipment type and age, and whether it is a loan or lease.
  • Collateral is the equipment, verified by the invoice and, for used systems, condition and service history.

Because medical lenders understand the equipment and the economics of a practice, matching your deal to the right lender is what secures the best rate — an imaging center, a dental practice, and a reference lab are underwritten differently.

How to Qualify for Medical Equipment Financing

Lenders underwrite both the equipment and the practice:

  1. The equipment. New, standard, resaleable medical equipment finances most easily because it holds value as collateral. Used and refurbished systems are financeable with condition and service records.
  2. The practice. Business and personal credit, practice financials and collections, and time in practice. Strong, growing collections make approval and terms better.
  3. New practices. A start-up or newly acquired practice can still finance equipment — lenders lean on the provider's credit, the business plan, and the equipment as collateral; see medical practice financing for the broader picture.
  4. Documentation. The equipment quote, practice financials, and basic details — having them ready is the biggest factor in funding speed.

New, Used, and Refurbished Medical Equipment

Medical equipment finances across the condition spectrum. New equipment from the manufacturer has clear value, a warranty, and often the best terms and lowest down payment. Used and refurbished systems — common and cost-effective in imaging and dental — are financeable when the age, condition, and service history check out; certified-refurbished imaging with a service contract finances especially well. Financing a quality refurbished system can be the most capital-efficient way to add capacity, and it still qualifies for Section 179 treatment when placed in service.

A Real Medical Equipment Financing Example

Say a growing imaging center is adding a $450,000 MRI system. Rather than pay cash, the practice finances it over seven years with little or no money down, secured by the scanner. Under Section 179 and bonus depreciation, a large share of the $450,000 can often be deducted in the first year even though only a few payments have been made — while the scanner immediately begins generating scan revenue that covers the payment. Two things happen at once: the practice keeps its cash for staffing and operations, and the first-year tax deduction offsets much of the year's cost. The same logic applies to a $120,000 dental CBCT-and-chair build-out or a $60,000 lab analyzer. Run your equipment and practice numbers, then get matched for real terms.

Types of Medical Equipment Financing Companies

“Medical equipment financing companies” is not one category. Five distinct types compete for practice equipment deals, and they price, underwrite, and structure very differently. Knowing which you are talking to explains most of the variation you will see in quotes.

Vendor and manufacturer finance arms

Most large imaging, dental, and lab manufacturers run a captive finance desk, or partner with one, and will present financing at the point of sale. It is convenient and sometimes subsidized with promotional rates to move equipment. The trade-off is that the offer is tied to that manufacturer’s hardware, so it gives you no leverage on the equipment price itself and no comparison against outside money.

Banks and credit unions

Banks generally offer the lowest rates for established practices with strong financials and an existing relationship. They are also the slowest and the most conservative on used or refurbished equipment, start-up practices, and specialty devices with thin resale markets. Expect a longer document list and a decision measured in weeks rather than days.

Independent equipment lessors

Independent lessors specialize in equipment as an asset class and are usually the most flexible on structure — fair-market-value leases, $1 buyouts, deferred and step payment plans, and seasonal schedules. They are more willing to finance used and refurbished systems because they understand secondary-market values. Rates sit above bank pricing in exchange for that flexibility and speed.

SBA lenders

SBA 7(a) fits when equipment is one piece of a larger need — acquiring a practice, opening a location, or bundling equipment with working capital and leasehold improvements. Terms run far longer than a standard equipment loan and down payments are lower, but approval typically takes 30 to 60+ days, which rules it out when a scanner has to be installed this quarter. See SBA loans.

Brokers and lender marketplaces

A broker submits one application across multiple funding sources and brings back competing offers. This is the fastest way to see where your practice actually prices without filing four separate applications and taking four credit inquiries. What matters is whether the broker discloses how it is compensated and whether the offers are real approvals rather than indicative quotes. Axiant Partners operates this way.

Medical Equipment Financing FAQs

Can you finance medical equipment?

Yes. Medical equipment financing is a well-established category. Imaging systems, dental and lab equipment, diagnostic and surgical devices, and exam-room technology can all be financed with a loan or lease secured by the equipment, for new, used, and refurbished purchases. Terms depend on the equipment, your practice financials, and whether it is a loan or a lease.

Can you use Section 179 on financed medical equipment?

Generally yes. Section 179 lets a practice deduct the full purchase price of qualifying equipment in the year it is placed in service, up to the annual limit, even when the equipment is financed and only a few payments have been made. Bonus depreciation can apply on top. This first-year deduction is a major reason practices finance rather than pay cash. Confirm the specifics with your accountant.

What down payment do you need for medical equipment financing?

Down payments are often low, and 0%-down or 100% financing is available for established practices with strong credit. Newer practices, start-ups, or used equipment may require a down payment. The exact amount depends on the equipment, your credit, and the practice financials.

Can a new practice finance medical equipment?

Yes. A start-up or newly acquired practice can finance equipment even without a long operating history — lenders lean on the provider’s credit, the business plan, and the equipment as collateral. Financing the equipment is often part of a broader medical practice financing plan for opening or acquiring a practice.

Can you finance used or refurbished medical equipment?

Yes. Used and refurbished medical equipment, common in imaging and dental, is financeable when the age, condition, and service history check out. Certified-refurbished imaging with a service contract finances especially well, and it still qualifies for Section 179 treatment when placed in service.

What medical equipment can be financed?

Nearly all durable practice equipment: medical imaging (MRI, CT, X-ray, ultrasound), dental equipment (chairs, CBCT, CAD/CAM), laboratory equipment (analyzers, centrifuges), diagnostic and monitoring devices, and surgical, exam-room, and practice fit-out equipment. Each category has its own financing considerations, but all use the equipment as collateral.

What are the different types of medical equipment financing companies?

Five types compete for practice equipment deals and they price very differently. Vendor and manufacturer finance arms are convenient and sometimes subsidised, but tie you to that hardware. Banks and credit unions offer the lowest rates for established practices but are slowest and most conservative on used equipment and start-ups. Independent equipment lessors are the most flexible on structure and the most willing to finance refurbished systems, at rates above bank pricing. SBA lenders fit when equipment is bundled with an acquisition or build-out, but take 30 to 60+ days. Brokers and lender marketplaces submit one application across multiple funding sources so you see competing offers without multiple credit inquiries.
