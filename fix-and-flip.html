Fix and Flip Loans for Real Estate Investors Nationwide

Up to 80% Purchase & 100% Rehab Financing

Fix and flip loans provide short-term financing to purchase, renovate, and resell residential or commercial properties. Structured programs offer up to 80% of purchase price and 100% of rehab costs, helping investors maximize leverage and complete deals quickly.

What Is a Fix and Flip Loan?

A fix and flip loan is a short-term real estate loan designed for investors who buy distressed properties, renovate them, and sell for profit. Unlike traditional mortgages, these loans fund both the purchase and rehabilitation costs, with terms typically ranging from 6 to 18 months.

  • Short-term financing aligned with the flip timeline
  • Purchase and rehab funds in one facility
  • Quick approval and funding
  • Flexible structure based on the deal and borrower

How Fix and Flip Financing Works

01

Identify the Property

Find a distressed property with clear value-add potential. Establish purchase price and estimated rehab costs to determine loan needs.

02

Underwriting & Valuation

Lender evaluates the deal: purchase price, rehab scope, after-repair value (ARV), and your experience. Loan terms are based on LTV, LTC, and ARV guidelines.

03

Funding

Once approved, funds are disbursed-typically for purchase at closing and rehab in draws. Repayment is due upon sale or refinance of the property.

Maximum LTV, LTC & ARV Guidelines

Typical guidelines for structured fix and flip programs:

80% Max LTV (purchase)
100% Rehab financing
90% Max LTC
75% Max ARV

LTV = loan-to-value; LTC = loan-to-cost; ARV = after-repair value. Specific limits vary by lender and borrower strength.

Fix and Flip Loan Requirements

General requirements for most programs:

  • Down payment or skin in the deal (varies by program)
  • Rehab scope and contractor estimates
  • Experience (some programs available for newer investors)
  • Exit strategy: sale or refinance
  • Property in eligible state

Experienced flippers with strong track records may qualify for more favorable terms. First-time flippers can find programs with additional support.

What Can Fix and Flip Loans Be Used For?

Fix and flip financing typically covers:

  • Purchase of the property
  • Rehab and renovation costs (materials, labor, permits)
  • Carrying costs during the hold period (interest, insurance, utilities)
  • Closing costs and fees

Funds are structured to align with the project timeline-purchase at closing and rehab in draws as work is completed.

Hard Money vs. Structured Fix and Flip Loans

Two common structures—for a detailed comparison, see Fix and Flip Loan vs Hard Money Loan.

Hard Money

Asset-based lending with minimal income or credit requirements. Typically higher rates and fees, faster closing. Best for experienced investors with strong deals.

Structured Fix and Flip Loans

Programs with clearer terms, underwriting based on deal and borrower. May offer better rates and longer terms. Often suited for investors who want a more predictable process and competitive pricing.

What Determines Approval?

Lenders evaluate three main areas—for a detailed breakdown, see what lenders look for in a fix and flip loan.

  • The Deal: Purchase price, rehab budget, ARV, comparable sales, and profit margin. The numbers must support the loan and a reasonable return.
  • The Borrower: Experience, credit, liquidity, and track record. Some programs are available for newer investors with strong deals.
  • Exit Strategy: Clear plan to repay-typically sale within 6-18 months or refinance into long-term financing.

Typical Loan Terms

  • Term: 6-18 months (sometimes up to 24 months)
  • Interest rates: Typically higher than traditional mortgages due to short term and risk
  • Points: 1-3 points or more at origination
  • Draw schedule: Rehab funds released in stages as work progresses
  • Repayment: Balloon at maturity (sale or refinance)

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly can I close?

Hard money and structured fix and flip loans often close in 1-3 weeks, depending on the lender and deal complexity.

Do I need experience to qualify?

Some programs require a track record; others are available for newer investors with strong deals and realistic rehab plans.

How are rehab funds disbursed?

Typically in draws as work is completed. An inspection or appraisal is usually required before each draw.

What if I need more time to sell?

Some lenders offer extensions. Discuss hold period and extension options when applying.
