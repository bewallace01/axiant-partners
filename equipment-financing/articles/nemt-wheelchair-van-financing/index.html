NEMT & Wheelchair Van Financing for Medical Transport

How non-emergency medical transport operators finance wheelchair vans and ADA vehicles — conversion costs, fleet financing, and options for new NEMT businesses

Quick answer

NEMT (non-emergency medical transport) and wheelchair van financing covers the accessible vehicles that move patients to dialysis, appointments, and discharge. Cost by vehicle: converted wheelchair-accessible vans $50K–$90K; ADA cutaway buses $70K–$130K; stretcher (gurney) vans $60K–$100K. NEMT is one of the most common “buy a van, get a contract, grow a fleet” businesses in healthcare, and financing is what turns one accessible vehicle into a five-van operation. The wrinkle versus an ordinary cargo van is the accessibility conversion — the ramp or lift, lowered floor, and tie-down system — which adds cost and is part of what you finance. This guide covers vehicle costs, how the conversion is handled, and how new NEMT operators get approved. For the broader hub, see equipment financing.

Financing the Accessibility Conversion

The accessibility conversion is the difference between an ordinary van loan and NEMT financing. A lowered-floor minivan with an in-floor ramp, or a full-size van with a hydraulic lift, raised roof, and a Q’Straint-style tie-down system, adds roughly $15K–$35K to the base vehicle. Lenders finance the completed, converted vehicle — so get a single quote that itemizes the base van and the conversion, and make sure the converter is reputable, since the conversion is part of the collateral value. New stretcher and high-capacity ADA vehicles are usually purchased pre-converted from upfitters.

Financing Paths

  • Equipment / vehicle loan (48–72 months). The standard path for one or several converted vans; 10–20% down, term and rate by credit.
  • Fleet financing. As you add vehicles, lenders structure fleet lines so you can scale on contract growth rather than one approval at a time.
  • $1-buyout vs. FMV lease. $1-buyout to own and depreciate; FMV for lower payments and easy replacement as vans age out.
  • Startup-friendly structures. New NEMT companies bring a larger down payment plus broker (e.g., Medicaid NEMT broker) or facility transport contracts; thin files can look at equipment financing with bad credit.

What Lenders Look At

  • Contracts and payer mix — Medicaid NEMT broker agreements, facility or dialysis-center contracts, and private-pay volume support the payment.
  • Conversion quality and converter reputation — central to resale and collateral value.
  • Driver and insurance compliance — commercial auto and passenger coverage; some states license NEMT operators.
  • Time in business and credit — standard equipment financing requirements; startups offset with down payment and contracts.

A worked example: financing a wheelchair van

The accessibility conversion is what sets NEMT financing apart from an ordinary van loan. Take an operator financing a $70,000 converted wheelchair-accessible van — a base cargo or passenger van plus a lowered floor, ramp, and securements — with 10% down, leaving $63,000 over 60 months. At about 9% APR the payment is near $1,308 a month, readily covered by Medicaid NEMT broker trips or a dialysis-center contract running daily. Lenders finance the conversion as part of the vehicle, but they lean heavily on contracts and payer mix: a signed broker agreement or facility transport contract is often what turns the deal from a personal-credit decision into a business one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you finance an NEMT or wheelchair-accessible van?

Yes. Converted wheelchair vans finance as vehicles or equipment, with the accessibility conversion included, typically over 48–72 months.

Is the wheelchair conversion included in financing?

Usually yes — lenders finance the converted vehicle as a whole, including the lowered floor, ramp, and securements, since the conversion is integral to the van’s NEMT use and value.

Can a new NEMT business finance a van?

Yes. NEMT is a common startup. New operators qualify with a larger down payment plus broker (Medicaid NEMT) or facility/dialysis transport contracts that support the payment, along with proper licensing and insurance.

What do NEMT lenders look at?

Contracts and payer mix — Medicaid broker agreements, facility or dialysis-center contracts — plus the converted vehicle’s value, your credit, and time in business.

How much does a wheelchair-accessible van cost?

Illustrative ranges: converted wheelchair-accessible vans $50K–$90K; stretcher vans $60K–$100K; ADA cutaway buses $70K–$130K. The accessibility conversion alone adds roughly $15K–$35K to a base van. These are estimates, not quotes.

Can I finance the wheelchair conversion, not just the van?

Yes. Lenders finance the completed, converted vehicle. Get one quote itemizing the base van and the ramp/lift, lowered floor, and tie-down conversion so the financed amount and collateral value are clear.

Should I finance NEMT vans one at a time or as a fleet?

Start with an equipment/vehicle loan for your first van or two; as contracts grow, fleet financing lets you add vehicles on contract growth rather than re-qualifying for each unit.

Loan or lease for NEMT vehicles?

Choose a $1-buyout (or loan) to own and depreciate the vehicle, or an FMV lease for lower payments and easy replacement as high-mileage transport vans age out.
