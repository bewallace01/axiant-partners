Why Equipment Financing Works for New Businesses

Equipment financing is one of the most accessible options for startups and new businesses. Unlike working capital loans or unsecured lines of credit, equipment loans and leases are collateralized by the asset you're financing. If you default, the lender can repossess and resell the equipment. That reduces lender risk and often relaxes time-in-business requirements. Many equipment lenders accept businesses with as little as 6 months of operation when other factors are favorable. Compare this to SBA loans, which typically require 2+ years and heavier documentation. For new businesses, equipment financing often offers faster approvals and lighter paperwork.

Credit Score Expectations for Startup Equipment Financing

Personal credit carries more weight for new businesses because there's limited business credit history. Most equipment lenders prefer 660-680+ FICO for startups. Scores of 700+ often qualify for competitive rates, minimal or no down payment, and longer terms. If your credit is in the 600-650 range, expect slightly higher rates or a 10-15% down payment. Below 600, options narrow; see equipment financing with bad credit for specialty programs. The equipment itself helps: assets with strong secondary markets (construction equipment, commercial vehicles, medical devices) give lenders confidence and can offset weaker credit or shorter time in business.

Equipment Types That Approve More Easily for New Businesses

Not all equipment is equal in lenders' eyes. Assets with established resale markets are easier to finance because lenders can value and liquidate them if needed. Top categories for new business equipment financing include:

  • Construction equipment: Excavators, loaders, skid steers, dump trucks. See excavator financing, skid steer financing, and construction business financing.
  • Commercial vehicles: Semi trucks, box trucks, vans. See semi truck financing and box truck financing.
  • Restaurant and food service: Ovens, refrigeration, POS systems. See commercial kitchen financing and restaurant business financing.
  • Medical and dental: Imaging, exam equipment, surgical devices. See medical imaging financing and medical practice financing.
  • Manufacturing and industrial: CNC machines, conveyors, forklifts. See forklift financing and manufacturing business financing.

Highly customized or niche equipment is harder to finance. Used equipment may qualify with shorter terms; see can you finance used equipment. Browse equipment by type for industry-specific options.

Loan vs Lease for New Businesses

Both loans and leases can work for startups. Leases sometimes offer slightly more flexibility for newer businesses because the lessor retains ownership and can resell the asset. Monthly payments may be lower since you're not building equity. See equipment loan vs lease for a full comparison. Zero-down equipment financing is possible for strong credit; new businesses with 700+ FICO may qualify. TRAC leases can lower payments for vehicles and certain equipment; see TRAC lease benefits. Apply through a partner who has access to both loan and lease programs to surface the best fit.

Documentation New Businesses Need

Equipment financing typically requires less documentation than SBA or conventional bank loans. Expect to provide:

  • Bank statements: 3-6 months of business account(s) showing deposits and cash flow
  • Equipment quote or invoice: From your vendor or dealer with make, model, and price
  • Business formation documents: Articles of incorporation, operating agreement, or similar
  • Personal identification: Driver's license or passport
  • Tax returns (sometimes): Business and/or personal for larger amounts or stricter programs

Having everything ready speeds approval. See what lenders look at for equipment financing for the full underwriting checklist.

How to Improve Approval Odds as a New Business

  1. Strengthen personal credit: Pay down revolving debt, correct errors on your report. Aim for 680+ for best startup options.
  2. Show consistent revenue: Even 6-12 months of steady deposits helps. Avoid overdrafts and erratic patterns.
  3. Choose equipment with strong resale value: Construction, trucks, medical, and restaurant equipment approve more easily.
  4. Plan for a down payment: 10-15% can offset shorter time in business and improve terms.
  5. Apply through one channel: A marketplace or broker can submit to multiple lenders with one application, limiting credit inquiries.
  6. Consider a lease: Leases may offer more flexibility for startups; compare with loans.

When to Consider Alternatives

If equipment financing is out of reach, consider:

  • SBA loans: Require 2+ years typically, but offer low rates and long terms. See SBA loans and equipment financing vs SBA.
  • Vendor financing: Some equipment dealers offer in-house financing, often with relaxed requirements for their products.
  • Working capital or line of credit: If you need flexibility beyond a single equipment purchase, see working capital loans and business lines of credit. New businesses may face stricter requirements for unsecured options.

Industry-Specific Considerations for New Businesses

Different industries face different equipment financing landscapes. Construction and trucking businesses often qualify more easily because equipment like excavators, loaders, and semi-trucks has strong resale markets and predictable depreciation. Restaurant owners financing commercial kitchen equipment may need to demonstrate 6-12 months of consistent sales. Medical and dental practices typically need to show practice revenue and sometimes patient volume. Manufacturing startups may face tighter scrutiny on customized machinery. Agriculture and farming operations can leverage seasonal revenue patterns—lenders familiar with ag financing understand cash flow cycles. Whatever your industry, emphasize the equipment's role in revenue generation and choose assets with identifiable market value.

Common Mistakes New Businesses Make When Applying

Avoid these pitfalls to improve your approval odds: (1) Applying too early—wait until you have at least 6 months of revenue and bank statements to show. (2) Incomplete documentation—submit everything requested upfront to avoid delays. (3) Choosing hard-to-finance equipment—highly customized or niche assets are tougher; prioritize equipment with established resale markets. (4) Multiple applications in a short window—each hard inquiry can temporarily lower your score; use a single channel that submits to multiple lenders. (5) Ignoring credit before applying—check your report, correct errors, and pay down revolving debt. (6) Overestimating revenue—lenders verify with bank statements; inflated numbers backfire. See red flags in equipment finance agreements to avoid unfavorable terms.

Approval Timelines for New Business Equipment Financing

Equipment financing often funds in 24-48 hours to a few days for straightforward applications. New businesses may take slightly longer as lenders verify revenue and business stability. Complex equipment or larger amounts can extend the process. Having documentation ready—bank statements, equipment quote, formation documents—speeds underwriting significantly. Some lenders offer same-day decisions for simple deals. See how fast equipment financing can be approved for typical timelines and how to speed up the process.

Typical Rates and Terms for Startups

Rates for new businesses may be slightly higher than for established companies, reflecting higher perceived risk. Strong credit (700+) can still secure competitive terms. See typical equipment financing rates for ranges and what drives pricing. Shorter terms (24-36 months) are common for newer businesses; longer terms may be available as you build history. Leases sometimes offer lower monthly payments than loans for startups, since you're not building equity—weigh total cost vs. cash flow when choosing. Factor in any origination fees, documentation fees, or insurance requirements when comparing offers.

Frequently Asked Questions About Equipment Financing for New Businesses

How long does my business need to be in operation? Many programs accept 6+ months. Some specialty lenders consider businesses with 3-4 months of revenue when credit and equipment are strong. Traditional programs prefer 12+ months. The longer your track record, the more options and better terms you'll access.

Can I finance multiple pieces of equipment as a new business? Yes. Bundled equipment or a fleet can be financed together. Stronger credit and revenue support larger totals. Start with one piece if your profile is borderline; successful repayment builds history for future requests.

Does equipment financing help my business credit? Yes. Timely payments can report to business credit bureaus. See can equipment financing help build business credit for how it strengthens your profile over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a new business get equipment financing?

Yes. Many equipment lenders accept businesses with 6+ months in operation when credit (often 650+) and revenue are strong. Some programs accept newer businesses; terms may be shorter or require a down payment.

What credit score do I need for startup equipment financing?

Startups typically need 660-680+ personal FICO for the best options. Lower credit may qualify with specialty lenders, larger down payments, or equipment with strong resale value. See equipment financing with bad credit for alternatives.

Is equipment financing easier than SBA for new businesses?

Often yes. Equipment financing is asset-backed and typically has lighter documentation and more flexible time-in-business requirements than SBA loans, which usually require 2+ years.
