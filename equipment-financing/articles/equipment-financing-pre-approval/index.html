What Is Equipment Financing Pre-Approval?

Pre-approval is a conditional commitment from a lender: based on your application, they indicate you qualify for a certain amount and rate (or rate range). It is not a guarantee of funding until you have a specific equipment purchase and the lender completes final underwriting. Pre-approval tells you what you can afford and gives you a baseline to compare other offers. It differs from prequalification, which is a softer, preliminary check; pre-approval typically involves a credit pull and more verified information, resulting in a firmer indication of terms.

Why Get Pre-Approved Before Shopping?

  • Know your budget: You shop with a clear ceiling. No guesswork about what you can finance.
  • Compare dealer financing: Dealers often have preferred lenders. Your pre-approval gives you a rate to beat. If dealer financing is better, use it; if not, use your pre-approved lender.
  • Negotiate from strength: Saying “I already have financing” signals you are a serious buyer and may improve dealer pricing on the equipment itself.
  • Speed up the process: When you find the right equipment, your financing is already in progress. You can close faster.

How Long Does Pre-Approval Take?

Equipment financing pre-approval often takes 24–48 hours, sometimes same day. Equipment lenders move quickly because the asset secures the loan; underwriting is more streamlined than for unsecured loans. Marketplaces that submit to multiple lenders can return preliminary approvals within hours when your documentation is complete. Does Pre-Approval Affect My Credit?

It depends. Some lenders use a soft inquiry for pre-approval, which does not affect your score. Others use a hard pull, which can lower your score by a few points. Ask the lender or marketplace whether they use soft or hard inquiries before you apply. If you are rate-shopping, multiple inquiries for the same type of financing within a short window (e.g., 14–45 days) are often counted as one for scoring purposes. Still, limiting hard pulls is wise. For a general overview of prequalification and credit impact across loan types, see how to prequalify for a business loan.

How Long Is Pre-Approval Valid?

Pre-approvals typically last 30–90 days. Lenders want reasonably current information; after that window, they may need to refresh your file (e.g., new bank statements, updated credit pull). If your pre-approval expires before you purchase, you can usually re-apply or request an extension. Check the terms of your specific pre-approval.

What You Need for Pre-Approval

Requirements vary by lender. Typical items include:

  • Business basics (name, entity type, industry, time in business)
  • Revenue (monthly or annual, approximate)
  • Equipment type and approximate amount (e.g., “excavator, $150,000” or “commercial kitchen, $75,000”)
  • 3–6 months of bank statements
  • Business formation documents

You may not need a specific equipment quote for pre-approval; an estimated amount and type often suffice. For the full document list, see equipment financing requirements.

Using Pre-Approval When Negotiating with Dealers

When you visit a dealer, you have two financing options: theirs or yours. Use your pre-approval as leverage. If the dealer offers a better rate or terms, take it. If not, use your pre-approved lender. You can also negotiate on the equipment price separately; sometimes dealers are more flexible on price when they know you have alternative financing. Do not feel pressured to use dealer financing if your pre-approval is stronger. Compare the total cost (rate, term, fees) of both options before deciding.

Pre-Approval vs. Final Approval

Pre-approval is conditional. Final approval occurs when you have a specific equipment purchase (quote, VIN, or invoice), and the lender completes underwriting. If your situation has not changed and the equipment fits within your pre-approved amount and type, final approval is often quick. If the equipment is different (e.g., different category, much higher price), the lender may need to re-evaluate. Keep your pre-approval current and communicate any changes to your lender.
