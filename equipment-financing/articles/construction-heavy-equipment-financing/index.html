How to Lease Heavy Equipment: Lease Types, Terms and Costs

Quick answer

To lease heavy equipment, you get a dealer quote, choose a lease type and apply with 3-6 months of bank statements; most lessors decide in 24-72 hours and fund in 5-10 business days. What Is a Heavy Equipment Lease and How Does It Work?

A heavy equipment lease is a contract where a leasing company (the lessor) buys the machine and you pay a fixed monthly amount to use it for a set term, usually 2 to 7 years depending on your credit and the machine. You run it, insure it and maintain it as if it were yours. At the end you return it, buy it, or renew, depending on the lease type you signed.

Lessors like heavy iron. Excavators, dozers, wheel loaders and skid steers hold their value, have clear resale pricing and can be repossessed and resold if a borrower defaults. Machines from brands with well-documented resale values (Caterpillar, John Deere, Komatsu, Bobcat, Kubota) are the easiest to place. That is why a contractor with steady deposits and fair credit can usually lease new or used machinery without a long bank process. If you would rather own from day one, see construction equipment financing for the loan side.

What Are the Main Types of Heavy Equipment Leases?

There are two main types of leasing agreements: an operating lease, usually written as a fair market value (FMV) lease, and a finance or capital lease, usually written as a $1 buyout. Construction fleets also see two variations: TRAC leases on titled trucks and rent-to-own deals from rental yards.

Is It Better to Lease or Buy an Excavator?

It depends on how long the machine stays in your yard. Here is the same $150,000 mid-size excavator priced three ways at 8% over 60 months, the rate a 720+ borrower with two years in business can expect:

The FMV lease saves about $408 a month, roughly 13% below the loan, which fits the 10-20% gap most lessors quote. You pay for it at the end: keep the machine and you write a second check for the residual. Contractors who keep machines for 7-10 years usually come out ahead buying or using a $1 buyout. Those who trade up every 3-5 years, or who want the lowest payment while a new contract ramps up, usually lease. The rate moves the payment more than most people expect. The same $150,000 excavator over 60 months is about $3,041 a month at 8% and about $3,337 at 12%. New machines get the longest terms. Used equipment usually takes 10-20% down and a shorter term, and lessors prefer machines under 5-7 years old with reasonable hours. Many leases ask for the first and last payment up front instead of a down payment. A dealer trade-in can cover that.

What Do You Need to Qualify for a Heavy Equipment Lease?

  • Credit: 600+ opens most lessors. 550-599 goes to specialty programs that lean on cash flow and collateral. Below 550, expect 25% or more down or a cosigner. See equipment financing with bad credit.
  • Time in business: 1-2+ years is preferred; many programs start at 6 months, and contracting experience before you formed the company counts.
  • Bank deposits: steady deposits that cover the new payment with room to spare.
  • Documents: formation documents, 3-6 months of business bank statements, the equipment quote and the owner's driver's license. Larger transactions add 1-2 years of business tax returns. The full list is in equipment financing requirements.

How Do You Lease Heavy Equipment, Step by Step?

  1. Get the dealer quote. An itemized quote with make, model, year, hours, serial number and the total including freight and tax. Used machines over 7 years old or with high hours often need an inspection report.
  2. Decide how long you will keep it. If you will run it past the term, price a $1 buyout lease or a loan. If you expect to trade up in 3-5 years, price an FMV lease.
  3. Check your credit. 600+ opens most lessors. 550-599 goes to specialty programs, and below 550 usually takes 25% or more down or a cosigner.
  4. Gather the file. 3-6 months of business bank statements, formation documents and the owner's ID. Larger deals add 1-2 years of business tax returns.
  5. Compare at least two offers. Get the dealer's lessor and a third-party quote, then compare the payment, the end-of-term price and the fees, not just the rate.

With a complete file, most lessors decide in 24-72 hours and fund in 5-10 business days after you sign. A pre-approval before you visit the dealer gives you a number to negotiate against.

What Construction Equipment Can You Lease?

Almost anything with a serial number and a resale market:

  • Excavators: mini (under 6 tons), mid-size (6-20 tons) and large. The most-financed construction asset.
  • Bulldozers, wheel loaders and track loaders for grading, site prep and moving material.
  • Backhoes, skid steers and compact track loaders, the everyday machines for landscapers and small contractors.
  • Dump trucks, which can also go on a TRAC lease.
  • Cranes, compactors, rollers, graders and pavers for commercial and road work.

The work changes the structure. Demolition machines take a beating, so lessors often want shorter terms. Road and quarry fleets run larger, longer-lived machines that support longer terms, and mining equipment may need a lessor with industry experience. General contractors leasing several units often do better putting them with one lessor.

What Should You Check Before Signing a Lease?

  • The end-of-term price in writing. "Fair market value" with no floor or cap can turn into a bigger buyout than you planned.
  • Blanket liens. Some lessors file a UCC lien on all business assets, which can block your next financing. Ask for a lien on the leased machine only.
  • Early payoff terms. If you might pay off when a project wraps, avoid heavy prepayment penalties.
  • Return conditions. On an FMV lease, read the hours and wear limits so a return does not come with a repair bill.
  • Dealer vs third-party. Dealer financing is convenient and sometimes carries promotional rates; an outside lessor may beat it. Get both and compare total cost. If a lessor offers an equipment finance agreement instead, how an EFA compares with a lease explains the difference.

How Are Heavy Equipment Leases Taxed?

An FMV lease payment is generally deducted as a business expense. A $1 buyout lease is usually treated like a purchase, so you depreciate the machine and may be able to take Section 179 expensing or bonus depreciation, the same as with a loan. The rules are specific and change, so confirm with your CPA before you pick a structure for tax reasons. The Section 179 guide walks through the math.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a lease agreement for equipment?

It is a contract where a leasing company buys the equipment and you pay a fixed monthly amount to use it for a set term, usually 2 to 7 years. You insure and maintain it. At the end you return it, renew, or buy it, depending on whether it is an FMV lease or a $1 buyout lease.

What are the two main types of leasing agreements?

An operating lease, usually an FMV lease, has the lowest payment and lets you return the machine or buy it at fair market value. A finance or capital lease, usually a $1 buyout, prices close to a loan and you own the equipment at the end.

Is it better to lease or buy an excavator?

Buy, or use a $1 buyout lease, if you will keep it 7-10 years. Lease on FMV terms if you trade up every 3-5 years or need the lowest payment. On a $150,000 excavator at 8% over 60 months, a loan is about $3,041 a month and an FMV lease with a 20% residual about $2,633.

What credit score do you need to lease heavy equipment?

600+ opens most lessors. 550-599 goes to specialty programs, and below 550 usually takes 25% or more down or a cosigner. Rates run from about 7-9% APR at 720+ with two years in business to 16-22% at 580-639.

Can you lease to own heavy equipment?

Yes. A $1 buyout lease ends with you owning the machine for $1, and a rent-to-own deal credits 50-100% of the rent, often for 6-12 months, toward the purchase price. The remaining buyout is usually financed as a used-equipment loan over 36-60 months.

Can you lease used construction equipment?

Yes. Used excavators, dozers, loaders and skid steers are leased and financed every day. Expect 10-20% down and a shorter term, and lessors prefer machines under 5-7 years old with reasonable hours.

How fast can you get approved for a heavy equipment lease?

Most lessors decide in 24-72 hours when the file is complete: application, 3-6 months of bank statements and the dealer quote. Funding usually follows in 5-10 business days after you sign.
