Equipment Financing: What It Is and When to Use It

Equipment financing includes equipment loans and leases. The equipment itself secures the financing, so lenders can approve borrowers with lower credit scores than unsecured loans require. Many equipment lenders accept 550–600+ FICO because they can repossess and resell the asset if you default. See equipment financing with bad credit for details.

Funding is fast: 24–72 hours after approval for streamlined programs. Applications are simpler than SBA; you typically provide bank statements, equipment quote, and basic business info. Equipment financing funds only the equipment purchase, not working capital or real estate. For a single piece of machinery, a truck, or a technology upgrade, equipment financing is often the fastest path. Terms typically match the useful life of the asset—3–5 years for technology, 5–7 years for heavy machinery. Some programs offer 100% financing with no down payment for qualified borrowers. SBA Loans for Equipment: 7(a) and 504

SBA loans are government-backed business loans. Two programs fund equipment:

  • SBA 7(a): Flexible use. Funds equipment, working capital, inventory, real estate, and business acquisition. Up to $5 million, 10–25 year terms. Variable or fixed rates within SBA caps.
  • SBA 504: Fixed assets only. Funds owner-occupied commercial real estate and long-term equipment (10+ year useful life). 10% down, 40% SBA second lien, 50% bank first. Long-term fixed rates.

SBA loans favor established businesses with 2+ years in business, solid credit (660–680+), and clean documentation. Approval takes 30–60 days or more. The payoff: lower rates, longer terms, and higher leverage than conventional loans. SBA 504 equipment loans require the equipment to have a 10+ year useful life; 7(a) is more flexible for general equipment. Startups and businesses with credit below 660 often find SBA out of reach, which is why equipment financing exists as a faster, credit-flexible alternative. When to Choose Equipment Financing

  • You need funding quickly. A contract deadline, broken machine, or time-sensitive opportunity does not wait 60 days. Equipment financing funds in 24–72 hours after approval; SBA takes 30–60+ days. For urgent needs, equipment financing is the practical choice.
  • Your credit is below 660. Equipment financing is asset-backed; many lenders accept 550–600+ when the equipment has strong resale value. SBA lenders typically require 660–680+.
  • You only need equipment. No working capital, no real estate. Equipment financing is purpose-built and streamlined for equipment-only transactions.
  • You are a newer business. 6–12 months in business may qualify for equipment financing with strong revenue. SBA favors 2+ years.
  • You want minimal paperwork. Bank statements, equipment quote, and basic docs vs SBA's business plan, tax returns, and financial statements.

When to Choose an SBA Loan

  • You have strong credit (660+). SBA loans offer the best rates for qualifying borrowers. If you qualify, the long-term savings can be meaningful.
  • You can wait 30–60+ days. SBA is not for emergencies. Plan ahead; the process takes time.
  • You need equipment plus working capital or real estate. A 7(a) loan can fund equipment and working capital in one transaction. A 504 can combine real estate and equipment.
  • You want the longest terms and lowest rates. 10–25 year terms and SBA-capped rates beat most equipment financing for prime borrowers. The total interest savings over the life of the loan can be substantial when you qualify.
  • You are buying a building. SBA 504 is ideal for owner-occupied commercial real estate with 10% down. Equipment financing does not cover real estate.

Cost Comparison: Equipment Financing vs SBA

Prime borrowers (700+ FICO) typically get better rates with SBA loans. A $100,000 equipment purchase: SBA 7(a) might offer 9–11% APR over 10 years; equipment financing might be 10–14% over 5–7 years. SBA wins on rate and term length when you qualify.

For credit-challenged borrowers (600–650 FICO), equipment financing may be the only realistic option. SBA lenders often decline below 660. Equipment financing at 18–24% APR over 5 years is expensive, but it gets you the equipment. Refinancing into SBA or lower-rate equipment financing later is possible when your credit improves. Real-World Scenarios

Contractor needs an excavator in 2 weeks. A construction company wins a project and needs a $120,000 excavator. SBA would take 60+ days. Equipment financing approves in 48 hours and funds within a week. The contractor gets the machine, starts the job, and pays a higher rate but captures the contract. Equipment financing is the right choice.

Manufacturer with 700 FICO plans equipment upgrade. A 5-year-old manufacturing business needs $200,000 in CNC equipment. They have 6 months before the current machines need replacement. They apply for SBA 7(a), get approved at 10% APR over 10 years, and lock in low monthly payments. SBA is the right choice.

Startup restaurant needs kitchen equipment. A restaurant open 8 months needs $50,000 in refrigeration and cooking equipment. SBA favors 2+ years in business. Equipment financing approves with 15% down and 48-month terms. Equipment financing is the right choice. They can refinance with SBA or a lower-rate lender once established.

Documentation: What You Will Need

Equipment financing: Typically 3–6 months of bank statements, equipment quote or invoice, business formation documents, and sometimes tax returns. Applications are streamlined; some lenders approve with minimal paperwork for qualified equipment and revenue profiles.

SBA loans: Business plan, 2–3 years of tax returns (personal and business), profit and loss statements, balance sheet, debt schedule, use of funds narrative, and personal financial statements. SBA requires more documentation because the process is more thorough and the government backs a portion of the loan. Plan for several weeks of gathering and organizing documents. If you are unsure which path fits your profile, applying through a single channel that evaluates both SBA and equipment financing can surface your best options without multiple applications.

Equipment Loan vs Lease: Does It Matter for SBA vs Equipment Financing?

Equipment financing includes both loans and leases. SBA 7(a) and 504 typically fund equipment purchases (loans), not leases. If you prefer a lease structure—lower monthly payments, flexibility to upgrade at term end, or operating lease tax treatment—equipment financing is the path. SBA funds ownership; equipment financing funds both ownership and lease structures. See equipment loan vs lease for the full comparison.

Can You Use Both?

Yes. Businesses often combine SBA and equipment financing:

  • SBA 504 for real estate + equipment: One 504 project can finance an owner-occupied building and long-term equipment. 10% down, fixed rates.
  • SBA 7(a) for working capital, equipment financing for machinery: Use 7(a) for payroll and inventory; use equipment financing for a forklift or CNC machine. Each serves a different need.
  • Equipment financing now, SBA later: Get equipment quickly with equipment financing. When your business matures and credit strengthens, refinance with SBA or conventional equipment loan for better terms.

Quick Decision Framework

  • Need funding in under 2 weeks? → Equipment financing.
  • Credit below 660? → Equipment financing (SBA typically requires 660+).
  • Credit 700+ and can wait 60 days? → SBA likely offers better rates and terms.
  • Need equipment plus working capital or real estate? → SBA 7(a) or 504 can combine in one deal.
  • Prefer a lease over a loan? → Equipment financing (SBA funds purchases, not leases).
  • New business (under 2 years)? → Equipment financing is more accessible.

Summary: Equipment Financing vs SBA Loan

Choose equipment financing when you need speed, have credit below 660, or want a simple equipment-only transaction. Choose an SBA loan when you have strong credit, can wait 30–60+ days, and want the lowest rates and longest terms. Both fund equipment; the right choice depends on your timeline, credit, and use of funds. If you are on the fence, apply once and let lenders evaluate both options.
