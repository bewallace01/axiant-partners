Low Doc Equipment Financing: What Application-Only Really Means

How far a one-page application goes, who qualifies, and when a lender will still ask for tax returns

What Low Doc Means on an Equipment Deal

“Low doc” is about paperwork, not credit and not the down payment. The lender decides on less evidence because the machine secures the loan and the deal is small enough for that to be enough. The usual split:

Application-onlyFull documentation
Typical sizeUp to about $250,000Above that, or any size for weaker files
What you sendApplication, quote, 3-6 months of bank statements, IDAdd 2-3 years of tax returns, interim P&L, balance sheet, debt schedule
Typical decision1-3 daysAbout 1-2 weeks, longer for large deals
Who it fits2+ years in business, about 600+ creditNewer, larger or more complex deals

The threshold is a lender policy, not a rule, and it moves with the equipment, your credit and the lender. Some write smaller application-only limits; some go higher for long-established customers. It is not the same as no credit check equipment financing: an application-only lender still pulls credit.

Who Qualifies for Application-Only Approval

The lender is replacing financial statements with a few signals it trusts. Each one you are missing makes a request for more documents likelier:

  • Time in business. About two years is the common line. Startups are usually asked for more, or for a larger down payment.
  • Owner credit. Roughly 600 or higher is typical; recent late payments, liens or a bankruptcy usually end the application-only route.
  • Bank statements. Steady deposits that cover the new payment comfortably, with few overdrafts.
  • The equipment. Machines with active resale markets, from an established dealer, are easiest. Private-party sales and specialized equipment draw more questions.
  • Total exposure. Lenders commonly add the new deal to what you already owe them. A second machine can push you over the threshold even when each deal on its own is small.

What It Costs Compared With a Full-Doc Deal

Less paperwork does not automatically mean a higher rate. For a strong two-year-old business, an application-only deal often prices in line with a full-doc one, because the lender is taking a small, well-secured risk. The rate follows credit, time in business and the machine’s age, across the roughly 6-22% APR range commonly seen on equipment finance agreements.

Where low doc can cost more is in the structure: a shorter term on used equipment, a larger down payment when credit is thin, or documentation fees. Ask for the APR and the total repayment in writing, not just the payment. The FTC has warned financing companies against misrepresenting terms to small businesses.

Worked Example: One Machine Under the Line, One Over

Illustrative numbers, not a quote. A contractor with three years in business and 690 credit buys two machines a year apart:

DealAmountDocuments asked forPayment at 9%, 60 months
First machine$180,000Application, quote, 6 months of statementsAbout $3,737
Second machine$140,000Same, plus 2 years of returns and a P&LAbout $2,906

The second machine is smaller, but with the first still on the books the lender’s exposure is about $320,000, above its application-only line, so it asks for full financials. Having returns and a current P&L ready keeps the second deal to days rather than weeks. Payments are computed at an assumed 9% and do not include taxes or fees.

How to Prepare a Low Doc Application

  1. Get a written quote with the year, make, model, serial number or hours, and price.
  2. Download 3-6 months of business bank statements as PDFs from the bank, not screenshots.
  3. Check your own credit first. Checking your own report doesn’t hurt it, and it shows whether a recent item will need explaining.
  4. Know what you already owe on other equipment, so you can tell whether a deal will cross a lender’s line.
  5. Keep last year’s return ready anyway. If the lender asks, sending it the same day keeps the decision fast.

Then get matched with equipment lenders. If the down payment is the bigger concern, read 100% equipment financing; for the whole picture, see equipment financing. Equipment you finance may also qualify for Section 179 under IRS Publication 946; ask your accountant.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is low doc equipment financing?

Equipment financing approved on a short application, an equipment quote and recent bank statements, without tax returns or financial statements. It is commonly offered up to about $250,000 to businesses with about two years of history and 600+ credit. These are typical ranges, not a quote.

Does low doc mean no credit check?

No. Application-only lenders still review the owner's credit. Low doc only means fewer financial documents.

Is low doc equipment financing more expensive?

Not necessarily. For established businesses with good credit it often prices like a full-doc deal. Costs rise with weaker credit, older equipment or a shorter time in business.

Can a startup get application-only equipment financing?

It is harder. With under two years of history, lenders usually ask for more documents, a larger down payment or stronger personal credit.

Why did the lender ask for tax returns on a small deal?

Usually because your total exposure with that lender crossed its application-only limit, or because of something in the credit report, the bank statements or the equipment itself.
