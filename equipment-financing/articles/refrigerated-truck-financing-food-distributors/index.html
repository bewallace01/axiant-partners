Refrigerated trucking is one of the highest-growth segments of commercial transportation, driven by e-commerce grocery, restaurant delivery, and pharmaceutical cold chain. Reefer trucks are also among the most expensive commercial vehicles per ton of cargo capacity because the refrigeration unit adds $15K–$30K on top of the chassis cost. This guide covers what reefer truck financing looks like by class, who the major lenders are, and the maintenance-history piece that matters more in reefer than in dry-van financing. The Reefer Unit: Carrier vs Thermo King

Two manufacturers dominate the U.S. reefer unit market:

  • Carrier Transicold (Carrier Global): Vector and Supra series. Common spec on Class-8 reefer freight. Service network nationwide.
  • Thermo King (Trane Technologies): T-Series and Precedent. Equally common; slight preference among certain fleets.

Both run $15K–$30K on a new truck and $25K–$40K for a replacement reefer retrofit. Service costs are the bigger variable — reefer units run 2–3x the hourly maintenance of the tractor itself. Lenders ask for reefer unit maintenance records on used truck purchases.

Approval Requirements

  • FICO: 600+ minimum, 680+ for best rates
  • Time in business: 2+ years standard for owner-operators; established food distributors qualify on revenue history
  • Maintenance records on used trucks: reefer service history matters more than chassis odometer
  • Insurance: commercial auto + cargo + spoilage coverage for reefer loads
  • Customer contracts: for food distributors, lenders want to see grocery, restaurant, or wholesale customer mix
  • Financials: 2–3 yr business tax returns, 3–6 mo bank statements, YTD P&L

Loan vs Lease for Reefer Trucks

Reefer trucks see 5–7 year refresh cycles due to wear-and-tear and emissions regulation changes. Operating lease often makes sense:

  • Operating lease: 36–60 month terms, lower monthly than equivalent loan, easy refresh at term end. Best when you don't intend to run the truck its full 8–10 year useful life.
  • Equipment loan: 48–72 months, ownership at end, Section 179 + bonus depreciation in year one. Best when you intend to run the truck 8–10 years and recover residual value at trade-in.

Tax piece: equipment loans + Section 179 typically net better after-tax than operating leases for profitable trucking operations. Talk to your CPA before signing.

A worked example: a distributor reefer fleet

Food distributors often finance reefers as a fleet, not a single truck. Take a distributor adding three refrigerated straight trucks at $80,000 each — $240,000 — with 10% down, leaving $216,000 over 60 months. At about 9% APR the payment is near $4,484 a month, supported by the contracted delivery routes that justify the trucks. Lenders weigh the refrigeration units as part of the collateral, and a distributor with signed grocery or foodservice contracts underwrites far better than a speculative buildout, often qualifying for one facility across the whole fleet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can food distributors finance refrigerated trucks?

Yes. Reefer straight trucks and tractor-trailers finance through equipment and vocational-vehicle lenders, often as a fleet under one facility, typically over 48–72 months.

Does the refrigeration unit affect reefer financing?

Yes. Lenders treat the reefer unit as part of the collateral, so name-brand units such as Carrier and Thermo King with service records hold value and finance better than unknown or neglected ones.

Can I finance multiple reefer trucks at once?

Yes. A growing distributor can often finance several units under a single facility or master lease, with pricing that improves as fleet volume and operating history build.

What do lenders look at for distributor reefer trucks?

Contracted delivery routes and revenue, the reefer brand and condition, new-versus-used and mileage, your credit, and time in business. Signed grocery or foodservice contracts are the strongest signal.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to finance a refrigerated truck?

Refrigerated truck costs vary significantly by class: refrigerated box trucks (Class 5–7) run $60K–$150K, Class-8 reefer day cabs run $150K–$250K, Class-8 sleeper reefers $200K–$300K, and reefer trailers $60K–$120K standalone. Financing at 8–14% APR over 48–72 month terms means monthly payments of $1,400–$5,500 per truck.

What credit score is needed for reefer truck financing?

600+ FICO qualifies established food distributors and reefer operators. 680+ gets best rates. New owner-operators or sub-650 FICO operators typically need 20–25% down. Reefer financing tends to scrutinize fuel and maintenance history on prior trucks more than other categories — reefer units (Carrier Transicold, Thermo King) are expensive maintenance items.

Can I finance a used refrigerated truck?

Yes — used reefer trucks up to 8–10 years old finance at the same rate as new with a 12-month dealer warranty. Direct private-party purchases (auction, Craigslist) typically require 20–25% down. The reefer unit's service history matters as much as the truck's odometer — lenders ask about it specifically because reefer unit replacement runs $25K–$40K.

Should I finance a refrigerated truck or rent reefer capacity?

Finance if you have consistent volume. Reefer trucks at 70%+ utilization make economic sense to own. For variable or seasonal demand below 50% utilization, renting from a fleet (Penske, Ryder) is usually cheaper than owning. Most established food distributors own their core fleet plus rent peak-season capacity from a 3PL.

Does the reefer unit need separate financing?

Usually no — on a new truck purchase, the reefer unit (Carrier Transicold, Thermo King) is spec'd and installed by the truck dealer and financed as part of the single truck price. For a reefer retrofit on a dry van conversion, the reefer unit alone ($15K–$30K) is financed separately as equipment.
