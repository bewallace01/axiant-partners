Can You Get Equipment Financing With Zero Down?

Yes. Many equipment financing programs offer 100% financing for qualified businesses. Equipment loans and leases are asset-backed: the equipment itself secures the financing. If you default, the lender repossesses and resells it. That collateral reduces lender risk and allows zero-down programs for borrowers who meet credit and revenue thresholds. Zero-down financing is more common for equipment than for real estate or unsecured loans—and far more achievable than many business owners assume. See down payment requirements for the full range of options.

Why do lenders offer zero down? Because equipment holds value. A semi truck, excavator, or medical imaging machine can be repossessed and resold. Lenders rely on that collateral plus your credit and cash flow. When all three align, zero down is a standard option, not an exception. The key is meeting the qualification bar for your lender and equipment type.

Who Qualifies for No-Money-Down Equipment Financing?

Lenders typically look for:

  • Credit score: 650–700+ FICO. Stronger credit (720+) has the best odds. Below 650, expect a down payment for most programs.
  • Time in business: 1–2+ years preferred. Some lenders accept 6+ months with strong credit and revenue.
  • Revenue: Consistent monthly revenue ($15,000–$25,000+ often helps). Bank statements show ability to repay.
  • Equipment type: Assets with strong resale markets (construction, trucks, manufacturing, medical, restaurant) approve more easily.
  • Clean financials: No major defaults, minimal overdrafts, stable deposit patterns.

If your credit is below 650, a down payment of 10–20% can improve approval. See equipment financing with bad credit for strategies.

Equipment Type: What Qualifies for 100% Financing?

Equipment with established resale markets is easiest to finance with zero down. Lenders can value and liquidate it quickly. Favorable categories include:

  • Construction: Excavators, loaders, skid steers, dump trucks, compactors
  • Transportation: Semi-trucks, box trucks, trailers, refrigerated trucks
  • Manufacturing: CNC machines, forklifts, lathes, industrial equipment
  • Medical: Imaging (MRI, CT), dental, surgical, lab equipment
  • Restaurant: Commercial kitchens, refrigeration, POS systems, dishwashers
  • Landscaping: Commercial mowers, zero-turns, skid steers, trailers

New equipment typically qualifies more easily than used. Highly customized or niche equipment may require a down payment. Browse equipment financing by type for industry-specific options.

Zero-down approval varies by industry because lenders assess resale value and risk differently. Here's what typically applies:

Trucking & transportation: Semi-trucks and trailers have strong resale markets. Trucking-specific lenders often offer zero down for established carriers with 680+ credit and 6+ months of freight settlement statements. First-time owner operators may need 10–15% down. Loan vs Lease: Which Offers Zero Down?

Both equipment loans and leases can offer 100% financing. Leases sometimes have a slight edge for zero-down because the lessor retains ownership and bears residual risk; they may approve no-money-down when the equipment has strong resale value. Equipment loans also offer 100% financing for qualified borrowers. TRAC leases for commercial vehicles often include zero-down options. Compare structures in equipment loan vs lease. Is No-Money-Down Equipment Financing More Expensive?

Financing 100% of the equipment means the lender has more exposure if you default. Rates may be slightly higher than with a 10–20% down payment. For strong-credit borrowers, the difference is often modest—perhaps a few points on the rate. For moderate credit, a down payment can meaningfully improve terms. How to Improve Your Odds of Zero-Down Approval

  • Strengthen your credit: Pay down revolving balances, dispute errors on your report, and avoid new credit applications before applying. A 50-point improvement can shift you into a stronger tier and unlock zero-down programs that were out of reach.
  • Demonstrate revenue stability: Clean bank statements, consistent deposits, no overdrafts. Lenders want to see that you can comfortably cover the payment from operating cash flow. Seasonal businesses should apply when their strongest months are reflected in the statements.
  • Choose strong-resale equipment: Construction and trucking equipment approve more easily. Brand names (Caterpillar, John Deere, Freightliner, Exmark) and standard configurations help. Custom or niche equipment may require a down payment.
  • Apply when revenue is strong: Avoid applying during your slowest month. If you're a landscaper, apply in spring or summer; if you're a contractor, apply when you have active projects and clear cash flow.
  • Consider a small down payment: Even 5% can improve approval and rates if you are borderline. Sometimes the difference between "approved with 5% down" and "declined for zero down" is minimal in dollar terms but meaningful for approval.
  • Apply through a marketplace: One application can reach multiple lenders; some may offer 100% when others do not. Lenders have different risk appetites and program structures. Casting a wider net increases your odds of finding a zero-down offer.

When a Down Payment Makes More Sense

No money down is not always the best choice. A down payment can:

  • Reduce your interest rate and total cost
  • Lower your monthly payment
  • Improve approval odds if your credit is moderate
  • Shorten the payback period

If you have the cash and want to minimize cost, a 10–20% down payment may save thousands over the life of the financing. If preserving capital is the priority and your credit is strong, 100% financing can make sense. See down payment requirements for the full tradeoff.

How to Apply for Zero Down Equipment Financing: Step by Step

Following these steps improves your odds of approval and speeds the process:

  1. Get an equipment quote. Obtain a written quote from your dealer with make, model, and total price. Lenders need this to structure the financing. No quote, no approval.
  2. Check your credit. Pull your report and fix errors. Know your score before applying. If you're below 650, plan for a down payment or take 30–60 days to improve credit.
  3. Gather documents. 3–6 months of bank statements, business formation docs, tax returns if required, and industry-specific documents (e.g., freight settlements for trucking). See equipment financing requirements for the full checklist.
  4. Apply once, reach multiple lenders. Use a marketplace like Axiant Partners so one application goes to several lenders. Different lenders have different zero-down thresholds; some may approve when others require a down payment.
  5. Compare offers. Review rate, term, monthly payment, and total cost. Zero down may come with slightly higher rates; factor that into your decision.

Typical approval timeline: 24–48 hours for equipment loans and leases when documentation is complete. See how fast equipment financing can be approved.

Used Equipment and Zero Down

Zero down is more common for new equipment. Used equipment can qualify, but lenders may require 5–10% down for items 3–5 years old, and 10–15%+ for older units. Resale value is harder to predict for used assets—condition, hours, and market demand vary. Strong credit (700+) and established revenue can sometimes unlock zero down on used equipment, especially when the unit is recent model year and from a brand with strong resale (e.g., late-model skid steer, semi truck, or medical device). If you're set on used and zero down, apply to multiple lenders; some specialize in used equipment and may offer 100% financing when others do not. See financing used equipment for full details.

Common Myths About Zero Down

  • "Zero down means bad terms." Not necessarily. Qualified borrowers get competitive rates. The premium for 100% financing is often modest for strong credit.
  • "I need perfect credit." 650–700+ is typical, not 800+. Many businesses qualify with good (not perfect) credit and solid revenue.
  • "Only big businesses qualify." Small businesses with 1–2+ years in business and stable cash flow qualify regularly. Revenue and credit matter more than company size.
  • "Zero down is a scam." Legitimate lenders offer 100% financing. Red flags: "guaranteed" approval with bad credit, no credit check, or extremely high rates. See red flags in equipment finance agreements.

Documentation for Zero-Down Applications

Lenders want to verify that you can repay. Typical requirements: 3–6 months of bank statements, equipment quote or invoice, business formation documents, and sometimes tax returns. Industry-specific documents (freight settlements for trucking, contracts for construction) strengthen the file. Strong documentation supports approval and can tip the scale toward zero down when you're borderline. See equipment financing requirements for the full checklist.

Red Flags to Avoid

Beware of "guaranteed" zero-down with bad credit or "no credit check" programs. Legitimate 100% financing requires qualification. Extremely high rates, confusing terms, or pressure to sign quickly are red flags. Reputable lenders will explain the structure, rates, and total cost clearly.
