What Are Typical Equipment Financing Rates?

Rate ranges, pricing drivers, and how to secure better terms

← Back to Equipment Financing Articles

Last updated: March 3, 2026

Equipment financing rates are not one-size-fits-all. They depend on credit score, time in business, equipment type, and overall risk profile. Pricing is risk-based and varies by lender program. Understanding what drives rates helps you evaluate offers and improve your terms.

Quick Answer

Learn typical equipment financing interest rates, what affects pricing, and how credit, term length, and down payment impact total cost. Focus on Typical Equipment Financing Rate Ranges, What Determines Your Equipment Financing Rate?, Equipment Loan vs. Lease Rates. This guidance applies to most U.S. lenders and programs.

Typical Equipment Financing Rate Ranges

Equipment financing rates are influenced by the Federal Reserve, prime rate, and lender risk appetite. Rates depend on market conditions and lender programs. Because equipment is collateralized, rates are generally more competitive than unsecured business loans. General guidelines by credit tier:

  • Strong credit (700+): Lower competitive rates
  • Good credit (650–699): Moderate rates
  • Fair credit (550–649): Higher rates
  • Below 550: Limited programs with elevated pricing

Rates may be structured as fixed interest rate, factor rate (common in certain programs), or lease rate factor. Asset-backed equipment financing is typically more competitive than unsecured working capital. For lower credit tiers, see equipment financing with bad credit for rate expectations and strategies.

What Determines Your Equipment Financing Rate?

Credit Score

Credit is the strongest driver of rate. Higher scores lead to lower interest rates, longer repayment terms, and lower down payment requirements. See credit score requirements for equipment financing to understand your tier.

Time in Business

Established businesses (2+ years) typically qualify for stronger programs. Newer businesses may see higher rates, shorter terms, and additional documentation requirements.

Equipment Type & Liquidity

Equipment with strong resale markets receives more favorable terms. Examples include construction equipment, commercial trucks, agricultural machinery, and manufacturing equipment. Specialized or niche equipment may carry slightly higher pricing. For pre-owned assets, see can you finance used equipment.

Loan Term Length

Longer terms often increase total interest cost but reduce monthly payments. Shorter terms result in lower total interest paid and higher monthly payments. Typical financing ranges from 24 to 84 months.

Down Payment Contribution

A higher down payment may reduce lender risk and improve rate terms. For details on how much may be required, see down payment requirements for equipment financing.

Equipment Loan vs. Lease Rates

Rates vary depending on structure: equipment loan, $1 buyout lease, or fair market value (FMV) lease. Lease structures sometimes present pricing differently using rate factors instead of traditional APR, which can make direct comparison trickier. When comparing, ask for the effective rate or total cost of the transaction so you can evaluate apples to apples. A $1 buyout lease functions similarly to a loan–you'll own the equipment at the end–while an FMV lease may have lower monthly payments but a balloon or purchase option at term end. Compare options in our guide on equipment loan vs lease.

What Is More Important Than the Interest Rate?

Rate matters, but total financing cost depends on interest rate, term length, fees, down payment, and prepayment flexibility. A loan with a slightly higher rate but no origination fee and a longer term might result in lower monthly payments and similar total cost. Also consider monthly cash flow impact, equipment productivity, return on investment, and tax implications. If the equipment will generate revenue that exceeds the financing cost, the "rate" becomes less important than the net benefit. Focus on strategic capital efficiency rather than rate alone–choose the structure that best supports your operations and growth.

Fixed vs. Variable Rates

Fixed rates are most common and provide predictable monthly payments. Businesses can forecast cash flow accurately, avoid rate fluctuation risk, and maintain budget confidence. Most equipment loans and leases use fixed rates tied to the prime rate or a similar benchmark at the time of origination. Variable rates are less common in traditional equipment financing but may appear in certain structures. If you choose a variable rate, understand how and when it can change–some programs cap increases or adjust only at specific intervals.

Fees and Total Cost of Financing

Beyond the stated rate, consider origination fees, documentation fees, and any prepayment penalties. A lower rate with high fees may cost more than a slightly higher rate with minimal fees. Calculate the total cost over the life of the loan or lease–principal, interest, and fees–to compare offers accurately. Some lenders offer no-fee programs for qualified borrowers; others charge 1–3% in origination. Ask for a clear breakdown before committing.

How to Secure Better Equipment Financing Rates

  • Maintain strong personal credit–pay bills on time and keep utilization low
  • Reduce revolving credit utilization below 30% before applying
  • Provide accurate financial documentation to avoid delays or re-underwriting
  • Choose equipment with strong resale demand–construction, trucks, manufacturing
  • Consider a modest down payment contribution to reduce lender risk
  • Shop multiple lenders–rates and structures can vary meaningfully

Preparation and transparency often improve approval speed and pricing. Working with a partner who connects you to multiple lenders can help you compare offers and secure the best terms for your profile.

Final Thoughts

Rates vary based on credit strength, time in business, equipment type, and overall risk profile. Review the full structure of the transaction–term length, flexibility, and alignment with operational goals–rather than focusing solely on interest rate.
