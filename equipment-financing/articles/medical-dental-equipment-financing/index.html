How to Lease or Finance Medical and Dental Equipment for Your Practice

FMV and $1 buyout leases, loans and vendor programs for imaging, dental chairs, lab and exam-room equipment, with rates and approval times

Quick answer

To lease medical equipment, you choose an FMV lease (lowest payment; return or upgrade at the end) or a $1 buyout lease (you own it for $1), send the vendor quote and 3-6 months of bank statements, and most lessors decide in 24-72 hours. Established practices commonly qualify at 620-650+ credit with 0-10% down; new practices usually put 10-20% down with 670+ personal credit. Rates run about 7-9% APR at 720+ to 16-22% at 580-639. What Is a Medical Equipment Lease and How Does It Work?

A medical equipment lease is a contract where a lessor buys the equipment from the vendor and your practice pays a fixed monthly amount to use it for a set term. You insure and maintain it. At the end, what happens depends on the lease you signed: return or upgrade it, renew, or buy it.

Healthcare equipment leases easily. Used dental and medical equipment sells routinely, so lessors can value it, and practices bring steady patient revenue. Equipment from established brands (Sirona, Planmeca, GE, Siemens, Philips) with FDA clearance and a documented resale value is the easiest to place. The four ways to pay for it:

Should a Practice Lease or Buy Medical Equipment?

Lease what dates quickly and buy what lasts. Imaging, lasers, practice software and IT change fast, and an FMV lease lets you hand back an outdated scanner and move to the current model. Dental chairs, sterilizers, autoclaves and exam tables do the same job for a decade, so a $1 buyout lease or a loan that ends in ownership usually costs less.

Here is a $120,000 imaging system over 60 months. Lease payments typically run 10-20% below a loan on the same equipment:

Lessors price on the owner's credit and the practice's time in business first, then on the equipment. Typical ranges, not quotes:

Many healthcare lessors approve established practices at 620-650+ credit, often with 0-10% down, and 0%-down or 100% financing is available to established practices with strong credit. Below 550, expect 25% or more down or a cosigner. Larger systems such as MRI and CT can carry longer terms because they cost more and last longer; software-only purchases get shorter ones.

What Do Lessors Look At for a Practice?

  • Collections: consistent deposits that cover the new payment. If insurance reimbursement lags, show your AR aging and how you collect.
  • Time in business: 1-2+ years gets the best terms; startups qualify on a strong owner profile.
  • Owner credit: personal FICO carries most small-practice deals.
  • The equipment: standard systems with clear resale value lease more easily than custom builds.

New practices can lease too, but expect 10-20% down, 670+ personal credit and a personal guarantee. Some lessors specialize in practice startups and offer deferred or step payments while patient volume ramps. Apply early and bring a business plan. What Medical and Dental Equipment Can You Lease?

  • Dental: chairs, intraoral, panoramic and CBCT X-ray, sterilization, handpieces, curing lights, lab equipment, digital scanners and software. See dental equipment financing.
  • Imaging: X-ray, ultrasound, MRI, CT, mammography, fluoroscopy and PACS. See medical imaging financing.
  • Medical and surgical: exam tables, surgical lights, anesthesia, monitors, sterilizers and endoscopy.
  • Lab and diagnostics: analyzers, centrifuges, microscopes, refrigerators and point-of-care testing.
  • Veterinary: X-ray, ultrasound, dental, surgical and lab equipment.

Practice type changes the deal. Dental offices finance chairs and digital systems constantly; imaging centers and surgery centers need larger amounts and longer terms; multi-location practices often finance per location, so name the install address and match the entity and insurance to it.

Should You Use Vendor Financing or an Independent Lessor?

Dental supply companies and imaging manufacturers offer financing through captive or partner lenders. It is convenient, often approved at the point of sale, and sometimes carries promotional or 0% rates for strong buyers. Independent lessors are usually more flexible on structure (FMV, $1 buyout, deferred and seasonal payments) and more willing to finance used and refurbished systems. Get one quote from each. Before you sign, check for a lien on all practice assets instead of just the equipment, heavy prepayment penalties, and fees that are not in the rate.

If the equipment is part of opening a location or buying a practice, an SBA 7(a) loan can bundle it with build-out and working capital at a longer term, but approval typically takes 30-60+ days. See equipment financing vs SBA loan.

How Are Medical Equipment Leases Taxed?

FMV lease payments are generally deducted as an operating expense. A $1 buyout lease or a loan is treated as a purchase, so the practice may be able to expense the equipment under Section 179 or take bonus depreciation in year one, which often decides whether a purchase happens this year or next. The rules change; confirm with your CPA. See the Section 179 guide.

How Do You Lease Medical Equipment, Step by Step?

  1. Get the vendor quote. Include model, serial numbers, delivery, installation and training, and the warranty and service terms.
  2. Pick the structure. FMV lease for equipment you will upgrade, $1 buyout lease or loan for equipment you will keep.
  3. Gather the practice file. Formation documents, 3-6 months of business bank statements and the owner's ID. Imaging and other deals over $100,000 often add financial statements and proof of licensing.
  4. Compare vendor and independent offers. Get the vendor's program and at least one independent lessor, and compare the payment, end-of-term price and fees.
  5. Sign and schedule the install. Name the lessor as loss payee on your insurance and line up delivery, so funding conditions are met on time.

With a complete file, most lessors decide in 24-72 hours and fund in 5-10 business days after signing. Underwriters often ask about service contracts, calibration and downtime, so a maintenance plan and the manufacturer's service contacts in the file speed things up. For used equipment, include inspection results and remaining useful life.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a lease agreement for medical equipment?

It is a contract where a lessor buys the equipment and your practice pays a fixed monthly amount to use it for a set term. You insure and maintain it. At the end you return or upgrade it, renew, or buy it, depending on whether it is an FMV lease or a $1 buyout lease.

Who owns the equipment in a finance lease?

The lessor holds title during the term, but a finance lease, usually a $1 buyout, is built so the practice owns the equipment at the end. For taxes it is generally treated as a purchase, so the practice depreciates it or takes Section 179. In an FMV lease the lessor owns it outright.

Is it better to lease or buy medical equipment?

Lease equipment that dates quickly, such as imaging, lasers and software, so you can upgrade at the end. Buy, or use a $1 buyout lease, for chairs, sterilizers and exam tables you will keep for a decade. Lease payments typically run 10-20% below a loan on the same equipment.

What credit score is needed to lease dental or medical equipment?

Many healthcare lessors approve established practices at 620-650+. Rates run about 7-9% APR at 720+ with two years in business and 16-22% at 580-639. New practices usually need 670+ personal credit and 10-20% down.

Can a new practice lease equipment?

Yes. Expect 10-20% down, stronger personal credit and a personal guarantee. Some lessors specialize in startups and offer deferred or step payments while patient volume builds.

How long does medical equipment lease approval take?

Most lessors decide in 24-72 hours when the file is complete, and vendor programs sometimes pre-approve the same day. Funding usually follows in 5-10 business days after signing.

Is medical equipment financing better than a bank loan?

It depends. Equipment financing is secured by the equipment and usually approves faster with less paperwork. A bank may price lower for an established practice with strong credit. Get both quotes.
