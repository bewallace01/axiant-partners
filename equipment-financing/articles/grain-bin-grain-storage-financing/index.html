What Is a Farm Storage Facility Loan?

It is a USDA loan program, run by the Farm Service Agency, that finances on-farm storage and handling for the crops you grow: grain bins, dryers and the equipment that moves grain between them. You apply at your county FSA office. FSA sets and publishes the program's interest rates, loan limits, down payment and terms, and they change, so get the current sheet from the county office before you compare it with anything else.

It is not the only way to pay for storage. Most bins and dryers are financed by private ag lenders and lessors, and FSA's regular operating loans can buy farm equipment too. The right choice comes down to timing, paperwork and how the payment lines up with your grain sales. For the rest of the farm, see agricultural loans.

Does On-Farm Grain Storage Pay for Itself?

Often, yes, and that payback is the case a lender wants to see. Selling at harvest usually means selling into the seasonal price low and the widest basis. With bins and a dryer, you can harvest at the right moisture, condition the crop and sell when basis narrows or the market carries. Storage also speeds harvest, since there is no line at the elevator, and cuts the drying fees and shrink you pay to third parties. When you apply, put numbers on that per-bushel improvement and the elevator costs you avoid.

Can You Get Seasonal Payments on a Grain Bin Loan?

Yes. Ag lenders commonly set annual or semi-annual payments timed to grain sales instead of flat monthly bills. Take a $120,000 system (a bin, a dryer and an auger) with 15% down, leaving $102,000 over seven years at 8%:

What Do Lenders Look At on a Grain Storage Loan?

  • Operation size and acreage: bushel capacity should fit production; lenders weigh storage against acreage.
  • Seasonal cash flow: the crop calendar and marketing plan behind an annual payment.
  • Site work: the full quote including concrete, electrical and erection.
  • Credit and history: standard equipment financing requirements; established operations get the best terms.

How Do You Apply for Grain Storage Financing?

  1. Size storage to your production. Match bushel capacity and drying to your acreage and crop so the system fits the operation.
  2. Get the full project quote. Include the bin or dryer plus concrete, electrical and erection so the lender sees the total cost.
  3. Build the marketing case. Show the basis and carry you expect to capture and the elevator fees you will avoid.
  4. Choose the route and payment schedule. Price the FSA storage loan against a private loan or lease, with annual or semi-annual payments timed to grain sales.
  5. Apply with the plan and financials. Send the project quote, marketing plan and farm financials. Start before harvest so the system is up in time.

Private equipment lenders usually decide in 24-72 hours and fund in 5-10 business days. Get matched with ag equipment lenders for bins, dryers and handling, or read the Section 179 guide before you time the purchase.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a farm storage facility loan?

It is a USDA Farm Service Agency program that finances on-farm storage and handling, such as grain bins and dryers, for crops you produce. You apply through your county FSA office, which publishes the current rates, limits and terms.

Is a farm storage facility loan better than a private equipment loan?

It is worth pricing first if you can plan ahead of harvest. A private ag equipment loan or lease is faster, usually deciding in 24-72 hours, and fits emergencies, bigger projects or bins bundled with other equipment. Compare the payment schedule and total cost of both.

How much does a grain bin cost?

Illustrative ranges: small farm bins of about 5,000-20,000 bushels run $15,000-$50,000, large farm bins of 30,000-100,000 bushels $50,000-$150,000, grain dryers $40,000-$300,000 and handling systems $30,000-$200,000. Add concrete, electrical and erection. These are estimates, not quotes.

Can I get seasonal payments on grain storage financing?

Yes. Ag lenders commonly set annual or semi-annual payments timed to grain sales. On $102,000 at 8% over seven years, that is about $19,591 a year, or $9,656 twice a year.

Does on-farm grain storage pay for itself?

Often, yes. Storage lets you avoid selling into the harvest price low, capture basis improvement and carry, speed harvest, and cut third-party drying and shrink fees. That per-bushel gain is what usually covers the payment.

Can I finance used grain bins and dryers?

Yes. Used bins and dryers finance well. Lenders weigh the dryer's condition and the bin floor, aeration and structure, and sound site work keeps terms competitive.

Does Section 179 apply to grain storage equipment?

Qualifying grain bins, dryers and handling equipment used in a farming business generally qualify for Section 179 expensing and bonus depreciation. Confirm the specifics with your CPA, including any structures treated as real property.
