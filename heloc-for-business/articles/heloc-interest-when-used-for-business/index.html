Is HELOC Interest Tax Deductible?

People want a yes or a no, and the honest answer is that the question needs one more fact: what did the money pay for? Interest on borrowing is generally sorted by the use of the money, not simply by what secured it. The same HELOC can be treated one way for a draw that bought inventory and another way for a draw that paid for a vacation.

That is why home mortgage interest rules and business interest rules are best thought of as two separate sets of rules. Which one your interest falls under, and whether it is deductible and against what, can turn on tracing the money to its use, on your entity type, and on how the funds moved into the company. An article cannot give you your answer. It can make the conversation with your accountant a short one.

Both IRS sources are federal and current: IRS Publication 936 for home-secured interest and IRS business expense guidance for interest on money used in a trade or business. Neither replaces advice on your own facts, and state treatment can differ from federal.

How Does Interest Tracing Work?

Whatever the treatment turns out to be, supporting it rests on showing where the money went. That is the part you control, and the part most people neglect.

Say you draw $50,000 at 9%, about $4,500 of interest a year. If $30,000 went to the business and $20,000 to personal spending, the interest splits the same way: about $2,700 tied to the business use and $1,800 to the personal use. If all $50,000 landed in your personal checking account first and was spent from there, proving that split becomes much harder.

What keeps tracing simple:

  • Draw for one purpose at a time rather than taking a lump and spending it across categories.
  • Move business draws straight into the business account, not through a personal account.
  • Keep the paperwork for what the money bought, matched to the draw.
  • Don't mix a business draw with personal spending in the same account and period.
  • Tell your accountant when it happens, not at year-end.

What Will Your Accountant Want to See?

  • The loan documents, showing what secures the line and when it was opened.
  • A schedule of draws with dates and amounts.
  • What each draw funded, with invoices.
  • The account trail from draw to expense.
  • Your entity structure, since treatment can depend on how the business is organized.

Without those, an accountant is guessing, and a guess is not a position you want to defend.

How Much Does a $50,000 HELOC Cost per Month?

HELOC rates usually run about 8-12%, against about 11-15% for a bridge loan (see bridge loan vs HELOC). On $50,000 drawn, the interest alone is about $333 a month at 8% and $500 at 12%, or $4,000-$6,000 a year. Your payment can be higher if the line also requires principal. That interest figure is the number your accountant will sort between business and personal use.

What Are the Downsides of Using Home Equity for a Business?

The interest treatment is a second-order question next to the fact that the borrowing is secured by your home. A favorable treatment does not make an unwise loan wise, and the difference it makes is small next to what is at stake if the business does not work. See what happens to a HELOC if the business fails and personal guarantee and home equity risk.

If you would rather keep the house out of it, a business line of credit works the same way for draws and keeps the trail inside the business by default; HELOC vs business line of credit compares the two. The HELOC for business guide covers how much you can borrow against your home. If you want to see both options side by side, compare business funding routes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is HELOC interest tax deductible if I use it for my business?

It can be, but it is not automatic. The treatment turns on what the money was used for, whether you can trace it from the draw to the business expense, your entity type and how the funds moved into the company. This is general information, not tax advice; confirm your position with your CPA.

Does it matter what I spent the HELOC money on?

Yes, use is central. Interest on borrowing is generally sorted by what the borrowed money funded rather than by what secured it, which is why a single line drawn for several purposes can have its interest split between categories.

Is home equity loan interest treated the same way?

The same questions come up, because a home equity loan is also borrowing secured by your home: what did the money pay for, and can you show it? IRS Publication 936 covers interest secured by a residence. Your CPA can tell you how it applies to your loan.

What is interest tracing?

Following borrowed money from the draw to what it actually paid for. It is what supports any treatment you claim, and it is far easier when a draw goes straight into the business account for one purpose than when it lands in a personal account and is spent from there.

How much does a $50,000 HELOC cost per month?

HELOC rates usually run about 8-12%. On $50,000 drawn, interest alone is about $333 a month at 8% and $500 at 12%. Your actual payment depends on the line's terms and whether you are also paying down principal.

What are the downsides of using home equity for a business?

Your house secures the borrowing, so if the business fails you still owe the line and your home still backs it. Any tax benefit is small next to that risk. Decide whether to pledge the house on the merits of the borrowing, then ask your CPA how to treat the interest.
