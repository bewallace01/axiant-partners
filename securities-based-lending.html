Securities-Based Lending for All Businesses

Portfolio-Backed Capital from $10,000 to $10,000,000+

Securities-based lending provides access to capital by using eligible investment assets as collateral. It can deliver liquidity without requiring liquidation of long-term portfolio positions.

What Is Securities-Based Lending?

Securities-based lending (SBL) allows qualified borrowers to secure financing against eligible marketable securities, such as stocks, bonds, and diversified funds.

  • Uses eligible investment holdings as collateral
  • Can provide faster liquidity than many traditional credit products
  • Helps maintain portfolio exposure while unlocking working capital
  • Often used for business liquidity, acquisitions, and bridge needs

How Securities-Based Lending Works

Typical process:

  • Review of portfolio composition, concentration, and asset quality
  • Advance-rate calculation based on eligible collateral classes
  • Approval of facility size, structure, and draw mechanics
  • Ongoing collateral monitoring and margin maintenance

Borrowers receive financing capacity while the lender manages collateral coverage requirements throughout the facility term.

Typical Amounts & Facility Structure

SBL structures commonly start around $10,000 and can scale to $10,000,000+ based on collateral strength and borrower profile.

  • Advance rates vary by asset type and concentration
  • Revolving and term structures may both be available
  • Rate, covenants, and monitoring standards vary by lender

Common Use Cases

  • Business working capital and seasonal liquidity support
  • Acquisition deposits and short-duration bridge needs
  • Tax-related liquidity events
  • Strategic capital while avoiding portfolio liquidation timing risk

Securities-Based Lending vs Business Term Loan

Feature Securities-Based Lending Business Term Loan
Primary Collateral Investment portfolio Business assets and cash flow
Funding Speed Often faster Typically slower
Portfolio Exposure Maintained Not applicable

Securities-Based Lending vs Line of Credit

Feature Securities-Based Lending Business Line of Credit
Collateral Basis Eligible securities portfolio Business receivables/cash-flow profile
Availability Changes Based on market value and eligibility Based on lender credit policy and usage
Best For Portfolio-backed liquidity strategy Recurring operating liquidity needs

What Do Lenders Evaluate?

  • Portfolio composition and diversification
  • Asset concentration risk
  • Liquidity and marketability of pledged holdings
  • Borrower credit and repayment profile
  • Requested facility purpose and structure

Benefits of Securities-Based Lending

  • Access capital without immediate asset liquidation
  • Can preserve long-term investment strategy
  • Useful for time-sensitive liquidity opportunities
  • Potentially competitive pricing for strong collateral profiles

Risks & Considerations

  • Market volatility can affect borrowing capacity
  • Margin calls may require additional collateral or paydown
  • Concentration limits may reduce advance rates
  • Not all securities are eligible collateral

When This Structure Makes Sense

For a detailed breakdown of strategic use cases, see when you should use securities-based lending.

  • You hold a sizable eligible portfolio and need liquidity
  • You want to avoid selling investments at unfavorable timing
  • You need short-to-medium term capital with flexibility
  • You can manage collateral requirements under market volatility
