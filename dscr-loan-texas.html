Quick Answer: DSCR Loans in Texas

DSCR loans are available across Texas on 1–4 unit rentals, typically at 20–25% down with a minimum ratio of 1.00–1.25. Two things make Texas different from most states, and both work in a borrower’s favour on timing and against them on cash flow: Texas is a non-judicial foreclosure state, which lenders price favourably, and it carries some of the highest effective property taxes in the country, which is the single largest drag on DSCR here.

Property Tax Is the Deciding Variable in Texas

Texas has no state income tax and funds local government through property tax instead. Effective rates commonly land in the 1.5–2.5% range depending on county and municipal overlay — well above the national average near 1%.

On a $350,000 rental that is roughly $5,250 to $8,750 a year, against perhaps $3,500 in a 1% state. That difference of up to $5,250 comes straight out of net operating income, and at a 1.20x target it reduces supportable debt by tens of thousands of dollars.

Two practical consequences. First, never underwrite a Texas deal on the seller’s current tax bill — if they held a homestead exemption, your assessment as a non-owner-occupant will be higher, and lenders underwrite to the reassessed figure. Second, protest season matters: a successful valuation protest improves DSCR permanently.

Non-Judicial Foreclosure and Why Lenders Like Texas

Texas is a non-judicial foreclosure state with one of the fastest timelines in the country — a lender can typically move from default to a first-Tuesday trustee sale in a matter of months rather than the year or more a judicial state requires.

Lenders price recovery time. A short, predictable foreclosure path is part of why Texas rentals often see competitive DSCR terms and why lender appetite here is deep. It is genuinely good news for a borrower shopping rate — and a reminder that the collateral remedy is quick if the property stops performing.

Rent-to-Price Reality in the Major Metros

DSCR is arithmetic, and the arithmetic differs sharply across Texas:

  • Houston — historically the friendliest large Texas metro for rent-to-price ratios, which is why so much DSCR volume concentrates here. Flood zone status drives insurance and must be checked before underwriting.
  • San Antonio — lower entry prices and steady rents make ratios workable at moderate leverage; generally the easiest of the big four to clear 1.20x.
  • Dallas–Fort Worth — strong rent growth, but price appreciation has compressed ratios. Suburban submarkets usually pencil better than core.
  • Austin — the hardest Texas metro for DSCR. Prices ran ahead of rents, and files here frequently need 30%+ down to clear a 1.00x floor.

Holding Texas Property in an LLC

Texas is a straightforward state for entity ownership. There is no state-level real estate transfer tax, which keeps closing costs lower than in many states, and Texas LLCs are inexpensive to form and maintain, with no annual report fee for most filers under the franchise tax threshold.

Nearly all DSCR lenders will close in an LLC here. Have the operating agreement, EIN letter, and certificate of formation ready at application. Note that transferring an existing homestead-exempt property into an LLC will end that exemption — a tax consequence worth modelling before you move title.

Insurance: Wind, Hail and Flood

Texas insurance is not a formality. Hail is a genuine underwriting factor across North Texas, coastal counties carry windstorm exposure often written through the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association, and Houston-area flood zones can add materially to annual cost.

Because insurance sits inside operating expenses, it moves DSCR directly. Get a real quote before you go under contract, not an estimate — a $2,000 surprise in premium can be the difference between a 1.21x approval and a 1.14x decline.

A Texas Deal, Worked

A San Antonio single-family rents for $1,950/mo, so gross annual income is $23,400. Purchase price $265,000 with 25% down leaves a loan of $198,750.

Operating expenses on a realistic Texas basis: property tax at a 2.1% effective rate on the reassessed value is $5,565; insurance with hail exposure $2,400; maintenance $1,600; management at 8% $1,872; vacancy at 5% $1,170. Total $12,607, giving NOI of $10,793.

At 7.25% over 30 years the payment is about $1,356/mo, or $16,272 a year. DSCR = 0.66x. The deal fails, and it fails on tax and insurance rather than on rent.

This is why Texas files need underwriting on real figures. Had the seller held a homestead exemption, their tax bill might have shown $3,200 and the ratio would have looked like 0.79x — still failing, but for a reason the buyer would only discover late. Either the price has to come down, the rent has to be higher, or the leverage has to drop substantially.

Before You Make a Texas Offer

  • Pull the appraisal district record for the parcel and check whether the current owner holds a homestead or over-65 exemption. If so, your tax will be higher.
  • Model tax at the reassessed value, not the current assessed value, since a sale commonly triggers reassessment toward market.
  • Get an insurance quote naming the roof age — hail claims history in North Texas affects both price and availability.
  • Check flood zone in the Houston region specifically; a Zone AE designation adds a required policy.
  • Confirm the lender lends in Texas at your loan size — minimums around $75,000–$100,000 exclude some lower-priced submarkets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you get a DSCR loan in Texas?

Yes. Texas is one of the deepest DSCR markets in the country, with broad lender participation on 1-4 unit rentals at typically 20 to 25% down and a minimum ratio of 1.00 to 1.25. Lender appetite is strong partly because Texas is a non-judicial foreclosure state with a fast recovery timeline.

How do Texas property taxes affect a DSCR loan?

Significantly. Effective rates commonly run 1.5 to 2.5%, well above the national average near 1%. On a $350,000 rental that is roughly $5,250 to $8,750 a year straight out of net operating income. Never underwrite on the seller's tax bill if they held a homestead exemption, because your assessment as a non-owner-occupant will be higher and lenders use the reassessed figure.

Which Texas metro is best for DSCR loans?

Houston and San Antonio generally produce the most workable rent-to-price ratios, with San Antonio often the easiest of the big metros to clear 1.20x. Dallas-Fort Worth works better in suburban submarkets than in the core. Austin is the hardest, where prices ran ahead of rents and files frequently need 30% or more down to clear a 1.00x floor.

Can you hold a Texas rental in an LLC with a DSCR loan?

Yes, and nearly all DSCR lenders will close in an LLC. Texas has no state real estate transfer tax, which keeps closing costs lower than many states, and LLCs are inexpensive to maintain. Note that moving a homestead-exempt property into an LLC ends that exemption, which is worth modelling first.
