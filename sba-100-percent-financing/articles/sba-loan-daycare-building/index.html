Buying a Daycare Building With an SBA Loan

How licensed capacity drives the value of a child care building, what the SBA requires for older buildings, and a worked purchase with renovation

← Back to 100% SBA Financing Articles

Updated November 13, 2026

Quick answer

As of November 2026, an established child care operator can buy its building with an SBA 7(a) loan over up to 25 years, and some lenders go to 100% with renovation included. Licensed capacity drives the numbers: many states require about 35 sq ft indoors and 75 sq ft outdoors per child. Buildings from before 1978 need lead testing. Capacity Is What the Lender Underwrites

A daycare building is worth what it can be licensed for. This guide is about the building. For running or buying the center itself, see the childcare financing guide; for the zero-down structure in general, see 100% SBA financing.

State rules set capacity. Virginia and Minnesota, for example, both require 35 square feet of usable indoor space per child and 75 square feet per child using the outdoor play area at one time. Minnesota excludes hallways, bathrooms, kitchens and closets, and requires a play area of at least 1,500 square feet. Your state's numbers may differ, so check them before you sign.

Lead Testing for Older Buildings

SOP 50 10 8.1 treats a daycare as a child-occupied facility. If the building was built before 1978, the lender must get a lead risk assessment and test drinking water at every tap and fountain children might use. Both must be done within one year of the SBA loan number, and the lender can't disburse until the lead risk is minimized.

Budget for this and any fixes. Replacing fixtures or abating paint can go into the renovation line of the loan, and doing it before closing protects your license inspection too.

Zoning, Permits and the Play Yard

  • Zoning. Many towns allow child care only in some districts or by special use permit. A building that houses a licensed center today is the safest buy; converting an office or church building needs zoning and licensing checked first.
  • Outdoor space. The fenced play yard is used land, so it is financeable. Unused acreage is excess land, which SBA proceeds can't fund.
  • Deed restrictions. Some properties carry environmental deed restrictions against use as a day care center. The title search must clear this.
  • Extra space. If you lease part of the building out, your center must occupy at least 51%. See how the 51% occupancy rule is measured.

What Lenders Want From a Child Care Operator

  • Two or more years operating, with tax returns and current-year statements showing the payment is covered.
  • Your license and inspection history, plus enrollment and waitlist numbers.
  • Subsidy mix. Lenders look at how much tuition comes from state subsidy programs and how reliably they pay.
  • Credit. Many SBA lenders look for roughly 650-680 or higher; some go lower with strong cash flow.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get an SBA loan to buy a daycare building with no money down?

Some SBA 7(a) lenders finance up to 100% for an established operator that occupies the building and can show the cash flow to support the payment. Start-ups need at least 10%.

How many children can a building be licensed for?

It's set by your state. Many states require about 35 square feet of usable indoor space per child and 75 square feet per child outdoors, excluding hallways, bathrooms and kitchens.

Does the SBA require lead testing for a daycare?

Yes, for buildings built before 1978: a lead risk assessment and drinking water tests at all taps children could use, both within a year of the loan number.

Can renovation be included in the loan?

Yes. Improvements to the space your center uses can be financed with the building. Space leased to someone else can't be improved with SBA proceeds.

Is SBA 504 better for a daycare building?

A 504 offers a fixed rate on part of the loan but needs at least 10% down. A 7(a) can reach 100% with some lenders, usually on an adjustable rate.
