Financing a Contractor's Yard or Land Your Business Uses

When land qualifies for SBA financing, why a storage yard counts toward occupancy, and what happens to the acres you don't use

← Back to 100% SBA Financing Articles

Updated November 13, 2026

Quick answer

As of November 2026, an SBA 7(a) loan can finance land your business actively uses, such as a contractor's storage yard with a shop, over up to 25 years, and some lenders go to 100% for established companies. Land bought for the future, to resell or to lease out is excess land and can't be financed with SBA proceeds. Axiant Partners, a broker in Boca Raton, finds lenders for yard purchases. Used Land Versus Excess Land

SOP 50 10 8.1 bars SBA proceeds from property held for future development, sale, lease or investment, and from excess land: land not in immediate use as part of the project. So the test isn't whether it's land, it's whether your business uses it now. This is the land side of 100% SBA financing.

Usually financeableUsually not
Fenced storage yardAcreage held for a future building
Shop, office and wash bayLots you plan to sell or lease
Daily truck parking, stagingRaw land with no business use yet
Land under a new buildingSpeculative homes or spec buildings

Farming has its own rule: farm land beyond the operation's needs is excess, and the SOP specifically bars buying vacant or row-crop land for future use or to lease.

Why the Yard Counts Toward Occupancy

Your business must occupy at least 51% of an existing property's rentable space. The SOP lets exterior space count when it is actively used in the business, and names outdoor storage yards for general contractors, trucking companies and moving and storage companies as examples. Parking areas don't count.

Example: a 4,000 sq ft shop and a 30,000 sq ft storage yard you use, plus a 6,000 sq ft building you lease to a tenant. You occupy 34,000 of 40,000 square feet, or 85%. Counting buildings alone, you'd be at 40% and fail. See the 51% occupancy rule for the full measuring rules.

Environmental Checks on a Yard

Heavy and civil engineering construction (NAICS 237) is on the SBA's environmentally sensitive list, so those buyers start with a Phase I. So do trucking companies with service bays, truck washing or fuel tanks. Any site where fuel is dispensed needs a Phase I. A yard with past fueling, equipment repair or buried tanks gets close review whatever your trade. Other buyers over $250,000 start with a questionnaire and records search, which can lead to a Phase I.

Building on the Land

If you'll build a shop, SBA rules add a period for construction to the 25-year term. Above $350,000 of construction, the lender generally needs a licensed contractor with full performance and payment bonds, unless a construction manager controls the funds. When building new, you must occupy 60% of the rentable space.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use an SBA loan to buy land for my business?

Yes, if your business will use the land now, for example as a storage yard or for a building you'll construct. Land held for future use, resale or lease is not eligible.

What is excess land in an SBA loan?

Land that isn't in immediate use as part of the project. SBA proceeds can't pay for it, so it's carved out of the loan and paid another way, or left out of the purchase.

Does an outdoor storage yard count toward the 51% occupancy rule?

Yes, if it's actively used. The SOP names storage yards for contractors, trucking and moving companies. Parking areas don't count.

Can I buy farm land with an SBA loan?

Only land the farming operation needs. Excess farm land, and vacant or row-crop land for future use or to lease, can't be financed.

Can I get 100% financing on a yard?

Some 7(a) lenders go to 100% for established companies with the cash flow to cover the payment. Many ask for 10% or more, especially on land-heavy deals.
