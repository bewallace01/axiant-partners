SBL vs HELOC: Borrowing Against Your Portfolio vs Your Home

Both let you borrow without selling assets—but one pledges your investments and the other your home. How they compare on collateral, rates, speed, risk, and tax, and when each wins

If you have both an investment portfolio and home equity, you have two ways to borrow without selling anything: a securities-based line of credit or a home equity line of credit. They solve the same problem—access to cash while keeping your assets—but they pledge different collateral and carry different risks. This page compares them head-to-head so you can pick the right one. For the fundamentals, see how securities-based lending works.

Collateral and What You're Risking

This is the heart of the decision. With SBL, you pledge marketable securities and they stay invested—you keep any upside and dividends while you borrow. The tradeoff: if the market falls and your collateral value drops below the required level, you face a margin call and may have to add assets or repay quickly, and the lender can sell securities to cover the loan. Understand this fully in the risks of securities-based lending and the deeper margin-call and concentration traps.

With a HELOC, you pledge your home. There's no market-driven call, but the consequence of default is foreclosure—your residence is on the line. Which risk you prefer is personal: market volatility versus your house.

Cost, Speed, and Flexibility

  • Speed. SBL is usually faster—securities are liquid and easily valued, so there's no property appraisal or title work. A HELOC can take weeks.
  • Upfront cost. SBL typically has no appraisal or closing costs; HELOCs commonly do.
  • Rate. Both are often variable. SBL spreads usually improve with larger pledged balances; see typical SBL rates. Compare the all-in cost, not just the headline rate.
  • Borrowing power. SBL advance rates depend on the asset mix; how much you can borrow with SBL covers this. HELOC limits depend on appraised equity.

When Each One Wins

  • Choose SBL when you have a sizable, diversified portfolio, want speed and no closing costs, and prefer to keep your home out of it. It's well suited to short-to-medium-term needs where you expect to repay from cash flow or a coming liquidity event.
  • Choose a HELOC when you have substantial home equity but a smaller or concentrated portfolio, want a longer draw period insulated from market swings, or simply don't want to pledge investments you expect to keep compounding.

For the broader decision of when borrowing against a portfolio makes sense at all, see when to use securities-based lending. You can also weigh a business line of credit if the need is business-related.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between SBL and a HELOC?

An SBL is secured by your investment portfolio; a HELOC is secured by home equity. SBL funds faster, needs no appraisal, and keeps your investments intact but exposes you to a margin call. A HELOC offers longer terms and home-based collateral but involves an appraisal, closing costs, and foreclosure risk. The choice depends on which asset you'd rather pledge.

Is SBL cheaper than a HELOC?

It depends on rates and your profile. SBL rates are typically variable with a spread that improves with larger balances and usually no appraisal or closing costs; HELOC rates are often variable but carry setup costs. Neither is universally cheaper — compare the all-in cost, rate structure, and how long you'll carry the balance.

Which is riskier, SBL or a HELOC?

They carry different risks. With SBL, a market decline can trigger a margin call requiring you to add collateral or repay quickly, and the lender can sell securities. With a HELOC, defaulting can lead to foreclosure. SBL risk is tied to market volatility; HELOC risk is tied to your residence.

Can I get an SBL faster than a HELOC?

Usually yes. Because SBL is secured by liquid, easily valued securities, there's no property appraisal or title work, so setup is often days rather than the weeks a HELOC can take. That speed is a common reason borrowers choose SBL for time-sensitive needs.

When should I choose a HELOC over SBL?

A HELOC may fit better when you have substantial home equity but a smaller or concentrated portfolio, want a longer draw period insulated from market swings, or would rather not pledge investments you expect to keep growing. A sizable, diversified portfolio plus a need for speed favors SBL.
