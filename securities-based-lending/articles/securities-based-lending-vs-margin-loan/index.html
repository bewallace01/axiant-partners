Quick answer

Both an SBL and a margin loan borrow against your securities—the difference is purpose. A securities-based line of credit is typically a non-purpose loan: use the money for almost anything except buying more securities. A margin loan is a purpose loan built to buy additional investments. That distinction puts them under different rules. Both can face a margin call if your collateral falls. If your goal is buying more stock, use margin; if it's cash for real estate, a business, taxes, or bridging a gap, a non-purpose SBL is the matched tool.

"Borrowing against your portfolio" describes two different products, and people routinely conflate them. The difference isn't cosmetic—it determines what you're allowed to do with the money and which rules apply. This page draws the line clearly between a non-purpose securities-based line of credit and a margin loan, so you pick the right one. The Core Difference: Purpose

The defining distinction is what the borrowed money can be used for:

  • Securities-based line of credit (non-purpose). You can use the proceeds for nearly any purpose—real estate, a business, a tax bill, an emergency, a bridge loan—except buying, carrying, or trading securities. That prohibition is the whole point of "non-purpose."
  • Margin loan (purpose). Designed primarily to buy more investments. It lets you leverage your portfolio to increase market exposure, which is a fundamentally different objective.

Because of that difference in purpose, the two fall under different regulatory frameworks and carry different terms.

What They Share: Margin-Call Risk

Don't let the "non-purpose" label fool you into thinking SBL is risk-free. Both products are secured by securities, so both expose you to a maintenance call if the market falls and your collateral value drops below the required level—you may have to add assets or repay quickly, and the lender can sell your securities. The specific margin requirements differ between the two and across providers. Understand the mechanics in the risks of securities-based lending and the margin-call and concentration traps.

Cost and Borrowing Power

Both are typically variable-rate, benchmarked to a short-term index plus a spread that usually improves with larger pledged balances. Neither is universally cheaper—compare the actual rate, the advance rate against your specific holdings, and any fees. See typical SBL rates and how much you can borrow with SBL.

Which Should You Use?

  • Use a margin loan when your goal is to buy more securities and you accept margin's leverage mechanics.
  • Use a non-purpose SBL when you need cash for almost anything else—closing on real estate, funding a business need, covering a tax bill, or bridging a liquidity gap—without selling your portfolio. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an SBL and a margin loan?

Both borrow against your securities, but the key difference is purpose. A securities-based line of credit is a non-purpose loan you can use for almost anything except buying more securities. A margin loan is a purpose loan designed primarily to buy investments. They fall under different rules, and SBL often offers flexible terms for general borrowing.

Can I buy stocks with a securities-based line of credit?

Generally no. A non-purpose SBL specifically prohibits using the proceeds to purchase, carry, or trade securities — that restriction is what separates it from a margin loan. If your goal is to buy more investments, a margin loan is the product designed for that.

Is a margin loan or SBL cheaper?

It varies by provider, balance size, and rate environment, so neither is universally cheaper. Both are typically variable-rate with a spread that often improves with larger balances. Compare the actual rate, the advance rate against your holdings, and any fees rather than assuming one is always less expensive.

Do both SBL and margin loans have margin calls?

Yes. Because both are secured by securities, a decline in collateral value can trigger a maintenance call requiring you to add assets or repay, and the lender can sell securities. Margin requirements and maintenance levels differ between the two and between providers, so understand the thresholds before borrowing.

Which should I use, SBL or a margin loan?

Match the product to the purpose. Use a margin loan to buy more securities. Use a non-purpose securities-based line of credit when you need cash for general purposes such as real estate, a business need, a tax bill, or bridging a liquidity gap, without selling your portfolio. When the use isn't buying investments, SBL is usually the better match.
