What Are the Risks of Securities-Based Lending?

Because your investment portfolio secures the facility, market movements can directly impact borrowing capacity and repayment pressure.

1. Market Volatility Risk

The most significant risk is portfolio value fluctuation. If markets decline:

  • Collateral value drops
  • Loan-to-value ratio increases
  • Available credit can shrink

2. Collateral Call Risk

If LTV exceeds approved thresholds, lenders may require:

  • Additional collateral
  • Partial loan paydown
  • Portfolio rebalancing

Failure to meet collateral requirements can increase liquidation risk.

3. Concentration Risk

Highly concentrated portfolios can reduce advance rates and increase exposure to single-asset shocks.

  • Single-stock concentration can increase volatility impact
  • Sector concentration can amplify drawdowns
  • Diversified portfolios generally qualify for better terms

4. Variable Interest Rate Risk

Many SBL facilities use variable-rate pricing. Rising rates can increase carrying costs and pressure cash flow.

5. Over-Leverage Risk

Because SBL provides quick liquidity, borrowers may over-borrow relative to operating cash flow and risk tolerance.

6. Liquidity & Market Shock Risk

In severe market events, assets can move sharply while liquidity conditions tighten. This combination may accelerate collateral stress.

How to Reduce Risk

  • Borrow below the maximum approved LTV
  • Maintain a liquidity reserve for volatility periods
  • Diversify pledged holdings
  • Use proceeds for productive, time-sensitive opportunities

Who Should Use Securities-Based Lending Carefully?

  • Borrowers with concentrated portfolios
  • Those without a liquidity cushion
  • Borrowers using the facility for speculative purposes

Minimum Loan Amount

Securities-based lending facilities typically start at $10,000, with larger facilities available for stronger collateral profiles.

A Worked Example: How a Collateral Call Unfolds

Picture a $1,000,000 portfolio with a 60% LTV facility ($600,000 limit) and $500,000 drawn — a high 83% utilization of the limit. A sharp correction knocks the portfolio down 25% to $750,000. Now the most the lender will lend against it is 60% of $750,000 = $450,000, but you owe $500,000. The lender issues a collateral call for the shortfall (often more, to restore a buffer). If you have cash or additional securities to pledge, you post them and move on. If you don't, the lender sells enough of your holdings to cure the call — locking in losses at the bottom and potentially triggering a taxable gain. The same correction is a non-event for a borrower who had drawn only $250,000: even after the drop, the $750,000 portfolio still supports $450,000, comfortably above the balance. The risk isn't the market move itself — it's the combination of a market move and a thin cushion.

Variable Rates & Over-Leverage

Two quieter risks compound the collateral-call risk. SBL facilities are almost always variable-rate, so the cost of carrying a balance rises with benchmark rates — a line that was cheap when you opened it can get materially more expensive, which matters most if you carry a large balance for a long time. And because the credit is so easy to draw, it's easy to over-leverage relative to the cash flow that actually services it: SBL is liquidity against assets, not income, so a balance your business or income can't comfortably cover in interest becomes a problem the moment markets or rates turn. Used as short-to-medium-term, conservatively-sized liquidity, SBL is powerful; used as cheap permanent leverage drawn to the max, it's fragile.

Final Thoughts

SBL can be an efficient way to access capital, but it requires disciplined risk management. The primary risk drivers are market volatility, collateral sensitivity, and leverage. Review current securities-based lending options and structure conservatively to protect downside.

A business line of credit avoids the margin-call risk that comes with securities-based lending.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the risks of securities-based lending?

Six core risks: market volatility shrinking collateral, collateral calls when LTV breaches thresholds, concentration risk, variable-rate pricing, over-leverage relative to cash flow, and liquidity shocks during severe market events.

What is a collateral call in securities-based lending?

If your portfolio value falls and the loan-to-value rises above approved thresholds, the lender can require you to add collateral or pay down the line, sometimes forcing asset sales.

How can you reduce securities-based lending risk?

Borrow well below the maximum LTV (facilities run 50-75%), keep the portfolio diversified, and avoid over-leverage relative to operating cash flow.

Who should be cautious with securities-based lending?

Anyone with a concentrated, volatile portfolio or who is already near maximum LTV, since collateral calls become much more likely.
