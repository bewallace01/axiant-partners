The Best SBA Loans, Reviewed

Best Overall

SBA 7(a) Loan

The SBA's flagship program and the best overall for most businesses. The 7(a) loan is remarkably flexible—working capital, equipment, acquisitions, partner buyouts, and refinancing higher-cost debt—with amounts up to $5 million, low rates, and terms up to 10 years (25 for real estate). It's the best long-term value when you qualify. Explore the full SBA loan guides.

Best for: most established-business needs · Watch-out: 30-90 day process, more paperwork

Best for Real Estate

SBA 504 Loan

Purpose-built for owner-occupied commercial real estate and major fixed assets. The 504 pairs a lender with a Certified Development Company, often delivering long terms and competitive fixed rates—keeping payments low on a large balance for buying, building, or renovating property.

Best for: property and heavy fixed assets · Watch-out: appraisal and longer timeline

Best for Speed

SBA Express

When you need a faster answer on a smaller amount, SBA Express offers a streamlined path with quicker initial decisions than a standard 7(a). A strong fit when you want SBA-backed terms but can't wait through the full timeline.

Best for: smaller, time-sensitive SBA needs · Watch-out: lower maximum than standard 7(a)

Best for Newer / Very Small

SBA Microloan

Designed for newer and very small businesses that need modest sums and may not yet qualify for a full 7(a). Microloans are a strong on-ramp for early-stage companies—pair with our startup financing hub for the broader picture.

Best for: early-stage, smaller amounts · Watch-out: caps limit larger needs

Best for Working Capital

SBA 7(a) for Working Capital

The 7(a) also shines for general working capital—funding operations, hiring, and growth with a long repayment runway and low cost. When you don't need money in days, it beats higher-cost short-term options. Compare with working capital loans for speed.

Best for: low-cost operating capital · Watch-out: not the fastest option

Best for Acquisition

SBA 7(a) for Business Acquisition

Buying a business or buying out a partner is a classic 7(a) use case—long terms and low rates make acquisition financing manageable. See our SBA articles on acquisition structures and what lenders look for.

Best for: acquisitions and buyouts · Watch-out: thorough underwriting of the target

Best SBA Loans FAQs

Which SBA loan is best?

It depends on the use. The SBA 7(a) is best overall and most flexible (up to $5M). The 504 is best for owner-occupied real estate. SBA Express is best for a faster decision on a smaller amount, and a microloan is best for newer or very small businesses.

What is the difference between an SBA 7(a) and 504 loan?

The 7(a) is the flexible general-purpose loan for working capital, acquisitions, equipment, and more. The 504 is purpose-built for owner-occupied real estate and major fixed assets, often with long terms and fixed rates.

What is the easiest SBA loan to get?

For smaller amounts, SBA Express is faster and more streamlined, and microloans are designed for newer, very small businesses. None are guaranteed, but these lower the bar relative to a large 7(a) or 504.

How long does an SBA loan take?

A standard 7(a) or 504 commonly takes 30-90 days. Express is faster on smaller amounts. If you need money in days, a non-SBA option like working capital may fit better.
