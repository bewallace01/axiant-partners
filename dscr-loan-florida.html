DSCR Loans in Florida: Why Insurance Decides the File

Quick Answer: DSCR Loans in Florida

DSCR loans are widely available across Florida on 1–4 unit rentals and short-term rentals, typically at 20–25% down with a minimum ratio of 1.00–1.25. Florida has two state-specific factors that dominate every other consideration: insurance cost, which is the largest single threat to DSCR in this state, and judicial foreclosure, which lengthens lender recovery and shows up in pricing.

Insurance Is the Whole Game in Florida

Nothing moves a Florida DSCR calculation more than insurance. Windstorm and hurricane exposure has pushed premiums to levels that routinely decide whether a file qualifies, and in coastal counties the annual cost can run several times what the same property would pay inland in another state.

Because insurance sits in operating expenses, it comes straight out of net operating income. A property that pencils at 1.25x on a $2,500 premium can fall below 1.00x at $9,000 — on identical rent. That is not a hypothetical; it is the most common reason Florida DSCR files fail.

Practical rules for Florida: get a bound quote before you go under contract, never underwrite on the seller’s premium, and check the roof age first — many carriers will not write a roof over a certain age at all, which can make a property effectively unfinanceable regardless of its rent.

Judicial Foreclosure and What It Costs You

Florida is a judicial foreclosure state. Every foreclosure moves through the court system, and timelines are among the longest in the country — often well over a year, and historically far longer during periods of court backlog.

Lenders price recovery time, so a slower remedy generally means slightly tighter terms in Florida than in a fast non-judicial state such as Texas. It rarely changes whether you get approved, but it is a real reason two otherwise identical properties in different states quote differently.

Short-Term Rentals: Florida’s Particular Case

Florida is one of the largest short-term rental markets in the country, which makes the STR question more pressing here than almost anywhere else.

Many DSCR lenders ignore nightly revenue entirely and underwrite the appraiser’s long-term market rent instead. A property earning strong seasonal income can therefore be underwritten on a far lower number. Others accept documented STR history, typically through a platform revenue report or a market analysis.

Local rules matter too. Several Florida municipalities restrict or license short-term rentals, and a lender will want to see that the use is permitted. Confirm both the lender’s STR treatment and the local ordinance before you rely on nightly income — see how to compare DSCR lenders for who does what.

Holding Florida Property in an LLC

Florida imposes documentary stamp tax on both the deed and the note, which makes closing costs here higher than in a state such as Texas. Budget for it — it is a real line item on a purchase and again on a refinance.

LLC ownership is standard for Florida rentals and nearly all DSCR lenders will close in an entity. Have the operating agreement, EIN, and articles of organisation ready. Florida LLCs file an annual report with a fixed fee; keep the entity in good standing, because a lapsed status will stop a closing.

A Florida Deal, Worked

A Jacksonville single-family rents for $2,050/mo — gross annual income $24,600. Purchase price $290,000 with 25% down leaves a loan of $217,500.

Operating expenses on a realistic Florida basis: property tax at roughly 1.1% $3,190; insurance at an inland-Florida figure of $3,600; maintenance $1,700; management at 8% $1,968; vacancy at 5% $1,230. Total $11,688, giving NOI of $12,912.

At 7.25% over 30 years the payment is about $1,484/mo, or $17,808 a year. DSCR = 0.73x.

Now move the same house to a coastal county where windstorm coverage runs $9,000 instead of $3,600. NOI falls to $7,512 and DSCR drops to 0.42x. Identical rent, identical price, and the deal moves from marginal to impossible on insurance alone. Inland Florida and coastal Florida are effectively two different lending markets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you get a DSCR loan in Florida?

Yes, and Florida is one of the largest DSCR markets in the country, covering 1-4 unit rentals and in many cases short-term rentals. Typical terms are 20 to 25% down with a minimum ratio of 1.00 to 1.25. The two state-specific factors that dominate are insurance cost and judicial foreclosure timelines.

How does Florida insurance affect DSCR loans?

More than any other factor. Windstorm and hurricane exposure has pushed premiums to levels that routinely decide whether a file qualifies. A property that clears 1.25x on a $2,500 premium can fall below 1.00x at $9,000 on identical rent. Get a bound quote before going under contract, never use the seller's premium, and check roof age first since many carriers will not write older roofs at all.

Do DSCR lenders count short-term rental income in Florida?

Some do and many do not. A large share underwrite the appraiser's long-term market rent regardless of nightly revenue, which can value a strong seasonal property far below what it earns. Others accept documented STR history through a platform revenue report. Confirm both the lender's treatment and the local ordinance, since several Florida municipalities restrict or license short-term rentals.

Why are Florida DSCR terms sometimes tighter than other states?

Florida is a judicial foreclosure state, meaning every foreclosure moves through the courts with timelines among the longest in the country. Lenders price recovery time, so a slower remedy generally means slightly tighter terms than in a fast non-judicial state such as Texas. It rarely changes approval, but it explains pricing differences between otherwise identical properties.
