Small Business Lending Statistics 2026

Quick answer

About 45% of small employer firms applied for financing in the prior 12 months, the highest share since 2021, and roughly 46% received the full amount they asked for. Applicants to small banks fared best, fully approved about 57% of the time. The SBA 7(a) and 504 programs together approved 84,840 loans worth about $45.1 billion, and the average 7(a) loan was $477,571 in FY2025. Sources: the Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey and the U.S. SBA.

Key Small Business Lending Statistics

  • ~45% of small employer firms applied for financing in the prior 12 months—the highest share since 2021 (Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey).
  • ~46% of applicants received the full amount they requested, up from 43% a year earlier (Fed SBCS).
  • ~57% full-approval rate at small banks—the highest of any lender type (Fed SBCS).
  • 84,840 SBA 7(a) and 504 loans approved, totaling ~$45.1 billion in FY2025 (U.S. SBA).
  • ~$477,571 average SBA 7(a) loan size in FY2025 (SBA lending reports).
  • 29% of applicants used online/fintech lenders in 2025, up from 17% in 2020 (Fed SBCS).

Figures are drawn from the most recent published Federal Reserve and SBA data as of 2026. See Sources for links. Confirm primary sources for the latest figures.

Small Business Lending Statistics FAQs

What percentage of small businesses apply for financing?

About 45% of small employer firms applied for financing in the prior 12 months—the highest share since 2021, per the Federal Reserve Small Business Credit Survey.

What is the small business loan approval rate?

Roughly 46% of applicants received the full amount requested (up from 43%). Applicants to small banks were most likely to be fully approved, at about 57% (Fed SBCS).

What is the average SBA 7(a) loan size?

Approximately $477,571 in FY2025. The SBA 7(a) and 504 programs together approved 84,840 loans totaling about $45.1 billion (U.S. SBA).

How many small businesses use online lenders?

The share using online/fintech lenders rose from 17% (2020) to 29% (2025)—but 60% of those borrowers reported higher-than-expected costs (Fed SBCS).
