Why Your Line of Credit Application Keeps Pending

What’s holding it up—and how to move it forward

← Back to Business Line of Credit Articles

Last updated: March 19, 2026

Business lines of credit are flexible—draw when you need, repay, and reuse. But when your application sits in pending status for weeks, it’s usually because of a few repeat causes: incomplete bank statements, documentation that triggered a verification step, a lender backlog, or a request you didn’t see. This guide explains why your line of credit application keeps pending and what you can do to unstick it. For typical timelines, see how fast you can get approved for a business line of credit; for qualification barriers, see what’s holding you back from a business line of credit.

Quick Answer

Line of credit applications stay pending when bank statements are incomplete (missing pages), something triggered verification, the lender has a backlog, or you haven’t responded to a request. Fix by submitting full PDF bank statements (all pages), responding within 24—48 hours to any request, and following up for status if it’s been more than a week. See business line of credit requirements and documents needed for a business line of credit for the full checklist.

1. Incomplete Bank Statements

The most common reason business line of credit applications stay pending: incomplete bank statements. Lenders need full PDF statements—every page for every month requested, usually 3—6 months. When you submit screenshots, partial pages, or only the first and last page of a multi-page statement, the underwriter can’t complete the review. They may send a request for the full statements, but if that email goes to spam or a shared inbox nobody checks, the file sits. Some lenders don’t follow up aggressively; they let the file age until you reach out.

Multiple accounts add complexity. If you have several business accounts, the lender may need statements for all of them—or need clarity on which account is primary. Submitting only one account when revenue flows through multiple accounts can trigger questions and delay. Conversely, submitting every personal and business account without explanation can overwhelm the underwriter and slow the process.

Fix: Download full PDF statements from your bank for every month requested. Include all pages. If you have multiple business accounts, provide the primary operating account and clarify which receives most revenue. Don’t use screenshots. See documents needed for a business line of credit. A complete statement set from day one prevents the most common hold-up.

2. Verification or Manual Review Triggered

Some applications route to manual underwriting or trigger additional verification. Large or irregular deposits, multiple accounts with complex transfers, a business address that doesn’t match records, or application data that doesn’t align with bank activity can flag a file. Fraud prevention is a priority—lenders would rather slow down a legitimate application than fund a fraudulent one. Verification can involve a phone call, request for additional documentation, or secondary review by a different team. That process adds days or weeks.

New businesses, businesses with recent ownership changes, or businesses with thin credit files may get extra scrutiny. If your bank statements show deposits from an account that isn’t in your name, or many transfers between personal and business accounts, the lender may want to understand the flow before approving.

Fix: Ensure your application data matches your bank statements—business name, address, revenue figures. If you have unusual deposits or transfers, include a brief note (e.g., "Loan from partner," "Sale of asset"). Respond immediately to any verification request. If the lender calls, answer or return the call within 24 hours. Delay on your side extends the pending status.

3. Lender Backlog

Line of credit lenders see volume spikes—month-end, quarter-end, seasonal rushes, or when rates move. When application volume is high, underwriting queues lengthen. A file that would normally get attention in a few days can sit for 1—2 weeks simply because the lender is overloaded. There’s little you can do about lender capacity, but you can avoid making it worse: a complete file gets through faster when it’s finally their turn; an incomplete file gets sent back and re-queued.

Fix: Apply with a complete file so that when the lender reaches your application, they can approve it without another round of requests. If you’ve been pending more than a week, follow up for a status update. Ask for an expected decision date. If the lender can’t provide one, consider applying through a marketplace—your application can go to multiple lenders, and some may have shorter queues. Get matched to compare options.

4. Unseen or Unanswered Request

Lenders often send document requests or clarification questions by email. If that email goes to spam, a shared inbox nobody monitors, or an old address, you never see it—and the lender waits. They may follow up once or twice, but if they don’t hear back, the file can be marked inactive or withdrawn. Even a single unanswered request can keep your application pending for weeks.

Fix: Add the lender’s domain to your safe-sender list. Check spam and junk folders. Ensure the email on your application is one you check daily. If you haven’t heard anything in 3—5 business days, proactively reach out: "I wanted to confirm you have everything you need. Is there anything outstanding from my side?" A quick call or email can surface a stuck request.

5. Application in Manual Underwriting Queue

Not all line of credit applications get automated approval. Borderline credit, complex revenue patterns, higher requested limits, or secured lines requiring collateral review can route a file to manual underwriting. Manual review takes longer—an analyst must look at the full file, possibly request additional information, and make a judgment call. Files in manual review can take 1—3 weeks or more, especially during busy periods.

Fix: If you know your profile is borderline (credit, revenue, time in business), expect that manual review is possible. Submit the strongest possible file so the analyst has what they need. If you’ve been pending more than two weeks, ask whether your file is in manual review and what the typical timeline is. Some lenders can provide a status update or expedite if you have an urgent need.

6. Slow Response to Prior Requests

If the lender asked for something and you took a week to respond, your file went to the back of the queue. When you finally respond, it re-enters the pipeline—often behind new applications. Each slow response adds days. Multiple slow responses can turn a 1—2 week process into a month-long one.

Fix: Respond to every lender request within 24—48 hours. If you need time to gather something, reply immediately with a realistic date: "I will have the complete statements by Thursday." Silence keeps your application pending. Designate someone to monitor for lender communication during the application process.

7. Credit or Revenue Needs Secondary Review

Some lenders use a tiered approval process. Applications that pass an initial screen but fall in a gray zone—credit score near the cutoff, revenue that’s borderline, or an industry that requires extra review—get flagged for a second look. That second look can take additional time, especially if the first underwriter needs to escalate or get sign-off from a senior team.

Fix: Understand your profile before applying. If your credit is near the lender’s typical minimum, or your revenue is at the low end of their range, expect that secondary review is possible. See what credit score is needed for a business line of credit and business line of credit requirements. Target lenders that fit your profile to reduce the chance of a long pending review.

What to Do Right Now

If your line of credit application is pending: (1) Check spam and ensure you haven’t missed a request. (2) Reach out to the lender for a status update—ask explicitly if they’re waiting on anything from you. (3) If they need something, send it within 24 hours. (4) If they say everything is received and it’s just backlog, ask for an expected decision date. (5) If they can’t give you a date or it’s been 2+ weeks with no movement, consider applying elsewhere.
