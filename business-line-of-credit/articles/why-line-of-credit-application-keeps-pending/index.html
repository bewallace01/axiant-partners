Why Your Line of Credit Application Keeps Pending

A revolving line is sized, not just approved, so it gets reviews a term loan doesn't. What each pending status means and what moves it

← Back to Business Line of Credit Articles

Updated November 11, 2026

Quick answer

A line of credit stays pending longer than a loan because the lender is sizing a limit you can draw again and again, not approving one payment. It checks existing liens and advances, how your deposits support a limit, and, on secured lines, the collateral. Online lenders often finish in 3-7 business days; banks commonly take 1-3 weeks. Those are typical ranges, not promises; Axiant Partners, a business-loan broker in Boca Raton, sees most stalls come from liens, limits or a counteroffer waiting on you.

What Each Pending Status Usually Means

Portals use different words, but a line of credit application tends to move through the same stages. Knowing which one you're in tells you whether to wait or act.

Status you seeWhat's usually happeningYour move
Submitted / receivedAutomated checks: identity, credit pull, bank connectionNothing yet; confirm the bank link worked
In review / under reviewAn underwriter is sizing the limit from deposits and debtsAnswer questions the same day
Pending documentsSomething is missing or unreadableSend exactly what was asked, as full PDFs
Pending verificationBusiness, ownership or bank data didn't matchExplain the mismatch in writing
Approved, pending acceptanceA limit was offered, often lower than you askedAccept, negotiate or decline
Approved, pending setupAgreement, guarantee or draw account still openSign and finish the account link

The last two catch people out. An offer that sits in your inbox shows as pending on the lender's side, and an approved line can't fund until the agreement is signed and the draw account is verified.

Why Lines of Credit Get More Review Than Loans

With a term loan the lender knows its exposure on day one. With a revolving line you can draw, repay and draw again, so the lender underwrites the worst case: the full limit drawn when your business is at its weakest. That shows up in four places.

  • Limit sizing. The underwriter works out how large a line your deposits and existing payments can carry. If you asked for more than that, the file sits while they decide whether to counter. See why a line of credit limit comes in low.
  • Existing liens. Many lines are secured by a general lien on business assets. If another lender or a cash advance company already filed a UCC-1, it generally comes first, so the new lender may want it released, subordinated or paid off before it approves.
  • Existing advances. Daily or weekly debits from a merchant cash advance reduce the cash a line could be repaid from, and some lenders won't open a line behind one.
  • Collateral review on secured lines. A line secured by receivables or inventory is sized by a borrowing base, and bank asset-based lenders may run a field exam of your books before closing.

How Long Can a Lender Leave It Pending?

Not indefinitely. Under Regulation B a lender generally has to tell you within 30 days of a completed application whether it approved, made a counteroffer or declined. If the application is incomplete, it has 30 days to tell you what's missing, and the notice has to say what it needs and give you a reasonable time to send it. The rules for business credit vary with the business's revenue, with lighter requirements above $1 million, so ask the lender directly: "Is my application complete, and if not, what exactly is missing?"

If you hear nothing for a week, that question usually gets a straight answer. If the lender says the file is complete and gives no decision date, it's reasonable to apply elsewhere, keeping in mind that each new application can mean another credit inquiry.

Worked Example: A Line Stuck Behind an Old Lien

Illustrative. A contractor applies for a $75,000 line with an online lender on a Monday. The automated checks pass the same day and the status moves to "in review". On Wednesday the underwriter's lien search finds a UCC-1 filed two years earlier by an equipment lender, covering all business assets, even though that loan was paid off last spring. Nothing moves for a week, because the lender's request for a lien release went to an inbox nobody checks.

When the owner calls, the fix takes two days: the old lender sends a paid-in-full letter and files a UCC-3 termination. The underwriter then offers $50,000 instead of $75,000, sized to the deposits, and that offer shows as "approved, pending acceptance" until the owner signs. Two of the three delays were on the borrower's side, which is typical.

Pending at Renewal: When an Existing Line Is Under Review

Many lines, especially at banks, run on a 12-month term and are re-reviewed before renewal. During that review the lender may ask for new financial statements or tax returns, and some pause new draws until it finishes. Send the renewal package early, before the maturity date, so the line doesn't lapse while it sits in review. If a draw is refused or the limit is cut at renewal, see why a draw request gets declined and why lines get cut or revoked.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my line of credit application still pending?

Usually because the lender is still sizing the limit, found an existing lien or cash advance, is waiting on a document, or has made a counteroffer that's waiting for your reply. Ask whether the application is complete and what is missing.

How long does a business line of credit approval take?

Online lenders commonly decide in 24-72 hours and fund in about 3-7 business days. Bank lines commonly take 1-3 weeks, and secured or SBA lines longer. These are typical ranges, not promises.

What does approved pending mean on a line of credit?

The lender has offered a limit but something is still open: you haven't accepted the offer, signed the agreement or personal guarantee, or verified the account draws will go to.

How long can a lender keep a credit application pending?

Under Regulation B a lender generally must notify you within 30 days of a completed application, and within 30 days tell you what's missing if it's incomplete. Requirements are lighter for businesses with more than $1 million in revenue.

Can a UCC lien hold up a line of credit?

Yes. If another lender already filed a lien on business assets, it generally has priority, so a new line lender may ask for a payoff, a termination or a subordination before approving.
