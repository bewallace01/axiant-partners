Secured Business Line of Credit: The Fix When Credit or Revenue Holds You Back

How a secured line works, what it costs next to an unsecured one, and the five things that most often keep a business from getting approved.

← Back to Business Line of Credit Articles

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

A secured business line of credit is a revolving line backed by collateral, usually receivables, inventory or equipment, so the lender takes less risk and you get a higher limit or a lower rate than on an unsecured line. Secured bank lines run about 8–11% APR with limits from $25,000 to $500,000+, against 10–13% for unsecured bank lines that want 680+ credit. It is the usual fix when credit, revenue or time in business keeps you from an unsecured line. The trade-off is a lien on the pledged assets and regular reporting.

What is a secured business line of credit?

It works like any business line of credit: you get a limit, draw what you need, pay interest only on what you have drawn, and can draw again as you repay. The difference is that you pledge specific assets, most often accounts receivable, inventory or equipment. Because the lender can recover from those assets, it will usually approve a file it would turn down unsecured, and it prices the line lower. Typical 2026 ranges:

Secured lines generally price about 2–4 percentage points below unsecured ones. Asset-backed lines for larger companies often run $250,000 to $2,000,000+. Most lines are variable, tied to prime. For the full rate picture, see typical business line of credit rates.

How much can you borrow on a secured line?

The collateral sets the ceiling. On receivables, lenders often advance 80–85% of eligible invoices, so $200,000 of eligible receivables supports a line of roughly $160,000–$170,000. Inventory gets a lower advance rate because it is harder to sell quickly. See business line of credit collateral for what lenders accept.

What does $100,000 drawn on a secured line cost per month?

You pay interest only on what you draw. With $100,000 drawn at 8% APR, interest is about $667 a month; at 11% it is about $917. The same draw on an unsecured bank line at 13% runs about $1,083. Principal comes on top as you pay the balance down, and draw fees or an annual fee add to the real cost.

Secured vs unsecured: which line should you apply for?

Start unsecured if you qualify: approval rests on revenue, credit and time in business, and there is less paperwork. Unsecured lines often still require a personal guarantee and a general UCC filing. If you are declined or the limit is too small, a secured line is the next step. You pledge assets and accept a UCC filing on them plus periodic reporting, and in return you get a higher limit and a lower rate. Owners who are stuck between products can also compare a working capital loan vs a line of credit. The business line of credit overview covers both.

What is holding back your line of credit approval?

1. Credit below the lender’s bar

Unsecured lines lean heavily on credit. Many lenders want 660–680+ FICO for a meaningful unsecured limit; many programs accept 550+ with lower limits and higher rates, and secured lines are more forgiving. Fix: target lenders that work with your tier, or improve your score (pay down revolving debt, fix errors, avoid new late payments) and reapply in a few months. See what credit score you need for a business line of credit.

2. Revenue or deposits that don’t support the limit

Lenders size the line to your revenue and cash flow. Inconsistent deposits, falling revenue or a request too big for your financials leads to a decline or a tiny limit. Fix: ask for an amount that fits your revenue and show 6–12 months of stable or growing deposits. If you are seasonal, provide 12 months so the lender sees the full cycle, and name a clear use, such as inventory or a payroll bridge.

3. Bank statements that tell the wrong story

Overdrafts, low balances and erratic activity suggest a revolving line could be risky. Fix: clean up your banking for 2–3 months with no overdrafts, consistent deposits and reasonable balances, all in one primary operating account. See why business lines of credit get cut or revoked so you don’t repeat the problem after approval.

4. Too much existing debt

Lenders look at total debt service. If you are already stretched by term loans, other lines or daily remittances such as an MCA, expect a decline or a small line. Fix: pay down what you can, especially high-cost or daily-payment debt, and document any refinance plan. See how to get out of bad business debt.

5. Time in business or documentation

Many lenders prefer 12–24 months in business. Newer businesses can sometimes qualify with strong revenue and clean documents, usually at a smaller limit. Incomplete or inconsistent paperwork slows or kills applications, so send a complete, consistent file. If you are new, see business line of credit for startups.

What are the downsides of a secured line of credit?

  • Your assets are on the line. If you default, the lender can take the pledged receivables, inventory or equipment.
  • More paperwork. Expect a UCC filing, asset verification and periodic reporting, such as receivables agings.
  • Fees. Some lines charge 1–3% per draw plus an annual fee, which raises the effective cost.
  • Variable rates. Most lines float with prime, so your cost moves when rates do.

What to do next

Check your credit and target lenders that accept your tier. Clean up bank statements for 2–3 months, reduce existing debt where you can and ask for a limit that fits your revenue. If an unsecured line isn’t coming together, list the receivables, inventory or equipment you could pledge and price a secured line. Watch for red flags in line of credit offers, and when you are ready, get matched with line of credit lenders that fit your profile.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a secured business line of credit?

A revolving line backed by collateral such as receivables, inventory or equipment. You draw what you need and pay interest only on the balance, like any line. Because the lender can recover from the pledged assets, secured bank lines run about 8–11% APR versus 10–13% for unsecured bank lines.

Is a secured line of credit easier to get?

Usually, yes. The collateral lowers the lender's risk, so a secured line can be approved when weaker credit, uneven revenue or a short history would sink an unsecured application. The limit depends on the assets: on receivables, lenders often advance 80–85% of eligible invoices.

What stops a business line of credit from being approved?

Most often it is credit below the lender's bar, revenue or deposits that don't support the limit, bank statements with overdrafts or erratic deposits, or too much existing debt. New businesses and incomplete documentation also block approval.

Can I get a line of credit with bad credit?

Sometimes, through a secured line or an online lender, but expect a lower limit and a higher cost; many programs start at 550+. Cleaning up 90 days of bank statements and reducing existing debt improves your odds more than waiting on your score alone.

What credit score do I need for a line of credit?

Many lenders want 660–680+ for a meaningful unsecured line, and 680+ usually gets better rates. Secured or smaller lines accept lower scores. See what credit score is needed for a business line of credit.

What are the downsides of a line of credit?

Most lines are variable, so your cost rises with prime, and some charge 1–3% per draw plus an annual fee. A secured line also puts the pledged assets at risk and adds a UCC filing and periodic reporting. Lines can also be cut or frozen if your numbers slip.

Can I get a line of credit with a new business?

Some lenders offer lines to young companies with strong revenue and clean banking, usually at a smaller limit; many prefer 12–24 months in business. See business line of credit for startups.
