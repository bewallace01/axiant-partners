How Much Hotel Experience Do SBA Lenders Want?

The SBA sets no minimum years. Lenders still have to document who will run the hotel, and existing owners get two real advantages under the 2026 rules

Updated November 13, 2026

Quick answer

Many lenders expect prior hotel ownership or general manager time. A first-time buyer can bridge the gap with a strong GM or a manager. What the SBA Actually Requires

SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 asks lenders to weigh the depth of management experience in the industry or a related one. On a purchase, Appendix 15 also requires a written description of your industry experience and management team, and of how daily operations will run if you aren't on site every day. There is no fixed number of years. That's why the answer varies so much by lender, and it's covered here rather than in the hotel financing overview.

What Lenders Commonly Look For

These are common lender practices, not SBA rules:

  • Prior ownership of a hotel of similar size and service level is the strongest case.
  • General manager or regional experience, often a few years, with responsibility for the P&L, revenue management and staffing.
  • Related experience such as multi-unit restaurants, property management or senior living can count when paired with an experienced GM.
  • Brand approval. Flagged hotels need the franchisor to approve you as a franchisee. That is a separate test from the lender's, but lenders notice it.

Strong experience often moves a lender on other terms too: more willingness on an independent, a smaller equity ask, or a faster approval.

How a First-Time Buyer Can Show It

  • Hire the GM before closing. Include the GM's resume and a signed offer letter in the application, plus how long the GM has run similar hotels.
  • Keep the seller's team. A transition agreement with the seller and key staff can help, and lenders often like a seller who stays on for a period.
  • Use a management company carefully. A third-party manager can supply experience. Unless its agreement is part of a listed brand's disclosure documents, the lender must check it doesn't give the manager sole control of staff, finances and bank accounts. If it does, you become an ineligible passive owner.
  • Start smaller. A first hotel with fewer rooms and a strong flag is easier to place. See flagged vs independent hotels.
  • Write a real operating plan that covers revenue management, staffing, sales and capital spending, not just a resume.

If You Already Own a Hotel

Under Appendix 15, an existing business that has operated for at least two full fiscal years under its current owners, buying 100% of another business in the same four-digit NAICS industry group, is a Business Expansion. For hotels, that group is traveler accommodation. Two rules then work in your favor:

First hotel (Initial Acquisition)Second hotel (Business Expansion)
SBA minimum equity10%, can't be reduced10%, lender may reduce or eliminate
Debt service coverage1.25x1.15x
Condition for less equityNot availableEnough liquidity, and no negative net worth at last year-end

If the lender waives the equity, it can't add dedicated working capital to the loan within 90 days. Working capital for the deal has to come from cash or a line of credit.

Checklist

  1. Resume for every owner and the GM, focused on hotel P&L results.
  2. Operating history of hotels you run now: financials and STR or brand reports.
  3. Management plan showing who runs the hotel day to day.
  4. Any management agreement, reviewed for control terms before you apply.
  5. Franchisor approval status if the hotel is flagged.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy a hotel with no hotel experience?

Sometimes. SBA rules set no minimum years, but lenders must document management. First-time buyers usually need an experienced GM, a strong brand or a manager whose agreement passes the passive-business review.

How many years of hotel experience do lenders want?

There's no SBA number. Many lenders look for prior hotel ownership or a few years as a general manager with P&L responsibility.

Can a hotel management company count as my experience?

It can help, but the lender must make sure the agreement doesn't make you a passive owner, unless it's part of a listed brand's disclosure documents.

Is it easier to finance a second hotel?

Often. An owner with two full years of operations buying another hotel can qualify at 1.15x coverage, and the lender may reduce or waive the 10% equity.

Does franchisor approval replace lender approval?

No. The brand approves you as a franchisee and the lender approves the loan. Lenders consider the brand's approval, but they make their own decision.
