How to Calculate DSCR: The Formula and Worked Examples

The formula first, then a rental worked line by line, a table of how rate and loan size move the ratio, and what to prepare before a lender runs it

Quick answer

DSCR = net operating income ÷ annual debt service. A rental with $23,616 of net operating income and $23,100 of yearly principal, interest, taxes and insurance has a DSCR of 1.02. Most DSCR lenders want at least 1.00, and 1.20 to 1.25 is commonly treated as comfortable. The DSCR Formula, Worked Through

DSCR = Net Operating Income ÷ Annual Debt Service

Take a single-family rental bought for $300,000, renting at $2,400 a month, financed with a $225,000 loan over 30 years at about 6.9%.

LineMonthlyAnnual
Gross rent$2,400$28,800
Less vacancy allowance (5%)−$120−$1,440
Less property management (8%)−$192−$2,304
Less maintenance reserve−$120−$1,440
Net operating income$1,968$23,616
Principal & interest$1,480$17,760
Taxes$310$3,720
Insurance$135$1,620
Annual debt service (PITI)$1,925$23,100

$23,616 ÷ $23,100 = 1.02. The property covers its debt, but only just. A ratio of 1.00 means rent exactly covers the payment; below 1.00 you fund the gap every month; above 1.00 is cushion. On a program that wants 1.20 this file doesn't work as structured, and the fix is arithmetic: a smaller loan, a lower rate or a different property.

Working backward is often more useful. At 7%, the largest loan this rent supports is about $228,900 for a 1.00 ratio, $179,600 for 1.20 and $169,800 for 1.25. On a $300,000 purchase, a 1.25 minimum means roughly 43% down, not 25%. That is why the ratio, not the loan-to-value, usually sets the size of a DSCR loan. Figures are rounded and come from standard monthly amortization.

What Goes in Net Operating Income

Net operating income is gross rent minus operating expenses, before any mortgage payment. Underwriters usually deduct:

  • Vacancy, as an allowance even if the unit is leased today
  • Property management, often imputed as a percentage of rent even if you self-manage
  • Maintenance and reserves on many programs
  • Taxes, insurance and HOA dues, unless the program counts them in debt service instead

Leave out the mortgage, capital improvements, depreciation and your own labor. Depreciation trips people up: it is often the biggest line on a Schedule E, but it is a tax deduction, not cash spent. For which rent counts, see what counts as rental income on a DSCR loan.

What Goes in Annual Debt Service

Debt service is twelve months of payments on the loan being underwritten. Programs differ on what that includes, and it changes the answer more than anything else:

DenominatorEffect on the ratioWhere you see it
Principal + interest onlyHighest ratioSome portfolio programs; taxes and insurance move to the expense side
PITI: principal, interest, taxes, insuranceLower ratioThe common convention on single-family rentals
PITIA: PITI plus HOA duesLowest ratioCondos and properties with an association

Ask which convention a lender uses before comparing quotes. The same property can show 1.25 one way and under 1.00 another, and both underwriters are doing it right.

DSCR Preparation: What to Gather Before You Apply

Most ratios that fail in underwriting fail because an input changed, not because the math was wrong. Gather the figures a lender will use and run the ratio yourself first:

  1. Rent. The signed lease, plus a realistic market rent. Underwriters generally use the lower of the lease and the appraiser's market rent opinion.
  2. Taxes after purchase. Estimate the reassessed bill, not the seller's current one.
  3. A bound insurance quote. Premiums in coastal and wildfire markets have moved sharply; an old estimate is a common reason a file fails late.
  4. HOA dues and special assessments, if any.
  5. The lender's convention. P&I or PITI, and whether it imputes management.
  6. The rate you'll actually get, then rerun at half a point higher to see your margin.

Our DSCR calculator takes the same inputs. The thresholds each program uses are on DSCR loan requirements.

How to Raise a Ratio That Falls Short

  • Put more down. As the second table shows, cutting the loan is the most direct lever.
  • Buy down the rate with points, if the payment drop clears the threshold.
  • Use an interest-only period where a program offers one and underwrites to it.
  • Correct expense assumptions with evidence, such as a bound insurance quote below the underwriter's estimate.

A strong personal balance sheet doesn't change the ratio. That's the point of a DSCR loan, and it cuts both ways.

DSCR on a Business Loan

Business lenders use the same idea with different inputs: the business's cash flow available for debt (often net income plus interest, depreciation and amortization) divided by all of its annual loan payments, sometimes with the owner's personal debts added in a global ratio. The thresholds are similar; our SBA loan requirements page covers what SBA lenders typically look for.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate DSCR?

Divide net operating income by annual debt service. Net operating income is rent minus vacancy, management, maintenance and other operating costs; debt service is a year of loan payments. $23,616 divided by $23,100 is a DSCR of 1.02.

What is a good DSCR for a rental property?

1.20 to 1.25 is commonly treated as comfortable, and 1.00 is the usual floor, where the property just covers its debt. Some lenders go below 1.00 with a larger down payment or reserves, and price for it.

Does DSCR include taxes and insurance?

It depends on the program. Many single-family rental programs use PITI (principal, interest, taxes and insurance), and condos add HOA dues (PITIA). Others use principal and interest only and count taxes and insurance as operating expenses.

How much can I borrow at a 1.25 DSCR?

Divide net operating income by 1.25 to get the most debt service allowed, subtract taxes and insurance if the lender uses PITI, and size the loan to that payment. In the example, $23,616 of income supports about $169,800 at 7% over 30 years.

Is depreciation included in the DSCR calculation?

No. Depreciation is a tax deduction, not a cash expense, so it is left out of net operating income.

What rent do underwriters use if my lease is above market?

Generally the lower of the lease and the appraiser's market rent opinion, so an above-market lease doesn't lift the ratio.
