DSCR Loan vs Conventional Loan: How Each Qualifies You and When Each Wins

One underwrites the property, the other underwrites you. Here is what that does to your rate, down payment, paperwork and how many rentals you can own

Quick answer

The difference is what gets underwritten. A DSCR loan qualifies on the property's rent: if the rent covers the payment 1.00-1.25 times, you qualify with no tax returns or debt-to-income test, usually with 20-25% down and a 620-680+ score, and close in 21-30 days. A conventional loan qualifies on your income and DTI and costs less, typically 1-2 percentage points lower with less origination, but caps you at about ten financed properties. On a $262,500 loan, one point of rate is about $174 a month.

If you are buying a rental or a small investment property, the loan choice usually comes down to DSCR or conventional. They look at completely different things, one at the property and the other at you, and that decides which is cheaper, which is faster and which you can even get. See DSCR loans for the product overview.

What Does Each Loan Qualify On?

  • DSCR loan: the property. The lender checks whether the rent covers the debt. Hit the lender's minimum ratio, commonly 1.00 to 1.25, and the deal qualifies on its own, with no tax returns or W-2s.
  • Conventional loan: you. The lender verifies your income, two years of returns and a debt-to-income ratio that includes every other mortgage you hold, plus credit and reserves.

What Does the Rate Difference Cost?

Take a $350,000 rental with 25% down, a $262,500 loan on a 30-year amortization. Assume the DSCR loan prices at 7.25% and you could get a conventional loan one point lower:

Loan Rate Monthly Per year
DSCR7.25%$1,791$21,489
Conventional6.25%$1,616$19,395
Difference1 point$174$2,094

That is about $2,094 a year, and DSCR origination of 1-2 points adds $2,625-$5,250 up front. That is the price of no income documentation, LLC title, no property cap and a faster close. If none of those matter to you, the conventional loan is the better deal. If they do, the premium is often worth paying, especially when the rental would not fit inside your DTI at all.

What Is the Downside of a DSCR Loan?

Cost, mostly. Expect roughly 1-2 percentage points above conventional investor pricing, 1-2 points of origination, and a prepayment penalty that runs three to five years on a declining scale such as 5-4-3-2-1. Down payments run 20-25%, and lenders want three to six months of PITIA in reserves after closing. If you plan to sell or refinance within two years, price the penalty before you sign; on a $262,500 loan, a 5% first-year penalty is over $13,000. See DSCR prepayment penalties.

When Does a DSCR Loan Win?

Choose DSCR when you are an investor and any of these apply: you are self-employed or write income down (so your returns understate what you earn), you already own several financed properties, you want to close faster with less paperwork, or you are buying in an LLC. As long as the rent comfortably covers the payment, the property carries the loan, and each new loan stands on its own property's cash flow, so your DTI never caps the next purchase. See DSCR loans for an LLC and DSCR rental loans for real estate investors.

When Does a Conventional Loan Win?

Conventional (agency) financing usually wins on rate and total cost when you can document strong income, your DTI is low, you are under the ten-property limit and you are buying in your own name. If you qualify conventionally and do not need DSCR's flexibility, you are paying for options you will not use. Your credit profile matters for both.

How Is DSCR Calculated?

DSCR is the property's income divided by its debt payment. Take a rental bringing in $3,000 a month with $400 of taxes, insurance and other operating costs, leaving $2,600; with a $2,000 principal-and-interest payment, the DSCR is $2,600 ÷ $2,000 = 1.30. Lenders do not all measure it the same way: some divide rent by the full PITIA payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and HOA), and the same property can read 1.28x on one method and 1.05x on the other, so ask which one your lender uses. Under 1.0, the rent does not cover the payment, which sinks the deal or forces a bigger down payment. Run your numbers in the DSCR calculator.

Next Step

If your tax returns do not show your real income or you are scaling a rental portfolio, DSCR is usually the answer; if your finances are clean and well documented, conventional usually costs less. Frequently Asked Questions

Full product overview: DSCR loans.

What is the difference between a DSCR loan and a conventional loan?

A DSCR loan qualifies on the property's rental cash flow, with no tax returns or debt-to-income test. A conventional loan qualifies on your personal income, tax returns and DTI. In short, DSCR underwrites the property and conventional underwrites you.

What is the downside of a DSCR loan?

It costs more: roughly 1-2 percentage points above conventional investor pricing plus 1-2 points of origination. Prepayment penalties over the first three to five years are standard, and you need 20-25% down and three to six months of reserves.

Are all DSCR loans 20% down?

No. Most purchases need 20-25% down, and some lenders go to 15% at higher pricing. Cash-out refinances are usually capped around 70-75% loan-to-value.

Why would someone use a DSCR loan instead of conventional?

To qualify without tax returns, buy in an LLC, keep buying past the conventional limit of about ten financed properties, or close faster. Self-employed investors who write income down use it most.

Is it hard to qualify for a DSCR loan?

Not if the property cash-flows. Most lenders want a DSCR of 1.00-1.25, a credit score of 620-680+, 20-25% down and three to six months of PITIA in reserves. A ratio just short of the minimum is usually fixed with more money down.

When does a conventional mortgage win?

When you can document strong income, have a low DTI, are under the ten-property limit and buy in your own name. Then conventional is typically 1-2 points cheaper, and the DSCR premium buys flexibility you do not need.

Do DSCR loans check my personal income?

No. A DSCR loan qualifies on the property's rental cash flow rather than your tax returns or DTI, which is why self-employed investors use it. Lenders still check credit and reserves.

Can I get a DSCR loan for a short-term rental?

Many DSCR lenders lend on short-term and vacation rentals, using market long-term rent or documented short-term income to compute the ratio. Expect somewhat more conservative terms and higher pricing than a long-term lease.
