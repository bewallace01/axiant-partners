Vending Machine Lease vs Loan vs Pay-Later Checkout

Four ways to pay for vending machines over time, what each one really costs, and which fits a route that moves machines between locations

The Four Options Side by Side

OptionWho owns itEnd of termFits best
Equipment loanYou, from day one (lender holds a lien)Lien releasedMachines you will keep
$1-buyout leaseLessor until the $1 buyoutYou own itBuying, with lease paperwork
FMV leaseLessorReturn, renew or buy at market valueUnits you may swap
Pay-later at checkoutDepends on the contractRead the contractSmall, urgent purchases

The first two are both, in substance, ways of buying the machine. The third is renting it. The fourth can be either, and the paperwork - not the checkout button - decides which.

Why Vending Is Different From Most Equipment

Most equipment stays where it is installed. Vending machines move. A machine that underperforms in one break room gets pulled and placed somewhere else, and a lost contract can leave several units in your warehouse at once. That changes the lease-or-buy decision in two ways:

  • Owning gives you freedom to redeploy without asking anyone. With a loan, you can move a machine anywhere, sell it or cannibalise it for parts once paid off.
  • An FMV lease gives you a way out of a model you no longer want - an older cash-only unit when your locations want card readers, for example - without having to sell it yourself.

Check the location clause in any lease. Some restrict where the equipment can be kept or require notice before it moves, which can be a problem for a route operator.

Pay-Later and Lease-to-Own at Checkout

Machine suppliers' websites increasingly offer instant pay-later or lease-to-own approval at checkout. For a single machine needed this week, the speed is real. The things to check before you click:

  • Is it a loan or a lease? A lease-to-own contract usually means you do not own the machine until the final payment or a buyout.
  • Total of payments. Multiply the payment by the number of payments and add any buyout. Compare that with the cash price.
  • Personal or business? Some checkout products are consumer credit. Business equipment belongs on business credit.
  • Early payoff. Whether paying it off early saves anything.

None of this makes checkout financing wrong. It makes it something to price against an equipment loan rather than accept by default.

Lay every offer out this way. The cheapest option is the one with the lowest bottom line for the outcome you want - ownership or flexibility - not the lowest monthly figure.

Taxes: Who Gets the Deduction

With a loan or a $1-buyout lease, you are generally treated as the owner, so you depreciate the machine and may be able to use Section 179 in the year it is placed in service; financed equipment qualifies the same as equipment bought with cash. With a true FMV lease, the lessor owns it and your lease payments are generally deductible as a business expense instead.

Which treatment is better depends on your tax position that year. IRS Publication 946 sets out the rules; confirm with your accountant before you choose a structure for tax reasons.

Which One to Pick

  • Stable placements, long life ahead: equipment loan.
  • Want to own, lessor offers better terms: $1-buyout lease.
  • Testing new locations or new machine types: FMV lease.
  • One machine, needed now, small amount: checkout offer, after comparing total cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to lease or buy vending machines?

Buy, with a loan or a $1-buyout lease, for machines you will keep in stable locations. Use an FMV lease when you want the option to return or upgrade machines that underperform. Compare every option on total of payments plus buyout.

What is lease-to-own for vending machines?

A lease where you make payments over a term and own the machine after the final payment or a buyout. Until then the lessor owns it. Check the total of payments and the buyout before signing.

Are pay-later offers at checkout a good way to buy vending machines?

They are fast and can suit a single, urgent purchase. They can also cost more in total than an equipment loan, so compare the total of payments with the cash price and with a loan offer first.

Can I move a leased vending machine to a new location?

Usually, but check the lease. Some leases restrict where equipment can be kept or require notice before it is moved.

Can I take Section 179 on financed vending machines?

Generally yes, if you are treated as the owner - a loan or $1-buyout lease - and the machine is placed in service that year. With a true FMV lease the lessor owns the machine. Confirm with your accountant.
