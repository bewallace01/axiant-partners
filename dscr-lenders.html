Best DSCR Lenders in 2026 — and How to Actually Compare Them

Quick Answer: Which DSCR Lender Is Best?

There is no single best DSCR lender, and any list claiming otherwise is usually published by one of them. The right lender is decided by your deal — specifically your DSCR, whether the property is a short-term rental, whether you need sub-1.0 financing, your loan size, and how long you intend to hold. A borrower at 1.35x on a long-term lease has a dozen good options and should shop on price. A borrower at 0.95x on a short-term rental has perhaps three, and should shop on who will actually close.

Start by knowing your number. Run the property through the DSCR calculator, then use the criteria below to narrow the field.

The Seven Criteria That Actually Separate DSCR Lenders

Rate is the obvious comparison and rarely the deciding one. These matter more:

  • Minimum DSCR. Some lenders stop at 1.25, others go to 1.00, and a smaller group writes no-ratio deals below 1.00. This single number eliminates most of the field for a marginal property.
  • Maximum LTV. Typically 75–80% on purchase and 70–75% on cash-out. A five-point difference changes how much cash you leave in the deal.
  • Short-term rental income. Many lenders underwrite long-term market rent only, ignoring what an STR actually earns. Others accept documented STR revenue. If you are buying a vacation rental, this question alone determines your lender list.
  • Prepayment structure. 5-4-3-2-1 and 3-2-1 are both common, and some lenders sell a buy-down. If you plan to refinance or sell inside two years, this outweighs a quarter-point on rate.
  • Loan minimum and maximum. Minimums commonly sit around $75,000–$100,000, which quietly excludes lower-priced markets. Maximums matter at the other end.
  • Entity titling and vesting. Nearly all DSCR lenders allow an LLC, but requirements around operating agreements, guarantors, and multi-member entities differ.
  • Seasoning rules on cash-out. How long you must own the property before pulling equity, and whether they lend on appraised value or purchase price. This is the rule that decides most BRRRR refinances.

Questions to Ask Before You Apply

  • What is your minimum DSCR, and do you have a no-ratio program?
  • Do you underwrite short-term rental income, or long-term market rent only?
  • What is the prepayment structure, and will you buy it down?
  • What is your seasoning requirement for cash-out, and do you use appraised value or purchase price?
  • Do you use principal and interest, or PITIA, in the DSCR calculation? This changes the ratio materially on the same property.
  • What is your minimum loan amount, and do you lend in this state?
  • What could cause this file to fall apart, and when would we know?

The PITIA question is the one most borrowers skip. A lender using principal and interest will quote a higher ratio on the identical property than one using PITIA — the same deal can read as 1.28x or 1.05x depending on the convention. Comparing quotes without asking makes the comparison meaningless.

Red Flags

A rate quoted before anyone has seen the property.

DSCR pricing depends on the ratio, LTV, credit, and property type. A number offered without those is marketing.

Large non-refundable upfront fees.

A third-party deposit covering appraisal is normal. A substantial “application fee” before any term sheet is not.

Vagueness about the prepayment penalty.

It should be stated plainly and early.

“Guaranteed approval.”

No lender can guarantee an approval before the appraisal and rent schedule come back.

Broker or Direct?

Going direct is often the right answer, and worth saying on a page like this. If your DSCR is comfortably above 1.25, the property is a standard long-term rental, and you already have a lender relationship, apply directly and keep it simple.

A broker earns their place when the deal is not standard: a ratio below 1.10, short-term rental income, a non-warrantable condo, a multi-member LLC, a cash-out inside the seasoning window, or a market where your usual lender does not operate. In those cases the value is knowing which of the lenders above is currently writing that exact deal — which changes far more often than any published list suggests.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who are the best DSCR lenders?

There is no single best DSCR lender, and most lists ranking one are published by that lender. Active names in the market include Visio Lending, Kiavi, Park Place Finance, Lima One Capital, RCN Capital, Velocity Commercial Capital, Angel Oak, Truss Financial and Easy Street Capital. Which is best depends on your DSCR, whether the property is a short-term rental, your loan size, and how long you plan to hold.

What is the minimum DSCR lenders will accept?

It varies by lender, which is why it is the first question to ask. Some stop at 1.25, many go to 1.00, and a smaller group writes no-ratio deals below 1.00 in exchange for a larger down payment and a higher rate. For a marginal property this single number eliminates most of the field.

Do DSCR lenders accept short-term rental income?

Some do and many do not. A large share of lenders underwrite long-term market rent from the appraiser's rent schedule regardless of what the property actually earns on a nightly platform. If you are buying a vacation rental, ask this before anything else, because it determines your entire lender list.

How do I compare DSCR loan quotes fairly?

Ask every lender whether they calculate DSCR on principal and interest or on PITIA. The same property can read as 1.28x under one convention and 1.05x under the other, so quotes are not comparable until you know. Then compare minimum DSCR, maximum LTV, prepayment structure, seasoning rules, and loan minimums, not just rate.

Can you get a DSCR loan through a broker?

Yes, and it helps when the deal is not standard: a ratio below 1.10, short-term rental income, a non-warrantable condo, a multi-member LLC, or a cash-out inside the seasoning window. If your ratio is comfortably above 1.25 on a standard long-term rental and you already have a lender relationship, going direct is usually simpler.
