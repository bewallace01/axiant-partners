Survey, documentation, insurance and trade history - what a marine lender asks for and in what order

What a Marine Lender Is Underwriting

Two things at once: a vessel that must be identifiable, insurable and saleable, and a trade that must generate enough to service the loan.

That dual test is why marine files carry more documentation than a straightforward equipment loan. A machine sits in a yard and is either there or not. A vessel moves, may be federally documented or state-titled, carries permits that may or may not transfer, and needs cover written for commercial operation rather than recreation.

None of it is difficult. It is simply more moving parts, and the parts run on other people's timetables.

Documentation Against State Title

How a vessel is registered decides how a lender secures against it, and the two routes are genuinely different.

Federally documented vessels carry a Certificate of Documentation issued through the National Vessel Documentation Center, and a lender records a preferred ship mortgage against it. That is the stronger position and it is why documentation is often preferred on larger commercial vessels.

State-titled vessels work more like a titled vehicle, with the lien recorded at state level.

Which applies affects both the security and the timetable. Documentation runs on its own schedule and cannot usefully be hurried, so establish early which route the vessel is on.

Why Insurance Stalls More Deals Than It Should

It reads as closing admin and it is a frequent cause of delay.

Cover for commercial operation is materially different from recreational cover — different exposures, different limits, and protection-and-indemnity cover that a recreational policy does not include. A quote obtained on the wrong basis has to be redone, and specialist marine cover is not always quick to place.

Tell your broker the actual trade at the outset: passengers carried, waters worked, crew numbers, whether the vessel operates at night or offshore. A policy issued against a vague description is a policy that may not respond.

Preparing the File

  • Book the survey early. It is the step most likely to change the deal, and finding that in week one is an adjustment rather than a collapse.
  • Run a lien search before making an offer.
  • Supply a full trading year, not the good months — seasonality is expected and reads badly when it looks concealed.
  • Document permits and certification, and establish whether they transfer.
  • Get commercial insurance quoted on the real trade.

Sequence matters more than speed. The survey and the lien search are the two steps that can end a deal outright, and both run on someone else's calendar rather than yours. Everything else — accounts, permits, insurance binders — can be assembled in parallel while you wait. Owners who reverse that order spend three weeks perfecting a financial package for a vessel the survey then disqualifies.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do commercial vessel lenders require?

An accredited condition and valuation survey, clear documentation or state title with every prior lien released, commercial hull and P&I insurance with the lender named, and evidence that the trade generates the revenue — usually a full trading year.

What is a preferred ship mortgage?

The lien a lender records against a federally documented vessel through the National Vessel Documentation Center. It is a stronger security position than a state-level filing, which is part of why documentation is often preferred on larger commercial vessels.

Why does insurance cause delays?

Because commercial cover is materially different from recreational cover, and a quote obtained on the wrong basis has to be redone. Tell your broker the real trade at the outset — passengers, waters, crew, night or offshore operation.

Should I supply my whole trading year?

Yes. Marine lenders expect seasonality and read the year as a whole. Supplying only the strong months does not disguise the shape, it makes the file look as though something is being concealed.

What is most likely to change the deal?

The survey. It is where undisclosed structural work, corrosion or machinery condition surfaces, and it can move both the valuation and the price. Booking it early turns that into an adjustment rather than a collapse.
