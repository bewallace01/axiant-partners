Quick answer

Pennsylvania small business loans come in five main types: SBA 7(a) loans up to $5 million, SBA 504 loans for owner-occupied buildings and equipment with as little as 10% down, equipment financing secured by the machine, and working capital loans and lines of credit that often decide in 24-48 hours. For working capital, most lenders want 6+ months in business, about $10,000 a month in revenue and a 550+ credit score; SBA and bank loans want 650-680+ and take 30-60+ days. Two SBA district offices, Eastern (Philadelphia) and Western (Pittsburgh), cover the state.

What do Pennsylvania lenders look for?

Requirements vary by program, but most Pennsylvania businesses qualify for something with these basics:

  • 6+ months in business. Newer businesses with strong personal credit can still qualify for some programs. SBA lenders want 2+ years.
  • About $10,000+ in monthly revenue. It shows the lender you can carry a payment.
  • 550+ credit score for most working capital. SBA and bank loans want 650-680+, and 600+ opens most equipment lenders.
  • 3-6 months of bank statements. SBA and bank loans add tax returns and financial statements.

Rebuilding your credit? There are still business loans for bad credit, usually at a higher rate and a shorter term.

Do banks offer small business loans in Pennsylvania?

Yes, and they're the cheapest source when you qualify. Banks make conventional term loans and most SBA 7(a) and 504 loans, but they want 650-680+ credit, 2+ years in business and full financials, and an SBA loan takes 30-60+ days. If your file is thinner or the need is urgent, online and alternative lenders decide faster on bank statements alone and charge more for it. Sending one application lets you see both sides before you choose.

SBA loans in Pennsylvania

Two SBA district offices cover the state: Eastern Pennsylvania (Philadelphia) and Western Pennsylvania (Pittsburgh). Both have deep lender networks, so where you are in the state doesn't limit your options.

  • SBA 7(a): up to $5 million for working capital, acquisitions, refinancing and general uses.
  • SBA 504: long-term, fixed-rate financing for owner-occupied real estate and equipment, with as little as 10% down.
  • SBA microloans: smaller amounts for early-stage and underserved businesses.

Financing by Pennsylvania industry

Pennsylvania has two financing personalities. The "eds and meds" economy in Philadelphia and Pittsburgh is steady enough for long, fixed payments. Marcellus gas country is strong but cyclical, and fixed payments can hurt in a down year. Most other businesses sit somewhere between.

Healthcare and universities

Health systems and universities anchor Philadelphia and Pittsburgh with steady, large-scale demand. Best fits: medical practice financing, equipment, term loans.

Energy and natural gas

The Marcellus Shale drives a large energy-services sector with revenue that swings. Best fits: equipment financing, working capital, revenue-based financing.

Manufacturing

From the steel heritage to Pittsburgh's robotics and advanced manufacturing. Best fits: manufacturing financing, equipment, term loans.

Agriculture

A leader in dairy and mushrooms, with a dense network of family farms around Lancaster. Best fits: agriculture financing, equipment, seasonal lines of credit.

Logistics and trade

The Lehigh Valley and central PA sit between the Midwest and the Northeast corridor, which makes the state a freight route. Best fits: trucking, warehousing, factoring.

Construction and trades

Steady commercial and residential work across the metros and growing suburbs. Best fits: construction financing, lines of credit, equipment.

Match the payment to how steady your revenue is

A medical practice can comfortably carry a fixed, long-term SBA loan because its revenue is predictable. A gas-services contractor probably shouldn't, because a down cycle can turn the same fixed payment into a problem. Contractors in gas country do better with revolving credit they draw on as work comes in, or revenue-based financing where the payment moves with collections.

Pittsburgh's shift from steel to robotics, AI, healthcare and advanced manufacturing means more equipment-heavy, growth-stage borrowers, and Philadelphia's life-sciences corridor adds more on the eastern side. For those firms, equipment financing and growth term loans or lines of credit fit better than anything rigid. Farms in the southeast run on seasonal cash, so a line of credit that covers inputs until harvest usually beats a lump sum.

Pennsylvania small business loan questions

How do I get a small business loan in Pennsylvania?

Match the loan to what the money is for, then apply once so several lenders can look at the same file. For working capital most lenders want 6+ months in business, about $10,000 a month in revenue, a 550+ credit score and 3-6 months of bank statements. SBA and bank loans want 650-680+ credit and 2+ years, and take 30-60+ days.

Do banks in Pennsylvania offer small business loans?

Yes. Banks make conventional term loans and most SBA 7(a) and 504 loans, and they offer the lowest rates, but they want stronger files: 650-680+ credit, 2+ years in business and full financials, with 30-60+ days to close. Online and alternative lenders approve thinner files faster, usually at a higher cost.

Which SBA district offices serve Pennsylvania?

Two: Eastern Pennsylvania, near Philadelphia, and Western Pennsylvania, in Pittsburgh. Together they cover the whole state, so you can get SBA 7(a), 504 and microloans wherever the business is located.

How much would a $50,000 business loan cost?

At an illustrative 15% APR over 36 months, $50,000 costs about $1,733 a month and about $12,398 in total interest. A longer term or a lower rate brings the payment down; a short-term working capital loan costs more. Run your own numbers in the business loan calculator.

Are there business loans for Pennsylvania healthcare practices?

Yes. Practices and clinics use equipment financing for medical equipment, term loans for expansion, and lines of credit for payroll while insurance receivables come in. Steady healthcare revenue also supports long-term SBA loans for buying a building.

Is there financing for Pennsylvania natural gas businesses?

Yes. Marcellus Shale energy-services revenue is strong but cyclical, so equipment financing for rigs and trucks, revolving working capital lines, and revenue-based financing tend to fit better than a large fixed payment.
