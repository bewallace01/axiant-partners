Real Estate Investing Loans: DSCR, Hard Money, BRRRR and Portfolio Options

Loans for rentals, flips, BRRRR deals, portfolios and small multifamily, with what each lender wants to see.

Quick answer

Real estate investing loans are underwritten mostly on the property, not your W-2. For rentals, a DSCR loan usually needs 20-25% down, 620-680+ credit and a ratio of 1.00-1.25, prices about 1-2 points above conventional investment loans, and closes in 21-30 days. For flips, a hard money loan covers up to 80% of the purchase and 100% of the rehab, capped near 70-75% of after-repair value, over 6-18 months. A $262,500 DSCR loan at 7.25% over 30 years is about $1,791 a month.

Investor loans work differently from the mortgage on your house. The lender cares most about what the property earns or will be worth after the rehab, so you can qualify without showing W-2 income, close in an LLC, and keep buying past the limits a conventional lender puts on you. The trade is a higher rate. Pick the loan by strategy: what you plan to do with the property decides which lender wants the deal.

DSCR loans for rental properties

A DSCR loan qualifies on the property's rental income. The lender compares the rent with the full monthly payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any HOA); 1.00 means the rent just covers it, and 1.25 or better earns the best pricing. What lenders typically want:

  • Down payment: 20-25% on a purchase, 15% with some lenders at a higher rate; cash-out refinances are usually capped at 70-75% loan-to-value.
  • Credit: 620-680+ to qualify, with the best pricing around 720+.
  • Reserves: 3-6 months of the payment, more on a cash-out.
  • Cost: about 1-2 points above conventional investment-property rates, plus 1-2 points of origination, and usually a 5-4-3-2-1 or 3-2-1 prepayment penalty.
  • Size and term: $75K-$3M is typical, up to $5M with a strong file; 30-year fixed is common, with 5/1, 7/1 and 10/1 ARMs available.

Example: a $350,000 rental with 25% down is a $262,500 loan. At 7.25% over 30 years, principal and interest come to about $1,791 a month, so the rent needs to cover that plus taxes, insurance and HOA to reach a 1.00 ratio. Check your own numbers in the DSCR calculator, and see the full program on DSCR loans. Specialty lenders here include Visio Lending, Lima One Capital, Kiavi, Easy Street Capital, Velocity Mortgage Capital and RCN Capital; retail banks rarely write DSCR loans.

Can an LLC get a loan for real estate?

Yes. DSCR lenders close in an LLC's name all the time, which is one of the main reasons investors use them instead of a conventional mortgage. Have the operating agreement and EIN ready on day one; a missing entity document is one of the most common causes of a slipped closing. DSCR lenders also put no cap on how many financed properties you hold, unlike conventional programs.

Hard money for fix-and-flip

A hard money lender lends against the deal: the purchase price, the rehab budget and the after-repair value (ARV). The property is the collateral, so your credit matters less than the spread between cost and ARV, though many programs still look for a mid-600s score.

  • Leverage: up to 80% of the purchase and 100% of the rehab, up to about 90% of total cost, and capped at roughly 70-75% of ARV, whichever is lower.
  • Cost: rates in the low-to-mid teens, usually interest-only, plus 1-3 points at closing.
  • Term: 6-18 months, sometimes 24.
  • Draws: rehab money is released in 3-5 draws against contractor invoices and inspections.
  • Exit: the lender wants a sale or refinance plan in writing at origination.

Kiavi, Lima One Capital, RCN Capital, Anchor Loans and Visio Lending are active here, along with many local hard money lenders who sometimes price well because they know the market. More on structure and requirements: fix-and-flip loans.

BRRRR: buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat

  1. Buy a distressed property with a hard money loan.
  2. Rehab it, usually over 60-90 days, funded by draws.
  3. Rent it at market rent.
  4. Refinance into a DSCR loan on the new appraised value, which now reflects the rehab, and pull your cash back out.
  5. Repeat with the cash you recovered.

It works when the value added by the rehab covers the purchase, rehab, holding and closing costs at the refinance. Most BRRRR investors plan for a 70-75% loan-to-value on the DSCR refinance.

How do you avoid 20% down on an investment property?

You usually can't avoid putting money in, but you can put in less or get it back sooner. Some DSCR lenders accept 15% down at a higher rate. A fix-and-flip loan can fund up to 80% of the purchase and all of the rehab. And BRRRR lets you recover much of your cash at the refinance if the rehab adds real value. Each of these costs more in rate or fees than a 25%-down DSCR loan, so price both before you choose.

Portfolio (blanket) loans

Once you own five or more rentals, separate loans get hard to manage. A portfolio loan puts 5-25+ properties on one note: one closing and one payment, all properties cross-collateralized, typically 5-10 year terms with a balloon, and rates similar to or slightly above individual conventional loans. Above about $2M in total, some are non-recourse. Lima One Capital, Visio Lending, CoreVest, Velocity Mortgage Capital and RCN Capital all offer them.

Commercial multifamily (5+ units)

At five units a property moves to commercial financing:

  • Fannie Mae Small Loans ($1M-$9M): 65-80% LTV, 5-10 year terms, 30-year amortization, often non-recourse.
  • Freddie Mac Small Balance Loans: similar, and sometimes more aggressive in select markets.
  • CMBS ($2M+): non-recourse, 10-year fixed is common.
  • Bank balance-sheet loans: more flexible underwriting, usually with recourse.

Walker & Dunlop, Berkadia, Marcus & Millichap Capital and Newmark arrange much of this financing. For owner-occupied and other commercial property, see commercial real estate loans.

Short-term rental financing

Airbnb and VRBO properties need lenders that underwrite projected short-term rental income (using AirDNA or Mashvisor data) instead of long-term rent comps. Underwriting is stricter because cities can restrict STRs and income swings by season. Visio Lending, Easy Street Capital and RCN Capital have STR programs.

Real estate investing loan FAQs

What loans can I get for real estate investing?

For rentals, a DSCR loan that qualifies on the property's rent, or a conventional investment-property loan if your personal income and property count allow it. For flips and the first half of a BRRRR, a hard money or fix-and-flip loan. Past five or so properties, a portfolio (blanket) loan, and for 5+ unit buildings, commercial multifamily financing.

Can an LLC get a loan for real estate?

Yes. DSCR lenders routinely close in the name of an LLC, and that is one of the main reasons investors use them. Have the operating agreement and EIN ready at application, because missing entity documents are a common cause of delay.

How do I avoid a 20% down payment on an investment property?

Some DSCR lenders go to 15% down at higher pricing. On a flip, a fix-and-flip loan can cover up to 80% of the purchase and 100% of the rehab, up to about 90% of total cost. A BRRRR refinance can also pull most of your cash back out, since DSCR cash-out refinances typically go to 70-75% of the new appraised value.

What is a DSCR loan?

A rental property loan underwritten on the property's income instead of your W-2s or tax returns. Most lenders want a debt service coverage ratio of 1.00-1.25, 20-25% down, 620-680+ credit and 3-6 months of payments in reserve. Rates run about 1-2 points above conventional investment-property pricing.

How does fix-and-flip financing work?

A hard money or fix-and-flip lender funds up to 80% of the purchase and 100% of the rehab, released in draws as work is done, with the loan capped at about 70-75% of after-repair value. Terms run 6-18 months with 1-3 points at closing. You exit by selling or by refinancing into a DSCR loan.

What is BRRRR?

Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. You buy and renovate with a short-term hard money loan, lease the property, refinance into a DSCR or conventional rental loan based on the new appraised value, and use the cash you pull out for the next deal.

How hard is it to get a commercial real estate loan?

Harder than a 1-4 unit rental loan. Commercial lenders typically lend 65-80% of value, want a debt service coverage ratio of about 1.20-1.35, and expect 25-30% or more down on investment property. The rent roll and the property's net operating income carry most of the decision.
