How the MCA APR Calculation Works

An MCA is quoted as a factor rate, not an APR — which is exactly why its true cost is so easy to underestimate. The calculator above converts your factor rate and term into an annualized rate so you can compare it honestly to a term loan or line of credit:

  1. Total payback = amount funded × factor rate
  2. Dollar cost = total payback − amount funded
  3. Cost per dollar = dollar cost ÷ amount funded
  4. Effective APR ≈ cost per dollar × (12 ÷ term in months)

Because an MCA is repaid fast through daily or weekly debits, annualizing a fixed cost over a short term turns a modest-looking factor into a high effective APR — and paying off faster usually raises it, since the factor doesn't shrink. MCA APR Calculator FAQs

How does the MCA APR calculator work?

Enter the amount funded, factor rate, and term in months. It computes total payback (funded × factor), the dollar cost, the cost per dollar, and annualizes that over your term to estimate the effective APR — plus the monthly and daily payment so you see the cash-flow impact.

Is the calculated APR exact?

It's a clear, conservative estimate — enough to see the order of magnitude. A precise APR that accounts for the declining balance is typically even higher, so treat the result as a floor on the real effective APR.

Why is the APR so much higher than the factor rate?

A factor rate ignores time; an APR annualizes the cost over how long you repay. Because an MCA is repaid quickly, annualizing a fixed dollar cost over a short term produces a high APR — and a shorter term can raise it further.

What should I do if my MCA's APR is very high?

If you can qualify, refinancing into a term loan or line of credit usually lowers both cost and cash drain. Consolidating stacked advances is another option. Apply once to compare refinance and consolidation offers.
