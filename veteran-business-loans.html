Quick Answer: Are There Business Loans for Veterans?

Veteran business loans are standard business loans with veteran benefits on top: SBA 7(a) (up to $5 million), SBA Express (up to $500,000), SBA microloans (up to $50,000) and private lenders. The VA itself does not make business loans; a VA home loan is for housing. The SBA reduces or waives certain fees for veteran-owned businesses (eligible firms pay no upfront guaranty fee on SBA Express), MREIDL helps when an owner or key employee is called to active duty, and VOSB/SDVOSB certification opens set-aside federal contracts. Lenders still underwrite credit and cash flow; many SBA lenders look for roughly 650-680+ credit.

Does the VA Give Business Loans?

No. The Department of Veterans Affairs does not lend to businesses. A VA home loan is for housing, not a business, so searches for a "VA business loan" or "VA small business loan" usually lead to the SBA, which backs the programs veterans actually use, plus private lenders. Being a veteran doesn't change the approval standards, but it can lower the cost of an SBA loan and open free counseling through Veterans Business Outreach Centers and Boots to Business.

If you're thinking of borrowing against a home bought with a VA loan to fund a business, ask the lender first and weigh what happens to your family if the business struggles.

SBA Loans for Veterans

Most small business loans for veterans are SBA loans. The SBA guarantees part of the loan, so banks can offer longer terms; veteran status adds fee relief on eligible loans rather than a separate loan type.

Can a veteran get a $100,000 SBA loan? Yes. $100,000 fits inside SBA 7(a) and SBA Express, but it's too large for a microloan, which tops out at $50,000. An existing business still needs the credit and cash flow to repay it; see what a $100,000 business loan takes.

The 20% rule: on SBA loans, every owner with 20% or more of the business personally guarantees the loan and submits a personal financial statement (SBA Form 413). See documents needed for an SBA loan.

SBA loans typically take 30-60+ days, so they're not for emergencies. Full program details are on SBA loans and the veterans SBA and MREIDL guide.

Startup, Franchise & Disabled Veteran Loans

Startup business loans for veterans. No federal program funds a veteran's startup just for having served, and brand-new businesses are hard to place. The realistic routes are an SBA microloan (up to $50,000) or an SBA 7(a) loan with at least 10% of the total project cost in equity from you, since a business operating one year or less counts as a start-up. Our guide to startup business loans for veterans walks through the options and a worked example.

Franchise loans for veterans. SBA 7(a) is the usual route to buy a franchise, and lenders like a known brand. The franchise must be listed in the SBA Franchise Directory, and you'll still bring at least 10% equity. See SBA loans to buy a franchise.

SBA loans for disabled veterans. Service-disabled veterans use the same SBA programs and veteran fee relief as other veterans; there's no separate SBA loan for them. SDVOSB certification adds access to set-aside federal contracts, and disability or retirement income can help show a lender you can get through the first year. Ask each lender how it counts that income.

What Do Lenders Require From a Veteran-Owned Business?

Veteran programs can lower costs or open contracts, but standard underwriting still applies. Most lenders look for:

  • 6+ months in business: newer businesses have options too (see startup financing)
  • ~$10,000+ in monthly revenue: consistent deposits matter most
  • 550+ credit: higher scores get better rates and open SBA options
  • 3-6 months of bank statements: the core document

Veteran Business Loan FAQs

Are there business loans specifically for veterans?

Veterans access all standard financing plus veteran-focused programs—SBA fee relief on eligible loans, the MREIDL program, and VOSB/SDVOSB certification for set-aside contracts. No single veterans-only loan, but real support. See our MREIDL guide.

What is the SBA Veterans Advantage program?

SBA efforts that reduce or waive certain guaranty fees for veteran-owned businesses on eligible loans, lowering the cost of SBA financing. Terms change, so confirm current relief when you apply.

What is the MREIDL loan?

The Military Reservist Economic Injury Disaster Loan helps a small business cover operating costs it can't meet because an essential owner or employee was called to active duty—a specific SBA program. Read more.

What is VOSB or SDVOSB certification?

Veteran-Owned and Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business certifications let you compete for set-aside government contracts. They don't directly grant a loan, but the contract revenue they win strengthens a loan application.

Does the VA give business loans?

No. The Department of Veterans Affairs does not make business loans; a VA home loan is for housing, not a business. Veterans borrow for a business through SBA programs and private lenders, where veteran status can lower fees but doesn't change approval standards.

What is the 20% rule for SBA loans?

Every owner with 20% or more of the business personally guarantees the SBA loan and submits a personal financial statement (SBA Form 413). It applies to veteran owners the same as anyone else.

Can I get a $100,000 SBA loan as a veteran?

Yes. $100,000 fits inside SBA 7(a) (up to $5 million) and SBA Express (up to $500,000), where eligible veteran-owned firms pay no upfront guaranty fee. A microloan tops out at $50,000. Approval still depends on credit and cash flow.

Are there startup business loans for veterans?

Not as a separate federal program. Veterans starting out usually use an SBA microloan (up to $50,000) or an SBA 7(a) loan with at least 10% equity. See startup business loans for veterans.

Are there SBA loans for disabled veterans?

There's no separate SBA loan for disabled veterans. Service-disabled veterans use the same SBA programs and veteran fee relief, and SDVOSB certification opens set-aside federal contracts.

Can veterans get a franchise loan?

Yes. SBA 7(a) is the usual route for buying a franchise. The brand must be listed in the SBA Franchise Directory, and you bring at least 10% equity. See SBA loans to buy a franchise.

How do veterans qualify for a business loan?

On the business: revenue, time in business, credit, and cash flow. Veteran programs can lower costs or open contracts, but standard underwriting applies. Apply to see your options.
