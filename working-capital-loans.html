What Is a Working Capital Loan?

A working capital loan is a business financing solution designed to cover operational expenses and day-to-day business needs. Unlike equipment or real estate financing, working capital is used for short-term operational needs.

  • Payroll
  • Inventory purchases
  • Vendor payments
  • Marketing initiatives
  • Contract fulfillment
  • Seasonal revenue fluctuations
  • Short-term growth opportunities
  • Purchasing owner-occupied real estate
  • Large fixed asset acquisitions
  • Special projects or needs

Types of Working Capital Financing

Working capital can be structured in different ways depending on your needs.

Business Line of Credit

A revolving credit facility that allows you to draw funds as needed.

  • Draw funds as needed
  • Pay interest only on the amount used
  • Funds become available again after repayment

Designed for: All businesses, companies with fluctuating revenue, ongoing operational needs.

SBA Working Capital Loan

SBA loans for longer-term working capital needs with favorable terms.

  • Competitive interest rates
  • Longer repayment terms
  • Structured amortizations

Designed for: All profitable businesses, long-term capital needs, debt refinancing.

Short-Term Working Capital Term Loan

A fixed-amount loan with a set repayment schedule.

  • Fixed loan amount
  • Fixed repayment schedule
  • Rapid funding (from 48+ hours)

Designed for: Businesses needing a lump sum for specific short-term needs.

Who Qualifies for a Working Capital Loan?

Typical qualifications for most programs include:

  • 2+ years in business
  • Consistent revenue history
  • 680+ credit score
  • U.S.-based operations
  • Positive cash flow (EBITDA)
  • Minimum $250,000 to $500,000 annual revenue (varies by program)

Lenders may require digital credit links and collateral depending on the structure.

Common Uses for Working Capital Financing

Working capital financing typically supports:

  • Covering payroll
  • Purchasing inventory or securing bulk discounts
  • Managing receivables and filling cash flow gaps
  • Upgrading marketing campaigns
  • Hiring new contractors
  • Mitigating revenue dips
  • Addressing unexpected operating expenses

Working Capital Loan vs. Business Line of Credit

Criteria Working Capital Loan Business Line of Credit
Structure Fixed lump sum, fixed term Revolving credit limit
Repayment Fixed schedule, pay on full amount Interest only on amounts drawn, repay and reuse
Funding Single disbursement Draw as needed
Best For Specific project or one-time need Ongoing, fluctuating operational needs

What Determines Rates & Terms?

Several factors influence pricing and structure:

  • Industry & consistency: Stable industries typically receive better terms
  • Time in business: Longer track record often improves eligibility
  • Credit score: Higher scores unlock lower rates
  • Loan amount: Larger facilities may have different structuring
  • Existing debt obligations: Debt service capacity is evaluated
  • Secured vs. unsecured: Collateral can improve terms

We help you access competitive rates and structure a solution tailored to your business.

When Is a Working Capital Loan Not Ideal?

Working capital financing may not be the best fit for:

  • Startup businesses (less than 2 years in business)
  • Poor credit history-other options may be more appropriate
  • Major equipment or real estate purchases-dedicated equipment or SBA financing may offer better terms
  • Business acquisition-acquisition financing is typically structured differently

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly can I receive working capital?

Depending on the program, funding can occur within 48+ hours for short-term loans. SBA working capital loans typically take 30-60+ days. Lines of credit may fund within 1-2 weeks once approved.

What is the minimum loan amount?

Most programs start at $10,000. Some lenders have higher minimums. We match you with programs that fit your needs.

Is collateral required?

Unsecured working capital is available for qualified businesses. Secured options may offer better rates and higher limits. Requirements vary by lender and structure.

Can startups qualify?

Most working capital programs require 2+ years in business. Startups may find alternatives such as SBA microloans or other products designed for newer businesses.
