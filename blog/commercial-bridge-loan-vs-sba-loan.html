Commercial Bridge Loan vs SBA Loan: What's the Difference?

Compare term length, cost, closing speed, and when to use each

← Back to Commercial Bridge Loans Blog

March 3, 2026 - Axiant Partners

When financing commercial property, you may decide between a commercial bridge loan and an SBA-backed loan (504 or 7(a)). Both finance commercial transactions but serve different purposes. The key difference: bridge loans are short-term transitional financing; SBA loans are long-term permanent financing.

Quick Comparison

Feature Bridge Loan SBA Loan
Term Length 6-24 months 10-25 years
Interest Structure Interest only Amortizing
Closing Timeline 7-21 days 45-90 days
Down Payment Equity based (25-40% typical) 10-20% typical
LTV (Loan-to-Value) 60-75% Up to 90% effective (504 structure)
Best For Speed & transition Long-term ownership

Explore commercial bridge loan options for transitional financing.

When a Bridge Loan Is Better

A commercial bridge loan may be the right choice when:

  • You need to close quickly
  • SBA approval will take too long
  • Property is not yet stabilized
  • You plan to refinance within 6-24 months
  • You are solving a short-term maturity issue

Bridge loans prioritize speed and flexibility.

When an SBA Loan Is Better

An SBA loan may be the right choice when:

  • You want long-term ownership
  • You want lower interest cost
  • You prefer 20-25 year amortization
  • You qualify for SBA underwriting
  • Timing is flexible

SBA loans are designed for permanent financing.

Cost Comparison

Bridge Loans:

  • Higher interest rates
  • Origination points
  • Short-term structure
  • Balloon payment

SBA Loans:

  • Lower long-term cost
  • Longer amortization
  • More documentation
  • Slower closing

Bridge loans prioritize speed. SBA prioritizes affordability.

Strategy Many Borrowers Use

A common approach:

  1. Close quickly with a bridge loan
  2. Refinance into SBA 504 or 7(a)
  3. Lock in long-term fixed financing

Bridge loans are often transitional steps toward SBA financing.

Risk Considerations

Bridge loan risk:

  • Short maturity
  • Balloon payment
  • Higher rate

SBA loan risk:

  • Slower approval
  • Strict documentation
  • Eligibility guidelines

Choosing structure depends on timeline and long-term plan.

Minimum Loan Amount

Both bridge and SBA structures typically start at $10,000 and scale upward based on qualification.

Final Thoughts

Commercial bridge loans and SBA loans serve different purposes:

Bridge loans are built for:

  • Speed
  • Transitional financing
  • Short-term leverage

SBA loans are built for:

  • Long-term ownership
  • Stability
  • Lower overall financing cost

A bridge loan may be appropriate for immediate closing or transitional financing. SBA financing may provide stronger long-term value if you plan long-term ownership and timing allows. Review structured commercial financing options to determine the right fit for your transaction.
