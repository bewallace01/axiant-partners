What Do Lenders Look at When Approving Equipment Financing?

Key underwriting factors that drive approval and terms

← Back to Equipment Financing Blog

March 3, 2026 - Axiant Partners

Understanding what lenders evaluate can improve approval odds, reduce delays, and help you structure transactions more effectively. Equipment financing is asset-backed, but lenders still assess multiple risk factors beyond the equipment itself. Here are the key factors they look at.

1. Credit Profile

Lenders evaluate personal credit, which affects approval likelihood, interest rate, term length, and down payment requirements. Typical tiers:

  • 700+ credit: Strongest pricing
  • 650–699: Competitive approval
  • 600–649: Higher risk tier, may need stronger offsetting factors
  • Below 600: Limited programs

See credit score requirements for equipment financing for more detail.

2. Time in Business

Lenders prefer established businesses (1–2+ years). Startups may still qualify based on owner credit, industry experience, and down payment strength. Longer operating history typically supports better terms.

3. Revenue & Cash Flow Stability

Lenders seek consistent revenue. They assess monthly bank deposits, revenue trends, existing debt obligations, and debt service coverage. Stable cash flow signals repayment capacity.

4. Equipment Type & Resale Value

The asset itself matters. Lenders consider equipment age, brand reputation, secondary market demand, liquidity, and useful life. Strong resale markets—construction equipment, commercial vehicles, manufacturing machinery—are easier to finance. For pre-owned assets, see can you finance used equipment.

5. Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratio

Lenders evaluate how much of the equipment's value is being financed. Higher LTV may require a larger down payment or affect approval. See down payment requirements for equipment financing for context.

6. Deal Structure: Loan vs. Lease

Approval can vary by transaction structure—equipment loan, $1 buyout lease, or fair market value lease. Flexibility depends on equipment liquidity and business profile. Compare structures in equipment loan vs lease.

7. Industry Risk Profile

Lenders consider industry stability. Some industries are seasonal, volatile, or higher default risk. Others—construction, agriculture, logistics, manufacturing—may have stronger historical performance and more favorable terms.

8. Documentation Preparedness

Complete, accurate documentation speeds approvals. Common items: equipment invoice or purchase agreement, business formation documents, bank statements, tax returns, and financial statements (for larger deals). For timing expectations, see how fast equipment financing can be approved.

How All These Factors Work Together

Approval is based on a full risk profile, not a single factor. Lenders assess credit strength, business viability, equipment liquidity, deal structure, industry risk, and equity contribution. Weaknesses in one area can be offset by strengths elsewhere—e.g., lower credit + strong revenue, older equipment + higher down payment, startup + strong industry experience.

Why Understanding Underwriting Matters

Knowing what lenders look for helps you: choose the right equipment, set realistic expectations, prepare documentation, improve negotiation leverage, and avoid unnecessary declines. Focus on approval criteria, not just rates.

Final Thoughts

Lenders evaluate business stability, equipment value, loan structure, and overall risk alignment—beyond credit score alone. Quality businesses can often secure financing even when they don't meet traditional bank standards.
