What Credit Score Is Needed for a Business Line of Credit?

Approval tiers, underwriting factors, and how established companies qualify

← Back to Business Line of Credit Blog

February 26, 2025 - Axiant Partners

This is a common question-and approval depends on factors like whether the line of credit is secured or unsecured, time in business, revenue stability, and overall risk profile. General credit score ranges can help you understand where you stand.

Typical Credit Score Ranges for a Business Line of Credit

700+ Credit Score (Strong Tier)

  • Competitive rates
  • Higher credit limits
  • Lower fees
  • Flexible repayment structures
  • Faster approvals

Businesses in this range typically qualify for premium unsecured working capital facilities.

650-699 Credit Score (Good Tier)

  • Solid approval odds
  • Competitive but slightly higher pricing
  • Moderate documentation requirements
  • Strong limit potential with solid revenue

Many established companies fall within this tier.

600-649 Credit Score (Near-Prime Tier)

  • Approval still possible
  • Higher interest rates
  • Lower credit limits
  • More conservative underwriting

Stronger revenue and cash flow can offset moderate credit profiles.

Below 600 Credit Score

  • Limited approval
  • Higher risk-based pricing
  • Potential requirement for secured structures, larger down payments, or asset backing

Most institutional lenders prefer borrowers above 600, especially for unsecured facilities.

Why Credit Score Matters for a Line of Credit

Unlike equipment financing, many business lines of credit are unsecured. That means lenders rely more heavily on credit history, debt repayment patterns, utilization ratios, and personal guarantee strength. Without specific collateral, risk evaluation becomes more dependent on borrower behavior.

Does Business Credit Score Matter?

Yes, especially for established companies. Lenders may review personal FICO scores, business credit profiles, existing trade lines, and payment history. A strong business credit file improves approval likelihood, credit limits, and pricing flexibility. If business credit history is limited, lenders may rely more heavily on personal credit.

Other Factors That Impact Approval

Credit score is important-but not the only factor. Lenders also evaluate:

  • 1. Time in Business: Most lenders prefer 1-2+ years of operation. Startups may face stricter underwriting.
  • 2. Revenue Stability: Consistent monthly deposits are critical. Lenders look for steady revenue trends, healthy cash flow, and the ability to support repayment.
  • 3. Debt Service Coverage: Lenders assess existing debt obligations, payment-to-income ratios, and overall leverage. Strong financial management can offset moderate credit scores.

Secured vs Unsecured Credit Score Expectations

Credit requirements vary by structure.

Unsecured Line of Credit: Typically requires stronger credit, offers faster approval, and has higher rates.

Secured Line of Credit: Backed by receivables or inventory, may allow slightly lower credit thresholds, and often offers larger limits. See our business line of credit page for a comparison, and our article on collateral requirements.

Can You Improve Your Approval Odds?

If your credit score is low, consider:

  • Reducing revolving credit utilization
  • Correcting reporting errors
  • Paying down high-interest debt
  • Strengthening business bank balances
  • Avoiding multiple hard inquiries

Small improvements can significantly impact pricing and limits.

What Credit Score Is "Good Enough"?

For established businesses: 650+ gives strong approval odds. 600+ may qualify with solid revenue. Below 600 becomes more restrictive. Every deal is evaluated holistically-revenue strength, industry visibility, and overall financial performance matter as much as the score alone.

Final Thoughts

Credit score plays an important role, especially for unsecured facilities. Lenders evaluate the full risk profile.
