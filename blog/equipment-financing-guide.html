Equipment Financing vs. Lease: A Complete Guide

Compare loans and leases, tax implications, and when each option makes sense

← Back to Blog

February 21, 2025 - Axiant Partners

When your business needs new equipment-from machinery and vehicles to computers and software-you typically have two main options: equipment financing (a loan to buy) or equipment leasing (rent with an option to buy or return). Here's how they compare.

Equipment Financing (Loan)

With equipment financing, you borrow money to purchase the equipment. You own it once the loan is paid off. Monthly payments are usually fixed, and the equipment often serves as collateral. This option works well when you plan to keep the equipment long term and want to build equity. Interest may be tax-deductible, and you may qualify for depreciation.

Equipment Leasing

With a lease, you pay to use the equipment for a set period. At the end, you may return it, upgrade, or buy it for a residual amount. Leases can require little or no down payment and may offer lower monthly payments than a loan. They're useful when you need to preserve cash flow, expect to upgrade often, or want to avoid owning outdated equipment.

Tax and Cash Flow

Loan interest and depreciation can reduce taxable income. Lease payments are often fully deductible as an operating expense, depending on the lease structure (true lease vs. capital lease). Your accountant can advise on the best structure for your situation.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose financing if you want to own the equipment and plan to use it for many years. Choose leasing if you prefer lower upfront cost, flexibility to upgrade, or want to keep the obligation off the balance sheet in certain cases.
