Business Line of Credit vs Term Loan: Which Is Better for Your Business?

Compare flexibility, cost, and capital needs

← Back to Business Line of Credit Blog

February 26, 2025 - Axiant Partners

Established business owners often compare two common structures when evaluating working capital financing: a business line of credit and a term loan. Both provide access to capital but function very differently. Choosing the right structure depends on how your business intends to use the funds, cash flow cycle, and capital needs. Understanding the differences helps avoid overborrowing, overpaying, or choosing the wrong financing structure.

What Is a Business Line of Credit?

A business line of credit is a revolving credit facility that provides access to funds up to a set limit. Instead of receiving a lump sum, you:

  • Draw only what you need
  • Pay interest only on the amount used
  • Reuse available credit as you repay

Example: If approved for a $500,000 working capital line of credit, you might draw $75,000 to cover inventory and later draw $10,000 for payroll-as long as you remain within the limit. This revolving structure is ideal for recurring or unpredictable capital needs. If exploring structured options for established businesses, reviewing a commercial business line of credit solution can provide clarity on eligibility and terms.

What Is a Term Loan?

A term loan provides a one-time lump sum disbursement with a fixed repayment schedule. Key characteristics:

  • Full amount funded upfront
  • Fixed monthly payments
  • Defined repayment term
  • No revolving access

Typical uses for term loans: equipment purchases, business acquisitions, expansion projects, real estate, and large one-time investments. Once funds are disbursed and repaid, the loan is closed.

Key Differences Between a Line of Credit and a Term Loan

  • Funding Structure: Line of credit = revolving; Term loan = lump sum
  • Interest: Line of credit = on amount drawn; Term loan = on full loan amount
  • Flexibility: Line of credit = ongoing access; Term loan = one-time use
  • Best For: Line of credit = operating capital; Term loan = fixed investments
  • Reuse: Line of credit = yes; Term loan = no

The choice depends on whether your business needs ongoing liquidity or project-based financing.

When a Business Line of Credit Is the Better Option

A working capital line of credit is often ideal when:

  • Revenue fluctuates seasonally
  • Receivables create temporary cash flow gaps
  • Inventory purchases are recurring
  • Payroll cycles require liquidity management
  • Short-term opportunities arise

Because you only draw funds when needed, it prevents unnecessary interest costs and allows for efficient capital management. Established companies with consistent revenue often use a line of credit as a liquidity cushion rather than a one-time funding solution.

When a Term Loan Makes More Sense

A term loan may be the better structure if:

  • You're purchasing fixed assets
  • You're funding a defined expansion project
  • You need a specific lump sum
  • Repayment schedule certainty is preferred

Equipment purchases are typically better suited for structured term financing rather than a revolving line. If evaluating fixed asset purchases, exploring equipment financing or other options aligned with long-term investments may be more appropriate.

Cost Differences: Line of Credit vs Term Loan

Line of Credit: Interest applies only to the amount drawn. Rates may vary based on structure. Some facilities include draw fees.

Term Loan: Interest applies to the full disbursed amount. Fixed amortization schedule. Predictable repayment timeline.

While term loans may sometimes appear lower in rate, they may cost more overall if you don't need the full capital amount immediately. Evaluating total capital efficiency is more important than comparing headline rates alone.

Underwriting Differences

Underwriting for both structures considers revenue stability, time in business, debt service coverage, credit history, and industry risk. Because many lines of credit are unsecured, lenders may focus more heavily on cash flow performance. Understanding what lenders evaluate when approving working capital financing can help you prepare documentation and improve approval odds.

Which Option Is Better for Established Businesses?

For established, revenue-generating companies: a line of credit often serves as a strategic liquidity tool; a term loan serves as project-specific capital. Many mature businesses maintain both-using a line of credit for operational flexibility and term loans for structured growth investments. The key is matching the financing structure to the purpose of the capital.

Final Thoughts

A business line of credit and a term loan serve different financial functions. If your business needs ongoing flexibility to manage cash flow cycles, vendor payments, or seasonal revenue shifts, a revolving line of credit may be the more strategic option. If you are funding a specific long-term investment, a term loan may provide clearer structure and amortization. Choosing the right capital solution requires evaluating how the financing aligns with your operational needs and financial profile. If your company is generating consistent revenue and exploring structured working capital solutions, reviewing available commercial line of credit options can help determine the most appropriate structure.
