What Are Typical Fix and Flip Loan Rates?

Rate ranges, what drives pricing, and how to secure better terms

← Back to Fix and Flip Blog

February 26, 2025 · Axiant Partners

One of the most common questions real estate investors ask when analyzing a deal is: "What are fix and flip loan rates right now?" The answer depends on credit score, investor experience, deal strength, ARV support, loan-to-value (LTV), and market conditions. Unlike traditional mortgages, fix and flip rates are structured around short-term, asset-based lending—pricing reflects both borrower risk and property risk. Below, we break down what investors should realistically expect nationwide.

Typical Fix and Flip Loan Rate Ranges

While rates fluctuate, common ranges for structured fix and flip loans include:

  • Stronger borrowers (700+ credit, experienced): Lower competitive rates
  • Mid-tier borrowers (680–699 credit): Moderate pricing
  • Lower-tier borrowers (600–679 credit): Higher risk-based pricing

In addition to interest rate, most fix and flip loans include origination points (typically 1–5+ points), interest-only payments, and 9–18 month terms. Exact pricing depends heavily on leverage and deal quality.

Why Fix and Flip Loan Rates Are Higher Than Conventional Loans

Fix and flip loans are short-term (9–18 months), asset-based, designed for distressed or undervalued property, and structured for speed. Because they fund properties traditional banks often won't finance, lenders price for market volatility, renovation risk, and short-term capital exposure. Strong deals with conservative leverage can still secure competitive terms.

What Determines Fix and Flip Loan Rates?

1. Credit Profile

Credit score matters even in asset-based lending. Higher scores typically mean lower interest rates, reduced origination points, higher leverage, and easier underwriting. Review credit score requirements for a fix and flip loan to clarify expectations.

2. Loan-to-After-Repair-Value (LTARV)

Leverage significantly impacts rate. Typical guidelines: up to 70–80% of ARV, up to 85% of purchase price, up to 100% of rehab (subject to ARV caps). Higher leverage = higher lender risk = higher pricing. Lower leverage often qualifies for stronger rate tiers.

3. Investor Experience

Experienced flippers typically receive better pricing, higher leverage, lower points, and more flexible extensions. Inexperienced investors may see slightly higher rates until they build a track record.

4. Market & Property Type

Rates may vary by local market volatility, property type (single-family vs multi-family vs light commercial), rehab complexity, and exit strategy clarity.

Points vs Interest Rate

Investors often focus only on interest rate—but points matter just as much. Points are upfront origination fees, typically a percentage of the loan amount. Example: 2 points on a $300,000 loan = $6,000. Sometimes a slightly lower rate comes with higher points, or vice versa. Total cost of capital should be evaluated holistically.

Hard Money vs Structured Fix and Flip Rates

Hard money lenders often provide extremely fast funding, higher leverage, and flexible underwriting—but often at higher interest rates, higher points, and with limited scalability. Structured fix and flip financing typically offers institutional underwriting, defined draw management, repeat capital programs, and more competitive long-term alignment. If you're scaling multiple projects, structure matters more than headline rate.

How to Secure Better Fix and Flip Loan Rates

Investors can improve pricing by: negotiating stronger purchase discounts, maintaining 680+ credit, providing accurate ARV comps, demonstrating prior flip experience, maintaining liquidity reserves, and keeping leverage conservative. Lenders price risk—strong deals reduce risk.

What Is a "Good" Fix and Flip Rate?

There is no universal "good" rate. Evaluate interest rate, points, leverage, extension options, draw management, and total project profitability. A slightly higher rate with strong leverage may outperform a lower rate with limited funding. Capital efficiency matters more than rate alone. If evaluating structured real estate investor financing, reviewing available fix and flip loan programs can clarify realistic pricing based on your deal profile.

Final Thoughts

Fix and flip loan rates vary by borrower strength, deal structure, ARV support, and leverage. While rates are generally higher than conventional mortgages, these loans provide speed, leverage, renovation funding, and short-term flexibility. For active investors, structured fix and flip financing can be a powerful capital tool when aligned with the deal. Understanding how pricing works helps investors analyze deals more accurately and protect profitability.
