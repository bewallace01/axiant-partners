How Long Does It Take to Close on a Commercial Real Estate Loan?

Typical timeline ranges and the underwriting factors that affect speed

← Back to Commercial Real Estate Loans Blog

March 2, 2026 - Axiant Partners

If you are buying or refinancing commercial property, closing timeline is one of the first strategic questions. Timeline varies by structure, borrower readiness, property complexity, and underwriting workflow.

Typical Closing Timelines by Loan Type

Loan Type Typical Timeline to Close
Conventional Commercial Mortgage 30-60 days
SBA 7(a) Real Estate Loan 45-75 days
SBA 504 Loan 60-90 days

Simple files can close faster, but more complex files require additional diligence and documentation.

1. Conventional Commercial Loan (Fastest Standard Option)

Conventional structures often close faster than SBA when documentation is complete and property risk is straightforward. Typical process includes:

  • Initial review and term sheet
  • Appraisal and environmental review
  • Financial underwriting
  • Final approval and legal closing

2. SBA 7(a) Real Estate Loan

SBA 7(a) can support owner-occupied transactions with flexible use of proceeds, but includes additional underwriting and compliance layers. Typical range is 45-75 days.

3. SBA 504 Loan (Longest but Most Structured)

SBA 504 often provides strong long-term structure for owner-occupied property, but usually has the longest timeline because of multi-party coordination and extra documentation checkpoints.

What Affects CRE Closing Speed?

Documentation Readiness

  • Business tax returns and financial statements
  • Personal financial statement and debt schedule
  • Property rent rolls and operating data
  • Purchase contract or refinance payoff detail

Missing documents are one of the top causes of closing delays.

Appraisal and Environmental Reports

Third-party reports can drive timeline variance. Faster scheduling and responsive data access help reduce delays.

Credit and Underwriting Strength

Stronger DSCR, cleaner credit profile, and clearer cash flow history typically move through underwriting with fewer conditions.

Property Type

Special-use or more complex assets often require deeper review than standard office, retail, or industrial properties.

Seller Timeline and Title Issues

Title, legal, and seller-side delays can affect timing even when credit and underwriting are strong.

Fastest Realistic Closing Scenario

For a straightforward owner-occupied purchase with complete documentation, conventional CRE loans can sometimes close in about 30 days.

When Should You Plan for 60-90 Days?

  • SBA-driven structures
  • Complex business entities or multi-tenant files
  • Higher documentation volume
  • Appraisal/environmental scheduling delays

Minimum Loan Amount

Commercial real estate loan programs generally start around $10,000 and scale by qualification and property value.

How to Close Faster

  • Prepare a complete underwriting file before application
  • Maintain current financial reporting
  • Respond quickly to lender conditions
  • Align loan structure with your scenario upfront

Final Thoughts

Most owner-occupied CRE deals close between 30 and 90 days depending on loan type and execution quality. If timing matters, compare commercial real estate loan options and align your deal with the right structure before submission.

Typical Closing Timelines by Loan Type

Loan Type Typical Timeline to Close
Conventional Commercial Mortgage 30-60 days
SBA 7(a) Real Estate Loan 45-75 days
SBA 504 Loan 60-90 days

1. Conventional Commercial Loan (Fastest Standard Option)

Conventional bank loans often close quickly when borrower and property documentation are complete.

  • Initial review and pre-approval
  • Appraisal and environmental reports
  • Property and borrower due diligence
  • Credit committee review and final docs

2. SBA 7(a) Real Estate Loan

SBA timelines are generally longer because underwriting includes program-level review and compliance documentation.

  • SBA-specific forms and disclosures
  • Additional lender and guarantor review
  • Government-backed process checkpoints

3. SBA 504 Loan (Longest but Most Structured)

SBA 504 can offer strong long-term value, but often has the longest timeline due to multi-party coordination and layered approvals.

  • Bank lender review
  • CDC and SBA workflow
  • Extended documentation package

What Affects CRE Closing Speed?

Documentation Readiness

  • Tax returns and financial statements
  • Business operating history
  • Property purchase/refinance package
  • Debt schedule and use-of-funds clarity

Appraisal and Environmental Reports

Third-party report timing is one of the most common causes of delay in CRE closings.

Credit and Underwriting Strength

Stronger credit, better DSCR, and cleaner financial trends typically reduce back-and-forth and speed final approval. See our guide on CRE credit score requirements.

Property Type

Special-use or complex properties often require deeper review than standard office, retail, or industrial assets.

Seller Timeline and Title Issues

Contract readiness, title clean-up, and seller responsiveness can all affect close speed.

Fastest Realistic Closing Scenario

In straightforward conventional files with complete documents, clean title, and no report delays, closings can happen in roughly 30 days.

When Should You Plan for 60-90 Days?

  • SBA 504 structures
  • Properties needing additional diligence
  • Complex ownership or collateral structures
  • Extended seller-side conditions

Minimum Loan Amount

Commercial real estate loan programs generally start around $10,000 and scale with property value and qualification.

  • Prepare complete documentation before applying
  • Work with financing structure that matches timeline goals
  • Order third-party reports early
