What Credit Score Is Needed for a Commercial Real Estate Loan?

Credit tiers, lender expectations, and what drives CRE approval

← Back to Commercial Real Estate Loans Blog

Last updated: March 3, 2026 · 6 min read · Axiant Partners

If you are purchasing or refinancing an owner-occupied commercial property, one of the first questions is credit score requirements. Most lenders evaluate clear tier ranges, but credit is only one part of commercial real estate underwriting. CRE financing is heavily cash-flow driven—DSCR, property fundamentals, and business stability often carry as much weight as the score itself. A borrower with moderate credit but strong cash flow and a solid property may still qualify, while a high-credit borrower with weak DSCR may face challenges. Understanding the full picture helps you prepare and choose the right program.

Typical Credit Score Requirements by Loan Type

Loan Type Typical Preferred Credit Score
SBA 504 680-700+
SBA 7(a) 660-680+
Conventional Commercial Mortgage 680-720+
Asset-Based / Alternative CRE 600-650+ (with compensating factors)

Stronger credit profiles typically receive faster approvals, lower pricing, and better leverage options. Each lender sets its own thresholds; some may be more flexible than others. Credit Score Tiers Explained

Lenders typically bucket borrowers into tiers. Higher tiers receive better pricing, leverage, and program access. Understanding where you fall helps set expectations and identify improvement opportunities before applying. Key tiers include:

720+ (Strong Tier)

  • Maximum leverage potential
  • Smoother underwriting
  • Stronger terms across lender channels

680-719 (Solid Tier)

  • Good conventional access
  • Strong SBA eligibility
  • Competitive pricing for owner-occupied loans

650-679 (Moderate Tier)

  • SBA programs often remain available
  • Conventional options may tighten
  • Slightly higher pricing likely

600-649 (Challenging Tier)

  • SBA may still be possible with strong compensating factors
  • Higher equity requirements may apply
  • Alternative structures are often considered

In this range, strong DSCR, property fundamentals, and liquidity can help offset the score. Discuss your full profile with lenders before assuming you will not qualify.

Why Credit Score Matters in CRE

Commercial real estate loans are often large, long-term obligations. Lenders use credit profile to evaluate repayment history, financial discipline, and risk behavior over time. A strong score signals that you have managed debt responsibly and are less likely to default. It can also unlock better pricing, higher leverage, and access to programs like SBA 504 that require solid credit. See SBA 504 vs conventional CRE loan for how credit tiers affect program eligibility.

What Matters More Than Credit Score

CRE underwriting is cash-flow driven. A borrower with 680 credit but strong DSCR, stable revenue, and a solid property may qualify where a 720-credit borrower with weak cash flow may not. Lenders evaluate the full package. Most lenders prioritize:

  • Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
  • Business revenue stability
  • Property fundamentals and occupancy quality
  • Time in business
  • Down payment and liquidity reserves

Personal vs Business Credit

Most owner-occupied CRE transactions evaluate both personal and business credit. Lenders typically pull personal credit for guarantors and may also review business credit reports from Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, or Equifax Business. For smaller businesses, personal credit often carries significant weight because the business may have limited trade history. Larger, established companies with strong business credit may rely more heavily on the business profile. Maintaining strong payment history in both profiles helps reduce underwriting friction. If one is weaker, address it before applying—lenders look at the full picture and may request explanations for derogatory items.

Below 600: Limited CRE Options

Scores below 600 narrow CRE options significantly. Most conventional and SBA programs prefer 650+. Alternative or asset-based lenders may consider lower scores but typically require higher equity, stronger collateral, or higher rates. If your score is in the 500s, focus on credit improvement before applying for commercial real estate financing. Paying down debt, disputing errors, and establishing positive payment history can help. Allow 6-12 months for meaningful improvement before reapplying.

How Credit Impacts Down Payment

Lenders may adjust equity requirements based on your credit profile. Strong credit can unlock lower down payment options, especially for owner-occupied properties.

  • Higher credit often supports lower equity requirements
  • Moderate credit may increase required reserves
  • Weaker profiles usually require additional safeguards

Lenders may require more down payment or post-closing liquidity when credit is below their preferred tier. See how much down payment is required for a commercial property loan for typical ranges by program and how credit affects equity requirements.

How to Improve Approval Odds

If your score is below your target tier, allow 3–6 months before applying to make improvements. Payment history updates typically take 30–60 days to reflect on credit reports. Correcting errors, paying down revolving debt, and avoiding new inquiries can help. Beyond credit, focus on DSCR, liquidity, and documentation quality—these often have as much impact on approval as the score. See what lenders look for in a commercial real estate loan for the full underwriting checklist.

  • Reduce revolving utilization
  • Resolve outstanding collections or reporting errors
  • Improve monthly operating consistency
  • Build stronger liquidity reserves
  • Strengthen DSCR with clear financial reporting

Minimum Loan Amounts

Commercial real estate loan programs generally start around $10,000 and scale based on property and qualification. Many lenders have minimum loan sizes of $250,000 or higher for commercial mortgages. Smaller transactions may need to work with community banks or specialized lenders. Credit requirements often scale with loan size—larger loans may require stronger profiles. Next Steps

Know your score before applying. Pull reports from all three bureaus and address any errors. If your score is below your target tier, allow time to improve before submitting applications. Many borrowers qualify even with scores in the 650-680 range when property cash flow and business fundamentals are strong—do not assume you are disqualified without speaking to lenders. Compare commercial real estate loan programs and get matched with lenders that work with your credit profile.

Final Thoughts

Most lenders prefer credit scores in the 650-720+ range, depending on structure and risk profile. For many borrowers, cash flow and property fundamentals carry as much weight as score. SBA programs may offer slightly more flexibility for moderate-credit borrowers when other factors are strong.
