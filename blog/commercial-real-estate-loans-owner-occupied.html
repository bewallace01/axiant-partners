Commercial Real Estate Loans for Owner-Occupied Businesses

Loan structures, down payment ranges, and lender criteria from $10,000 to $10,000,000

← Back to Commercial Real Estate Loans Blog

March 2, 2026 - Axiant Partners

Owner-occupied commercial real estate financing helps businesses purchase or refinance properties they operate from. Structure, underwriting standards, and down payment requirements differ by loan type.

What Is Owner-Occupied Commercial Real Estate Financing?

Owner-occupied CRE financing is used when the business occupies a meaningful portion of the subject property. Common property types include:

  • Office buildings
  • Retail storefronts
  • Industrial/flex facilities
  • Medical and specialty properties (program-dependent)

Commercial real estate loans can support both purchases and refinances for businesses ready to control their operating footprint.

Types of Commercial Real Estate Financing

1. SBA 504 Loan (Popular for Owner-Occupied Property)

  • Designed for long-term real estate financing
  • Typical rates can be competitive with fixed-rate components
  • Often supports lower equity injection than many conventional structures

2. SBA 7(a) Real Estate Loan

  • More flexible use of proceeds in many cases
  • Supports owner-occupied purchases and refinances
  • Commonly used by businesses that need broader structure flexibility

3. Conventional Commercial Mortgage

  • Typically offered by banks and institutional lenders
  • Often requires stronger credit, cash flow, and equity injection
  • Works well for established borrowers with strong financials

How Much Down Payment Is Required?

Loan Type Typical Down Payment
SBA 504 ~10% in many scenarios
SBA 7(a) ~10-20% typical
Conventional ~20-35% typical

Stronger borrower profiles may qualify for more favorable leverage.

Loan Amounts and Terms

Commercial real estate loan requests typically range from $10,000 to $10,000,000, depending on property value and borrower strength.

  • SBA 504: often supports larger owner-occupied projects with long-term structure
  • SBA 7(a): generally up to program limits with flexibility in use
  • Conventional: terms and maximums vary by lender and collateral

What Do Lenders Look For?

  • Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
  • Business and guarantor credit profile
  • Time in business and operating history
  • Property strength and marketability
  • Liquidity and post-closing reserves

Strong documentation and consistent financial reporting improve approval outcomes.

Owner-Occupied vs Investment Property

Many SBA and business-purpose CRE programs focus on owner-occupied properties. Pure investment structures may require different loan types, equity levels, and underwriting standards.

When Is a Commercial Real Estate Loan the Right Move?

  • Your business needs stable, long-term occupancy control
  • You want to build equity instead of paying rent
  • You are expanding and need operational capacity
  • You want to refinance existing property debt
