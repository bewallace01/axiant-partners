What Do Lenders Look for When Approving a Business Line of Credit?

Key underwriting factors that drive approval and terms

← Back to Business Line of Credit Blog

March 3, 2026 - Axiant Partners

Many business owners ask: what are lenders actually reviewing when I apply for a business line of credit? Understanding underwriting criteria improves approval odds and reduces delays. Commercial underwriting evaluates overall financial health and stability—not just a single credit score. Here are the key factors lenders analyze when approving a commercial line of credit.

1. Revenue Stability

Revenue stability is one of the most important underwriting factors. Lenders typically review:

  • Monthly bank deposits
  • Revenue consistency
  • Seasonal fluctuations
  • Year-over-year growth trends

Stable, predictable revenue signals lower repayment risk. Lenders need confidence in consistent cash flow for revolving and reusable lines of credit. Reviewing a commercial business line of credit facility can clarify how revenue impacts approval.

2. Time in Business

Most lenders prefer 2+ years in operation. Established operating history reduces risk; startups may face stricter underwriting. Longevity demonstrates business stability and management experience.

3. Credit Profile (Personal & Business)

Lenders evaluate personal FICO score, business credit profile, payment history, revolving credit utilization, and existing debt obligations. Higher credit scores improve approval odds, rates, and credit limits.

Understanding credit score requirements for a business line of credit helps assess readiness before applying.

4. Debt Service Coverage

Lenders assess whether the business can comfortably manage additional credit. They review existing loan payments, merchant cash advances, credit card balances, and other financing obligations. High existing leverage can reduce approval odds or limit credit amounts. Healthy debt service coverage improves flexibility.

5. Industry Risk Profile

Industries considered higher risk include seasonal, high-volatility, and cyclical sectors. Others are viewed as stable and predictable. Lenders assess historical performance, industry default rates, and revenue stability. Stronger industries often receive more favorable terms.

6. Secured vs. Unsecured Structure

When applying for a secured line of credit, lenders also evaluate accounts receivable aging, inventory levels, asset liquidity, and financial statements. Secured facilities often require more documentation but may provide higher limits, lower rates, and a stronger long-term capital structure.

Review do you need collateral for a business line of credit to clarify underwriting differences between secured and unsecured options.

7. Requested Credit Limit

The larger the requested limit, the deeper the underwriting review. Higher limits often require stronger financials, additional documentation, and lower leverage ratios. Applying for a realistic amount aligned with revenue improves approval probability.

Final Thoughts

When approving a business line of credit, lenders evaluate far more than credit score alone. They assess revenue stability, time in business, debt obligations, industry risk, collateral (if applicable), and overall financial health. Revenue-generating businesses with consistent cash flow and responsible financial management typically qualify for competitive facilities. Structure your request strategically by understanding underwriting criteria.

If your business meets these benchmarks, reviewing structured business line of credit options can help you find the right fit.
