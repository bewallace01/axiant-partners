Merchant Cash Advance Alternatives: 7 Options Compared

What to use instead of an MCA, what each typically costs, how fast it funds, and which one fits the problem you are actually trying to solve

← Back to Merchant Cash Advance Articles

Updated November 13, 2026

Quick answer

As of November 2026, the most common MCA alternatives are a business line of credit (typically 8-13% APR at banks, 14-30%+ online), an online term loan, invoice factoring (about 1-5% per invoice), equipment financing, revenue-based financing and SBA 7(a). Each usually costs far less than an MCA's 1.15-1.50 factor, but takes longer or asks more. Axiant Partners, a business-loan broker in Boca Raton, compares them for you. Typical ranges, not a quote.

Match the Alternative to the Problem

An MCA gets used for every kind of shortfall because it is fast. The cheaper options are narrower, so start from what is actually short:

  • Customers pay late. That is a receivables problem: factoring advances against the invoices instead of your future sales.
  • Sales are seasonal. A line of credit drawn in the slow months and repaid in the busy ones.
  • A machine or vehicle. Equipment financing, secured by the asset, over its working life.
  • A growth project with a payback. A term loan, or SBA 7(a) if it can wait a few weeks.
  • Equity you already own. A loan against a truck, equipment or real estate.

Why Businesses End Up With an MCA Anyway

  • Speed. Funding in a day or two. The fix is to apply for a line while you don’t need it, so it is there when you do.
  • Credit. Lower scores get fewer bank offers. Secured options, factoring and equipment financing weigh the asset or the invoice more than the score.
  • Documents. An MCA asks for bank statements; a term loan may want tax returns. Having them ready removes most of the gap.
  • An existing MCA. Open positions put off banks. See refinancing an MCA into a term loan.

If You Already Have an MCA

An alternative can also replace an advance you already have, but check the rules first. SBA 7(a) cannot refinance an active MCA under SOP 50 10 8.1; the narrow exception is explained in can you refinance an MCA with an SBA loan. A non-SBA term loan, a consolidation loan or a loan against an asset you own are the usual routes. If the debits are already more than the account can carry, read options when you can’t make MCA payments before taking anything new.

How to Compare an MCA Offer With an Alternative

  1. Put both in dollars: total repaid minus cash received.
  2. Put both in APR, using the method in how much a merchant cash advance costs.
  3. Compare the monthly bite against your slowest month.
  4. Read prepayment terms: an MCA often owes the full payback even if repaid early; most term loans don’t.
  5. Apply once and let a broker return several offers. Get matched.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best alternative to a merchant cash advance?

It depends on the problem. A business line of credit fits repeated, seasonal gaps; invoice factoring fits slow-paying customers; equipment financing fits a machine or vehicle; a term loan fits a one-time need. Each typically costs far less than an MCA.

Is a business line of credit cheaper than an MCA?

Usually by a wide margin. Lines commonly run about 8-13% APR at banks and 14-30%+ online, while an MCA at a 1.30 factor over 6 months is about 109% APR. These are typical ranges, not a quote.

Can I get an MCA alternative with bad credit?

Often. Invoice factoring leans on your customers' credit, and equipment financing and asset-secured loans lean on the collateral, so a lower score matters less than it does at a bank.

How fast can an MCA alternative fund?

Online lines of credit and term loans often fund in days, factoring within days of setup, and equipment financing in days to weeks. SBA 7(a) takes weeks.

Can an SBA loan pay off a merchant cash advance?

Not an active one, under SOP 50 10 8.1, apart from a narrow conversion path. A non-SBA term loan, consolidation loan or asset-secured loan is the usual route.

Is revenue-based financing an MCA alternative?

Yes, for businesses with recurring or online revenue. It is also priced with a factor, about 1.10-1.50, but usually over longer terms, which lowers the annual cost.
