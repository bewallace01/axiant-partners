When revenue dips, the fixed daily debit that felt manageable suddenly isn't—and taking another advance to cover it is the trap. Before that happens, there are legitimate ways to lower the payment. The most overlooked is a right many owners don't know they have: reconciliation. This page covers reconciliation, hardship and forbearance requests, how to ask, the real risks, and when the smarter move is to stop delaying and actually refinance. It's part of the broader MCA relief toolkit.

Reconciliation: The Right You May Already Have

Many MCA agreements include a reconciliation clause. Because an MCA is meant to be repaid as a percentage of revenue, the fixed daily or weekly debit is really just an estimate of that percentage. Reconciliation is the mechanism to true it up: if your actual receipts fall, you can request that the funder adjust the debit downward so it stays proportional to revenue—and refund or credit any over-collection.

This is not a favor; where the clause exists, it's how the product is supposed to function. The catch is that it usually isn't automatic—you have to request it and provide documentation (bank or processor statements) within the timeframe the agreement specifies. Read your agreement for the reconciliation terms, and see red flags in MCA agreements for clauses that weaken or complicate this right.

Hardship Reductions and Forbearance

If your agreement lacks a usable reconciliation clause—or you need more relief than reconciliation provides—you can still ask the funder directly for a temporary reduced debit or a short forbearance. Funders sometimes agree, for a simple reason: a business that keeps paying something is worth more to them than one that defaults. A lower daily debit or a brief pause can keep you operating and keep them collecting. None of this is guaranteed, and what's offered varies by funder and how distressed your account looks.

How to Ask (and Strengthen Your Case)

  1. Act before you miss a payment. Leverage and goodwill are highest while you're current. Asking after a default is much harder.
  2. Document the shortfall. Recent bank and processor statements showing the revenue drop make the request credible.
  3. Name what you want. Request reconciliation specifically if your agreement provides it; otherwise ask for a defined reduced debit or forbearance period.
  4. Get it in writing. An informal verbal reduction that isn't documented can be reversed. Confirm the new amount, duration, and how the balance is affected in writing.

The Risks: A Delay, Not a Discount

  • The balance doesn't shrink. A reduced payment stretches the same total over a longer period—you carry the debt longer, and the fixed factor cost remains.
  • Fees or conditions may apply. Some arrangements add cost or strings; read them carefully.
  • Undocumented relief can vanish. If it isn't in writing, the funder can revert to the full debit.
  • It doesn't solve a cost problem. If the real issue is the advance's sky-high effective APR, forbearance only postpones it.

When to Refinance or Consolidate Instead

Forbearance and reconciliation are best used as a short-term bridge—something to stabilize cash flow while you pursue a durable fix. If you can qualify, that durable fix is usually a refinance into a term loan (lower payment and lower total cost) or, if you're carrying several advances, a consolidation into one payment. Compare the two in consolidation vs refinancing. The goal is to replace the expensive advance, not just slow it down.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you get reduced payments on an MCA?

Sometimes. Many agreements contain a reconciliation clause that adjusts the debit to your actual revenue, which can lower it when sales drop. Even without that clause, funders may grant a temporary hardship reduction or forbearance because they prefer slower payment to default. Neither is guaranteed, and a lower payment usually extends the term.

What is an MCA reconciliation clause?

It lets you ask the funder to true up your fixed debit to a set percentage of actual receipts. If sales fall, reconciliation can lower the debit so it stays proportional to revenue. You typically must request it and provide documentation such as bank or processor statements within the timeframe the agreement specifies.

How do I ask an MCA funder for a hardship reduction?

Contact the funder before you miss a payment, explain the revenue shortfall, and back it with recent bank and processor statements. Ask specifically for reconciliation if your agreement provides it, or a temporary reduced debit or forbearance if not. Put the request and any agreement in writing.

What are the risks of MCA forbearance?

A reduced payment usually doesn't reduce the total you owe; it stretches the same balance over a longer period. Some arrangements add fees, and an undocumented reduction can be reversed. Forbearance buys breathing room but is a delay, not a discount. If cost is the real problem, refinancing or consolidating addresses it more directly.

Is it better to refinance than to ask for forbearance?

If you can qualify, refinancing into a term loan or line of credit usually beats forbearance because it replaces high-cost factor-rate debt with lower-cost amortizing debt, cutting both the payment and the total cost. Use forbearance or reconciliation as a short-term bridge, and pursue a refinance or consolidation as the durable fix.
