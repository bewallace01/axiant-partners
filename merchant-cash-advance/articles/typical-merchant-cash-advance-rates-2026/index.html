How to Calculate the Cost of a Merchant Cash Advance

An MCA is priced with a factor rate instead of an interest rate, so the cost is a fixed dollar amount agreed on day one. Four numbers turn it into something you can compare with a loan:

  1. Payback. Advance × factor rate. $100,000 × 1.35 = $135,000.
  2. Dollar cost. Payback minus the cash you actually receive. If a fee is deducted from the funding, subtract the net amount, not the headline advance.
  3. Term. Payback divided by the daily (or weekly) debit. $135,000 at $750 a business day is 180 business days, about 8.6 months.
  4. APR. The annual rate at which the cash received equals the stream of debits. A spreadsheet’s RATE function does it: RATE(180, -750, 100000) × 252 = about 88%.

Step 4 is the one providers skip. It matters because a cost of 35% looks like a 35% rate, but you never have the full $100,000 for the whole term: the debits start the next business day, so on average you hold about half of it.

How the Holdback Sets the Term, and the Cost

You rarely choose the term. On a true split, the provider takes a fixed share of card sales, commonly 10-20%, so busy weeks repay faster. On a fixed ACH debit, the term is set when you sign. Either way, a faster payback means a higher APR for the same dollar cost.

Example: $5,000 of daily card sales at a 15% holdback is $750 a day, which repays a $135,000 payback in about 180 business days. If sales rise to $6,000, the debit is $900 and the same advance is repaid in 150 days, at a higher APR. If sales fall, a reconciliation clause should lower the debit; ask whether the agreement has one and how you request it.

Fees and Clauses That Change the Real Cost

  • Fees deducted from funding. An origination or underwriting fee taken out of the advance raises the APR. A $100,000 advance at 1.35 over 9 months is about 84%; with $3,000 deducted, you receive $97,000 and it is about 93%.
  • Early payoff. Many agreements owe the full payback even if you repay early. A written prepayment discount is the exception; get it in the contract.
  • Stacking. A second advance on top of the first adds a second daily debit and is often priced higher. See stacked MCAs.
  • Renewals. Rolling the unpaid balance into a new advance charges the factor again on money you already owed.

Florida’s commercial financing disclosure law (Fla. Stat. 559.9613) requires the provider to show the amount funded, the amount actually disbursed, the total repaid, the total dollar cost, the payment terms and any prepayment cost. It does not require an APR, which is why the calculation above is yours to do.

What Drives Your Factor Rate

MCA pricing has little to do with the prime rate. Providers price on their own models:

  • Deposit and card volume: higher and steadier earns a lower factor.
  • Negative days and overdrafts push it up.
  • Time in business: a longer track record lowers it.
  • Industry: some carry a premium or are excluded.
  • Open advances: existing positions raise the factor or cause a decline.

How large an advance those same numbers support is a separate question, covered in how much you can qualify for.

Before You Sign

Run the four steps on the offer in front of you, then price the same amount as a line of credit or term loan. A line at a typical 8-13% APR at a bank, or 14-30%+ online, costs a fraction of an MCA for the same months. Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the cost of a merchant cash advance?

Multiply the advance by the factor rate to get the payback, then subtract the cash you actually received. Divide the payback by the daily debit to get the term, and annualize with a rate function to get the APR. $100,000 at 1.35 repaid at $750 a business day is $35,000 over about 180 days, or about 88% APR.

How much does a $100,000 merchant cash advance cost?

At a typical factor of 1.25-1.45, $25,000-$45,000 on top of the $100,000. Repaid over 6-12 months by daily debits, that is roughly 46-158% APR. These are typical ranges, not a quote.

What are typical merchant cash advance factor rates in 2026?

Commonly 1.15 to 1.50. Stronger, steadier deposits and fewer open advances earn the lower end; weaker files and stacking push toward the top.

How do you convert a factor rate to an APR?

Find the payback and the number of payments, then solve for the rate at which the cash received equals the payments. With 21 business-day debits a month, a 1.30 factor is about 109% APR over 6 months and about 55% over 12.

Why does a shorter MCA cost more as an APR?

The dollar cost is fixed when you sign. Repaying it faster means the same cost is spread over less time and a smaller average balance, so the annual rate rises.

Do fees change the cost of an MCA?

Yes. A fee deducted from the funding means you receive less but repay the same. On $100,000 at 1.35 over 9 months, a $3,000 deduction moves the APR from about 84% to about 93%.

Does a merchant cash advance have to disclose an APR in Florida?

No. Florida's commercial financing disclosure law requires the total funded, amount disbursed, total repaid, total dollar cost and payment terms, but not an APR. You can compute it from those figures.
