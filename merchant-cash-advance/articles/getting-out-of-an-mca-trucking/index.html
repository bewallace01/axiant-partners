Trucking runs on cash that arrives 30 to 60 days late. Fuel, repairs, insurance, and driver pay don't wait for a broker to cut a check—so when a tight week hits, a merchant cash advance is easy to grab. Then the daily debit starts competing with the fuel card, and you're in the cycle. The good news for carriers: you have an exit most other businesses don't, because you invoice creditworthy customers. This page covers why trucking companies end up in MCAs and the carrier-specific way out, as part of the broader MCA relief playbook.

Why Carriers End Up in MCAs

The trap is structural, not a failure of management:

  • Slow pay. Brokers and shippers commonly pay on net-30 to net-60 terms, but your costs are daily.
  • Front-loaded costs. Fuel, tolls, repairs, insurance, and payroll all hit before the load gets paid.
  • Easy access. MCAs fund fast with minimal paperwork, so they're the path of least resistance in a crunch.
  • Stacking. One advance rarely covers a fleet's gap, so carriers take a second or third—and the combined daily debits swallow the revenue. See why businesses get stuck in the MCA cycle.

The Trucking-Specific Fix: Freight Factoring

Here's the move other industries can't always make: instead of borrowing against future revenue, sell the revenue you've already earned. With freight factoring, you sell an unpaid load invoice and get most of its value up front—often within a day—while the factor waits to collect from the broker or shipper. It's not a loan and adds no debt, and approval leans on your customers' credit, not just yours.

That matters because factoring fixes the actual cause of the MCA: the payment-timing gap. Once invoices turn into same-week cash, the reason you reached for an advance largely disappears. Learn the mechanics in freight and staffing factoring.

Clear the Existing Debt: Refinance or Consolidate

Factoring stops the bleeding going forward, but you still need to clear the advance you already have:

  • Refinance into a term loan. If the MCA is current and your revenue is steady, swap the daily debit for one affordable monthly payment. Follow the 30-day refinance playbook.
  • Consolidate stacked advances. Carrying two or three? Consolidation rolls them into one lower payment.
  • Tap equipment value. Carriers often have truck and trailer equity that can help fund a payoff. Explore trucking financing.

The winning play is usually both: factoring to remove the cause, plus a refinance or consolidation to retire the debt.

What Carriers Should Watch For

  • Don't stack another advance. Taking a new MCA to cover fuel is how the cycle deepens. Factoring is the cash-flow answer, not more advances.
  • Act before default. A defaulted MCA can trigger UCC-lien enforcement and aggressive collection. See what happens if you default on an MCA.
  • Mind existing UCC liens. An MCA's blanket lien can complicate factoring or a new loan until it's released. See MCA UCC liens and refinancing.
  • Know your real cost. Convert your factor rate to an effective APR so you can see how much factoring + a refinance saves: calculate your MCA's real APR.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do trucking companies end up with MCAs?

Carriers face fast, recurring costs like fuel, repairs, and driver pay while brokers and shippers pay 30 to 60 days out. That gap pushes owner-operators and small fleets toward fast cash, and MCAs are easy to get. The high daily debit then competes with fuel and payroll, which is how carriers get stuck.

What's the best way for a trucking company to get out of an MCA?

The trucking-specific tool is freight factoring: sell your unpaid load invoices for immediate cash, which removes the cash-flow gap that drove you to the advance. Pair factoring with a refinance into a term loan or a consolidation of stacked advances to replace the daily debit. Which combination fits depends on your revenue, credit, and number of advances.

How is freight factoring different from an MCA?

Freight factoring isn't a loan and adds no debt — you sell an invoice you already earned and get most of its value up front, then the factor collects from the broker or shipper. An MCA is an advance on future revenue repaid through daily debits at a high effective cost. For carriers with creditworthy customers, factoring solves the underlying timing problem.

Can I refinance a trucking MCA into a term loan?

Often yes. If the advance is current and you have steady revenue and reasonable credit, you can refinance the balance into a term loan or line of credit at a far lower cost. Carriers can also tap equipment value or factoring to fund the payoff. Act while the advance is current, before a default narrows your options.

I have several stacked MCAs on my trucking business. What now?

Stacked advances are common in trucking. Consolidation can combine them into a single lower payment to stop the daily bleed, and factoring can keep cash flowing while you stabilize. Acting before you fall behind matters, because defaulting on a trucking MCA can trigger aggressive collection.
