One of the most common MCA-relief questions is whether "real" financing—an SBA loan or a bank loan—can be used to pay the advance off. The answer is yes, and when it works it's the cheapest exit available. But qualification and timing are everything. This guide covers how each path refinances an MCA, who realistically qualifies, the timelines, and the faster fallbacks if you're not bank-ready yet. Paying Off an MCA With an SBA 7(a) Loan

The SBA 7(a) program permits debt refinancing as an eligible use of proceeds, and high-cost debt such as an MCA is a classic refinance candidate. Done right, it converts punishing daily debits into a single monthly payment on a long amortization at a capped, relatively low rate—often the best long-term economics of any exit.

  • Best for: businesses that can wait ~60–90 days and want the lowest long-term cost.
  • Typical qualifying signals: solid personal credit, a couple of years in business, healthy revenue, eligible industry, and adequate debt-service coverage after the new loan.
  • Timeline: roughly 60–90 days—the slowest path, so not a fit when debits are an emergency by themselves.
  • Mechanics: in a refinance structure the lender pays off the MCA at closing, the daily debits stop, and you begin one monthly payment.

Because of the timeline, SBA is often best paired with a faster bridge: stop the bleed now with a specialty lender, then refinance into the SBA loan when it closes.

Paying Off an MCA With a Conventional Bank Loan

A bank term loan can also retire an MCA—usually at a single-digit to mid-teens APR over three to seven years, and faster than SBA (about two to six weeks, quicker if you bank there already). The tradeoff is the strictest bar:

  • Typical expectations: strong credit, multiple years in business, healthy revenue, and clean financials.
  • The MCA yellow flag: an active advance can make banks cautious—they want to see it was a one-time event, not a symptom of ongoing cash-flow stress.
  • Reality check: if you could already qualify for a bank term loan, you probably wouldn't have taken the MCA. Apply, but don't count on it as your only plan.

The Honest Qualification Bar

Both SBA and bank paths reward exactly the profile that MCA borrowers often don't have at the moment: established credit, time in business, and cash flow that comfortably covers a new payment. That's not a reason to skip them—it's a reason to apply and line up a realistic fallback in parallel. The worst outcome is waiting months for an SBA approval that doesn't come while the daily debits grind you down.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you use an SBA loan to pay off an MCA?

Yes. The SBA 7(a) program allows debt refinancing, and high-cost debt like an MCA can qualify to be refinanced into a 7(a) term loan with a much lower rate and longer amortization. The catch is qualification and timing: underwriting typically takes 60–90 days and requires solid credit, time in business, revenue, and adequate debt-service coverage.

Can a regular bank loan pay off a merchant cash advance?

Yes, a conventional bank term loan can pay off an MCA, usually at a single-digit-to-mid-teens APR over three to seven years. Banks have the strictest bar, often wanting strong credit, multiple years in business, healthy revenue, and clean financials. An active MCA can be a yellow flag.

How long does it take?

SBA 7(a) generally takes about 60–90 days. A conventional bank term loan can take roughly two to six weeks, often faster where you already have a deposit relationship. Because daily debits can be urgent, some businesses use a faster specialty lender first and pursue SBA or bank refinancing as the longer-term plan.

What if I don't qualify?

Most businesses that took an MCA can't immediately qualify for a bank or SBA loan. Realistic fallbacks include specialty MCA consolidation or refinance lenders, consolidating multiple advances into one payment, or negotiating a restructure. As your credit and revenue improve, you can refinance into cheaper SBA or bank debt later.

Does the new loan pay the MCA funder directly?

In a debt-refinance structure, the new lender typically disburses the payoff to the existing creditor, so the MCA funder is paid off at closing and the daily debits stop. You then make a single monthly payment on the new loan. Always confirm the payoff and UCC-lien release process with both lenders.
