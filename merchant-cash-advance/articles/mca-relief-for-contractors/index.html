Construction is a cash-flow business disguised as a building business. You buy materials and make payroll weeks—sometimes months—before a draw funds or a general contractor pays. When that gap opens at the wrong moment, a merchant cash advance is the fastest plug, and the daily debit quietly becomes a second problem layered on the first. This page covers why contractors end up in MCAs and the trade-specific ways out, as part of the broader MCA relief playbook.

Why Contractors End Up in MCAs

  • Progress billing. You bill in stages and get paid in stages—but materials and labor are needed up front, before the bill is approved.
  • Retainage. A percentage of every payment is held back until the project closes, locking up cash you've already earned.
  • Slow GC and owner pay. Net-30 to net-60 (or worse) is common, and disputes can stretch it further.
  • Front-loaded payroll. Crews get paid weekly regardless of when the draw lands.
  • Stacking. One advance rarely covers a full cycle, so contractors take another—and the daily debits pile up. See why businesses get stuck in the MCA cycle.

Fix the Cause: Factoring and a Credit Line

The reason you reached for an advance is a timing gap—so close the gap directly:

  • Invoice factoring / contract financing. Sell your unpaid progress invoices for most of their value now, instead of borrowing against future revenue. It's not new debt, and approval leans on your customers' credit. Learn the mechanics at invoice factoring.
  • Business line of credit. Revolving access you draw on for the next job's materials and payroll, then repay as draws fund. Explore a business line of credit or broader contractor financing.

Once invoices turn into usable cash and you have a credit line for materials, the pressure that drove you to the MCA eases.

Clear the Debt: Refinance or Consolidate

  • Refinance into a term loan. If the advance is current and revenue is steady, swap the daily debit for one affordable monthly payment. Follow the 30-day refinance playbook.
  • Consolidate stacked advances. Carrying several? Consolidation rolls them into one lower payment.
  • Compare the two. See consolidation vs refinancing to pick the right path.

What Contractors Should Watch For

  • Protect your bonding. An active MCA and its UCC lien can complicate surety relationships and new financing. Clearing the advance and releasing the lien strengthens your position—see MCA UCC liens and refinancing.
  • Don't stack to make payroll. Another advance deepens the cycle; factoring or a credit line is the cash-flow answer.
  • Act before default. A defaulted contractor MCA can trigger aggressive collection—see what happens if you default on an MCA.
  • Know your real cost. Convert your factor rate to an effective APR so the savings are obvious: calculate your MCA's real APR.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do contractors take MCAs?

Contractors front labor and materials long before they get paid. Progress billing, retainage held until a project closes, and slow general-contractor pay create long gaps between spending and collecting. When payroll and supplier bills come due before a draw funds, a fast MCA is an easy fix — and the high daily debit becomes its own problem.

What's the best way for a contractor to get out of an MCA?

Match the fix to the cause. Invoice factoring or contract financing turns unpaid progress invoices into cash, and a line of credit gives revolving access for the next job. To clear the existing advance, refinance into a term loan or consolidate stacked advances. Many contractors combine factoring or a credit line for cash flow with a refinance to retire the debt.

Can a contractor refinance an MCA into a term loan or line of credit?

Often yes. If the advance is current and the business has steady contract revenue and reasonable credit, the balance can be refinanced into a term loan or line of credit at a far lower cost. Acting while the advance is current, before any default, keeps more options open and protects bonding and future borrowing.

Will an MCA hurt my ability to bond or get a construction loan?

It can. An active MCA signals cash-flow stress, and its UCC lien can complicate new financing and surety relationships until cleared. Replacing the advance with a line of credit or term loan and releasing the UCC lien generally strengthens your position with lenders and sureties. Address it before it affects a bid or bond.

I stacked MCAs to keep crews paid. How do I get out?

Stacking is common in construction because one advance rarely covers a full payroll cycle. Consolidation can combine the advances into a single lower payment, while factoring or a credit line keeps cash flowing for the next draw. Act before falling behind, since defaulting can trigger aggressive collection and threaten liens and bonding.
