1. Verification or Bank Setup Holdups

MCA providers verify your bank account and, for card-based repayment, set up the split or ACH link with your processor. If verification fails (wrong routing number, account name mismatch, micro-deposit delay) or the processor integration takes longer, funding is pushed back. Some banks also place holds on new ACH debits, which can delay the first draw.

Fix: Double-check your bank account and routing numbers before submitting. Ensure the business name on the account matches your application. Complete micro-deposit verification as soon as you receive it. If you use a processor, confirm they can integrate quickly. See how to apply for a merchant cash advance for a full checklist.

2. Stacking or Capacity Review

If you have existing MCAs or other daily-remittance products, the provider may pause to review capacity. They want to confirm you can service both the existing obligation and the new advance. If they see multiple daily draws or a high total remittance relative to revenue, they may request more documents or reduce the advance—or delay while they reassess.

Fix: Disclose all existing MCA and daily-payment obligations upfront. If you’re stacking, expect extra scrutiny and possible delay. Consider paying down an existing advance before applying for a new one. See MCA mistakes that keep you in a cycle and why you’re stuck in the MCA cycle.

3. Documentation Gaps

Providers need bank statements, processing statements, and sometimes ID or proof of address. If statements are missing pages, blurry, or from the wrong period, they’ll request new ones—adding days. If you use multiple bank accounts and only provide one, they may ask for the rest. Each round of requests pushes funding back.

Fix: Submit complete, legible bank statements for the period requested (often 3—6 months). Include all accounts where business revenue deposits. If you use a processor, provide processing statements. Respond to any follow-up within 24 hours. See merchant cash advance requirements.

4. Identity Verification Stalls

Many providers use third-party identity verification (e.g., ID upload, selfie, address check). If the system flags a mismatch—address doesn’t match records, ID is unclear, or there’s a fraud alert—funding can be put on hold until it’s resolved. Manual review adds 1—3+ days.

Fix: Use a clear, current ID. Ensure your address matches what’s on file with your bank and credit. If verification fails, contact the provider immediately—don’t wait. Have backup documents (utility bill, lease) ready if they ask.

5. Slow Response to Provider Requests

Every day you take to respond to a document request or verification step adds a day to funding. Providers don’t fund until their checklist is complete. If they email you Friday and you reply Monday, you’ve lost a weekend—and possibly slipped in their queue.

Fix: Check email and portal daily. Aim to respond within 24 hours, ideally same-day. If you’re traveling or unavailable, have someone else monitor or set an auto-reply so the provider knows when to expect a response. See how fast can you get a merchant cash advance.

6. Weekend or Holiday Timing

ACH and bank processing typically don’t run on weekends or holidays. If you sign on Friday afternoon, funding often doesn’t land until Tuesday. Some providers also have cutoff times—if you sign after 2 p.m., funding may roll to the next business day. It’s not a delay per se, but it extends the wait.

Fix: Sign earlier in the week when possible. Ask the provider about cutoffs and when to expect funds. Plan around weekends and holidays so you’re not caught short. See how fast can you get a merchant cash advance for typical timelines.

7. Provider Backlog or Internal Review

During busy periods, providers can have a backlog. Your file may sit in a queue. Some deals also get flagged for manual review—e.g., higher amounts, new industries, or something that doesn’t fit their normal pattern. Internal review can add 1—5 business days.

Fix: Apply when you have time—avoid last-minute rushes. If you’ve been waiting more than a few days with no response, follow up politely. Ask if there’s anything else needed. If one provider is slow, consider whether another might move faster—get matched to reach multiple options.

What to Do Right Now

If your MCA funding is delayed: (1) Respond to every request within 24 hours. (2) Verify your bank and routing numbers; complete any micro-deposit or split setup immediately. (3) Submit complete, legible documentation. (4) Disclose existing MCAs and daily obligations. (5) Follow up if you’ve heard nothing for 2—3 days. For traps to avoid, see red flags in MCA agreements. Merchant Cash Advance: Remittance, Total Cost, and Cash-Flow Fit

MCAs purchase future receivables and collect through agreed remittance—often daily or weekly. Total cost is not the same as APR; always translate offers into total dollars repaid and calendar debits relative to your deposit cycles. If remittance collides with payroll or vendor pulls, the advance can create stress even when sales look healthy.

Underwriters evaluate card volume trends, chargebacks, existing stacked positions, and bank behavior. Disclosure quality matters: undisclosed advances discovered in review slow funding and can reduce trust.

Application Discipline

  • Complete statements: sequential months with all pages.
  • Processor verification: timely access and accurate MIDs.
  • Stacking map: every active advance with payment amounts.
  • Use of funds: specific and tied to revenue timing.

Comparing Offers and Avoiding Harmful Structures

Compare factor or total payback, remittance frequency, fees, and any reconciliation clauses. Ask what happens if sales slow. If a deal feels rushed, pause—clarity beats speed when debits are frequent.

Alternatives may fit better when eligible: working capital loans, business lines of credit, or equipment financing for asset purchases.

Post-Funding Controls and Exit Planning

After funding, monitor balances and remittance against forecast weekly. If performance weakens, communicate early. If you plan payoff or refinance, request payoff letters and coordinate sequencing when multiple positions exist.

Businesses with a defined exit plan fare better than those that roll renewals indefinitely. Treat MCAs as a bridge, not a permanent operating baseline.

Governance, Documentation, and Long-Term Strategy

Assign one owner for funder communication and keep a stipulation log with due dates. Archive executed agreements and track effective remittance start dates. After three months, review whether total cost and cash impact match expectations—if not, adjust operations or pursue refinance with a complete file.

Scenario Planning and Stress Testing

Build a simple monthly model with base and stress sales. In stress, reduce revenue 10–20% and check whether remittance still leaves room for payroll, rent, and taxes. If not, reduce the requested amount or choose a different product.

Document assumptions and revisit them monthly. Revolving and remittance-based products amplify operational variance—small changes in sales can swing liquidity quickly without proactive monitoring.

Verification and Data Mismatches

Delays frequently trace to mismatches between stated volume and verified processor or bank data. Mid changes, recent processor migrations, or split processing across multiple accounts can confuse reconciliation. Funders may pause to confirm ownership of accounts, authorized signers, and the exact flow of card settlements into bank deposits.

Bank login verification sometimes fails when credentials expire, MFA blocks third-party access, or business profiles at the bank are outdated. Updating signers and resolving access issues before submission reduces round trips.

Stacking, Payoff Letters, and Third Parties

If payoffs of existing advances are part of the funding plan, delays often occur while waiting for payoff letters or while coordinating wire timing. Multiple positions may require sequential payoffs; missing a wire window can push closing by days. Brokers and ISOs can add coordination overhead when communication chains are long.

Legal, Tax, and Exception Items

Outstanding tax issues, UCC filings, or litigation can trigger legal review. Seasonal businesses may need additional months of statements to establish pattern stability. International ownership structures or complex corporate hierarchies can also extend timelines. Providing organized documentation upfront is the most reliable way to shorten delays.

Incomplete Applications and Missing Pages

Bank and processor statements frequently arrive missing pages or summary sections. Underwriters need continuous months without gaps. PDF exports directly from banking portals, with every page numbered, reduce resubmission cycles. If you operate multiple locations or MIDs, include a cover sheet that maps each account to its role in deposit flow.

Fraud Review and High-Risk Patterns

Sudden spikes in average ticket, unusual geographic concentration, or elevated keyed transactions can trigger fraud holds. Respond promptly with invoices, delivery evidence, or customer communication logs where appropriate. Silence extends holds.

Calendar Effects and Holiday Windows

Funding around bank holidays shifts wire and ACH windows. Build buffer into vendor deadlines so a one-day slip does not cascade into operational problems.

Signer Availability and Legal Review

Delays happen when required signers travel without access to notaries or when entity documents are stale. Maintain current articles, operating agreements, and authorized signer lists in a shared folder so submissions stay complete.

Prior Defaults and Workout History

Prior workouts or forbearance on other obligations may require narrative explanation and supporting correspondence. Organized histories shorten legal review.

International Wires and Correspondent Banks

Cross-border ownership or offshore accounts add compliance steps. Build extra days into funding timelines when those elements appear.

Underwriter Capacity and Queue Effects

Peak seasonality in small-business lending can lengthen internal queues even when your file is clean. Submit early in the week when possible and confirm receipt of each document batch. Ask for a checklist of outstanding items so you are not waiting on unstated requests.

Insurance Certificates and Landlord Consents

Some funders request evidence of insurance or landlord acknowledgment for collateral-dependent deals. Missing COIs or expired policies add days. Keep renewals on calendar and upload current certificates promptly.

ACH and Micro-Deposit Verification

Bank verification micro-deposits can fail when account filters block small credits. Whitelist verification sources and confirm trial deposit completion the same day.

Third-Party Verification Vendors

Some funders use external verification services for bank or processor data. Vendor outages or API errors can pause files through no fault of the applicant. Maintain alternate contact paths and respond quickly to re-auth requests.

Corporate Resolutions and Board Approvals

Entities that require board or member votes for borrowing should complete resolutions before submission. Retroactive approvals after terms are set can invalidate timelines.

Title Companies and Payoff Coordination

When UCC or lien releases involve third-party title services, add time for their processing. Confirm payoff amounts the morning wires send to avoid short payments that reset timelines.

Disaster and Business Interruption Context

Recent disasters or forced closures may require narrative context and insurance claim documentation. Underwriters separate temporary disruption from structural decline when evidence is clear.

Duplicate Submissions and Conflicting Applications

Multiple applications through different channels can create conflicting information in lender systems. Coordinate so amounts, ownership percentages, and use of funds match across submissions. Inconsistencies trigger manual reconciliation and extend timelines.

Document Expiration and Re-Verification

IDs and some business licenses expire. If a funding window crosses a renewal date, upload refreshed documents before they expire to avoid reverification holds.

Name Normalization and DBAs

Deposits under a DBA must map cleanly to the borrowing entity name on the application. Provide assumed-name certificates where required so verification does not stall on identity matching.

Holiday and Payroll Week Conflicts

Biweekly payroll on bank holidays can shift ACH timing. Note these effects when explaining week-to-week balance variance.

Internal Review Escalations

Files that exceed standard approval authority may require secondary credit or risk committee review. These escalations add time but are normal for larger amounts or non-standard industries. Ask for expected escalation timelines when your file routes upward.

Small documentation gaps often create outsized calendar impact; fix the smallest missing item first.
