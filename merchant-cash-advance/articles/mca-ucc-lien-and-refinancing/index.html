If you've tried to refinance an MCA or get a new loan and been told there's a "lien in the way," this is almost always a UCC filing from your advance. UCC liens are routine in the MCA world—but they're also the quiet reason a lot of refinance attempts stall. This page explains what the lien is, why it blocks new financing, exactly how it gets released, and what to do about a stale lien on an advance you already paid off. It pairs with the broader MCA relief playbook.

What a UCC Lien Is

A UCC-1 financing statement is a public filing—made with the secretary of state—that puts the world on notice that a creditor has a security interest in some or all of a business's assets. Because an MCA is structured as a purchase of future receivables, funders file a UCC-1 to establish their claim on those receivables. The filing isn't a lawsuit or a judgment; it's a notice of a secured interest, and it's standard across the industry.

The key variable is scope. Some filings are narrow; many MCAs file a blanket lien covering essentially all business assets—receivables, inventory, equipment, the lot. Blanket scope is what turns a routine filing into a refinancing obstacle.

How It Blocks New Financing

When you apply for a term loan, line of credit, or even invoice factoring, the prospective lender runs a UCC search as part of underwriting. If it finds an existing blanket MCA lien, it sees that another creditor already claims first position on your assets. Most lenders want first position themselves, so they will either:

  • Decline until the MCA lien is cleared, or
  • Require the lien be released or subordinated as a condition of closing.

This is why a refinance that "should" work can stall: the new money is available, but the old lien is in the way. The fix is to coordinate the payoff and release as part of the deal—exactly what a good refinance process does. See the 30-day refinance playbook.

The Payoff-to-Release Process

  1. Get a written payoff letter from the MCA funder stating the amount to satisfy the advance.
  2. The new lender pays the funder directly at closing (in a refinance), or you pay it off from another source.
  3. The funder files a UCC-3 termination removing its UCC-1 from the public record.
  4. The lien clears and your new lender takes its position. Release commonly takes about two to four weeks after payoff, so build that into the timeline.

Because the release isn't instant, lenders sometimes fund into escrow pending confirmation, or close on a written commitment that the funder will terminate. Coordinating this is part of why working through a marketplace helps—the payoff and release get sequenced rather than left to chance.

Stale and "Zombie" Liens

Sometimes an advance is paid off but the funder never files the UCC-3 termination. The lien sits on the public record even though the debt is gone—a "stale" or "zombie" lien—and it can block your next loan as if the advance were still outstanding. To handle it:

  • Keep proof of payoff. Save the payoff letter and confirmation of payment.
  • Request the termination in writing and follow up to confirm the UCC-3 was actually filed.
  • Run your own UCC search before applying for new financing so there are no surprises in underwriting.
  • Escalate if needed. If a funder won't terminate a lien securing a satisfied debt, an attorney can help compel it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do MCA funders file a UCC lien?

An MCA is structured as a purchase of future receivables, and funders file a UCC-1 to publicly establish their claim on those receivables and often broader business assets. It puts other lenders on notice of the funder's secured interest and is standard practice across the MCA industry.

How does an MCA UCC lien block new financing?

Many MCAs file a blanket UCC-1 covering all business assets. When you apply for a new loan, the lender runs a UCC search, sees the lien, and may decline or require it cleared because it wants first position. A blanket MCA lien can block a term loan, line of credit, or invoice factoring until it is subordinated or released.

How do I get an MCA UCC lien released?

The lien is released when the advance is paid off and the funder files a UCC-3 termination. In a refinance, the new lender typically pays the funder directly at closing and the funder files the termination shortly after. Release commonly takes about two to four weeks after payoff, so coordinate timing between the old funder and new lender.

What is a UCC-3 termination statement?

A UCC-3 amends or terminates an existing UCC-1. When an MCA is paid off, the funder files a UCC-3 termination to remove its lien from the public record, clearing the way for new financing. If a paid-off advance's lien is never terminated, it becomes a stale lien that can still block future borrowing.

What if a paid-off MCA's UCC lien is still showing?

Stale liens happen when a funder fails to file the UCC-3 after payoff. Keep your payoff letter, request written confirmation the funder will terminate, then follow up to confirm the UCC-3 was filed. If the funder is unresponsive, an attorney can help compel termination of a lien securing an already-satisfied debt.
