Merchant Cash Advance Requirements: What You Need to Qualify

Card volume, bank deposits, time in business, and what MCA providers look for

← Back to Merchant Cash Advance Articles

Last updated: March 18, 2026

Merchant cash advance (MCA) requirements focus on your business’s ability to repay through a share of daily or weekly revenue—usually card sales or bank deposits. Providers typically want to see meaningful monthly volume, a few months in business, and bank statements (and sometimes processing statements). Credit is often less critical than revenue consistency. Quick Answer: Typical MCA Requirements

Most MCA providers look for:

  • Card volume or bank deposits: often $5,000–$10,000+ per month (minimums vary)
  • Time in business: commonly 3–6+ months; some accept newer businesses
  • Bank statements: 3–6 months to verify deposits and behavior
  • Application and ID: completed application and owner ID
  • Credit: often secondary; many accept 500+ or lower when volume is strong

1) Card Volume or Bank Deposits

The main requirement is verifiable revenue that can support the daily or weekly remittance. For card-based MCAs, providers look at monthly card sales; for ACH-based advances, they use bank deposit history. Minimums vary—often $5,000–$10,000 per month—and higher, consistent volume typically supports larger advances and sometimes better factor rates. For how amounts are calculated, see how much you can qualify for with a merchant cash advance.

2) Time in Business

Many providers want 3–6+ months of history so they can see consistency. Newer businesses may qualify with strong volume but might get smaller advances or shorter terms. Longer history can improve advance size and pricing.

3) Bank Statements

Providers use bank statements to verify deposits, check for overdrafts, and assess banking behavior. Provide 3–6 months, all pages, no gaps. Frequent overdrafts or declining deposits can reduce approval or amount. Clean statements help.

4) Credit (Often Secondary)

MCA underwriting emphasizes revenue and remittance capacity more than credit. Many providers accept 500+ FICO or lower when volume is strong. Credit can still affect factor rate or advance size. For details, see what credit score is needed for a merchant cash advance.

5) Application and ID

You’ll complete an application (business name, ownership, revenue, use of funds) and provide government-issued ID. Some providers request merchant processing statements if repayment is tied to card splits. For the process, see how to apply for a merchant cash advance.

Requirements by Industry

MCAs are common in retail, restaurants, and service businesses with strong card or deposit volume. Requirements can vary by industry because of seasonality or risk. See MCA for restaurants, MCA for retail stores, and MCA for auto repair shops for industry-specific context.

MCA vs Working Capital Loan: How Requirements Differ

Working capital loans often require stronger credit and more documentation (e.g. tax returns for larger amounts) but can offer lower cost and fixed payments. MCAs prioritize volume and speed with less emphasis on credit. Compare in merchant cash advance vs working capital loan. If you have strong credit and predictable cash flow, a working capital loan or line of credit may be cheaper long-term.

Red Flags and Traps

MCA cost is driven by factor rate and holdback; terms can be complex. Before you sign, understand total repayment, daily/weekly remittance, and what happens if you pay early. See red flags in MCA agreements and MCA mistakes that keep you in a cycle. Compare with revenue-based financing vs MCA if you want a different repayment structure.

Pre-Application Checklist

Before applying: (1) Confirm monthly card volume or deposits meet the provider’s minimum. (2) Gather 3–6 months of bank statements (all pages). (3) Have a clear use of funds. (4) Understand factor rate, holdback, and total repayment. (5) Check for existing MCAs or daily remittances that could limit capacity. (6) Compare providers for cost and terms so you avoid traps. For speed, see how fast you can get a merchant cash advance.

Common Reasons MCA Applications Are Declined

Declines often happen when: monthly volume or deposits are below the provider’s minimum, statements show frequent overdrafts or declining deposits, the business is too new with no track record, or there are already multiple advances or daily remittances that would overextend cash flow. Cleaning up bank statements, waiting for a few stronger months, or paying down existing advances can help. If you need lower-cost options, compare MCA vs working capital loan and RBF vs MCA.

Example: How Daily Remittance Affects Cash Flow

Suppose you take a $30,000 advance with a 1.25 factor rate (repay $37,500) and a 15% daily holdback from card sales. If you do $1,000 in card sales per day, $150 goes to the provider daily. At that rate you’d repay in about 250 days. If sales drop to $500/day, you still pay 15% ($75/day), which is a higher share of revenue and can strain operations. That’s why understanding holdback percentage and your lowest-volume periods matters before you sign. See red flags in MCA agreements and MCA mistakes that keep you in a cycle.

Bottom Line

Merchant cash advance requirements center on verifiable card volume or bank deposits, a few months in business, and bank statements. Credit is often secondary.
