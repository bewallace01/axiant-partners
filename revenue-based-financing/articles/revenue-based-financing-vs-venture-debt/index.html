Revenue-Based Financing vs Venture Debt

Both fund growth without selling much equity—but one needs VC backing and warrants, and the other just needs revenue. How RBF and venture debt compare, and which fits your stage

Quick answer

Revenue-based financing (RBF) repays as a percentage of revenue, carries no warrants, and doesn't require venture-capital backing—it's fully non-dilutive. Venture debt is a term loan typically offered to VC-backed startups, repaid on a fixed schedule with interest, and usually includes warrants (a small equity stake for the lender). RBF avoids both warrants and the VC prerequisite and is available on your revenue; venture debt can offer larger amounts to companies with strong investor backing and runway. Your stage and whether you're VC-backed largely decide the fit.

Founders who want growth capital without giving up big chunks of equity usually land on two options: revenue-based financing or venture debt. They sound similar—both are alternatives to a priced equity round—but they have very different prerequisites and costs. This page compares them so you can match the right one to your stage. For the basics, see what revenue-based financing is and how it works.

The Core Differences

  • Who can get it. RBF qualifies on your revenue, so bootstrapped and non-VC-backed companies can access it. Venture debt generally requires that you've already raised institutional equity—lenders lean on your VCs' backing and the runway it implies.
  • How you repay. RBF flexes with monthly revenue and you repay a fixed total. Venture debt is a fixed amortizing schedule with interest, regardless of how the month goes.
  • Equity cost. RBF is non-dilutive with no warrants. Venture debt is debt, but it commonly carries warrants—a small, future equity stake—so it has a modest dilutive component.
  • Size. Venture debt facilities can be larger, sized to extend runway between equity rounds; RBF amounts scale with revenue.

When Each One Wins

  • Choose RBF when you have real revenue, want fully non-dilutive capital with no warrants, aren't VC-backed (or don't want to depend on it), and prefer payments that flex with sales. It's a natural fit for SaaS and D2C businesses. See RBF requirements.
  • Choose venture debt when you're already venture-backed, need a larger facility to extend runway between equity rounds, and are comfortable with warrants and a fixed repayment schedule.

RBF can also reduce how much equity you need to raise at all—letting revenue, not dilution, fund growth. Know the limits too: when RBF is not the right option (notably, pre-revenue companies).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between RBF and venture debt?

RBF repays as a percentage of monthly revenue, carries no warrants, and is available on your revenue without requiring VC backing. Venture debt is a term loan for venture-backed startups, repaid on a fixed schedule with interest, and usually includes warrants. RBF avoids warrants and the VC prerequisite; venture debt can offer larger amounts to backed companies.

Does RBF require venture-capital backing?

No. RBF qualifies primarily on your revenue and its consistency, so it's available to bootstrapped and non-VC-backed companies, including many SaaS and e-commerce businesses. Venture debt is generally extended to startups that have already raised institutional equity.

Does venture debt dilute my ownership?

Venture debt is debt, not equity, but it usually includes warrants that let the lender buy a small amount of equity later, so it carries a modest dilutive component. RBF is generally non-dilutive with no warrants, so you keep full ownership. If avoiding any dilution is a priority, RBF has an edge.

Which is better for a SaaS company?

It depends on stage and backing. A SaaS company with strong, predictable recurring revenue wanting flexible, non-dilutive capital without raising equity often fits RBF well. A venture-backed SaaS company needing a larger facility to extend runway between rounds, comfortable with warrants and fixed repayment, may prefer venture debt.

Can RBF replace another equity round?

Often yes, if you have revenue. RBF and similar non-dilutive options let revenue-generating companies fund growth without selling equity, extending time between rounds or reducing how much you raise. It's not a fit for pre-revenue companies, where equity or venture debt tied to a round may be more appropriate.
