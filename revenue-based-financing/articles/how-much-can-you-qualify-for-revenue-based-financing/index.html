What Impacts Your Funding Limit?

Revenue Consistency

Stable monthly deposits, limited volatility, and a predictable customer base support higher qualification. Erratic revenue may reduce the multiplier or cap.

Growth Trajectory

Positive growth often supports larger amounts and better terms. Declining revenue can reduce approval size, timing, or structure.

Gross Margins

Margin strength influences revenue-share capacity, funding docs, and repayment cap. Stronger margins typically support higher amounts.

Credit Profile

Credit score (550+ preferred) affects maximum funding and repayment caps. See credit score requirements for revenue-based financing for typical tiers.

Existing Debt Obligations

Current debt reduces available revenue share capacity and perceived repayment capacity. Lenders consider existing obligations when sizing new funding.

Revenue Share Percentage & Total Repayment

Key influences: revenue share percentage (often 3-10%), total repayment cap, and estimated repayment duration. Higher qualification amounts may come with adjusted share percentages or caps. Balancing growth needs with sustainable repayment is critical.

How to Increase Your Qualification Amount

  • Improve revenue consistency
  • Strengthen credit profile
  • Reduce short-term debt where possible
  • Maintain clean banking activity
  • Improve customer retention and recurring revenue

Minimum Funding Amount

Most programs have a $10,000 minimum. Below that threshold, RBF is typically not offered.

When Larger Amounts Are Possible

Larger funding is more common for:

  • Established SaaS companies
  • High-growth e-commerce brands
  • Subscription-based businesses
  • Companies with strong recurring revenue models

A worked example: from revenue to offer

Say a business runs $120,000 in average monthly revenue with steady deposits and a year of history. On a common 1.5x monthly-revenue model a funder might offer around $180,000; on a 10–30% of annual revenue model (roughly $1.44M annualized) the range lands near $145,000–$430,000 — so a realistic offer sits in the $150,000–$250,000 zone, with the top reserved for strong consistency and credit. The funder then sets a repayment cap (often 1.2–1.5x the advance) and a revenue-share percentage; at a 1.35 cap, $200,000 advanced is repaid as $270,000, collected as a fixed slice of each month’s sales until the cap is met.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much revenue do I need to qualify for revenue-based financing?

Most funders want consistent monthly revenue — often a minimum around $10,000–$15,000 a month — plus a few months of history. The more stable and higher your deposits, the larger the offer.

How fast can revenue-based financing fund?

Because approval leans on bank-verified revenue rather than heavy underwriting, many RBF advances fund within a few business days of a complete application.

Is revenue-based financing the same as a merchant cash advance?

They are close cousins — both advance capital repaid from revenue against a capped total — but RBF usually uses monthly revenue and targets recurring-revenue and online businesses, often with more transparent terms than a daily-remittance MCA.

What can I use revenue-based financing for?

RBF suits growth spending that scales with revenue — inventory, marketing, hiring, or equipment — rather than covering a structural loss. Because repayment flexes with sales, it fits seasonal or uneven cash flow better than a fixed loan payment.

Will revenue-based financing show up on my credit report?

Often it does not report to the consumer credit bureaus the way a term loan can, because it is structured as a purchase of future revenue rather than a traditional loan. The funder will still verify your bank activity, however, and may file a UCC lien that other business lenders can see when you apply elsewhere.
