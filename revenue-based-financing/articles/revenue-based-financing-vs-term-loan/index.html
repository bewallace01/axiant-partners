Revenue-Based Financing vs Term Loan: Which Fits Your Business?

One flexes your payment with sales; the other is a fixed payment at a lower cost. How RBF and a term loan compare on cost, flexibility, qualification, and risk—and when each wins

Quick answer

Revenue-based financing (RBF) repays as a percentage of monthly revenue—flexing up in strong months and down in slow ones—while a term loan is a fixed payment at a stated rate. RBF prioritizes flexibility and easier, faster qualification but usually costs more (a flat fee that doesn't shrink if you repay early). A term loan prioritizes predictability and lower total cost but has a stricter bar. Choose RBF for variable, seasonal, or recurring revenue (SaaS, subscription e-commerce); choose a term loan for predictable cash flow funding a defined project at the lowest cost.

Both products give you a lump sum to grow, but they repay in fundamentally different ways—and that difference should drive your choice. The Core Difference: Flexible vs Fixed

  • RBF ties repayment to a percentage of your monthly revenue. You repay a fixed total (the advance plus a flat fee), but when you pay it flexes with sales—more in good months, less in slow ones. There's no fixed due amount that can crush a bad month.
  • Term loan gives you a lump sum repaid in fixed installments at a stated interest rate over a set term, regardless of how the business performs that month.

Cost: Why the Term Loan Usually Wins

A term loan is priced as an interest rate over time, so a faster payoff means less interest. RBF charges a flat amount on top of the advance that generally doesn't shrink if you repay early—you're buying flexibility and easier access, and that premium is real. If you can qualify for a term loan and the lowest total cost is your priority, the term loan typically comes out ahead. When Each One Wins

  • Choose RBF when your revenue is strong but variable or seasonal, you run a recurring-revenue model like SaaS or subscription D2C, you want payments that scale with sales, and you'd rather not pledge heavy collateral or give up equity. It's often faster and more accessible than a bank loan—see RBF requirements.
  • Choose a term loan when you have predictable cash flow, can qualify, and are funding a defined project where the lowest cost matters more than payment flexibility.

Also know the limits: when RBF is not the right option.

Many Businesses Use Both Over Time

These aren't mutually exclusive across a company's life. A common path is to use RBF for speed and flexible payments early, then refinance into a lower-cost term loan or line of credit as revenue, credit, and operating history strengthen—capturing accessibility now and lower cost later.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between RBF and a term loan?

RBF is repaid as a percentage of monthly revenue, so the payment flexes with sales and you repay a fixed total rather than interest over a schedule. A term loan is a lump sum repaid in fixed installments at a stated rate regardless of performance. RBF prioritizes flexibility; a term loan prioritizes predictability and usually lower cost.

Is RBF cheaper than a term loan?

Usually no. A term loan typically has a lower total cost because it's priced as interest over time, while RBF charges a flat amount that doesn't decrease if you repay faster. You pay for RBF's flexibility and easier qualification. If you can qualify for a term loan and value lowest cost, the term loan often wins.

When should I choose RBF over a term loan?

RBF fits businesses with strong but variable or seasonal revenue, recurring-revenue models like SaaS and subscription e-commerce, and companies wanting payments that scale with sales without heavy collateral or equity. It's often faster and more accessible than a bank loan for younger companies. A term loan fits established businesses with predictable cash flow funding a defined project.

Does RBF require collateral or equity?

RBF is generally non-dilutive, so you don't give up equity, and it usually relies on revenue history rather than heavy hard-asset collateral, though terms vary. A term loan may require collateral and a personal guarantee depending on the lender and amount. If avoiding dilution and minimizing collateral matter, RBF can be attractive — but compare total cost.

Can I refinance RBF into a term loan later?

Often yes. Many businesses use RBF for speed and flexibility early, then refinance into a lower-cost term loan or line of credit as revenue, credit, and operating history strengthen. Planning that path lets you capture RBF's accessibility now and a term loan's lower cost later.
