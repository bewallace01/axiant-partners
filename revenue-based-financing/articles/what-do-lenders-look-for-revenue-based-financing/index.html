1. Monthly Revenue Volume

The first screen is usually top-line revenue. Lenders often request recent bank statements and revenue reports to confirm baseline monthly volume. Revenue volume directly influences how much you can qualify for.

  • Consistency of deposits
  • Sufficient revenue size for requested funding
  • Seasonal behavior understanding

2. Revenue Stability and Trends

Trend quality matters as much as absolute volume.

  • Month-over-month consistency
  • Growth trajectory or stability
  • Volatility and concentration risk

3. Gross Margin and Cash Flow Quality

Because repayment is tied to revenue, lenders still evaluate underlying cash flow health and margin profile.

  • Ability to support revenue-share payments
  • Operating margin resilience
  • Working capital pressure points

4. Banking Activity

RBF underwriting often includes detailed bank statement analysis.

  • Deposit consistency
  • Overdraft frequency
  • Predictability of inflows
  • Overall cash management behavior

5. Time in Business

Most programs prefer businesses with operating history, often 1-2+ years, since longer track records reduce forecast uncertainty.

6. Credit Profile

Credit is reviewed, though it is usually secondary to revenue performance.

  • Repayment behavior
  • Recent derogatory events
  • Overall risk discipline

7. Industry Type

Certain sectors are favored due to predictable recurring revenue behavior. Highly cyclical or unstable sectors may receive tighter terms.

8. Customer Base Diversification

Businesses with diversified customer revenue streams generally underwrite more favorably than highly concentrated accounts.

What Matters Most?

In most RBF underwriting files, priority generally follows:

  • Monthly revenue volume
  • Revenue consistency and trend
  • Cash flow and margin quality
  • Time in business
  • Credit profile

Traditional collateral is usually less central than in asset-based lending.

Minimum Funding Amount

Revenue-based financing commonly starts around $10,000 and scales based on recurring revenue performance and risk profile.

How the Factors Combine in a Real File

Underwriters do not score these eight factors in isolation — they read them together to judge whether your future revenue will reliably cover repayment. A business with moderate time in business and a 640 credit score can still earn a strong offer if monthly revenue is high, deposits are consistent, and the customer base is diversified. Conversely, a long-established business with a great score can be capped or declined if its revenue is volatile, its margins are thin, or one client represents most of its sales.

The practical takeaway: a single weak factor rarely sinks a file, but two or three weak factors stacked together do. If your revenue is strong and steady, that one strength offsets a lot. If it is lumpy, expect the other factors to be scrutinized harder.

Red Flags That Weaken a File

Just as important as what lenders want to see is what makes them pull back:

  • Frequent negative days or overdrafts — the clearest signal that current cash flow is already stretched.
  • Stacked advances — multiple existing daily or weekly debits competing for the same revenue.
  • Sharp recent revenue decline — a downward trend matters more than a single strong month.
  • Heavy transfers in and out that make true operating revenue hard to verify.
  • Customer concentration — losing one account would erase the revenue the advance depends on.

Most of these are fixable. A few months of clean, positive banking and a pause on taking new advances will often move a borderline file into approval.

Final Thoughts

RBF approval is primarily revenue-driven. If your business has stable monthly deposits and predictable top-line performance, you may qualify even without traditional collateral-heavy structure. Review available revenue-based financing options and compare fit by profile before applying.

Merchant cash advances are RBF's closest cousin and often cheaper for short-term gaps.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do lenders look for in revenue-based financing?

Mainly monthly revenue volume and its stability, gross margin and cash-flow quality, banking activity, time in business, and to a lesser extent credit. Consistent, healthy deposits matter most.

What matters most for RBF approval?

Revenue consistency. Steady, predictable monthly deposits with limited volatility support the largest offers, while erratic revenue lowers both the amount and the terms even if the average is high.

Does credit matter for revenue-based financing?

It matters less than for a bank loan but is not ignored. Strong revenue can offset a weaker score, while serious derogatory marks can still cap the offer or raise the cost.

What red flags weaken an RBF file?

Declining revenue, frequent negative days or overdrafts, heavy existing remittances from stacked advances, and customer concentration. Cleaning those up before applying improves the offer.
