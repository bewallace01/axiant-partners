SBA loans are guaranteed by the SBA, but the rate, fees, speed, and underwriting standards vary materially by lender. The same business can get a 9.75% rate from one preferred lender and a 12% rate from another for the same $500K 7(a) deal — or get declined entirely by one and approved by another. This guide ranks the top SBA lenders in 2026 by category and explains how to pick. For program details see SBA loans and typical SBA loan rates.

Top SBA 7(a) Lenders

The 7(a) is the most common SBA loan. By annual volume (FY2024 SBA data, extrapolated to 2026):

  • Live Oak Bank (Wilmington, NC): #1 by 7(a) dollar volume for over a decade. Specialist in industry verticals — veterinary, dental, optometry, funeral, self-storage, wine & spirits, hospitality, government contracting. If you’re in one of their target industries, Live Oak is usually the fastest and most competitive option. Online application, no branch network. Min loan ~$150K, sweet spot $500K–$5M.
  • Huntington National Bank: #1 by 7(a) unit count. Strong general small business lender, dominant in Midwest. Excellent on Express loans (sub-$500K) where they can move quickly. Multi-state branch network helps for relationship pricing.
  • Newtek Bank: Non-bank turned bank (acquired in 2023), Preferred Lender. Specializes in $50K–$750K deals where speed matters more than the cheapest rate. Higher rate tolerance — will approve credit profiles that traditional banks decline. Often the right answer when bank lenders pass.
  • Wells Fargo: Large national lender, particularly active in 7(a) and 504. Best for existing Wells Fargo business banking customers — relationship pricing is meaningful.
  • JPMorgan Chase: Top-10 by volume, strongest for existing Chase business banking customers. Less competitive for new relationships.
  • U.S. Bank: Top-10 by volume, particularly strong on West Coast and Mountain West. Good for owner-occupied CRE deals.
  • Readycap Lending: Non-bank Preferred Lender. Active in small-balance 7(a) and 504. Faster than most banks on smaller deals.

Top SBA 504 Lenders

504 deals have two pieces: the CDC (which handles the SBA portion) and the bank (which handles the first mortgage). The CDC is usually local; the bank can be anywhere. Top first-mortgage banks for 504 in 2026:

  • Wells Fargo: Largest 504 first-mortgage bank in most years. Competitive rates, strong on standard owner-occupied CRE.
  • Byline Bank (Chicago): Top-5 by 504 volume nationally. Strong on multi-state owner-occupied real estate, particularly industrial and flex space.
  • Celtic Bank (Salt Lake City): Aggressive 504 first-mortgage pricing. Strong online application process. Good for borrowers without a local bank relationship.
  • Live Oak Bank: 504 specialist for their target verticals (vet, dental, hospitality). Will do both 7(a) and 504 in-house.
  • Mountain America Credit Union, Greater Nevada Credit Union, others: Regional credit unions are often the most competitive 504 first-mortgage source if you’re in their footprint. Always worth checking.

For the CDC piece, work with the local CDC in your project state (each state has several). The CDC pricing is similar everywhere because it’s set by the SBA debenture market — pick on responsiveness, not rate.

Top SBA Express Lenders

Express loans (max $500K) trade rate for speed. Top lenders:

  • Huntington National Bank: Largest SBA Express lender by volume. Streamlined process, good for $25K–$350K deals.
  • Wells Fargo / Bank of America / Chase: All three offer Express to existing business deposit customers. Relationship pricing.
  • Newtek: Active in Express for borrowers who don’t fit traditional bank credit boxes.
  • Lendio / SBA marketplaces: Useful for finding Express lenders who’ll fund credit profiles the big banks reject.

Best SBA Lenders by Industry

Industry expertise matters because SBA underwriters at industry-specialist lenders understand the business model:

  • Veterinary, dental, optometry, medical practice acquisition: Live Oak Bank, Bank of America Practice Solutions, U.S. Bank Practice Finance.
  • Hotels and hospitality: Live Oak, BankFinancial, Stearns Bank.
  • Restaurants and franchises: Huntington, Live Oak (for select franchise concepts), Wells Fargo.
  • Self-storage: Live Oak, ReadyCap, Byline Bank.
  • Manufacturing: Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank, regional community banks with manufacturing expertise.
  • Gas stations and c-stores: Newtek, Stearns Bank, regional specialists.
  • Franchise acquisition: Live Oak (for vetted franchise systems), Huntington, Wells Fargo. See SBA franchise acquisition.
  • Business acquisition (non-franchise): Live Oak, Newtek, Byline Bank, regional banks with M&A experience. See SBA business acquisition.

Bank vs Non-Bank SBA Lenders

Both bank and non-bank lenders can be SBA Preferred Lenders (PLP status), meaning they have delegated authority to approve loans without case-by-case SBA review. Key differences:

  • Banks (Live Oak, Wells, Chase, Huntington, etc.): Generally lower rates (price at or below SBA cap), can offer deposit relationships, slower on edge-of-credit-box deals.
  • Non-bank lenders (Newtek pre-2023, Readycap, others): Higher rate tolerance, faster on smaller deals, useful when banks decline. Typically price at the cap or near it.
  • Decision rule: Apply to a bank first if you have a clean credit profile. If you get pushback or slow responses, expand to non-bank Preferred Lenders. Don’t start with non-bank if a bank will say yes — you’ll overpay.

How to Pick the Right SBA Lender

Decision framework:

  • Have you already banked with a Preferred Lender for 2+ years? Start there. Relationship pricing is real.
  • Is your industry one Live Oak specializes in? Apply to Live Oak in parallel. They often beat the bank on rate and speed.
  • Is your loan under $500K and you need it fast? Newtek + Huntington Express in parallel.
  • Are you buying real estate? 504 with Wells Fargo, Byline, Celtic, or a local credit union as first mortgage + local CDC.
  • Are you uncertain about your credit profile? Apply through a marketplace that submits to 3–5 Preferred Lenders in parallel. You’ll see who’s willing and at what rate without committing to one lender first.

Red Flags When Choosing an SBA Lender

  • Upfront fees before commitment: Legitimate SBA lenders don’t charge upfront packaging fees before issuing a commitment letter. Some brokers do — clarify what you’re paying for.
  • “Guaranteed approval”: No SBA lender can guarantee approval until full underwriting completes. Walk away from anyone who says otherwise.
  • Pressure to skip 504 in favor of 7(a) for real estate: Some lenders steer real estate deals to 7(a) because it’s easier paperwork. 504 is usually cheaper for owner-occupied CRE — insist on running the numbers both ways.
  • No clear PLP status: Ask if the lender is a Preferred Lender. Non-PLP SBA lenders require SBA case-by-case review, adding 2–4 weeks to closing.
  • Vague rate quotes: A real quote includes prime spread, fees, and an estimated APR. “Our rate is competitive” is not a quote.
