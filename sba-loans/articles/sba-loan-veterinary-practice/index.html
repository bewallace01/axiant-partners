Veterinary Practice Loans: SBA Financing to Buy, Build or Equip a Practice

Loan sizes, down payments, equipment and real estate, and what lenders look for when a veterinarian buys or opens a practice

← Back to SBA Loans Articles All Articles

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

Most veterinary practice loans are SBA 7(a) loans: up to $5 million at about 10%, usually with 10-15% down and a 10-year term, covering the purchase price, equipment, build-out and working capital in one loan. Add an SBA 504 if you are buying the building (10% down). A solo general practice typically borrows $300,000-$700,000; a new practice usually needs 15-20% down. Buying a $600,000 practice with 10% down is about $7,136 a month at 10% over 10 years.

Veterinary practices are a good fit for SBA loans. Pet owners keep coming back, which makes revenue steady; the practice has real collateral in imaging, surgical and lab equipment, and often the building; and cash flow is predictable enough to support a 10-year loan. Lenders that do medical and professional practice financing already understand the model.

Practices like yours often price 25-50 basis points below the SBA rate cap (see SBA loan rates in 2026). Equipment rates follow your credit: about 7-9% APR at 720+ with two years in business, up to 16-22% at 580-639.

SBA 7(a) to buy a veterinary practice

The 7(a) is the main program for buying an existing practice. One loan can cover:

  • The purchase price (goodwill, patient records, non-compete)
  • Equipment (X-ray, surgical, lab, exam tables)
  • Build-out or renovation
  • Working capital for payroll, inventory and the transition

Lenders look at the practice's historical financials, the seller's transition plan and your experience. A seller note or a seller who stays on for a transition period both help. Compare SBA 7(a) vs 504 for when the 504 applies.

SBA 504 for the clinic building

If you are buying the building your practice occupies, the 504 splits it into a 50% bank first mortgage, a 40% CDC loan at a long fixed rate, and your 10%. Use it when the real estate is the main need; use the 7(a) when you also need the practice and working capital. See SBA loans for commercial property.

Equipment financing for vet clinics

Digital X-ray, ultrasound, surgical lasers, an in-house lab or anesthesia machines can go into a 7(a) or into a standalone equipment loan. Because the equipment secures the loan, approvals often come in 24-72 hours and lower credit can work. See equipment financing vs SBA loan, or medical and dental equipment financing for similar imaging and lab assets.

How Much Can You Borrow to Buy a Veterinary Practice?

Specialty or emergency practice$1 million-$3+ million
New build or significant build-out$500,000-$2+ million

These ranges include the purchase price, equipment and often working capital; buying the building adds to the loan. See SBA down payment requirements for equity expectations.

A worked example: you buy a solo practice for $600,000 and put 10% ($60,000) down. The $540,000 7(a) loan is about $7,136 a month at 10% over 10 years. A $1.2 million loan for a multi-doctor practice would be about $15,858 a month on the same terms. Lenders want the practice's cash flow to cover the payment at least 1.25 times.

What Do Lenders Need From a Veterinarian?

  • Credit: 660-680+ FICO preferred. See what credit score you need for an SBA loan.
  • Down payment: 10-15% on an acquisition; 10% on 504 real estate.
  • Experience: a DVM and clinical experience; practice management experience helps.
  • Cash flow: DSCR of 1.25x or higher, shown by the existing practice's financials.

A new graduate buying a practice may need a larger down payment, a co-signer or seller financing. See what lenders look for in SBA approval.

Documents for a veterinary practice loan

  • Practice valuation or purchase agreement
  • 2-3 years of practice tax returns and P&L
  • Equipment list and values
  • Seller transition agreement (if applicable)
  • Lease or real estate documents (if applicable)
  • License and professional credentials

Can You Get a Loan to Start a New Vet Practice?

Yes, but it is harder than buying one. An existing practice has revenue, a patient base and proven cash flow, so lenders underwrite real numbers. A new practice or de novo build has only projections, so expect 15-20% down, stronger credit and a detailed business plan. Some lenders specialize in new vet practices; others only do acquisitions. A common setup is equipment financing for the clinic equipment and SBA for the real estate and working capital.

When SBA May Not Fit

  • You need the money in under 30 days. SBA loans typically take 30-90 days; see how long SBA approval takes.
  • Your credit is below 660 and cannot be improved quickly.
  • You only need equipment and want it funded this week; an equipment loan is faster.
  • The practice has weak financials or the seller will not support the transition.

See SBA loan alternatives if you do not qualify.

Bottom Line

For most veterinarians, a practice loan means an SBA 7(a) with 10-15% down, a 504 if the building is part of the deal, and equipment financing when speed matters. Typical loans run $300,000 for a solo practice to $3 million or more for specialty and emergency hospitals. Get the practice financials, valuation and transition plan ready early. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use an SBA loan to buy a veterinary practice?

Yes. SBA 7(a) loans are commonly used for veterinary practice acquisitions and can combine the purchase price, equipment, build-out and working capital in one loan, usually with 10-15% down. SBA 504 can finance the building if you are buying it.

How much are typical veterinary practice loans?

A solo general practice usually borrows $300,000-$700,000, a multi-doctor practice $700,000-$1.5 million, and a specialty or emergency practice $1 million to $3 million or more. The amount depends on practice size, location, equipment and whether real estate is included.

What is the monthly payment on a veterinary practice loan?

A $540,000 SBA 7(a) loan (a $600,000 practice with 10% down) is about $7,136 a month at 10% over 10 years. A $1.2 million loan on the same terms is about $15,858 a month.

Can SBA finance veterinary equipment?

Yes. SBA 7(a) can finance X-ray, surgical and lab equipment, exam tables and other clinic equipment, and SBA 504 can finance long-life equipment. For equipment only, an equipment loan is usually faster, with approvals often in 24-72 hours.

What credit score do I need for a vet practice loan?

SBA lenders typically prefer 660-680+ for veterinary practice loans. Strong practice cash flow can give some flexibility, and a new graduate may need a co-signer or a larger down payment.

How long does a veterinary practice SBA loan take?

Typically 30-90 days from application to funding. The practice valuation, seller transition terms and lender review drive the timeline, so start early if you have a closing date.
