Using Your 401(k) to Buy a Business: ROBS and Other SBA Down-Payment Sources

How a ROBS rollover turns retirement savings into your equity injection, what a 401(k) loan or withdrawal costs you, and the other sources SBA lenders accept

← Back to SBA Loans Articles

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

You can use a 401(k) to buy a business without the early-withdrawal penalty or income tax through a ROBS (rollover for business startups): you set up a C-corporation with its own retirement plan, roll your 401(k) into that plan, and the plan invests in the company. That money can then serve as the equity injection on an SBA loan, typically 10% of the project, so $50,000 on a $500,000 purchase. A straight withdrawal is taxed and can be penalized, and a 401(k) loan counts as borrowed money. Lenders also accept savings, gifts, home equity and seller standby notes.

Most buyers have the down payment problem before they have a loan problem. SBA loans need an equity injection of about 10% of the project, and at least half of it has to be money that is not borrowed. For a lot of people the biggest pile of money they own is a 401(k). Here is how to use it, what it costs, and what else lenders accept. For how much you will need, see how much down payment an SBA loan requires.

How Do You Use a 401(k) to Buy a Business?

The standard way is a ROBS. Instead of cashing out, you move the money into the business as an investment:

  1. You form a C-corporation to own the business; the ROBS structure is built around a C-corp.
  2. The C-corp sets up its own retirement plan, and you roll your existing 401(k) or IRA into it.
  3. The plan buys stock in the C-corp, which puts the cash in the company.
  4. The company uses that cash as the equity injection, alongside the SBA loan and any seller note.

Because the money never leaves the retirement system as a distribution, there is no early-withdrawal penalty or income tax when it is set up correctly. It has to be set up by a specialist provider; Guidant Financial, Benetrends and Tenet Financial Group are among the specialized ROBS providers. Done right, it is a common and well-accepted source of equity injection.

ROBS vs 401(k) Loan vs Withdrawal: Which Should You Use?

Option How it works As equity? Watch out
ROBS rolloverYour new C-corp's retirement plan invests your rolled-over 401(k) in the companyYes; no penalty or income tax when set up correctlyNeeds a specialist provider and a C-corp
401(k) loanYou borrow from your plan and repay itBorrowed money, so it may be limitedThe repayment counts against your cash flow
Straight withdrawalYou cash out part of the accountYes, after tax and any early-withdrawal penaltyYou keep less than you take out

A 401(k) loan looks simple, but SBA lenders treat it like any borrowed injection: the repayment goes into your global cash-flow analysis, and borrowed money can only make up part of the injection. A straight withdrawal avoids that problem, but you lose part of the money to tax, so you need a bigger withdrawal to net the same down payment. For most buyers putting in more than a small amount, ROBS is the option that keeps the most money working.

Can You Use 401(k) Money to Start a Business?

Yes. A ROBS works for a startup or a new franchise unit the same way it works for an acquisition, because the money is an equity investment in your C-corp, not a payment to a seller. The harder part with a startup is the loan, not the 401(k): SBA lenders underwrite a new business on projections, so expect them to want more equity and a stronger plan. Franchise buyers often pair ROBS with an SBA 7(a); see franchise financing options.

Seller standby notes

On a business acquisition or partner buyout, a seller note on full standby (no payments for the life of the SBA loan) can satisfy up to half of the required injection. The seller effectively finances part of your down payment, which cuts your cash at closing. Structure it with your lender early; the full rule table by deal type is in SBA seller note and equity injection rules.

What lenders check on every source

  • Sourcing and seasoning: the lender traces where the money came from and how long it has been in your account. A sudden unexplained deposit raises questions.
  • Borrowed funds: money borrowed for the injection adds a payment to your global cash-flow analysis and can be limited or disallowed.
  • Gift documentation: a gift letter stating no repayment is expected, and a clean paper trail.
  • Real commitment: the SBA wants the injection to be genuine equity, not leverage in disguise.

Worked Example: Building a $50,000 Injection

Say you are buying a business for $500,000 and need a $50,000 equity injection. One lender-friendly mix: $30,000 from documented savings, a $10,000 gift from a family member with a gift letter, and a $10,000 seller note on full standby. If your savings are short, a ROBS rollover can replace the savings piece entirely. Every piece must be sourced and seasoned, so money that landed in your account last week without an explanation will be questioned.

The same injection falls apart if it is undocumented. A large deposit you cannot source, borrowed money that creates a payment competing with the SBA loan, or a seller note that is not truly on standby can each be disqualified. Every dollar needs a paper trail showing it is yours, or a gift, and not new debt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use my 401(k) to buy a business?

Yes. The usual route is a ROBS (rollover for business startups): you form a C-corp with its own retirement plan, roll your 401(k) into it, and the plan invests in the company. Set up correctly by a specialist, it avoids the early-withdrawal penalty and income tax, and the money can count as your SBA equity injection.

Can I use my 401(k) money to start a business?

Yes. A ROBS works for a startup or new franchise the same way as for an acquisition, because the money is an equity investment in your C-corp. The financing is the harder part, since SBA lenders underwrite a startup on projections and usually want more equity.

Can I use a 401(k) loan for an SBA down payment?

Sometimes, but it counts as borrowed money. The repayment goes into the lender's global cash-flow analysis, and borrowed funds can only make up part of the injection, so a ROBS or your own savings is cleaner.

How much tax will I pay if I withdraw from my 401(k) to buy a business?

A straight withdrawal is taxed as income, and if you are under retirement age it can also carry an early-withdrawal penalty, so you keep noticeably less than you take out. Your exact cost depends on your tax bracket; ask your tax advisor. A properly structured ROBS avoids both the tax and the penalty.

Can a seller note count toward the SBA down payment?

Yes, within limits. A seller note on full standby, with no payments for the life of the SBA loan, can count toward up to half of the required equity injection on a change of ownership.

Can you use home equity for the SBA down payment?

Sometimes. A HELOC or cash-out refinance adds a payment that the lender includes in your global cash-flow analysis, so it may be limited or disallowed if it strains repayment. Gift funds and your own cash are cleaner.
