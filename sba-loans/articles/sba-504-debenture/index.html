SBA 504 Debenture: How the CDC Loan Is Funded, Priced and Paid

The monthly debenture sale behind every 504 loan, how its rate is built from Treasury yields, and how to work out the interest you'll actually pay

← Back to SBA Loans

Updated November 11, 2026

Quick answer

A 504 debenture is the SBA-guaranteed bond a Certified Development Company (CDC) issues to fund its share, about 40%, of a 504 project. Debentures are pooled and sold to investors each month, and the rate is set on that pricing date as a spread over the 10-year Treasury (5-year for 10-year debentures). In the October 2026 example published by NADCO, that gave a 5.85% debenture rate and a 6.984% effective rate after ongoing fees, in line with the ~6-7% typical this year. How a Debenture Funds the CDC Loan

The CDC doesn't lend its own money for the long term. It approves your loan with SBA, the project closes with interim money, and the CDC's share is then refinanced by selling a debenture into a monthly pool. Investors buy participation certificates in that pool, and the sale proceeds pay off the interim loan. From then on you make one fixed monthly payment on the CDC loan for 10, 20 or 25 years.

StepWhat happensWho
1. ApprovalSBA approves the 504 loan and the debenture amountCDC and SBA
2. Interim financingConstruction or purchase is funded before the debenture is sold; usually the bankInterim lender
3. Closing packageLoan documents go to SBA counsel by the district cut-off dateCDC
4. Pooling and pricingDebentures are grouped into that month's pool and the rate is setSBA's fiscal and selling agent
5. FundingSale proceeds are distributed and pay off the interim lenderCentral Servicing Agent
6. RepaymentYou pay monthly; investors are paid semiannuallyBorrower, CSA, trustee

Under 13 CFR 120.890 almost any qualified source can provide the interim money, as long as it isn't you or an associate and doesn't come from an SBA program. In practice it's usually the bank that holds the first mortgage. For how the bank and CDC halves fit together, see what an SBA 504 loan is.

The Monthly Sale Calendar

Your rate isn't known when you close. It's set on the pricing date of the first sale your loan makes it into, and missing a cut-off pushes it to the next month. NADCO's October 2026 funding guide gives this calendar for the October 2026 sale:

DateMilestone
Thu, Sep 10District cut-off: closing packages due to SBA counsel
Thu, Sep 24Central Servicing Agent cut-off for loan documents
Mon, Oct 5Last day a loan can be pulled from the sale
Tue, Oct 6Announcement of final pool figures
Thu, Oct 8Pricing date: rates set and pools formed
Wed, Oct 14Funding date: proceeds distributed

So a loan whose package reached SBA counsel by September 10 was priced on October 8 and funded on October 14, about five weeks later. Ask your CDC which sale your loan is aiming for, and what the interim lender charges until then.

How the Debenture Rate Is Set

The rate on a 504 loan and its debenture is set by SBA and approved by the Secretary of the Treasury (13 CFR 120.932). In practice it's a Treasury yield on the pricing date plus a spread that the market sets at the sale.

Debenture termBenchmarkTypical use
25 years10-year Treasury yieldReal estate
20 years10-year Treasury yieldReal estate, long-life equipment
10 years5-year Treasury yieldEquipment

Investors accept a spread over Treasuries because the borrower can prepay and because the certificates trade less often than Treasuries. Your credit doesn't change the debenture rate: every loan in the same pool gets the same rate. The bank's first mortgage is priced separately, and that's where most of the difference between two 504 quotes comes from. Current ranges are on our SBA 504 loan rates page.

Debenture Rate vs Note Rate vs Effective Rate

A 504 quote can show three rates, and they're not interchangeable. Here is how NADCO built them up for debenture offering 2026-26J:

PieceRateWhat it is
Treasury yield5.282%The benchmark on the pricing date
+ Spread0.57%Set by the market at the sale
= Debenture rate5.85%Paid to investors, semiannually
Note rate5.906%The rate on your monthly note
+ SBA ongoing guaranty fee0.209%Yearly, on the unpaid balance
+ CSA fee0.10%Central Servicing Agent
+ CDC servicing fee0.625% minimumYearly, on the unpaid balance
Full-term effective rate6.984%As published for 2026-26J

Adding the note rate and the three fees gives about 6.84%. NADCO publishes 6.984% for that offering and points to Eagle Compliance's 504 rate tutorial for how the full-term effective rate is calculated, so ask the CDC for its effective rate rather than adding the pieces yourself. Two more cautions. The effective rate isn't an APR, because it leaves out the upfront fees financed into the debenture (see SBA 504 loan fees). And the SBA annual fee depends on the fiscal year your loan is approved in: it's 0.203% for fiscal 2027, not the 0.209% in this example.

Worked Example: How to Calculate 504 Loan Interest

Illustrative, not a quote. Take the $414,000 gross debenture from our fees example and the 2026-26J note rate of 5.906% over 25 years. Standard monthly amortization gives a principal and interest payment of about $2,643.67 a month.

First monthHow it's worked outAbout
Interest$414,000 x 5.906% / 12$2,037.57
PrincipalPayment minus interest$606.10
Ongoing fees$414,000 x (0.209% + 0.10% + 0.625%) / 12$322.23
Total first paymentPrincipal, interest and ongoing fees$2,965.90

Over the first year that comes to about $24,251 of interest and $3,835 of ongoing fees, with about $406,527 still owed. Because the fees are charged on the unpaid balance, they shrink each year along with the interest. A 20-year term at the same note rate would be about $2,943.62 a month before fees. Your CDC and the Central Servicing Agent produce the official schedule, which can differ by a few dollars from this approximation. For every year of a 25-year schedule, see the SBA 504 amortization schedule.

Who Holds Your Debenture and Who You Pay

Once it's sold, your debenture sits in a pool held by a trustee, and investors own participation certificates in the pool. SBA guarantees timely payment of principal and interest on those certificates (13 CFR 120.941), which is why insurance companies, banks and money managers buy them at a fairly narrow spread. You never deal with the investors. Your monthly payment goes to the Central Servicing Agent, which SBA appoints to move funds among borrowers, CDCs and SBA (13 CFR 120.954); NADCO's guide names Guidehouse in that role. The CDC keeps servicing the loan for you.

The same structure explains the prepayment rule. Investors are owed a fixed stream of payments, so the debenture can be repaid only in full, with a premium in roughly the first half of the term. The premium table is on our amortization schedule page. To compare real bank and CDC pairings, get matched with SBA 504 lenders or call (561) 268-0465. Approval is up to the lender, the CDC and SBA.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an SBA debenture?

It's the bond a Certified Development Company issues to fund its share of a 504 loan, usually about 40% of the project. SBA guarantees it, and it's sold to investors in a monthly pool, which is how the CDC can offer a fixed rate for 10, 20 or 25 years.

How is the SBA 504 debenture rate set?

On the pricing date of the monthly sale, as a spread over the 10-year Treasury yield for 20- and 25-year debentures, or the 5-year yield for 10-year debentures. SBA sets the rate with Treasury's approval. In NADCO's October 2026 example, 5.282% plus a 0.57% spread gave 5.85%.

When is my 504 rate locked?

When your debenture is priced in the monthly sale, which happens after closing. Missing a cut-off moves you to the next month's sale and its rate. The bank's first mortgage can often be locked earlier.

What is the difference between the 504 note rate and the effective rate?

The note rate is the interest rate on your monthly note. The effective rate adds the ongoing SBA, CSA and CDC servicing fees. For offering 2026-26J the note rate was 5.906% and the full-term effective rate 6.984%. Neither is an APR, because upfront fees are left out.

How do you calculate interest on a 504 loan?

Multiply the unpaid balance by the note rate and divide by 12 for each month's interest, then add the ongoing fees on the same balance. On a $414,000 debenture at 5.906%, the first month is about $2,038 of interest plus about $322 of fees.

Who do I make 504 debenture payments to?

To SBA's Central Servicing Agent, monthly. The agent passes payments to the trustee, which pays investors semiannually. Your CDC still services the loan and is your contact.
