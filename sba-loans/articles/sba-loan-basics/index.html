SBA Loan Basics: What You Need to Know

A guide to Small Business Administration loans-rates, terms, eligibility, and how to apply

← Back to Blog

February 21, 2025 - Axiant Partners

SBA loans are government-backed financing programs that help small businesses get funding with favorable terms-often lower down payments, longer repayment periods, and competitive interest rates. Here's what you need to know before you apply.

What Is an SBA Loan?

The Small Business Administration (SBA) doesn't lend money directly. Instead, it guarantees a portion of loans made by banks and other lenders. That guarantee reduces risk for the lender, which can mean better rates and terms for you. Common SBA programs include 7(a) loans (general purpose), 504 loans (real estate and equipment), and microloans (smaller amounts).

SBA 7(a) Loans

The 7(a) program is the SBA's most popular. Loan amounts can go up to $5 million. Funds can be used for working capital, equipment, inventory, debt refinancing, and more. Terms and rates are set by the lender within SBA guidelines; you'll typically see longer terms and competitive rates compared to many conventional business loans.

SBA 504 Loans

504 loans are designed for major fixed assets-purchasing land, buildings, or long-term equipment. They involve a partnership between a lender, a Certified Development Company (CDC), and the SBA. Down payments are often lower than with traditional commercial loans, and terms can extend to 10-25 years.

Eligibility and How to Apply

Eligibility depends on the program and lender: business size, use of proceeds, and ability to repay. You'll need documentation such as business and personal financial statements, tax returns, and a business plan. The process can take several weeks.
