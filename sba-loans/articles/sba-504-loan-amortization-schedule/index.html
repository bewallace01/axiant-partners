How a 504 Loan Amortizes

A 504 project has two loans with two schedules. The bank's first mortgage covers about 50% of the project. The Certified Development Company (CDC) loan covers about 40% and is funded by an SBA-guaranteed debenture. You put in about 10%. The SBA side is the predictable one: the debenture carries one fixed rate for its whole term and pays down to zero with level payments. The bank sets its own rate, amortization and maturity, and that is where a balloon or a rate reset usually comes from.

PieceShare of projectHow it pays down
Bank first mortgageAbout 50%Bank's terms; often amortized 20-25 years with a 5-10 year maturity or reset
CDC debentureAbout 40%Fixed rate, level payments, fully amortized over 10, 20 or 25 years
Your injectionAbout 10%Paid at closing; more for some startups and special-purpose buildings

The 10-year debenture is used for equipment; 20- and 25-year debentures are for real estate. For how the rate is set, see SBA 504 loan rates, and for the program itself, what an SBA 504 loan is.

10 vs 20 vs 25 Years: Payment and Total Interest

Same $620,000 and the same assumed 6.5%, so only the term changes. In practice longer debentures often price a little differently, so treat this as a comparison of terms, not of quotes.

Debenture termAbout per monthTotal interest
10 years$7,039.97$224,797
20 years$4,622.55$489,413

Going from 20 to 25 years lowers the payment by about $436 a month but adds about $146,000 of interest. The 25-year term mostly helps debt service coverage, the ratio lenders test against your cash flow. If coverage is comfortable, the 20-year term costs far less over time.

The Bank Piece and Its Balloon

The schedule above is only the SBA side. Keep the same project and assume the bank lends $750,000 at 7.75%, amortized over 25 years but maturing in 10. The payment is about $5,664.97 a month, so the two loans together come to about $9,851 a month.

Paying Off a 504 Debenture Early

The debenture can be prepaid, but SBA Form 1504 allows it only as a whole, not in part, and adds a repurchase premium in the early years. The premium is the remaining balance times the debenture's stated interest rate times a factor that falls each year:

Debenture termFactor by yearNo premium from
20 or 25 years1.00 in year 1, then down 0.10 a year to 0.10 in year 10Year 11
10 years1.00 in year 1, then down 0.20 a year to 0.20 in year 5Year 6

Using the example's balance and, for simplicity, 6.5% as the stated rate: paying off in year 4 (after about $587,187 is left at the end of year 3) costs about $587,187 x 0.065 x 0.70, or $26,717. In year 7 it's about $14,231. From year 11 it's zero. The stated rate on the debenture is lower than the all-in effective rate, so a real premium would usually come in a bit under these figures. The bank loan has its own prepayment terms, which vary by lender. If a sale is likely within a few years, see SBA 504 refinance and compare the premium with the rate you'd save.

How to Build Your Own 504 Schedule

  1. Get the gross debenture amount. Ask the CDC for the gross figure, which includes financed fees, not the net 40%.
  2. Use the effective rate. The CDC can quote the all-in rate that includes ongoing servicing and SBA annual fees.
  3. Pick the term. 25 or 20 years for real estate, 10 for equipment.
  4. Model the bank loan separately. Use its rate, its amortization and its maturity date.
  5. Add the two payments. Test the total against your cash flow, the way the lenders will.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an SBA 504 loan have a balloon payment?

The CDC debenture doesn't: it amortizes fully over 10, 20 or 25 years with a fixed payment. The bank's first mortgage can. Many are amortized over 20 to 25 years but mature or reset after 5 to 10, so check the bank's maturity date.

What is the monthly payment on an SBA 504 loan?

It depends on the debenture amount, rate and term, plus the bank loan. As an illustration, a $620,000 debenture at 6.5% is about $4,186 a month over 25 years, $4,623 over 20 and $7,040 over 10. A $750,000 bank loan at 7.75% over 25 years adds about $5,665.

How much of a 504 loan is paid off after 10 years?

On a 25-year debenture at about 6.5%, roughly 22%. In the example, $620,000 falls to about $480,570 after 10 years. A 20-year debenture pays down faster because more of each payment goes to principal.

Is there a prepayment penalty on an SBA 504 loan?

Yes, on the debenture for the first half of the term. SBA Form 1504 sets a premium of the balance times the stated rate times a factor: 1.00 in year 1, falling 0.10 a year to zero from year 11 on 20- and 25-year debentures, and falling 0.20 a year to zero from year 6 on 10-year debentures.

Can you make extra payments on a 504 debenture?

The debenture form allows repurchase as a whole, not in part, so a partial prepayment doesn't work the way it does on many bank loans. Ask the CDC how it handles extra payments. The bank loan follows the bank's own terms.
