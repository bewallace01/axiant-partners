Gas Station Financing: SBA 7(a), 504 and Conventional Options

How to finance buying or building a fuel-and-c-store site, and why the Phase I, the underground tanks and the fuel supply agreement decide the deal

Quick answer

Most gas station financing is an SBA 7(a) or 504 loan with 10-25% down. A conventional bank loan usually wants 30% or more because of the environmental risk. The 7(a) can roll the real estate, fuel equipment, inventory, branding fees and working capital into one loan of up to $5 million. Every lender requires a Phase I environmental site assessment on the underground tanks before closing. A $1.2 million station with 15% down leaves a $1,020,000 loan: about $9,269 a month at an illustrative 10% over 25 years.

Buying a gas station with a convenience store is part real estate deal, part retail deal and part environmental project. Lenders know how to finance it, but a few items decide whether it closes: the tank review, fuel and inside sales you can prove, and the fuel supply agreement. Here is how the money usually comes together.

Can You Get an SBA Loan for a Gas Station?

Yes. Gas stations and convenience stores are eligible for SBA loans, and the 7(a) is the usual choice because one loan covers the whole purchase. The catch is the lender, not the program: many SBA lenders decline fuel sites as a matter of policy because of the tank risk, and the environmental review has to follow the SBA's gas station rules starting with a Phase I. Here is why many SBA lenders decline gas stations, and where else to go.

How Much Money Do You Need to Buy a Gas Station?

Your cash need is the down payment plus closing costs and the environmental reports. On a $1.2 million station, 10-25% down is $120,000 to $300,000. A qualified buyer with fuel-site experience can land near 10-15%; a first-time operator, a weak environmental report or thin fuel-sales history pushes it toward 20-25%. SBA 504 treats a gas station as special-use property, which can mean 20% down (see SBA down payment rules).

A worked example: you buy a station with an attached c-store for $1.2 million and put 15% ($180,000) down. The $1,020,000 loan runs about $9,269 a month at 10% over 25 years, the kind of term you get when real estate is most of the deal. If the price were mostly business value with little real estate, the term would be closer to 10 years and the same loan would run about $13,479 a month. The lender will want the station's cash flow to cover that payment about 1.20 times after paying a store manager.

Why Does the Environmental Review Matter So Much?

This is the part that is unique to fuel. The underground storage tanks that hold gasoline and diesel can leak, and cleanup liability can run into the hundreds of thousands of dollars or more and stays with the property. So every lender requires a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment before closing. If the Phase I flags a recognized environmental condition, a Phase II (soil and groundwater sampling) follows, which typically adds 4-8 weeks, and any contamination has to be resolved or insured before money moves. An unresolved finding is the most common reason a gas station loan falls apart, so order the Phase I early.

How Do Lenders Read Gas Station Numbers?

A station has two profit centers, and lenders weigh them differently:

  • Fuel margin is thin and moves around: high volume, few cents a gallon. Lenders normalize it rather than trusting one strong year.
  • Inside (c-store) sales carry the real margin: tobacco, beverages, snacks, lottery and food service. A site with strong inside sales underwrites better than one living on fuel volume.
  • Branded vs unbranded: branded sites may carry fuel-supply or image-upgrade commitments a lender will review; unbranded sites avoid those but can show thinner fuel margin.

What Do Lenders Check on a Gas Station Loan?

  • A clean Phase I (the gating item) and current tank compliance and registration.
  • Fuel volume and inside sales you can verify: fuel reports, POS data and tax returns that reconcile, not just the seller's pro forma.
  • The fuel supply or jobber agreement: term, volume commitments and any branding paybacks.
  • DSCR of about 1.20x or better after a market manager salary, plus your operating experience.
  • Cash-handling controls: these are cash-heavy businesses, so consistent deposits matter.
  • Location and real estate: traffic counts and the value of the site, which can support a 504.

Frequently Asked Questions

What down payment do you need to buy a gas station?

Plan on about 10-25% down. SBA 7(a) and 504 are the most common loans and can put a qualified buyer near 10-15%; first-time operators, weaker environmental reports or limited fuel-sales history push it toward 20-25%. Conventional financing for gas stations is harder to get and usually wants 30% or more because of the environmental risk.

How much money do you need to own a gas station?

Enough for the down payment, closing costs and the environmental reports. On a $1.2 million station, 10-25% down is $120,000 to $300,000. With 15% down, the $1,020,000 loan runs about $9,269 a month at an illustrative 10% over 25 years.

Can you finance a gas station with an SBA loan?

Yes. SBA 7(a) (up to $5 million) can roll real estate, fuel equipment, inventory, branding fees and working capital into one loan, and SBA 504 fits larger real-estate-heavy purchases or ground-up builds. Many SBA lenders still decline fuel sites as policy, so the work is finding one that funds them.

Why does a gas station loan require an environmental report?

Gas stations have underground storage tanks, and a leak can create cleanup liability that attaches to the property. Lenders require a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment before closing; if it flags possible contamination, a Phase II (soil and groundwater testing) follows. Unresolved contamination is the most common reason a gas station loan dies.

What interest rate is typical on a gas station loan?

SBA 7(a) loans run about 9.75-12.25% in 2026, and gas stations tend to price at or near the SBA cap. On a 504, the CDC portion runs about 5.5-6.75% fixed and the bank portion about 7-9%. Equipment loans for dispensers or a car wash follow your credit, about 7-22% APR.

Do fuel supply or branding agreements affect financing?

They can. Branded stations (Shell, BP and others) often carry fuel supply or image-upgrade obligations, and a lender will review the supply agreement term, volume commitments and any branding-incentive paybacks that could come due. Unbranded stations avoid those commitments but may show thinner fuel margins.
