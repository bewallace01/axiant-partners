Businesses SBA Lenders Often Decline, and Where to Go Instead

Some businesses are barred by SBA rules. Many more are eligible but turned down by most SBA lenders. Telling the two apart decides where to apply next

← Back to SBA Loans

Updated November 13, 2026

Quick answer

Two different lists. The SBA bars certain types outright under 13 CFR 120.110, such as lenders, passive real estate landlords, apartment buildings, gambling-led businesses and cannabis. Many other businesses are eligible but declined by most SBA lenders, such as gas stations, restaurants and bars, vape shops and delivery routes. Those can still find a lender. Ineligible Under SBA Rules

SOP 50 10 8.1 lists the types no 7(a) or 504 lender can finance, for any purpose, including buying a building for the business's own use. The main ones:

Ineligible typeNote
Lenders and finance cos.Banks, finance, factoring and bail bond companies; pawn shops and check cashers can qualify on revenue mix
Passive real estateLandlords, developers, property held to lease; apartment buildings and mobile home parks included; hotels qualify with 50%+ stays of 30 days or less
Life insurance carriersIndependent agents are eligible
Pyramid or multilevelPyramid or multilevel sales plans
Gambling-led businessesOver one-third of revenue from gambling; casinos and skill-game businesses at any share
Illegal activityIllegal under federal, state or local law; includes marijuana businesses; hemp and CBD only within federal law and FDA rules
Prurient sexual contentOver 5% of revenue
Restricted patronageOr discriminatory hiring

Speculative businessesIncluding R&D, oil wildcatting and spec homes outside Builders CAPLines
Prior loss to governmentUnless fully satisfied
Abroad or Coastal BarrierLocated outside the U.S. or in the Coastal Barrier Resources System

If your business is on this list, no SBA lender can help, and the question is which non-SBA product fits. Real estate secured loans and conventional term loans are the usual starting points.

Eligible, but Often Declined

These are SBA-eligible businesses that many SBA lenders exclude under their own credit policy. Lenders differ, which is the reason to shop the deal. Typical reasons and realistic options:

BusinessWhy lenders hesitateWhere to look instead
Gas stations and USTsUnderground tank contamination risk; SBA requires a Phase I for NAICS 457 and Appendix 7 reviewLenders that do gas stations, real estate secured
Restaurants and barsThin margins, high failure rates, alcohol liabilityRestaurant financing, restaurant working capital
Vape and smoke shopsFast-changing regulation and product bansRevenue-based financing
Delivery routesFedEx-style contracts: revenue depends on one contract that can endDelivery van financing
Fleet-heavy companiesDepreciating vehicles carry the collateralFleet truck financing, truck title loans
Projections-only start-upsNo cash flow history to underwriteStartup financing
Standalone working capitalNo hard asset behind the loanLines of credit, invoice factoring

What the SBA Rules Actually Allow for These

  • Start-ups. SBA allows them. A business operating a year or less needs at least 10% equity, and projections must show debt service coverage of 1.15 within two years. Many lenders still want industry experience and some history.
  • Working capital. Eligible. When 50% or more of the proceeds are working capital, the lender has to justify it in the credit memo, which is one reason many avoid it.
  • Gas stations. Eligible, but the environmental investigation must start with a Phase I, which adds cost and time and can uncover cleanup.
  • Restaurants and bars. Eligible if they don't restrict patronage. A restaurant employing servers of only one gender is the SOP's own example of an ineligible one.
  • Credit elsewhere. Every SBA lender must certify you can't get the money on reasonable terms without the SBA. A strong borrower may be told to use a conventional loan.

Declined Once? Separate the Two Kinds of No

A "no" because your business type is ineligible is final across all SBA lenders. A "no" based on credit policy only covers that lender. If you were declined for cash flow, credit or missing paperwork, SBA loan denial reasons explains how to fix the file; if SBA simply isn't the right product, SBA loan alternatives compares the options by credit profile.

Three questions get to the bottom of a decline:

  1. Was it the business type or the file? Ask which policy it failed.
  2. Is the problem the collateral? If yes, adding real estate or a building purchase can change the answer.
  3. Is debt the real issue? If you have stacked advances, an SBA loan probably can't pay them off yet; see what debt an SBA loan can refinance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What businesses are not eligible for SBA loans?

Under 13 CFR 120.110: lenders and finance companies, passive real estate businesses, life insurers, pyramid sales, businesses with over one-third of revenue from gambling, illegal businesses, prurient businesses, government-owned and political businesses, speculative businesses and a few more.

Can a gas station get an SBA loan?

Yes, gas stations are eligible, but the environmental review must begin with a Phase I and follow SBA's gas station appendix. Many SBA lenders decline them as policy, so it takes the right lender.

Can a restaurant or bar get an SBA loan?

Yes, they're eligible. Many SBA lenders limit or avoid them because of failure rates and liability, so approval depends heavily on the lender and on cash flow history.

Can a cannabis business get an SBA loan?

Marijuana businesses generally can't, because federal law still prohibits marijuana. Hemp and CBD businesses can qualify only if their products meet federal law and FDA rules.

Can an apartment building get an SBA loan?

No. Apartment buildings, mobile home parks and businesses that own real estate to lease are passive businesses and ineligible. Hotels can qualify if most revenue comes from stays of 30 days or less.

If one SBA lender declines my business, will all of them?

Only if the business type is ineligible under SBA rules. A decline based on lender policy only reflects that lender, and others may approve it.
