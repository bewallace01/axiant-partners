Daycare Business Loans: SBA, Real Estate and Startup Financing for Childcare Centers

Loans to buy, build, equip or start a childcare center, and what lenders check before they say yes.

Home › Industries › Daycare Business Loans

Updated November 20, 2026

Quick answer

Most daycare business loans are SBA loans. An SBA 7(a) loan funds a center purchase, a buildout or a franchise startup up to $5 million, usually with about 10% down when you buy an existing center. SBA 504 finances an owner-occupied building with 10% equity and a 25-year amortization. Equipment loans cover classrooms and playgrounds ($25K-$300K), and a line of credit covers payroll between tuition cycles. A new center is harder: lenders want 5+ years of childcare experience and 25-30% down. Plan on 30-90 days for the license transfer.

Lenders like childcare more than most small businesses. Enrollment-driven revenue is predictable, there's a clear path to owning the building, and the big franchise concepts are well known to SBA lenders. The hard parts are licensing and staffing, and a good lender will ask about both early. This guide covers the loans that fit each kind of deal: buying a center, owning the building, starting from scratch, and equipment. For the building itself, see SBA loans for a daycare building.

The SBA loan is the workhorse because its long terms keep the payment low against tuition revenue. It takes 30-60+ days, so start early if you're working against a closing date or a lease.

How do you finance buying a daycare center?

Most childcare purchases combine an SBA 7(a) loan with a seller note. Here is a representative $1.5 million purchase of an 80-child center:

  • SBA 7(a): $1.2 million (80% of the price). Over 10 years at 10.75% APR, that's about $16,361 a month in principal and interest.
  • Seller note: $225,000 (15%). $75,000 of it sits on full standby, meaning no payments for the life of the SBA loan, which lets it count as half of the 10% equity the SBA requires. The rest is repaid on terms you agree with the seller.
  • Your cash: $75,000 (5%).

The lender will want to see that the state license can transfer to you, enrollment data (capacity, utilization, waitlist), a staff retention plan with credentials, tuition stability, fire and health inspection history, and a Phase I environmental report if the building is part of the deal. The license transfer is usually the slowest step, at 30-90 days depending on the state. For how the equity and seller note rules work in any purchase, see business acquisition financing.

Should a daycare own its building?

Often, yes. Compliant childcare space is hard to find because of zoning and licensing rules, and owning the building protects you from a landlord who sells or won't renew. SBA 504 is built for this:

  • 10% down: a bank lends 50% of the project, the SBA-backed portion 40%, and you bring 10%.
  • 25-year amortization on the real estate, with a fixed rate on the SBA portion.
  • Your center must occupy at least 51% of the building if you lease part of it out.

An SBA 7(a) loan can also buy the building, over up to 25 years. Space rules, licensed capacity and a worked example are in SBA loans for a daycare building, and SBA 504 vs conventional CRE compares the two routes.

How much do you need to start a daycare center?

A new center buildout typically runs $200K-$1.5M, and a franchise startup $300K-$2M. Where you land depends on the number of children you're licensed for and whether you lease or buy. Expect to put in 25-30% yourself: on a $600,000 project, that's roughly $150,000-$180,000.

Can you get a loan to start a new daycare? Yes, but lenders want more than they would for a purchase: 5+ years running a center or working in early childhood education, a signed lease or LOI, a clear licensing path, and month-by-month projections showing how enrollment ramps. If you're starting from scratch with no operating history, see startup financing with no revenue.

Franchise or independent?

  • Franchise (Goddard School, Primrose Schools, Kiddie Academy, Children's Lighthouse, La Petite Academy): lenders treat established concepts as lower risk, and SBA approvals tend to move faster for known brands. You pay an initial franchise fee and an ongoing royalty, so read the franchise disclosure document for the exact amounts.
  • Independent: lenders want stronger personal financials and more management experience. You keep more of the margin and control your curriculum, hours and pricing.

How much does a $50,000 equipment loan cost?

Classroom furniture, kitchen equipment, playgrounds and curriculum technology are usually financed with an equipment loan secured by the equipment. With a 680-719 credit score, rates typically run 10-13% APR over 4-6 years. At 12% over 60 months, $50,000 costs about $1,112 a month.

What to look for in a daycare lender

A lender that already has childcare loans on its books will know how licensing transfers work in your state, how to read enrollment and waitlist data, and why a staffing plan matters. General lenders often stall on those same questions. Ask any lender how many childcare deals it closed in the past year and whether it has financed your franchise brand. State programs and grants

Many states run childcare-specific money alongside regular loans:

  • Facility loan funds: some states, including California, New York and Illinois, run state-level childcare facility loan programs.
  • Quality rating incentives: states often tie tax credits or grants to QRIS (Quality Rating and Improvement System) ratings.
  • Federal CCDBG subsidies: these flow through the states to families and providers and steady your tuition revenue; they aren't loans.
  • Local incentives: some cities offer incentives for new centers in childcare deserts.

Check your state's Department of Human Services or Office of Child Care for current programs.

Daycare business loan questions

What loans can I get for a daycare business?

SBA 7(a) loans are the main tool for buying a center, building one out or opening a franchise, up to $5 million. SBA 504 finances an owner-occupied building with 10% equity, equipment loans cover classrooms and playgrounds, and a line of credit covers payroll between tuition payments.

How do I finance buying a daycare?

Most purchases use an SBA 7(a) loan plus a seller note. The SBA minimum equity is 10%, and a seller note on full standby can supply up to half of it, so about 5% can come from your cash. Lenders will check that the state license can transfer, along with enrollment, waitlist, staff retention and tuition history.

How much money do I need to start a daycare center?

A new center buildout typically runs $200K-$1.5M and a franchise startup $300K-$2M, depending on size and whether you own the building. For a startup, lenders usually want 25-30% of the project from you, so a $600,000 project means roughly $150,000-$180,000 down.

Can a new daycare business get an SBA loan?

Yes, but it's harder than buying an operating center. Lenders typically want 5+ years in early childhood education or center management, 25-30% equity, a signed lease or LOI, a clear licensing path and detailed projections. Established franchise brands have SBA lender relationships that make startups easier.

How much would a $50,000 daycare equipment loan cost?

With a 680-719 credit score, equipment loans typically run 10-13% APR. At 12% over 60 months, $50,000 of classroom, kitchen or playground equipment costs about $1,112 a month. Stronger credit (720+, 2+ years in business) can bring the rate to 7-9%.

Are there state childcare grants or loans?

Yes. Some states, including California, New York and Illinois, run childcare facility loan programs, and many tie grants or tax credits to their QRIS quality rating. Federal CCDBG money reaches operators as subsidies through the states rather than as loans. Check your state's Department of Human Services or Office of Child Care.
