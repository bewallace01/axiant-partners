100% SBA Financing: Buying Your Business's Building With No Money Down

Some 7(a) lenders will finance the entire purchase for an established, cash-flowing business. Who qualifies, what it costs, and why most lenders still want money down

← Back to SBA Loans

Updated November 05, 2026

Quick answer

Some SBA 7(a) lenders finance up to 100% of an owner-occupied building for an established, cash-flowing business, on loans up to the SBA's $5 million limit over 25 years, with fees rolled in. Your business must occupy at least 51%. Start-ups and first-time purchases of a business don't qualify for zero down. Axiant Partners, a broker in Boca Raton, can find these lenders. Why 100% Is Possible on This Kind of Loan

SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 sets minimum equity for start-ups (one year or less in operation) and for changes of ownership, at 10%. For an existing business buying its own building, with no change of ownership, the SOP has no such fixed percentage. Instead, the lender or the SBA has to decide that the equity position and resulting debt-to-worth are acceptable. That leaves room for some lenders to go to 100%.

It's lender appetite, not an SBA entitlement. Many 7(a) lenders still ask for 10% or more as their own policy. A "no" from your bank doesn't mean the SBA forbids it, which is where a broker can help.

Who Usually Qualifies

These are common among lenders offering this structure, not SBA requirements:

  • Established operations. Two or more years, with cash flow last year and this year that supports the new payment. Projections alone rarely work.
  • Credit. Many SBA lenders look for roughly 650-680 or higher, and some go to the mid-660s with strong cash flow.
  • Clean history. A prior bankruptcy is often considered after about seven years, with a reasonable explanation.
  • Personal guarantees. Owners generally guarantee SBA loans, and lenders will usually take the building as collateral.
  • Citizenship. All direct and indirect owners and required guarantors must be U.S. citizens or nationals who live in the U.S. or its territories, under the SOP.

Many SBA lenders exclude gas stations and anything with underground tanks, restaurants and bars, vape shops, FedEx routes and fleet-heavy companies, along with standalone working capital and projections-only deals. If that's you, look at a real estate secured loan or working capital instead.

The Occupancy Rule and What Owner-Occupied Means

Under 13 CFR 120.131 and the SOP, you must permanently occupy and use at least 51% of the rentable property in an existing building, and may lease out up to 49%. For new construction it's 60%, with stricter rules on the rest. An investor buying a building to rent doesn't qualify, so the owner-occupied test is what makes this possible.

  • Holding companies. A related real estate company can own the building and lease all of it to your operating company, which must then meet the same occupancy test.
  • Extra land. Land in the purchase should be used by the business, for example paved for parking or used as a yard. Lenders question excess land.
  • Improvements. Small tenant improvements can often be included, but lenders commonly want them funded within a few months.

What to Expect After You Apply

  1. Gather three years of business and personal returns, plus year-to-date statements and your debt schedule.
  2. Get the contract and property details. Lenders want the address, price and how much of the building you'll occupy.
  3. Prequalify across lenders. Appetite for zero down varies widely.
  4. Order third-party reports. Expect an appraisal and environmental review, which is important for shops with lifts, tanks or chemicals.
  5. Close. SBA real estate deals usually take longer than a bank's. Plan for it in your purchase contract.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy my business's building with no money down?

Sometimes. Some SBA 7(a) lenders finance up to 100% for an established business that occupies at least 51% of an existing building and has the cash flow to support the payment. Not every lender offers this.

Does the SBA require a down payment on commercial real estate?

The SOP sets a 10% minimum for start-ups and changes of ownership. For an existing business buying its own building, the lender or SBA instead decides whether the equity position is acceptable, and many lenders set their own minimum.

Why does a start-up not qualify for 100%?

A business that has operated for one year or less needs at least 10% equity on a 7(a) loan, based on total project cost. The same 10% minimum applies to a change of ownership, including real estate bought in that deal.

Can I rent out part of the building?

Yes, up to 49% of an existing building, as long as your business permanently occupies and uses at least 51% of the rentable property.

How is 100% 7(a) different from an SBA 504 loan?

A 504 generally needs at least 10% from you, and 15-20% in some cases, in exchange for a fixed rate on the SBA portion. A 7(a) can reach 100% with some lenders, usually on an adjustable rate.

Are there businesses that can't get this?

Investors buying rental property aren't eligible. Many lenders also exclude gas stations, restaurants and bars, vape shops, delivery routes and fleet-heavy companies, though lenders differ.
