Commercial Real Estate Loans for Owner-Occupied Businesses

Structured Property Financing from $10,000 to $10,000,000+

Commercial real estate financing helps All businesses purchase, refinance, or improve owner-occupied property used for operations. Programs include SBA 504, SBA 7(a), and conventional commercial mortgages.

What Is a Commercial Real Estate Loan?

A commercial real estate (CRE) loan is financing used to acquire or refinance property used primarily for business operations.

Common owner-occupied examples include:

  • Office buildings
  • Retail storefronts
  • Industrial units
  • Medical or professional buildings
  • Mixed-use facilities mostly occupied by your business

Most lenders require at least 51% owner occupancy for owner-occupied structures.

Types of Commercial Real Estate Financing

SBA 504 Loan (Popular for Owner-Occupied Property)

Typical structure:

  • Up to 90% total project financing
  • 10% to 20% down payment in many cases
  • Long-term fixed-rate component
  • Lower equity requirements than many conventional options

See owner-occupied SBA 504 and 7(a) options.

SBA 7(a) Real Estate Loan

Often used for:

  • Property acquisition with business financing combined
  • Refinance plus expansion costs
  • Flexible use of proceeds

Conventional Commercial Mortgage

Typical profile:

  • Strong credit and stable cash flow preferred
  • 20% to 30% down payment common
  • May offer lower rates than non-bank options for strong borrowers

How Much Down Payment Is Required?

Loan Program Typical Down Payment
SBA 504 10% to 20%
SBA 7(a) 10% to 20%+
Conventional 20% to 30%

Down payment varies by credit profile, property type, occupancy, and lender policy.

Loan Amounts & Terms

Commercial real estate loans generally range from $10,000 to $10,000,000+ depending on project size, cash flow, and program structure.

  • Amortization commonly 20 to 25 years
  • SBA terms can provide long repayment windows
  • Conventional loans may include fixed or variable periods
  • Rates depend on credit profile, debt service coverage, and property fundamentals

What Do Lenders Look For?

CRE approvals usually evaluate both business and property strength:

  • Revenue stability
  • Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)
  • Credit profile (business and personal)
  • Down payment or equity contribution
  • Property condition and occupancy quality

Strong cash flow and experienced operations improve approval odds and terms.

Owner-Occupied vs Investment Property

Owner-occupied CRE is often treated differently than investor-only property.

  • Owner-occupied financing typically has lower equity requirements than investment property loans
  • SBA programs are designed for operating businesses that occupy the property
  • Investment-only CRE generally follows separate underwriting guidelines

When Is a Commercial Real Estate Loan the Right Move?

CRE financing can be a strong fit when:

  • You are paying high rent and need long-term occupancy control
  • Your business has stable revenue and profitability
  • You want to build long-term equity in a property
  • You are consolidating operations into a dedicated location
